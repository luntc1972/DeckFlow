## Summary

The video explains "counterweight commanders": commanders chosen not to synergize directly with the 99 but to offset a deck's weakness, forming a mutualistic whole. The concept emerges from rigorously interrogating what the command zone guarantees you. The Malfegor deck pairs grindy low-cost creatures and discard with a commander that clears opposing boards; the 99 nullifies Malfegor's hellbent downside. Radha, Heir to Keld covers low-cost ramp, letting the deck run nothing below four mana with only 41 lands, boosting cascade and topdeck quality. Glissa Sunslayer gives a greedy big-mana deck a guaranteed early utility play and blocker. Liesa cuts life totals in a hatebears/stax shell. The Francisco/Malcolm deck (with Time Sieve) is engine, not counterweight. Limitations: if the commander is the deck's only source of a key role, removal or a wipe stalls it — the Torsten deck (only draw source, behind a 7-9 mana wall) illustrates this; Radha shrugs off early removal because it's an inevitability deck. Counterweight decks risk becoming "piles of generic dudes" lacking synergy. The Kambal budget combo-control deck uses lifegain to "control them with your life total," bait boardwipes, hate noncreature decks, and fuel Sanguine Bond wins. Takeaway: a commander's power comes from context.
