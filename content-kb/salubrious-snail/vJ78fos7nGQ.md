## Summary

The video explains slot compression: fitting more of each desired card category (finishers, draw, removal) into an EDH deck by choosing cards that do multiple jobs. Midrange decks compress by running well-statted creatures that also draw cards or force discard; even finishers can double as blood-artist effects that discourage board wipes. High-curve decks like Glissa pay heavily for ramp but get removal and draw stapled to large bodies, winning without dedicated wincon slots. Hate bears use stax effects that are also creatures, oppressing opponents while pressuring life totals. Combo decks gain space by picking pieces that stand on their ownΓÇöaltars in artifact decks, sac outlets that buff, or generically good cards like Fanatical Devotion and Greater Good; Najeela can run low-cost warriors that advance both beats and combo. Synergy decks built around a card type, creature type, or attribute (e.g., Goreclaw power-4+) make supporting cards reinforce wincons and improve conditional tutors. Crucial caveat: smoothness and interaction aren't substitutes for a real way to winΓÇödecks need both substantive wincons and supporting consistency.

## Key Clips

- The idea of slot compression is basically thisΓÇöyou can fit more stuff into your deck, more things of each category like finishers or draw or removal, if you pick out and play cards that can do multiple things.
- For midrange decks, an easy way to do slot compression is to run decently-statted creatures that do other things your deck wants to do. In a dirt cheap Rakdos deck I built, the vast majority of the creatures either draw me cards or make my opponents discard cards.
- Fun factΓÇöif you pay a lot of mana for a creature, it will often be great for multiple things. My Glissa deck caches in a hefty 20 or so slots up front for ramp, but is rewarded with a creature base of powerful removal and draw spells stapled to 5/5s, 8/8s and 10/10s, ending up able to win games without a single dedicated wincon slot.
- Hate bears decks run stax effects that are also creatures, so they heavily oppress opponents' game plans while still having a boardstate to block and pressure life totals.
- For combo decks, pick cards that do both. If you need a sac outlet, you could run a bunch of altars in a deck with artifact synergies, or sac outlets that get buffed for an alternative beatdown wincon, or cards like Fanatical Devotion and Greater Good. If a combo piece is also valuable in its own right, the combo feels much less space-gobbling.
- For heavily synergistic decks focusing on a card type like equipments or auras, having supporting cards also fit those types means your wincons pack a ton of power, and conditional tutors will give you access to a good portion of your deck. Same for a Goreclaw deck where draw and removal mostly consists of creatures with power 4 or greater.
- On lands: MDFCs, utility lands, and other dual mode cards that get you lands are great. Don't run land counts in the low 30s in decks that aren't low-curvedΓÇöthrow in MDFCs or utility lands to bring your count up to 35 or 36, and high-curved decks running 36 lands can MDFC up to 38, 39, or into the 40s.
- An important thing to rememberΓÇöat its core, your deck still needs to do a thing that wins. I've fallen into the hole of synergy and support cards and built a deck that runs smoothly but lacks substantive wincons and gets outscaled. Playing smoothly and having interaction isn't a substitute for having a good way to end the gameΓÇöyou should have both.
