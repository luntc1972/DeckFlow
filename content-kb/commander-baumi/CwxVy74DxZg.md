## Summary

The video argues Commander decks need dedicated, powerful finishers because reaching 120 total life is hard with three opponents disrupting you, yet most popular EDHREC cards aren't true finishers. It pushes budget green as fully competitive, presenting nine cards costing ~€16 combined that rival Crater Hoof Behemoth (deemed overrated, not unnecessary). Mana Gorge Hydra is hailed as the biggest stat-stick in the game: a free creature with trample that grows with every spell cast, often gaining 10+ counters per turn and tanking board wipes like Blasphemous Act. Ramp finishers include Traverse the Outlands (ramps basics equal to greatest power), Spelunking (untaps lands, making ramp spells mana-positive), and Mana Reflection (doubles mana, self-paying with 12 sources). Azusa's/Izoni-style predation builds 4/4 beast armies and draws cards alongside ETB-payoffs. Overruns close games: Overwhelming Stampede works for wide and tall boards, while Endrek/Annie's forerunners give vigilance, trample, haste—enabling aggression plus defense. Bane of Progress punishes mana-rock-heavy tables, often entering as a 10+. Spara/Springheart Nantuko clones creatures cheaply and repeatedly on landfall, synergizing with everything green does. Core thesis: don't turtle—play overruns and actually end the game.

## Key Clips

- Crater Hoof Behemoth is overrated, not because it's not good, but because it's not necessary. The kinds of boards that Crater Hoof turns into a win are also the kinds of boards that can be enabled by other cheaper overruns. And when I say cheaper, I do mean monetarily cheaper.
- I've prepared a package of nine cards that cost a combined €16, just a tad bit more than a single Crater Hoof, while each being individually powerful enough to push a game to its conclusion. I'm going to assume monogreen for my explanations, but you can play these in any green inclusive deck.
- Mana Gorge Hydra is a free mana 1/1 with trample. It also gains a plus-one-plus-one counter whenever a player casts a spell. It's the biggest stat stick in the game. I usually expect Mana Gorge Hydra to get at least 10 counters per turn rotation. It will just straight up tank a Blasphemous Act, and most importantly it already has trample, meaning it can't really be blocked.
- Traverse the Outlands is a five mana sorcery that lets us ramp X basics, where X is the greatest power among creatures we control. We should have a four or five attack creature on the board at basically all times. Even with only that, Traverse will ramp out four lands for five mana, which is an absolutely insane rate.
- Spelunking is a free mana enchantment that when it enters lets us draw a card and put a land from our hand into play. Its real power comes from casting ramp spells and being able to immediately use our newly acquired mana, effectively reducing the casting cost of any single land ramp spell by one. What makes it better than other untappers is it also acts as ramp itself.
- Mana Reflection is a six-mana enchantment that says whenever we tap a permanent for mana, we add twice as much instead. So long as we have 12 mana sources in play, it immediately pays for itself. Tap six, play Mana Reflection, tap the other six for 12. Next turn, we are untapping with 24.
- Overwhelming Stampede is a five-mana sorcery that gives all of our creatures trample and plus-X-plus-X where X is the greatest power among creatures we control. What makes it special compared to other overruns is that it works well for both wide and tall boards. The downside is being a sorcery rather than a creature — that's where End-Raze Forerunners come in, an eight mana 7/7 with vigilance, trample, and haste.
- Bane of Progress is a six-mana 2/2 that destroys all artifacts and enchantments when it enters, then gets a plus-one-plus-one counter for each permanent destroyed this way. Because of the abundance of mana rocks, it usually enters as at least a 10, more commonly a 15 or more, and forces opponents into a state of rebuilding they can't catch up from.
