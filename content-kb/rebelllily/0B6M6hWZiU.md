## Key Clips

- The deck I played on Shuffle Up and Play is neither a clone deck nor a control deck. When I first played Commander I was not the spike; in fact I was the anti-spike. I like weird brews while my friends kept playing more and more powerful Commander decks.
- This got me thinking: what if I made a deck whose entire job is to steal the win at the last minute? Big Fireball, here's a Twincast, drew a lot of cards to dig for your win, here's a Cerebral Vortex, or my personal favorite, stop them when they get close with a Time Stop or Sudden Spoiling, then cast Worst Fears to control the player and execute the combo with a slight change of fireballing himself and the table.
- That's when I wondered: what if I role-played Nicol Bolas, master manipulator of the Multiverse? How would the ultimate Puppet Master play Magic the Gathering? He wouldn't be so obvious to show his hand; he'll seduce you to join his side, create new enemies for the foolish heroes to fight, and just when the plan starts to reveal itself it's already too late. The deck doesn't just control the win, you control the players.
- This is where the politics comes in. Yes, someone will always say 'I don't trust Bolas and I'm going to spend all my resources to deal with them.' What ends up happening is the two other players will often develop and eventually a real problem reveals themselves, and that's when you get leverage. This Bolas deck will never look the scariest even if you've drawn a lot of cards.
- This is also why you can never play Punisher cards like Gleeful Arsonist, because that generates threat. It gives you a face-up reason for the table to attack you.
- Golden rule one: always have an exit plan. This could be something like a Sudden Spoiling or an Illusionist's Gambit or just a piece of removal. If you can read someone is close to winning then never tap yourself out, so you can always give yourself an opportunity to save the table.
- Golden rule two: don't counter everything. Everybody has played against that control deck that stops literally everything and it's a dreary long game where nothing happens. You should only remove something when you get some social benefit out of it, which will maximize your ability to play the table and stay in the game.
- Golden rule four: plan for the long game. The tides can change very quickly. Always think a few turns ahead of generally who you can rely on the best to win the game with, and do everything you can to push the narrative of the game to the universe where you win off that player.
