## Summary

The creator revisits his year-old, controversial video "The Problem with Budget Cards," re-examining whether cheap, powerful cards harm Commander. He stands by most arguments but admits the intro argued poorly. Sol Ring, fast mana printed cheaply across precons, became a format symbol kept affordable through reprints; he believes not reprinting it would have curbed fast mana in casual EDH (85% of decks run Sol Ring versus 11% for the pricier Mana Crypt). His real objection is that fast mana warps game tempo, not classismΓÇöhe argues casual EDH has no power level to keep up with, and ubiquitous staples breed uncreative decks; better solutions are proxying or talking with playgroups. Cards mismatched in price and power warp deckbuilding: players add a few obvious 'vegetable' staples (ramp, interaction) then overload on synergistic win-more cards, hurting consistency. Variable power levels add variance, which equalizes between decks but draws opponent attention to high-variance decks. His conclusion: there's no inherent problem with cheap powerful cards; instead, players should predetermine how many of each card type they need, balance staples against synergy pieces, and choose cards suited to their deck and desired game experience.

## Key Clips

- Sol Ring being a staple is a flawed argument for reprinting it, because the card was only a staple due to its affordable price, itself a result of it not being legal in a large format. Printing a set for commander would naturally grow the format, grow the demand for Sol Ring, and without a reprint the card's price would be pushed in line with similar fast mana cards such as Manacrypt.
- Players will add the best options within their budget, and then a lot of them will notice the rapidly worsening power-to-money ratio and stop adding them at a certain point, even if running more of that type of card would be better for their deck. This leads to players adding a handful of the most powerful vegetable staples, and then overloading on synergistic cards, leading to decks that are crammed with win-more cards but lack a lot of the basic building blocks that lead to a deck performing well consistently.
