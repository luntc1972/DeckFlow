## Summary

A thought experiment proposing three (jokingly four) house-rule changes to improve Commander. First, reduce deck size from 100 to 80 cards, framed as a physicality fix: 100-card decks are unwieldy and hard to shuffle, especially for players with smaller hands or double-sleeved decks, while 80 cards still preserves Singleton variety. Second, lower starting life from 40 to 30. The creator argues 40 life is too large a buffer, encouraging passive ramp-and-engine play where players soak damage; red aggro and burn strategies become unviable. Lowering to 30 makes games more dynamic, shortens length toward a 60-75 minute norm, and widens viable strategies, though it would make some high-damage cards (e.g. Aurelia/'urio') stronger. Third, give the player going last a treasure token at game start to offset the mana disadvantage of acting fourth—letting them effectively 'go first' once, using familiar game pieces for easy adoption. The creator stresses these are experimental mods, that Commander is itself a community-made mod of Magic, and that playtesting is the most important part of game design. They invite viewers to try the 30-life and free-treasure changes and share their own house rules. The fourth joke 'rule': get rid of an unnamed card.

## Key Clips

- Commander wasn't made like the rest of magic — there was no design team that carefully crafted its intricacies. Some guy had an idea for his own way to play Magic and told his friends. The rules were designed to not work with magic, and then magic was designed to work with those new rules, which led to clunkiness within Commander.
- Rule change one: reduce deck size from 100 to 80. 100-card decks are too many physical cards, making decks unwieldy and hard to shuffle — particularly for players with smaller hands, and worse with double-sleeved decks. 80 cards still keeps plenty of Singleton variety while being much easier to handle.
- Commander isn't a balanced game. Combo is overwhelmingly the best strategy to win. 40 life per player is a huge amount to get through; normal magic is designed for 20 life, so many cards that are great in 1v1 can't work in Commander because their damage output is too low. Red suffers most — aggressive burn strategies aren't remotely viable.
- Commander incentivizes players to only ramp and build engines during the early game while soaking any damage, because with 40 life it's not enough to truly impact them. The gameplay incentives are built to enable and encourage this strategy, creating midgame value engines that try to outrun each other without engaging.
- Rule change two: lower starting life from 40 to 30. Every game tried at 30 felt more dynamic and natural because there's no longer a huge buffer before life points matter. Playing aggressively becomes much more viable and the range of viable strategies increases while nerfing some of the currently best ones.
- Going last in Commander can feel terrible — being fourth is a Mana disadvantage on turn two. The player going first uses two Mana to respond to one-Mana plays, while the player going last faces two-Mana plays with only two Mana, and this Rhythm persists the whole game; they never take the Mana initiative.
- Rule change three: the player going last starts the game with a treasure token in play. This way they get to effectively go first once per game, and it addresses the actual Mana-initiative problem rather than a scry. It only uses game pieces everyone already knows, making it easy to implement.
- The most important aspect of game design by far is play testing. Sometimes you think you understand how things will work out in practice and you're so far off it's not even funny. Try the changes — a free treasure for the last player and 30 starting life instead of 40 — before being too critical.
