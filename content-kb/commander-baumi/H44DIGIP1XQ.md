## Summary

This deck is built around Kroxa, Titan of Death's Hunger, a two-mana 6/6 that forces each opponent to discard on enter and attack, dealing 3 damage if they discard a land or can't discard. Kroxa dies on entry but can be cast from the graveyard by paying mana and exiling five other cards, after which he stays permanently and is highly resilient. The strategy grinds opponents out of resources: the recurring discard triggers, almost guaranteed once per turn, dismantle most strategies into a slugfest. Discard payoffs like Geth's Grimoire (draw on opponent discard), Bventa and Tinybones provide card advantage. Self-mill is needed to fuel Kroxa, but the builder favors engine pieces that incidentally mill (Ripples of Undeath, Lurrus-style value, Embrace the Unknown with retrace, Spinner of Souls) over pure self-mill. Many board wipes (Burn Down the House, Nuclear Fallout, Blasphemous Act, Decree of Pain, two Massacres) clear opponents while Kroxa survives. Additional beaters and instant-speed interaction round it out. The plan is to chip opponents down with Kroxa attacks while controlling the board. Around Γé¼60, intended for bracket 2. ## Key Clips

- Croxa, Titan of Death Hunger, is a two mana 6/6 that when he enters or attacks, forces each of our opponents to discard a card, and if they can't, or if they discard a land, then they also lose three life. When he enters, he dies immediately. But we can play Croxa out of the graveyard by paying four mana and feeding him five other cards, and when he reanimates this way, Croxa gets to stay on the battlefield for good.
- Since we get his trigger on both attack and ETB, it's almost guaranteed to happen at least once per turn. Most commander decks can't handle that. The first discard tends to be fine. The second is felt much more closely, and the third dismantles the majority of strategies and turns the game into a crawling slugfest where everybody is constantly struggling for any resources they can get.
- Geths is probably the strongest card in the deck. It's a four-mana artifact that lets us draw a card whenever one of our opponents discards a card. This gives us basically infinite card draw as soon as it hits the board and more or less wins the game by itself. Vexing Bauble and Tinybones, Bauble Burglar fill a similar role, providing us with various resources as we force our opponents to discard.
- Because three things are getting pointed at us every turn, the deck needs a lot of interaction, ideally sweepers, as those hit a lot of stuff and we don't really care about our board dying. Burn Down the House is a five mana spell that deals five damage to all creatures, killing the vast majority of board states, but Croxa powers through. We also run nuclear fallout, blood money, mutinous massacre, decree of pain, blasphemous act, and the Marauding Massacre.
- We need to self-mill in this deck; playing Croxa without actively filling our graveyard is quite difficult. However, I'd advise staying away from cards that only self-mill and instead focus on engine pieces that happen to dump things into our graveyard. Ripples of Undeath is a two mana enchantment that mills us for free every turn and lets us pay a mana and three life to put a card from among the milled into our hand.
- Embrace the Unknown is a red three mana sorcery that lets us impulse draw two cards, and it has retrace, meaning we can play it out of our graveyard by discarding a land card. This assures we can never get land flooded since we can always discard a land to dig deeper, any number of times. It's an amazing consistency piece that comes at basically no deck building cost.
- Lock, Cursed Treasure Hunter was an absolute MVP. He's a free-to-cast creature that can't be blocked by creatures with greater power. When he attacks, each player mills a card, we create a treasure token if a land was milled, and we get to cast a spell milled this way until end of turn. He's like Ragavan's big brother, generating mana and card advantage every turn while also helping us self-mill.
- It's a bit of a controlling deck, but it also likes to get on the board and fight. It just fights dirty. It's around 60 euros and intended for bracket 2 games. I ultimately took the deck apart because a lot of my opponents just didn't enjoy the play patterns they were forced into, and that took the fun out of it for me.
