## Summary

The reviewer dismisses the TMNT Universes Beyond set as a lazy reference set but digs out nine Commander-worthy cards, prioritizing resource-grind value over flashy finishers. Honorable mentions: Lessons from Life (better Urban Evolution), Ground Chuck (clunky mono-green mana doubler 8/8), and Swift the Mice (gamble-y free removal). Group one: Coin of Mastery buffs creatures via artifact mana, ideal for treasure/rock decks with under-statted utility creatures; Continue is instant-speed reanimation strong in Orzhov aristocrats but overpriced at €20; Endless Bottle Assault makes attacking ninja tokens like Adeline but self-copies for extra triggers, hard to sweep. Group two: Cool but Rude rummages on attack and becomes a discard payoff plus Gamble tutor; Super Combo is a replicate-able green bite that dodges counters and fizzling, best mono-green/Simic; Turtles in Time bounces all creatures then offers a draw-seven that shuffles away opponents' threats, great for Simic landfall. Top three: April O'Neil, blue 1/5 giving repeatable end-step card draw per card type cast; Ninja Pizza turns food into treasure-like mana, a food-deck staple; and the top pick, The Ooze, a colorless engine generating mutagen +1/+1 counter tokens with graveyard hate and critical-mass combo potential.

## Key Clips

- **[03:36]** Personally, I want my cards to always do what I expect them to do and I try to avoid low roll scenarios as much as possible. I don't even like mana leak even though it is objectively a pretty solid card, but when I'm countering a spell, I want it to be countered. I don't want to give my opponents the option of paying to get out of that.
- **[04:52]** Something that's easy to forget is that the vast majority of creatures commonly seen in Commander have kind of shitty stats. Utility creatures are usually under-statted. Buffing them even a little can go a long way at applying pressure. These small advantages accumulate quickly.
- **[06:42]** The real strength of continue isn't protection, but flexibility when paired with sacrifice. Getting to choose the timing to double up on up to four ETBs or death triggers, maybe even both, is fantastic. Orzhov aristocrats are going to love this card.
- **[09:34]** I really value easy looting and rummaging like this because it lets me play a high number of lands, usually at least 40, and still never worry about getting flooded. The best way to ramp in Commander is to simply always hit your land drop, because people are going to miss theirs and if you just play a land every single turn, then we're going to be ahead eventually.
- **[11:15]** There's this meme idea that green can do anything in commander. And that's just blatantly not true because green doesn't have board wipes. Apex Altisaur and Ezuri's Predation are our best options, but they are not great.
- **[13:35]** The order of operations here is very important. We first bounce all creatures, and then we shuffle. A problem bounce sweepers usually have is that our opponents can just play their creatures again. But if our opponents take the deal and shuffle their hand away, then their threats are gone. This also effectively kills commanders.
- **[15:24]** I always view commander as a struggle for resources. I think Cool but Rude is 10 times better than the Cloning or Shredder, not because it finishes the game quicker, but because it gets us into a position to finish the game. And that's what's actually tricky.
- **[21:44]** This is what I like to call a critical mass combo, where we don't need anything specific and rather we just want enough pieces that all do something loosely related until we eventually have so many that the engine starts powering itself.
