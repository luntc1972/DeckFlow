## Summary

The creator argues EDH brewing is largely "dead" because building decks is no longer necessary. Three forces drove this: precons now release with nearly every set instead of yearly; aggregators like EDHrec auto-surface synergy pieces; and new cards are explicitly designed for Commander, so every card is an "EDH card" that scales and snowballs. This lowers the barrier to entry and produces better-functioning decks, which the creator frames as genuinely good for casual players. The downside is homogenization toward a soft metagame: themed commanders (shrines, walls, curses, vehicles) play the same parasitic Wizards-printed staples, leaving roughly 15 unique slots. Made-for-Commander cards (One Ring, Smothering Tithe, Bolas's Citadel) noticeably out-power older jank, so unsupported archetypes fall behind and brewing them takes more effort. To brew uniquely, add restrictions: build around personal Scryfall evaluations instead of EDHrec rank, impose constraints (no creatures, basic-wastes-only mana, lands without landfall, mono-feel-without-the-color). The sponsor, Commander Template, helps via math-based (non-AI) suggestions for lands/ramp/draw/removal and Scryfall-tag card-finding. Unique brews now stand out more precisely because they're rarer.

## Key Clips

- One thing that I've learned about many of you is that there are a lot of people who don't really like brewing decks. Personally, I rarely look at deck tech videos. I don't buy pre cons because to me, making Commander decks is a key part of the Commander experience.
- One big change that happened to EDH is the frequency of precons. Precon decks used to come out once a year... Now, we get precons all the time associated with each new set release. Another change would be websites like EDHrec... And in the same vein, there are now more YouTube videos, TikToks, or Instagram Reels showing off people's decks than ever before.
- The final factor I want to talk about is that new cards are designed for EDH. You don't need to dig around for a card that happened to say each opponent versus target opponent... Cards now are made to be good in Commander. Everything scales, snowballs, creates value. Legendary creatures are designed first and foremost to be in the command zone and built around.
- When you build a shrine deck, you grab a commander that has shrine in the text. You play the 22 shrines that exist. You play the enchantment-focused ramp, card draw, removal, and suddenly you have most of the deck already built for you. When you include the mana base, you're left with only about 15-ish slots to make your deck unique... they feel a little bit like precons.
- Generally speaking, many designed for Commander cards are stronger than those which are not. This puts decks into tiers of sorts. There was a time when wall decks and color hack decks were the same level of jank... Now though, walls are completely different. It is a supported type... Wizards can't support every type or theme equally. So, a lot of them get left by the wayside.
- My buddy Travis did something interesting with his Ish Sah Trola deck. He didn't use EDHrec at all, just Scryfall searches without the EDHREC rank feature... he ended up with using cards that Travis thought were best based on his own evaluations, not what other people said... Plus, he added extra restrictions and there are no creatures in the 99.
- Another example would be to add restrictions to your list. Joey from EDHRECCast has a very interesting Hayabusa list that only has basic wastes in it... the deck has this other challenge where it needs to filter from colorless into green mana and then into white mana so we can blink the commander to actually do the shrine thing.
- So, is EDH brewing dead? Kind of. It's not necessary anymore... If you don't want to build a deck from scratch ever, then you never have to do that anymore... But, now that unique brews are much more rare, they actually stand out more... So, give brewing an EDH deck a try. I think you'll love it.
