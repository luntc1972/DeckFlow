## Summary

Argues against building Commander decks that answer every problem, using board wipes as the case study. Frames deckbuilding around archetypes—control, aggro, mid-range—each with strength/weakness tradeoffs; a deck is a finite resource, so adding one thing means cutting another. Rejects the 'mid-rangeification'/homogenization of decks; favors committing to an archetype's strengths over safe versatility. Key heuristic from draw math: to reliably draw a card type you need ~12 copies by turn 2, ~9 by turn 5, ~6 by turn 8. So a control deck wanting a wipe by turn 5-6 needs ~9 board wipes (and similar single-target removal); mid-range ~6. Running only 1-2 wipes is unreliable (needs ~7 tutors to find one)—better to run zero and plan not to need the effect. For the featured aggressive commander-damage 'Ma' mid-range deck (fight spells, sacrifice, resource denial), symmetrical wipes like Blasphemous Act or Damnation are bad—they kill your own creatures and invite responses. Prefer synergistic board-clearing that leverages the commander: Chandra's Ignition, Arcbond, and more fight effects instead of wipes. Play to strengths for more consistent, unique decks.
