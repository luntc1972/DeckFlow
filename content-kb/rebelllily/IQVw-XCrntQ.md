## Key Clips

- Every turn in Magic the Gathering you draw one card and you get to play one land. That's the clock every player at every table is running on. Regardless of deck, format, or even number of opponents. One card, one land, every turn for everyone. So that is one unit of value, the baseline.
- Whenever a card helps us exceed the neutral output of one card and one land per turn, its value becomes additive. So Rampant Growth is worth 0.5. Skyshroud Claim, two lands is worth one whole point. A Divination is now also worth one point. This equivalency between lands and cards as components of value is the foundation of how we connect lands, ramp, and card draw.
- This is what we call effective value. The unit is constant. The context, however, clearly isn't. Ramp's effective value is highest before you hit your threshold or the mana value where your game plan becomes operational. But after the threshold, where you already have enough mana, the ramp came too late.
- Ramp and draw are directly inverse each other in terms of effective value. Ramp without draw means you hit your threshold and then run out of gas. Draw without ramp means you're holding a full hand of cards you never get to cast. You remove one leg, the other one collapses. That's why it's tied.
- This is Liebig's law of the minimum, the agricultural concept that crop yield is constrained by whichever nutrient is most scarce regardless of how abundant the others are. If you have 12 ramp pieces and 12 draw spells, but only 28 lands, your deck can't function.
- The priority order of failures to address is lands first, ramp second, draw third. It's ordered by how fast and how permanently each failure beats you. Land failure is catastrophic and immediate. Ramp failure is a ceiling problem. Draw failure is the quiet one ΓÇö you won't notice it until turn seven when you're top decking.
- The combined ramp plus draw budget is always 24 slots. You always have at least 38 lands based on the hot garbage model. These are fixed numbers. What moves is how the 24 ramp plus draw budget splits. With four mana value commanders, easy 12 ramp and 12 draw.
- With higher five to eight mana value commanders, the higher your threshold, the more pressure ramp puts on your early game. You shift slots out of draw into ramp: 14 ramp and 10 draw. With low mana value commanders, you shift ramp slots into draw: eight ramp, 16 draw for a one or two drop commander, 10 ramp and 14 draw for a three mana value commander.
