## Summary

The creator, a self-described cruel control player, built a tribal gates deck with Child of Alara as commander—55 lands, ramp, draw, Maze's End and Crackling Perimeter wincons, plus many ways to sacrifice Child of Alara for repeated board wipes. The deck failed because boardwipes are 'asymmetric': literally symmetrical but experienced very differently across decks. They brutalize low-mid power synergistic midrange decks but barely affect combos, aggro abusing life totals, land-heavy strategies, or instant/sorcery value engines. A repeated-wipe gameplan ruins casual board-dependent decks while puttering helplessly against less board-reliant ones. The same profile applies to the creator's Glissa deck, which stomps midrange but flounders against pressure, counterspells, or combo. Boardwipes aren't bad overall—many decks are board-based—but a better build runs 3-5 wipes plus tutors rather than 8. The real lesson: be mindful constructing your interaction package; know what your deck is good and bad against, and make any holes intentional rather than incidental (e.g., the aggro Meria equipment deck deliberately lacks big-creature removal). Stax effects are also asymmetric but invert boardwipes—choking carefully-built combo/burst decks while sparing simple beatdown—so stax pairs better with a win plan than boardwipes do against combo.

## Key Clips

- In the context of commander, boardwipes are a type of effect that I would call 'asymmetric'. This is confusing on the surface, since they're generally literally symmetrical effects—they destroy everything. But what I mean here is that they impact different players massively differently.
- Board wipes won't stop combos, they won't prevent aggro decks from abusing your life total, they won't slow down large land counts, and they won't shut down your opponents generating a lot of instant and sorcery-based value.
- This deck will ruin the day of casual synergy decks, but against a less board-dependent deck it'll putter around and be a mild nuisance before ultimately getting crushed, with little to no chance of actually pulling off one of its wincons.
- A better version of my Glissa deck would probably be running 3-5 board wipes instead of 8, with tutors to bridge the gap, but years spent as a mono-black player forced me into the hard truth that unconditional tutors are bland as hell, so I don't play them.
- The interaction package of your deck, and what things it's particularly good or bad against, is something you should think about. Ideally, it should be well-balanced, and if there are holes they should be intentional rather than incidental.
- My Meria equipments deck has very little hard removal—burn and noncreature permanent destruction, yes, but not much for big creatures. This is mostly a matter of the fact that I'm playing an aggro deck... My deck's removal suite has a hole, but it's a calculated one.
- Stax effects generally operate the opposite way as board wipes—choking out burstier strategies that carefully plan out their resources to execute a specific gameplan, while having less of an effect on a deck whose gameplan consists of 'play treefolk' or something.
- You can build a deck with a bunch of stax effects and a win plan that can outrun a midrange deck, but you can't build a deck full of boardwipes with a win plan that can outrun a combo deck.
