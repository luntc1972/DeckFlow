## Summary

The creator shares tracking data from 154 games using their "lucky energy" house rule, showing flattened seat win rates (roughly 25/25/23/25%) versus the typical 30/25/25/20%, and argues unconditional duals like bond lands are poorly designed. ## Key Clips

- The new turbulent lands from Secrets of Strixhaven commander decks are tapped lands with dual typing that enter untapped if our opponents control eight or more lands. It's a subtle push in the right direction to address win rate disparities between seats, and they're perfectly playable even in the first or second seat.
- Across 154 tracked games using the proposed 'lucky energy' fix, seat one won 25.97%, seat two 25.32%, seat three 23.38%, and seat four 25.32% ΓÇö a vast improvement over the usual 30/25/25/20% distribution. The average winner seed deviation dropped to 0.08%, 7.5 times better than without it.
- Petrified Hamlet might be cEDH viable: an untapped colorless land that names a land card on entry, then activated abilities of those lands can't be used unless they are mana abilities. It doesn't turn off Gaea's Cradle but hits Otawara, Boseiju, Oboro lines, and can name Mirrodin or Command Beacon. Crop Rotation can act as an emergency stifle with it.
- Number one is Grave Researcher, a two-mana black 2/2. At the beginning of upkeep it surveils one, then if there are three or more creature cards in your graveyard it becomes prepared, and its spell is Reanimate. It's cheap, fuels its own requirements, and is a soft card advantage engine ΓÇö the best card of the set for social Commander.
- Emeritus of Truth is a three-mana white 2/2 whose prepared spell is Swords to Plowshares. It makes a 1/1 flying Inkling on entry (given to you or an opponent), becoming prepared if an opponent controls more creatures than you. Like Seal of Doom or Selfless Spirit, a prepared Emeritus directs opponents' actions in your favor ΓÇö they hesitate to play their threats.
- Number four is Emrakul, the Promised End ΓÇö a five-mana blue 5/5 with flying and ward 2 whose prepared spell is Ancestral Recall. Viewed as a six-mana 5/5 flyer with ward 2 that draws three cards on ETB, it's busted by itself, and it can re-prepare by attacking and exiling eight cards from the graveyard, which it refills by drawing.
- Number two is Flashback, a one-mana red instant giving a target instant or sorcery in your graveyard flashback equal to its mana cost until end of turn. It's a god-tier spell-slinger card, especially with mana-on-cast effects like Storm-Kiln Artist or Runaway Steam-Kin, making the flashback cost inconsequential.
- Professor Onyx-style Witherbloom planeswalker enters with five loyalty, plus-twos to gain three life, ramps up to seven, and is hard to kill in green-black. After a single turn it ultimates for a Sanguine Bond emblem ΓÇö narrow, but great in a lifegain shell.
