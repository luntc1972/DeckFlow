## Summary

The creator rejects fixes like descending scry because they treat symptoms, not the cause, and front-load complexity before the game starts. He identifies the real advantage as mana: player one effectively has an extra mana, so the fix should be mana-based. ## Key Clips

- Going last in Commander is a huge disadvantage. This is just a fact. Player one sits at loosely a 30% win rate and player two at loosely 20%. This is true in both social Commander and cEDH, but becomes more pronounced in higher power levels. Ideally, the win rates would be 25% across the board.
- In Commander, odds of the player going first winning are increased by 20% compared to the odds of the player going last. This is an enormous difference, and an effort should be made to alleviate it.
- A common option is to give the player going last some amount of scrying. Often, a sort of descending scry where the second player gets a bit of one as well, and the third a bit more, and the fourth the most. This absolutely works. But I still don't like it because it focuses on fixing the symptoms rather than the cause.
- The player going first always has an extra mana over everybody else. Or another way of looking at it is that the player going last is always a mana down. Given that, our solution should be focused on mana.
- A couple of months ago, I took matters into my own hands and started handing the player going last a treasure at the start of the game. This works great because it gives that player the option to self-determine a single turn of the game where they are effectively going first.
- The player going last starts the game with a Lucky Energy, a one-time use emblem that they can crack at any time to get a mana of any color, but it also doesn't have synergies with anything. Using a Pok├⌐mon card makes it obvious that it doesn't synergize with any other card effects because Pok├⌐mon cards don't synergize with Magic cards.
- The lucky energy rule just creates a new fourth player. Now, whoever's going third has the disadvantages that the fourth player previously had. But statistically, players two and three are actually pretty close to our desired 25% win rate. They don't need help, which means we can focus our attention on only the problem at hand, the player going last.
- For three-player games, I give it to the third player. And in five-player matches, both four and five get a lucky energy. The lucky energy is simply one of the many possible solutions. Any solution should be a mana-based solution.
