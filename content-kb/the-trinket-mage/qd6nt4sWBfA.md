## Summary

The creator argues blue is Magic's most-hated color, anchored by the 'Nope' counterspell shirt. He contends counterspells are functionally close to other removal—both stop a commander—but counters are uniquely flexible: they hit instants/sorceries and prevent ETB and dies triggers, though they're useless once a spell resolves and demand card advantage, making 'all counterspells' weak in Commander. Every color has a salt-inducing trick requiring unintuitive play to beat (skipping land drops vs. Braids, sandbagging vs. stax), but counterspells are the only such effect still commonly printed and played. Beating them means baiting them out at bad times, leading with cheap cards to overcome their mana advantage, and using table politics to redirect them—skills new players lack, so counters feel unbeatable. Blue was historically the strongest color (Power Nine, Modern bans), reinforcing the reputation. Crucially, counterspells are often the last spell cast, ending games; citing a Veritasium study that endings dominate memory, he says players remember being countered at the moment of victory. These factors compound into blue's reputation. He dislikes the shirt because it boxes blue in—you can run as many or few counters as you want, and fun is subjective.
