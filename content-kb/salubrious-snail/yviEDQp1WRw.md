## Summary

This video argues EDH players should run more removal without feeling it eats deck space, offering two methods. First, densify the removal you already run. Replace single-target removal with multi-removal (Hull Breach, Soul Shatter, Windgrace's Judgment, Decimate, Casualties of War) that hits several threats at once, cleaning up future problems like mana rocks and utility lands. Or use removal enginesΓÇöAura Shards, Royal Assassin, Trygon Predator, Astral Slide, stifle/pinger/flicker combosΓÇöthat destroy permanents over time, freeing slots for counterspells, draw, and tech. These cost more mana, so leanest proactive decks may decline; board wipes work but decks bounce back fast. Second, blend removal into the deck's plan: pick synergistic cards aligned with card-type, counters, tokens, or graveyard themes (Drown in Ichor, Lethal Scheme), and modal/MDFC/landcycling cards (Valorous Stance, Gix's Command, Angel of the Ruins) that double as lands, draw, or ramp. Examples: an Izzet Lilah deck running damage-plus-draw spells, a Radha cascade deck attaching removal to big creatures. Choosing removal requires knowing its job: fast combo decks want cheap single-target staples; grindy decks want long-term engines; higher power and non-blue decks need instant speed. Branch beyond the first few staples.

## Key Clips

- In conventional EDH deckbuilding, ways to interact with your opponents often ends up in the category of vegetablesΓÇöit's a type of card that's not flashy or exciting, but that you need to include regardless. The two big reasons to run more interaction: it leaves you with a better rounded deck, and it leads to more dynamic and interesting games rather than luck-based, one-sided stomps.
- The first method is to make the slots you're already spending on removal do more for you. There are two alternatives to single-target removal that can be comparatively compactΓÇömulti-removal, and removal engines. Multi-removal is a spell that removes multiple potential threats: Hull Breach can destroy both an artifact and an enchantment, Windgrace's Judgment can destroy each opponent's most valuable nonland permanent, and both Decimate and Casualties of War can blast cards of several card types at once.
- Removal engines are cards that can destroy things over time. Aura Shards is one of the most powerful examples, and there are creatures like Royal Assassin and Trygon Predator that can remove one permanent per turn. In a toolbox control deck, a small handful of removal engines can free up a ton of deck space to go toward counterspells, tech pieces, draw, and other categories.
- With multi-removal and removal engines, you're generally going to pay more mana for the added benefits compared to the single-target counterparts. For the leanest, most unapologetically proactive decks, that extra cost will likely be an unacceptable downsideΓÇöbut plenty of decks can afford to pay a little more mana to get more out of their removal.
- Board wipes often destroy your stuff as well, and a lot of decks can bounce back shockingly quickly from a board wipe, or even mitigate it entirely. You're not catching some doe-eyed Timmy off guard with the first Wrath of God he's ever seen. If you're considering your fourth or fifth board wipe for a deck, maybe consider there are other options out there for accomplishing a similar task.
- The second method is to widen your removal suite, but pick out removal that blends into the rest of what your deck is doing. One way is picking cards that align with your deck's synergiesΓÇöcard type is one of the most general options. An extension of this is modal cards like Valorous Stance, Silverback Elder, and Gix's Command, plus landcycling cards and MDFCs that are effectively modal. These cards can be lands or draw or ramp or protection in addition to being removal.
- Putting these options into practice requires understanding what removal is actually doing for your deck. In a lean, proactive combo deck, all dedicated removal is 2 mana or less to weave into a turn comfortably, killing creatures that threaten a turn 5-6 win. By contrast, removal engines belong in slower, grindier decks where the timeframe for winning is less concrete and removal exists to stay alive over an unknown duration.
- Instant speed vs sorcery speed removal comes down to power level. At higher powers where sudden infinite combo attempts might happenΓÇöespecially if you're not playing blue for counterspellsΓÇöinstant speed removal will likely be a must, and you want it to cost as little as possible to avoid wasted mana. Staples are staples for a reason. But once you've got your first three or four single-target staples, try branching out and consider alternatives that work better for your deck in particular.
