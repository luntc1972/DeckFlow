## Summary

This video frames aggro in Commander/EDH, where the 40-life format (and 120 across a table) makes conventional damage-racing impractical. Key insight: 40 life makes life-payment cheap (fetches, shocks, Phyrexian mana, pay-1-for-a-card) and makes blocking less necessary, so opponents play loose. Removing that cushion exposes them. The author's 'Aggro Ideology' is that pressuring life totals has value even without a kill: getting an opponent to ~15 life forces conservative play and threatens death within a turn. The second EDH-specific lever is commander damage (21 = lethal), effectively a 21-life format for commander-based decks. A diagram sorts aggro into pure voltron, pseudo-voltron (commander damage plus other damage), and disruptive aggro (multi-opponent damage plus stax); decks using neither lever rarely work. In-game priorities: get creatures connecting via evasion (innate, equipment, Wonder) or by removing blockers; maintain board against wipes by being fast, running permanent-based protection (Selfless Spirit), instants/counters, or Blood Artist effects. Balance speed versus resilience—faster decks need fewer answers. Develop target prioritization, focusing life-payers and players who can shut you down. Note that some pods view aggro as rude when knocked-out players idle for an hour.

## Key Clips

- **[01:10]** First of all, lifepay is so much less punishing. Fetches and shocks? Easy. Phyrexian mana symbols? Close enough to free. Paying 1 life for 1 card? An even more delicious bargain than usual. These effects, worthwhile but noticeably painful in a 20 health format, are now nearly painless.
- **[02:46]** My mantra in a game where somebody has a deck that is concerning and unpredictable but not necessarily totally dominant, is that they should be at 15 life. When somebody is at 15 life, they have to play much more conservatively. Having safe setup turns isn't a given, and they're generally one round of attacks away from death if they start doing concerning things.
- **[03:18]** If your commander deals 21 combat damage to an opponent, they lose. This means that if your deck is commander based, you're playing a 21 health format instead of the much more challenging 40 health format.
- **[05:04]** To connect with life totals, you need to get past blockers. The most common way of going about this is evasion, either innately on the aggressive creatures or applied through equipments or cards such as Wonder. An alternative method is eliminating those blockers.
- **[05:43]** Alternatively run protection spells—permanent-based is preferable for an aggro deck, with Selfless Spirit being the absolute gold standard if you're running white. You can also run instants that protect your creatures, or even counter-spells, but keep in mind that holding up mana will make your deck less mana efficient unless you have treasures or unless your creatures have flash.
- **[06:50]** Speed and resilience are factors that need to be considered—a deck that can outrun most combos and kill midrange before it stabilizes might not need much in the way of counterplays or resilience to obstacles, but a slower deck might want a lot more of those things. However, putting those things in a deck also tends to slow it down more, so it's a balancing act.
- **[06:54]** A key skill you'll want to develop is target prioritization. If your deck prefers to kill one player at a time, you need to pick a player to focus, and picking wrong might lose you the game. Primary targets that come to mind are players who pay a lot of life, and players who have especially strong ways to shut you down.
