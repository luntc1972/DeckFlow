## Summary

Streamer builds a mono-red Lathliss, Dragon Queen Commander deck designed as a beginner-friendly alternative to precons, which he considers too complex. Core goals: simple to play, budget (~$50), but powerful enough to be fun and competitive, since losing constantly isn't fun for new players. Central design rule is minimizing the number of distinct mechanics: he deliberately rejects goad, cascade, sagas, changelings, imprint, retrace, initiative, and mana-pool effects, and skips cards whose keywords aren't explained in their own text box. Landfall is accepted as simple enough. The two embraced support mechanics are impulse draw and treasures, plus discard-to-draw. He runs about 22-23 dragons (enough to reliably hit with top-of-library effects ~80% of the time), a 40-Mountain mana base, mana doublers (Caged Sun, Gauntlet of Power), cheap mana rocks, and ramp. He prefers board-wide AOE removal (Blasphemous Act, earthquake effects that spare flyers) over single-target removal, and dislikes commander-sniping. He pointedly refuses Sol Ring on principle. Cards are sorted into categories (dragon, ramp, draw, treasure, removal, AOE) then trimmed by mana curve and budget. He also tested historic 2004-2007 EDH decks played commander-shuffled-in.

## Key Clips

- The goal is a budget Lathliss, Dragon Queen mono-red dragon deck built specifically for beginners. The builder argues precons are too complicated for new players, so he designs his own ~$50 deck that is easy to play, reasonably powerful, and introduces as few mechanics as possible.
- Deckbuilding philosophy: every card you add is designing a game for the player. For a beginner deck, deliberately minimize the number of distinct mechanics ΓÇö skip cards that introduce Goad, changelings, sagas, Cascade, Myriad, etc., even when they are individually strong, because each new mechanic is something the player must manage.
- A card is only usable if its printing explains its own keywords. Cards whose only printings don't explain mechanics like Flashback are called 'incomprehensible' and cut. Conversely, cards that explain Exert, Cascade, or Collect Evidence on the card itself are acceptable.
- The two intended core mechanics of the deck are impulse draw (Reckless Impulse, Rinse and Resolve, Light Up the Stage) and Treasures (Patron of the Arts, Rapacious Dragon, big-score-style rituals). These are the deck's main ways to keep cards and mana flowing in red.
- Build process: first jam every plausible card, then sort them into broad categories (dragon, ramp, removal, treasure, card draw, AOE), then trim. He prefers cards that effectively 'draw a card' / replenish resources, and counts dragons that double as removal favorably.
- Lathliss goes wider than typical dragon decks ΓÇö give her a turn or two and you have six or seven dragons in play. The deck runs ~22-23 dragons (not 29), since drawing many dragons in a game already means you should win.
- Mana base is fixed at 40 Mountains, so the deck leans on mana rocks and mana doublers (Caged Sun, Gauntlet of Power, Gilded Lotus, Ruby Medallion, Worn Powerstone) to power out expensive dragons. He deliberately runs NO Sol Ring on principle, calling it overpowered fast mana that makes games less fun.
- Tuning by mana curve: the deck is heavy on two-mana ramp and on five-mana, so he cuts from the dense five-slot and protects the six-slot so Lathliss can reliably be cast there. Hypergeometric check shows ~22 dragons in 98 cards gives roughly 79-80% to hit a dragon off a top-six dig like Kagha/War Monger ΓÇö judged good enough.
