## Summary

This deck is built around Tataru Taro, a two-mana white dwarf adviser that draws a card on entry, offers an opponent a card draw, and creates a tapped treasure once per turn when an opponent draws outside their own turn. The deck uses white's shared card-draw effects (Secret Rendezvous, Wedding Ring, Fall of the First Civilization, Lost Song, scene of Chronicler, Temple Bell, Overworld Atlas, Oblation, Your Temple is Under Attack, Dawn's Truth) to give opponents cards and farm treasures, staggering triggers onto opponents' turns for extra mana. Despite resembling group hug, it never gives mana. It's actually a Black Vice deck: it floods opponents' hands, then punishes large hands with Black Vice, Ebony Owl, Iron Maiden, Viseling, Psychosis/Scrolling Crawler, and Champion of Mina's Tret. Rule of Law is the key white tool—limiting each player to one spell per turn caps the damage from gifted resources while keeping opponents' hands full. The pilot rates it a fun gimmick bracket-2 list, not strong, held together by mana and card draw from Tataru Taro. Cost ~85 euro. A demonstrated MTGO game showed it nearly becoming arch-enemy before narrowly losing.

## Key Clips

- Tataru Taro is a two mana white dwarf adviser that draws us a card when she enters. She then also gives an opponent the option to draw a card which will trigger her second ability. Once a turn, when an opponent draws a card outside of their own turn, we create a tapped treasure token.
- White has lots of shared card draw. Secret Rendezvous draws us and target opponent free cards. Wedding ring literally shares card draw between us and an opponent. They have synergy with Tataru because now we also get mana. This is particularly effective if we can stagger it.
- Scene of Chronicler makes it so that whenever a player casts their first multicolored spell for the turn, all other players draw a card. Since most commanders are at least two colors, it tends to trigger at least two or three times per game. Temple Bell and Overworld Atlas are artifacts we can tap to make all players draw, giving us complete control over the timing.
- You might think this is just a group hug deck, and you would be somewhat correct. However, unlike most group hug, we are limiting ourselves to only giving the other players additional cards. Under no circumstances will we feed them any mana, because we don't want them to play those additional cards. This is a Black Vice deck.
- Black Vice is a one-mana artifact that at the beginning of each opponent's upkeep deals one damage to that opponent for each card they have in their hand in excess of four. Ebony Owl, Iron Maiden, and Viceling all follow the same logic, punishing our opponents for having too many cards in hand. We fill up their hand and then burn them out.
- Rule of law is a free mana enchantment that says each player can't cast more than one spell each turn. It limits the damage they can deal with the resources we hand out, while ensuring they always have a large number of cards in hand, letting us push damage. It's a stax piece that becomes less effective the lower the power level, so it's fine in bracket 2.
- Scrolling Crawler burns our opponents for one whenever they draw a card and has all players draw at the start of our upkeep. Psychosis Crawler is similar but burns opponents when we draw. Champions of Minas Tirith makes us the monarch and asks opponents to pay as much mana as we have cards in hand or they can't attack us.
- Is this a strong deck? No, it's a gimmick deck and the gimmick is so specific that it's very difficult to make work. But the deck is held together by a lot of card draw, and Tataru Tower generates a reasonable amount of mana. Turns out mana plus card draw is really all you need to do quite a lot, even if the rest of the game plan isn't super strong.
