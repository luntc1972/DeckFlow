## Summary

Video argues removal is a key to victory (buys time, denies threats, unlocks your game plan), pushing back on the "play more removal" complaint from losing players. Four attributes to weigh: cost, speed (instant preferred), range (single/multi/sweep), and flexibility of targets. Removal in multiplayer is tempo-negative (the Arcane Denial argument); wider-range spells (Dismantling Wave over Disenchant, Pick Your Poison over Naturalize) offset this but cost more or run at sorcery speed. Three roles: survival (reactive instant-speed answers), unlocking (removing a specific piece that stops your plan—Rule of Law, Propaganda, Amaze vs. Voltron), and suppression/de-escalation (range and sweepers). Numbers: precons average ~12 removal; cEDH runs 4–8 single-target plus 8–10 counterspells (up to ~18 interaction). Aggressive decks want ~12 (lean toward unlock, fewer wipes); reactive decks ~18 (more flexibility, range, board wipes). Fit removal to theme/flexibility/scale: Ancient Animus (+1/+1 decks), Sundering Growth (tokens), Bushwhack, Supreme Will, Final Showdown, Aura Shards, Intrepid Hero, liquid-metal-coating combo. Example Ardenn/Jecca(?) aggro list of 12: survival—Generous Gift, Chaos Warp, Tibalt's Trickery, Get Lost; unlock—Cleansing Wildfire, Untimely Malfunction, Destroy Evil (Maze of Ith, Drannith Magistrate); suppression—Blasphemous Act, Tempest of Light, Pyrohemia, Argentum Armor.

## Key Clips

- **[01:37]** There are four important attributes of removal. Cost is the casting cost — cheaper is better. Speed is the timing of when you can cast it; the more reactive it is, the more it costs or the less it can target. Range is the number of targets it deals with. Flexibility is the variety of targets it can hit.
- **[02:21]** Removal in multiplayer is tempo negative. You Doom Blade a creature, and now you and the owner both go down one card plus the resources spent — the two other opponents are unaffected. That's the classic Arcane Denial argument.
- **[03:25]** Removal has three main roles: survival (stopping opponents from winning), unlocking (pushing through your own game plan), and suppression (controlling the board for deescalation).
- **[03:59]** If you need to stop your opponents, speed matters most because it has to be reactive instant-speed removal, and it probably has to be more narrow or cost-effective if your deck needs to develop on your own turn too.
- **[05:12]** In cEDH we normally see a range of 4 to 8 single-target removal spells, with counter spells usually at 8 to 10, giving a maximum of about 18 cards to interact efficiently, mostly at instant speed.
- **[07:20]** An aggressive deck wants less removal — around 12 spells — leaning toward removal that unlocks and less on board wipes, since you should be punching through to victory. A reactive deck wants 18 removal spells with more flexibility, range, and board wipes to reset the table.
- **[08:50]** Find removal that fits your deck's theme: Ancient Animus in a +1/+1 counters deck to remove a creature while adding counters to proliferate, Oblivion Ring effects in an enchantments-matters deck, or Sundering Growth instead of Disenchant in a tokens deck.
- **[09:29]** Explore removal engines that scale with time — something like Aura Shards or Intrepid Hero to shoot down big creatures turn after turn.
