## Summary

This video responds to The Trinket Mage's claim that green is the strongest color in casual Commander, arguing instead that white is strongest. The creator defines casual Commander as no fast mana, no/limited free spells, no tutors or infinite combos, themed decks, and fun over winning. He counters that white's catch-up ramp rivals green's land ramp via lines like Lotus Vale plus Claim Jumper, land reanimation (Redemption Choir, Sun Titan), and Cosmic Intervention fetchland loops. He argues Mass Land Destruction is a poor answer to ramp; tutor hate (Aven Mindcensor, Opposition Agent, Leonin Arbiter) and direct ramp hate handle it better without dragging games out. Green can't truly do everythingΓÇöit lacks real board wipes, counterspells, and instant-speed interaction, and its card draw is conditional on creatures. White's edge is mass removal like Planar Cleansing, Hour of Revelation, and Farewell, paired with mass reanimation, stax pieces, pillowfort, incremental card draw, broad archetype support, instant-speed protection, and political cards. White is also a better social color. He notes blue/black dominate competitively, red is weakest due to 40 life, and ultimately power doesn't matter in casual since fun is the point.

## Key Clips

- This is a response to Trinket Mage's video arguing green is the strongest color in casual Commander. I disagree ΓÇö in my opinion white is the strongest color in casual Commander.
- Defining casual Commander as it's meant here: no fast mana, no or limited free spells, no tutors, no infinite combos. Most decks are themed rather than a pile of good cards, and winning matters less than hanging out and having a good time.
- White's catch-up ramp isn't just a gimmick to keep up ΓÇö it can build massive leads. Example: Lotus Vale on turn three sacrificing two lands, then Claim Jumper ramping two lands back, giving six mana on turn four plus a free vigilance creature. You can also reanimate fetchlands with Redemption Choir, or loop a single fetchland three times with Cosmic Intervention.
- Mass Land Destruction is not the correct response to ramp. A ramp deck plays lots of lands, so it handles a blind Armageddon better than other decks. Tutor hate ΓÇö Aven Mindcensor, Opposition Agent, Stranglehold, Ashiok Dream Render ΓÇö and direct anti-ramp like Deep Gnome Terramancer or Confounding Conundrum stop ramp far better, and don't extend the game by ten hours.
- Green can't actually do everything. It has no proper board wipes (Apex Altisaur is not a real sweeper), no counterspells, no strong instant-speed interaction apart from Beast Within. Green is momentum-based, spending lots of mana on expensive creatures and needing them in play to access its best card draw.
- The strongest card type in casual Commander isn't ramp ΓÇö it's mass removal like Planar Cleansing: a six-mana sorcery that destroys all nonland permanents. Hour of Revelation, Devastating Mastery, and Farewell win games on the spot because most casual decks can't recover, and the caster knows it's coming so they can play around it ΓÇö especially with white's access to mass reanimation.
- Commander isn't just a mechanical game, it's a social game, and white is a better social color than green. A green player's huge stack of lands is visible to everyone; a white player who sacrificed six lands but actually has four mana hidden isn't as obvious. White also has political cards like Duelist's Heritage and Laelia that give social leverage.
- Red is the worst color in Commander, held back by the 40 starting life total. Many of Commander's balance issues, casual and competitive, would be eased if players started at 30 instead ΓÇö early chip damage would matter, green couldn't just ramp for six turns, and red's burn might apply real pressure.
