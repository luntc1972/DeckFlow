## Summary

Ian introduces the new Mulligan Strategy Calculator, an upgrade that lets players specify when they want to draw certain cards for their early-game plan and tells them which opening hands to keep. He demonstrates with a Slindsa beast-tribal Commander deck, aiming to ramp into the five-mana commander on turn four. He defines two categories: 38 lands (wanting four by turn four) and 13 ramp pieces (wanting one by turn three), plus a mulligan penalty for discarding, set at 20%. Computing yields a 67% penalized success rate (74% true), with extra lands or ramp each adding ~2.2%. The strategy table ranks keepable hands from most to least successful, indicating which to keep immediately versus after a free mulligan or in desperation. Lowering the penalty to 15% mulligans more aggressively; he recommends keeping it between 10-40% and not adjusting on the fly. Adding two lands and one ramp raises success over 6%. He advises targeting a 70-80% penalized success rate and favoring deck changes over penalty tweaks. Final tip: approximate category prioritization by requesting less-important categories a turn or two later than needed, softening the impact of missing them.

## Key Clips

- This calculator is an upgrade to the previous Mulligan calculator that lets you specify when you want to draw certain cards for your early game plan and tells you what hands to keep in order to make that happen.
- Example deck: a Beast Tribal Commander deck helmed by Slindsa, the Spike Stampede, a five-mana creature. Since Slindsa works best when cast before other beasts, the goal is to ramp into him on turn four. Inputs: 38 lands wanting four by turn four, 13 ramp pieces wanting one by turn three.
- The reported success rate is 67% with the cost of discarding factored in, but the turn-four Slindsa opening actually happens around 74% of the time. Adding an extra land or ramp each adds about 2.2% to the success rate, indicating the categories are balanced.
- Strategy table reads from most to least successful: four lands and one ramp is always a keep; three lands and one ramp is keepable; two lands and one ramp waits until the free mulligan; four lands and no ramp is just barely keepable after the free mulligan; three lands no ramp or one land one ramp become keeps only when desperate.
- Lowering the non-free mulligan penalty from 20% to 15% raised reported success rate about 2% and true success rate almost 4%, because the policy takes more mulligans for better hands. The strategy got pickier about no-ramp hands, pushing last-resort keeps back a mulligan.
- Recommended penalty range is 10 to 40%. Extreme values lead to strategies that toss decent hands or keep bad ones. Avoid changing it on the fly to hit a target mulligan count or success rate; if you want to improve the deck, the penalty is not the first thing to change.
- Adding two more lands and one more ramp piece (penalty back at 20%) raised the success rate over 6% and true success rate over 9%. Recommended stopping point for success rate including penalties is somewhere between 70 and 80%, a matter of personal risk tolerance.
- The calculator treats all categories with equal all-or-nothing importance, but you can approximate prioritization by requesting less important categories a turn or two later than needed. Moving the ramp goal from turn three to turn four or five softens the impact of missing ramp and shifts priority to lands.
