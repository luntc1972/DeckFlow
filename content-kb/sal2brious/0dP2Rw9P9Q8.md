## Summary

This video traces one builder's pursuit of a "fat control" battlecruiser vibeΓÇödrawing tons of cards, amassing huge mana, and becoming the table's archenemyΓÇöacross three decks. The original mono-black Sheoldred deck failed because its ramp was fragile rocks and doublers, its removal was mediocre and mono-creature-focused, and its wincons (Exsanguinate) were bursty and disjointed. The Glissa Sunslayer deck fixed these by adding green: lands-based ramp that sticks, diverse removal hitting all card types, and wincons baked into the ramp/draw/removal suites (top-end threats that double as utility), giving a compact, consistent plan with no dedicated tutors beyond land-fetch. Glissa supplies defense and card draw on-curve (turn-2 via one-cost ramp). However, Glissa proved too oppressive (24 interaction pieces) and too generic (best-in-class staples), feeling pubstompy at mid-power tables. The solution was a budget (~$75) Radha, Heir to Keld deck: a two-mana dork commander, Explosive Vegetation ramp, no sub-four-mana cards, and cascade payoffs. Radha lowers interaction density to 16 with deliberate holesΓÇöforcing aggressive answers via Captivating Crew or Molten PrimordialΓÇöand its budget, scrappy threats give a unique identity while preserving the core power-fantasy vibe.

## Key Clips

- My original mono-black Sheoldred deck ran 10 board wipes and 18 other pieces of removal. It was a removal pile packed with pet cards, far too high-curved with not nearly enough lands and ramp, and containing only crumbly, generic wincons. The general plan was to keep opposing boards empty of creatures, then tutor for Necropotence, mana doublers, and a big finisher like Exsanguinate.
- The Glissa deck fixed the mono-black deck's problems by adding green: ramp that sticks around comes from lands, not just rocks and doublers that get snuffed out by a couple removal spells. Green also handled card types beyond creatures that black struggled with, and let me omit bursty wincons like Exsanguinate in favor of winning through big creatures or noncreature wincons that require a far-ahead gamestate.
- Glissa was picked as commander before anything in the 99, so the deck was built around her. She provides two main thingsΓÇödefense and card draw. The defense punishes opponents attacking with non-evasive creatures, which matters for an absurdly greedy deck that pours effort into mana and card advantage rather than board. Her draw lets the deck run fewer total draw spells, skewed toward large-scale draw.
- The deck wants to play Glissa on curve, so it starts with a bunch of one-cost ramp to land her on turn 2. The ramp suite adds three-and-four-cost land grabbersΓÇöGrow from the Ashes for flexibility, Growth Spasm to boost to six mana next turnΓÇöplus beefier options like Bear Umbra, Boundless Realms, Vorinclex, and Zendikar Resurgent. A full seven cards above five mana perform a ramping function.
- The secret sauce: the deck's wincons are built into its ramp, draw, and removal suites. It runs 19 top-end threats, and of these 10 have removal attached, 3 have ramp attached, and 3 have draw attached. Because the support cards ARE the wincon, the deck has a very compact win planΓÇöit gets to have its cake and eat it too, for the low cost of running a shitload of ramp.
- I ran no tutors that grab anything other than lands. Tutors go against the deck's ethosΓÇöthe goal is consistency through good card quality and carefully portioned effects, so draw enough cards and you'll find what you need. The second reason: this is a comfort deck, and tutors offer possibilities that are anxiety-inducing. I don't want to end a game wondering whether I optimized my tutor lines.
- I stopped playing the Glissa deck for two reasons. It was too oppressiveΓÇöeven the trimmed-down 5 board wipes plus 19 other removal spells crushed the table too effectively to be a default comfort deck. And it was too generic: a best-in-class top-end and removal pile behind a generically good draw commander, which felt pubstompy in the mid-power battlecruiser space because opponents had seen all the cards before.
- The solution was my Radha, Heir to Keld deck: a 2-mana dork commander, Explosive Vegetation effects to big mana, then bombs and cascade above. It lowers removal density to 16 interaction spells with deliberate holesΓÇöweak against high-toughness creatures, forcing me to make deals or steal threats with Captivating Crew or Molten Primordial. The ~$75 budget keeps it scrappy, winning with Screamer Killer and Flameblast Dragon instead of Etali or Old Gnawbone, giving identity without losing the battlecruiser vibe.
