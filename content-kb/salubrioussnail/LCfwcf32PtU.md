## Summary

Snail argues "win conditions" are a misleading category in CommanderΓÇöalmost any card can win given the right board, so the better question is which cards don't help you win. Decks succeed by focusing inputs and outputs: inputs are conditions you meet (cycling, equipment, artifacts), outputs are results that advance one cohesive plan. Unfocused inputs cause dead draws and incoherent boards; unfocused outputs (value-tribal decks like Gavi) do many small things but lack teeth to close. For midrange decks, he frames games as "create parity" (build a beefy board via lands, ramp, draw, protection, creatures) then "break parity" using overruns, targeted buffs, goad, evasion, or blocker removalΓÇöwhile avoiding win-more cards (Epic Struggle, Crucible of Fire) that only win already-won boards. Aggro avoids parity; control creates it via wipes/stax then breaks it with resilient finishers. Removal buys time at a cost, so faster decks need less; interaction also clears obstacles to your own plan, and counterspells flexibly answer threats but waste held mana. Combos should emerge from optimized synergy, not splashed in. The takeaway: build every card choice toward winningΓÇöa wide early onramp plus focused outputsΓÇörather than bolting splashy bombs onto an unfocused shell.

## Key Clips

- You shouldn't ask the question "what cards in my deck are the ones that win the game," because the important question is "what cards in my deck are the ones that don't win the game?" If you're building a deck with the intent of winning, that goal should be present in some form throughout each and every one of your card choices.
- Most decks involve a system of inputs and outputs, with inputs being conditions that need to be met and outputs being results that contribute to the deck's gameplan. This framework points to the two places a deck can stumble with regard to focusΓÇöit can have unfocused inputs, and it can have unfocused outputs.
- Parity basically means a state of standstillΓÇöin the midrange context it's usually when multiple players have decent-sized boards but can't swing in for large damage due to a lack of evasion or a risk of counterattack. Divide the game into two key steps: "create parity" (get your beefy board going) and "break parity" (deviate so your creatures hit for a lot of damage).
- The most popular parity breakers in commander are overrun effects, but there's a myriad of other optionsΓÇötargeted buffs, goad, extra combat spells, mass evasion spells, and cards that disable or remove potential blockers. The important thing is that you have at least something to convert that board you worked hard to build into a win.
- One challenge is distinguishing a card that helps you win from a win-more card. The goal of a parity breaker should be to turn a game state that's not quite winning into one that wins, but some cards will only win with a boardstate that was probably already about to win. If something is a kinda dead card two thirds of the time, that other third had better be really good.
- For every removal spell you add, you increase your ability to hinder opponents but subtract from slots that bolster your own gameplanΓÇöthink of removal as something that buys you time, at a cost. As a side effect, faster decks won't need as many removal spells as slower decks, because opponents won't have time to fully develop threats before you're threatening lethal.
- Interaction isn't always just about stopping opponents' win plansΓÇöit can also remove obstacles to your own. This flexibility is where counterspells shine. I usually run at least 8 in my decks with blue, and more if the win plan is more fragile, such as one that's slow and board-based or a combo without backup options. The downside is the holding-up-mana problemΓÇöif the threat doesn't materialize, you've effectively wasted mana.
- Combo is basically synergy taken to the extreme, so combos should ideally emerge from trying to optimize your synergies, not from throwing Sanguine Bond and Exquisite Blood in a random black deck. If you throw a combo in your midrange deck and that combo is a lot better than your midrange win plan, you're not playing a midrange deckΓÇöyou're playing a weird, inconsistent combo deck.
