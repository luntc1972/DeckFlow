## Summary

The video argues against bolting a powerful combo onto a commander whose primary gameplan is something else (synergy/midrange). It distinguishes this from intentionally building the commander as a combo piece in a dedicated combo deck, which the creator endorses because it compacts the combo and frees deck space. The problem case is adding an out-of-place, far-stronger combo as an afterthought. Two issues result. First, the 'vibes' problem: a deck that mostly does weak things but occasionally does something wildly powerful breaks opponents' ability to read and strategize, undermining multiplayer skill. Second, the tangible problem: how you use your commander shapes how opponents respond to it. Examples—Zaxara plus Freed from the Real (46% run it) makes the commander an instant removal target; Tivit voting decks running Time Sieve (63%) guarantee the commander gets countered, punishing the fair gameplan. Atraxa with poison counters paints a target via visible, inevitable setup. The creator's own decks (Radha, Malcolm) work because conflicting commander roles are anticipated and defended—Malcolm runs many counterspells funded by its treasures. Core advice: know what kind of Magic you want; don't give opponents extra reasons to kill your commander unless you're prepared to fight for it.
