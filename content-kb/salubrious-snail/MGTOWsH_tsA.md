## Summary

This is a precon review of the Edge of Eternities World Shaper deck, commanded by Hearthhull, the Worldseed, a 4-mana Jund Spacecraft. Once charged with two power, Hearthhull taps to sacrifice a land, draw two cards, and gain an extra land play; at eight counters it becomes a 6/7 that drains opponents on land sacrifice. The deck's gameplan: sacrifice and recur lands to build mana and value, then convert that into damage via landfall, permanent-sacrifice payoffs, and graveyard-land checks. Strengths include a high 42-land manabase rich in budget fetches (10) and dual-typed lands, consistent self-mill of lands, and strong landfall/sacrifice payoffs with high floors, plus a deep, flexible removal suite (highlighting Mayhem Devil, Windgrace's Judgement, Planetary Annihilation). Weaknesses: weak early-game utility and too much redundant land-sacrifice payoff given the commander already does both. The reviewer's 10 swaps cut clunky payoffs (Loamcrafter Faun, Scouring Swarm, World Breaker, Mazirek, etc.) and add smoothers and landfall engines—most importantly Spelunking (land untapping), plus Lotus Cobra, Tireless Provisioner, Valakut Exploration, Undergrowth Recon, and two upgraded lands. Overall a solid, creative precon needing only minor rebalancing.

## Key Clips

- Hearthhull, the Worldseed is a 4 mana Spacecraft in Jund colors. Spacecraft are artifacts that can be leveled up by tapping creatures to gain more abilities, similar to crewing a vehicle, and a lot of them eventually become creatures as well. This set also brings a rules change where any legendary vehicle or spacecraft with a power/toughness box can be in the command zone.
- Once Hearthhull has had two power put into it, it gains the ability to tap, pay 1, and sacrifice a land to draw two cards and gain an additional land play this turn. At eight charge counters, it becomes a 6/7 with flying, vigilance, haste, and 'Whenever you sacrifice a land, each opponent loses two life.'
- This deck is trying to build mana and value through the process of sacrificing and replaying lands before turning things up to 11 with a suite of permanents that perform powerful effects when parts of this cycle happen. Six cards can recur multiple lands from the graveyard, along with payoffs: four landfall cards, nine permanent-sacrifice payoffs, and four cards that check lands in graveyards.
- There's an impressive density of budget fetches here—a whole 10. These make sense when you remember that the four most powerful land recursion spells in the deck return the lands to play tapped, which isn't a downside for any of these. The deck runs a high land count of 42, which is reasonable given a commander that both sacrifices lands and gives extra land plays.
- I ran playtests out to the end of turn 5, and there were a surprising number where I finished with 10 or even 12 lands in play, which can set up the deck for some serious midgame shenanigans if it can keep finding its way into nonland plays.
- Planetary Annihilation is a wild new design, dealing 6 damage to each creature and forcing each player to sacrifice down to 6 lands. It works satisfyingly with both the commander and the deck's sources of mass land recursion. Other removal highlights include Mayhem Devil, Windgrace's Judgement, and Pest Infestation.
- My two dislikes about this precon: it lacks early game utility plays, and it has too much land sacrifice and sacrifice payoff, especially considering the commander is both of these things already. The fix is to cut the trickier payoffs for more flexible ones and add cards to smooth the early game and help with setup.
- The most important addition by orders of magnitude is Spelunking—land untapping effects are disgustingly powerful in this deck and other options are pricey. Other adds: Lotus Cobra, Tireless Provisioner, and Valakut Exploration for landfall; Deeproot Wayfinder and House Cartographer as early smoothers; Undergrowth Recon for recursion; and Hugs, Grisly Guardian for draw and extra land plays.
