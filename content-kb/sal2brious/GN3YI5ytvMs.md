## Summary

This winter deck review covers seven Commander decks with synergy critiques and upgrade suggestions. A Breya/Ozolith modular-counter deck is judged too complex; the reviewer argues Astral Slide needs far more cycling cards to beat discrete flicker engines like Soulherder or Teleportation Circle, and suggests leaning into graveyard recursion. A Safana initiative deck looks solid but could exploit its end-step treasures via instant-speed plays, counterspells, and Commander Storm finishers. A Tormod zombie deck should drop Dargo for a black partner and cut weak madness cards, adding creature-death payoffs like Grave Pact. An Ardenn/Vial-Smasher politics deck wants more equipment and ramp swords. A Kykar Worldfire reset deck plays like control; cut creature-spirit duds, refine plot targets per reset card. A Marvo control deck blends bombs and life-drain demons, but the reviewer warns the damage clock is slow for bracket four. An Extus loop deck runs too many fogs and lacks win conditions, needing card draw. Two green decks—a Karlach extra-combat aggro build wanting more haste, and a Six retrace deck praised as clever and fair—round out the review.

## Key Clips

- **[02:28]** Astral Slide fundamentally demands quite a lot out of your deck in order to be worth running over another flicker engine. I've consistently kept a high 30s count of cycling cards in my Astral Slide deck. With less than half that number of cycling cards here, I think Astral Slide is going to be giving fewer triggers than a Teleportation Circle, and with significantly more mana spent to get there.
- **[03:23]** With that complexity in mind, discrete engines like Soulherder, Teleportation Circle, and Thassa, Deep-Dwelling seem like much more reliable options for flickers than a cycling-based engine in a deck with a lot of moving parts.
- **[06:52]** Treasures are uniquely potent because they enable instant speed plays well, since they're stored mana that's never wasted. For that reason I'd replace some ETB removal creatures with flash options like Ertai Resurrected or Kalitas, keeping the flicker synergies while letting the deck be more spry.
- **[22:22]** Toolbox as a strategy naturally leans toward control. This deck is running cards I'd expect from a dedicated aggro deck and cards I'd expect from a dedicated control deck, which hurts consistency. Most of its value engines are one-time use, which doesn't bode well for scaling.
- **[32:44]** Defensive cards are delaying tools above all else, but it's unclear what this deck with a ton of delaying tools is delaying until. A control deck delays until a board wipe or until it can take over, but there's only one board wipe here and the deck is light on win cons.
- **[37:47]** I probably wouldn't start going all in until I thought I had a solid chance of knocking a player out that turn. If you start swinging too early, you do a bunch of scary damage without meaningfully reducing your opponent's ability to gang up on you.
- **[41:50]** If you're trying to make this deck as strong as possible, you'd stick mostly to the lands-in-graveyard payoffs, since that's what green thrives at most in this equation. The diversity of payoffs serves to pump up the fairness of the deck instead.
