## Summary

This video argues land count should follow a deck's specific attributes, not a personal across-the-board preference, using a 41-land Radha battlecruiser deck as the example. Mana curve is the biggest factor: higher curves want more lands, partly because they win later and partly because their ways to dig for lands cost more. A deck with cheap draw/filtering can run fewer lands; one whose digging is expensive or conditional needs more to avoid stalling. The flooding worry can be solved either by cutting lands or by ensuring the deck spends excess mana well—via high curve, big card draw that discards spare lands, mana sinks (X costs, activated abilities), MDFCs, and utility lands. Radha (5.33 average cost, nothing under 4 mana, cascade, Captivating Crew, Panglacial Wurm) tolerates 41 lands and aggressive mulliganing for ramp; 42 lands maximizes 3-land openers. Higher counts also enable extra-land-play cards and better timing on lands like Bojuka Bog. Conversely, high-velocity storm, spellslinger, and enchantress decks (high "Cantrip Coefficient") want minimal lands. The author estimates ~38 for a median casual deck post-MH3, suggests Frank Karsten's formula as a starting point, then testing by tracking land versus nonland shortages.

## Key Clips

- The question I'll be looking to help you answer in this video is "how many lands should my deck have in it," and perhaps more specifically, "should my deck have more lands in it?"
- As a general pattern, lower curved decks will want less mana than higher curved decks, and will thus also want a lower land count than higher curved decks. However, in the context of commander this is more of a correlation than a rule. Put simply, you don't need to play a land on turn 7 if there is no turn 7.
- A deck with lots of low-cost draw and filtering can afford to run a lower land count as long as it can find the colors to cast those digging cards, whereas decks that feature draw and filtering that costs four or more mana will want to run a higher count in order to consistently get enough mana to pay for that draw or filtering.
- When a deck can readily access powerful draw that is a bit mana expensive, running a higher land count is often an all-around win—either you haven't gotten enough mana to play the draw yet, in which case a high land count helps you have a higher chance of getting there, or you have played the draw, in which case you're churning through enough cards to render that slightly increased land density negligible.
- We're worried that we'll draw a bunch of lands... This is a valid concern, and one way to mitigate it is to run fewer lands, but another way is to make sure your deck is consistently able to churn through excesses of mana. Tools include big draw, MDFCs, mana-sinks like X costs and multi-use activated abilities, and utility lands.
- Running a land count closer to your ideal land proportion in your opening hand gives you more wiggle room to use your mulligans to hunt for nonland cards, and 42 is the number of lands in deck that offers the highest chance for a 3 land hand.
- The Cantrip Coefficient: some decks care about what's on top of their deck more than others. Storm decks, as many Jhoira decks might be considered, care more about the top of their library than just about any other type of deck, and will thus usually try to run a very low land count. Enchantress and spellslinger decks also rank highly.
- Though I position 35-37 as a moderate count, with the number of MDFCs and utility lands we have these days I'd guess that the median casual EDH deck would actually be best served by a land count of somewhere around 38. In the post-MH3 EDH landscape I truly don't understand people who are clinging onto low-mid-30s land counts for their conventionally-curved midrange EDH decks. Add lands! They let you cast spells!
