## Key Clips

- You don't have to play at Galov. Edgar Marov here is my standin for the best-in-class cards, for the strongest options available, for optimization for power. Substitute Edgar Marov with Thassa's Oracle or whatever makes sense to you. It's fine to not play the strongest cards, to not keep your decks perfectly up to date, to not constantly optimize for power.
- The idea that as new sets come along, commander decks have to be maintained, and that otherwise they will become outdated when more powerful cards are released. This line of thinking originates from competitive games where that question is not only valid, but one that needs to be asked over and over again.
- Even if commander was an entirely competitive game, there would still be a vast multitude of reasons to not play only the decks or cards currently acknowledged to be the best. Somebody adapted Ruby Storm into Commander and won a large cEDH tournament with it, despite the deck not being on the radar before that.
- Social Commander isn't competitive. It's a casual game and its primary purpose isn't, or at least shouldn't be, to win the tournament. If we focus too much on winning, the social experience often diminishes. We should center on the people around us, the social interaction, the shared interest.
- I really dislike the current language of the bracket system because it refers to bracket 4 as optimized just because it's a high power bracket. This implies the players choosing the lower brackets aren't optimizing their decks, which is a grave misunderstanding. Players are absolutely optimizing their decks, just not for winning. A good friend put tremendous work into his deck, but that work included powering it down when it became too domineering. He optimized it for a social play experience.
- More competitively minded players tend to be more committed, and more committed folks are more likely to be outspoken, and their arguments are often snappier and easier to make. This creates an online culture of negativity surrounding issues like power creep, game balance, and prices. It's a cycle that makes everything seem a lot more catastrophic than it really is.
- Is Commander getting too expensive? No, it always was. Magic has always been too expensive. The recent cost increases accentuate the problem, but they didn't cause it. By definition a staple can't become as expensive as Smothering Tithe or Mystic Study, since at that point they stop being staples. Cultivate still rules the free mana slot and that's from 2010. Sol Ring, Dark Ritual, Swords to Plowshares, and Counterspell have all been here from the very beginning.
- If the high release frequency is too much for you, then just take a break. You don't even need to stop playing. Just don't keep up with Edge of Eternities or Spider-Man. Your commander decks will still be playable. The community will still be there. But if you're feeling a compulsion to stay up to date and you don't enjoy it, then it might be time to re-evaluate your relationship with the game.
