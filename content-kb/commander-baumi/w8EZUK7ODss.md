## Key Clips

- The Regalia is a four-mana colorless vehicle from Final Fantasy. It's a 4/4 with haste and crew one. Whenever it attacks, we get to flip cards from the top of our deck until we find a land. Then we put that land into play tapped and the rest of the cards on the bottom of our deck.
- The Regalia is a colorless ramp spell similar to Solemn Simulacrum, sharing its four colorless mana cost. It can be played in any deck and is reasonably easy to cast. But the Regalia has a couple of major advantages over Solemn that make it stand out far above the rest of the colorless ramp options.
- The first advantage is that the Regalia is a 4/4 with haste. Solemn, Scampering Scrapbot, they all share the same issue of being mostly useless for their mana cost. A 4/4 is a respectable stat line, especially with haste, easily breaking through most defenses on turn four and pushing relevant damage. Crew one is very easy and the vast majority of decks can meet that requirement.
- Every single turn, the Regalia puts a new land into play. While we don't get to choose the land, it's also not limited to any specific kind of land. We can hit multicolor and utility lands. In higher budget decks, particularly three or more colors, a mana base can often consist of only a handful of basics, so the Regalia grabbing everything is a major upside over Solemn or Cultivate.
- Comparable cards exist: Lumbering World Wagon rams a basic on enter/attack but has crew 4, a real cost. Primeval Herald is a four-mana 1/1 with trample that ramps a basic, but 1/1 is weak for a card looking to get hits in. Overlord of the Hauntwoods is a five-mana 6/5 that makes a tapped land of every color, stronger but five mana and harder to reach. Crucially, all of these are green.
- Green is notorious in Commander because ramping is really important and green is the best at ramping. Other colors often have to make do with generic options inferior to green's, but the Regalia is colorless and straight up on the power level of green ramp spells. I'm playing it in green decks and it's performing better than many native options.
- As a vehicle, the Regalia has a little inbuilt protection. Artifact removal will hit it, but creature removal is even more common and it can sometimes dodge that, unlike Overlord of the Hauntwoods which never dodges it. It often rams three, four, five lands while applying pressure. Right now it goes for like 20 cents, which I think is insane ΓÇö people haven't realized how broken it is. - The Soul Stone is a black two-mana legendary artifact. It's indestructible and taps for black mana. For an additional seven mana and sacrificing a creature, it gains a new ability that reanimates a creature every upkeep. It's caused an uproar as an unapologetic piece of power creep at mythic rarity tied to a Universes Beyond property, likely to end up a $50 automatic-inclusion card.
