## Summary

The creator shares a tip for cutting a large card pile (e.g. 170–175 cards) down to a 100-card Commander deck. Building on the prior principle of starting with the end/endgame state in mind, they acknowledge that this clarity is easier once you've played a deck but harder at the initial cut. Two tools address that: goldfishing, and the "33 card deck" method. The method: take your target deck size and divide by three, then build a small, focused, functional version of the deck you intend to make. This miniature deck reveals quickly whether the core idea works, and makes goldfishing easy. Goldfishing the 33-card version shows what does and doesn't function, and crucially identifies which types of cards you need and how many copies of each to make the deck reliably do its intended thing. Those findings then guide trimming the full 175-card list down to 100. Practical deckbuilding heuristic: prototype a one-third-size functional core, goldfish it to learn required card categories and quantities, then use that to make cuts.
