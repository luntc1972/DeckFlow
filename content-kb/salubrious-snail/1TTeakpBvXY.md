## Summary

The video argues it can be fine to run some "bad cards" in a Commander deck, and shows how to decide how many. It suggests using a hypergeometric calculator (Archidekt's or the creator's) not just to check how often you draw good cards, but how often you won't draw bad ones. Core idea: many decks have two phases, pre-engine and post-engine. Early on you have few cards and rely on synergies, draw, tutors, filtering, ramp, removal and defense to get "online"; once you're cycling lots of cards per turn, an occasional useless card matters little. Method: split nonlands into three buckets. Bucket 1 = premium draw/filtering. Bucket 2 = near-useless-early cards: hyper-situational cards, protection spells, late-game tech, finishers, pet cards. Bucket 3 = everything else (ramp, removal, narrower draw). Check odds of drawing a Bucket 1 card, and odds of drawing multiple Bucket 2 cards; ~5 situational cards rarely clump, double digits do. Counterbalance more Bucket 2 with more Bucket 1. He praises fast-lands for the early game. Examples: Radha, Heir to Keld's "funny seven-drop" slot (Panglacial Wurm, now Tyrant of Discord); Voltaic Construct combo; a Meria voltron deck (most Bucket 2). Consistent foundation buys room for jank.

- **[04:33]** He likes fast-lands because turns four and beyond you're drawing/looting/filtering to find lands, so the hardest turns to have an untapped on-color land are the first three—exactly what fast-lands fill.
- **[05:32]** Split nonlands into three buckets: Bucket 1 is high-quality draw and filtering; Bucket 2 is near-useless-early cards (hyper-situational cards, protection, late-game tech, finishers, bad pet cards); Bucket 3 is the rest—ramp, removal, narrower draw.
- **[06:24]** Ask how likely you are to draw multiple Bucket 2 cards: with about five hyper-situational cards the odds of drawing more than one are low, but in the double digits you get games drawing an Overwhelming Stampede and a Heroic Intervention in your first ten cards.
