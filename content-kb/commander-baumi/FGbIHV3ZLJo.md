## Summary

This video reviews Wizards' recent update to the Commander bracket system. The creator, who uses brackets for matchmaking at a local game store, observed that players consistently overestimate their decks' brackets to avoid pub-stomping, actively avoiding bracket two because it included precons and carried a stigma of weakness. He argues most recent precons are perfectly playable; they only seem weak because new, inexperienced players pilot them. He criticizes the language 'upgraded' and 'optimized' as toxic and poorly chosen, suggesting experience-based names like 'incremental' instead. The old bracket two's low floor (precons plus beginners) squeezed bracket one out of existence and made bracket free a chaotic melting pot, with a 'bracket 2.5' gap emerging. The update removed precons from bracket two, raising its power, giving bracket one room, and effectively expanding a usable three-bracket system into four. He praises the new staples-based differentiator between brackets two and three but warns the new turn-count expectations (9/8/6/4/0) are too harsh and risk turning guidelines into enforced rules, fueling power creep. He also discusses trimming the game changer list, opposes optional hybrid-mana mono-color, calls commander damage poorly designed, and proposes a 'lucky energy' token for the last player.

## Key Clips

- Players often overestimated the bracket of their deck. I constantly had people overestimating their own decks to make absolutely sure they are not accidentally pub stomping. At Command Fest in Bonn, somebody played an unmodified precon in bracket free, arguing it was a strong precon so it's not bracket two.
- Bracket two was seen as a lesser bracket, a space for incompetent decks. This was caused primarily because bracket two included precons, and many players shared the perception that precons are terrible. Two root causes: old precons used to be bad, and precons are most commonly played by new players who are still learning.
- Both 'upgraded' and 'optimized' are terribly chosen words. Upgraded implies upgraded precon, which narrowed the range of bracket two. Optimized implies decks in lower brackets aren't optimized ΓÇö Commander is exactly the kind of format where decks optimized around terrible ideas are meant to flourish. A better approach: name brackets around the expected play experience, e.g. bracket two as 'incremental' rather than 'core'.
- The most important part of the update: precons were removed from the bracket two definition. There's no longer a necessary floor, which raises the overall power level of the bracket and gives bracket one more room to breathe. Bracket two decks are now meant to be fully functional, reasonably powerful decks with room for pet cards or unique synergy pieces.
- The best addition are the differentiators at the top: what sets brackets two and three apart are staples. Are you playing cards because they're good by themselves (bracket three) or because they fit the context of the deck (bracket two)?
- New aspect: expected turns before a game ends, from the bottom up ΓÇö 9, 8, 6, 4, and 0 for cEDH. But winning on turn seven is quite fast for casual Commander even in bracket three. The Commander Clash Crew's recent stats episode showed matches in a fairly strong pod usually ended only after 9.3 turns. Treating these turn counts as rules will cause real power creep.
- cEDH takes on an odd role because it really shouldn't be part of the bracket system ΓÇö it's a fundamentally different game from social Commander. Including it feels like creating brackets for Uno and then putting chess at the top. But including cEDH is mostly an acknowledgement of that community, which has value, so it's not a big enough problem to need solving.
