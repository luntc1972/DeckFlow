## Summary

The video reviews three abandoned Commander decks to show when a deck idea isn't worth finishing. (1) Rograkh/Thrasios polymorph "turbostax" aimed at Niv-Mizzet Parun, Hullbreaker Horror, two Jin-Gitaxias, Nexus of Becoming, God-Pharaoh's Statue and Proteus Staff. It was scrapped as too generic: its bombs are staples with no synergistic core. A revisit would need a more specific partner like Kydele or Sakashima and at least three similar-niche targets. (2) Throne of the Grim Captain with Norman Osborn in Grixis. Norman's discard-and-cast ability made the discard plan too easy and generic, and Throne can't anchor a deck ("the Volo problem"). Throne should add spice to a deck with an existing identity, like a Mardu Kroxa and Kunoros version, which fit but wasn't fun to play. (3) Enigmatic Incarnation with Tuvasa the Sunlit, rule of law effects, Karametra, Words of Wind and Fear of Sleep Paralysis. The card is too slow. Enchantress and stax plans clash, and heavy stax like Sythis and Sanctum Weaver doesn't suit bracket 2. Heuristics: check how likely the plan is to come together and whether the deck is fun without its key card. Also consider opponents' experience and replay staleness, especially for cheese strategies.

- **[04:03]** If I were to take another crack at this polymorph idea it'd need a partner like Kydele or Sakashima with more specificity, and the polymorph targets would need to be far less generic. Fewer than three targets is gimmick territory, while too many gives a generic or inconsistent gameplan.
- **[07:31]** A discard subtheme in these colors and creature types was going to work, but the commander makes it too easy. Unlike specific payoffs like Sharuum or Ox of Agonas, Green Goblin lets him cast anything discarded that turn at a two-mana discount, turning the plan into generic Magic.
- **[08:28]** It's "the Volo problem": a card creating constraints that are fun to deckbuild around but don't create interesting gameplay on their own. Throne of the Grim Captain isn't well-situated to anchor a gameplan.
- **[09:04]** A Throne of the Grim Captain deck would need to be a mostly-formed deck where the card just happens to make sense, adding spice to a strong existing identity rather than creating identity on its own.
- **[14:45]** Enchantment decks can pump a mediocre card with generic enchantress effects or stax pieces, but the two clash, particularly rule of law effects, which stop you playing more than one enchantment per turn to draw cards unless some have flash.
- **[15:41]** Rule of law effects are powerful stax effects, and it doesn't make sense to fight low-power gameplans with high-power stax. Bracket 2 games can have interaction, but the tools should be a little lighter duty.
- **[16:52]** First exercise: think about the basic plan and how likely it is to come together versus the deck doing something else. For niche combos or hidden commanders, make sure the deck is fun even without the key card. The Enigmatic Incarnation deck doesn't function smoothly without it, while the Sharuum Astral Slide deck is still a fun artifact deck before the Slide.
- **[17:30]** Consider how the deck's gameplan fits an actual game: what decks it will face at its power level, what opponents' experience will be, and whether it stays enjoyable over multiple games. Cheesy strategies like Polymorph tend to be boring to play against more than once.
