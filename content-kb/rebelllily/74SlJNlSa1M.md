## Summary

The video argues that many synergy-based Commander decks lose despite having interaction, card draw, and a clear game plan because they play purely additively—each card incrementally builds on the last. Once a game escalates past a power threshold, simply adding effects is too slow; you need cards that scale your plan to close out the game. The creator calls these 'turbochargers': not combos or game-enders, but cards that add or multiply the effect of everything you play (often using the word 'each'). Additive examples: Torbran staples extra damage onto each burn spell; Glorious Anthem pumps the whole board. Multiplying examples: Fiery Emancipation doubles red damage; Combat Celebrant-style extra combat steps double creature damage via double strike, turning a 1-mana Llanowar Elves into a 4-damage threat. The advice: when designing a deck, include a dedicated package of four to six cards that pay off or scale your plan—cards that let you 'become the problem' on demand. Examples given include extra-combat spells for an artifact-token spellslinger deck, and Scapeshift plus Valakut as a single turbo button in a lands deck to convert future mountains into burn. This also makes games more engaging by letting you choose when to ramp up.

## Key Clips

- For some reason I can't win with this deck — it's a common problem I see online. The deck has interaction, synergy, card draw and a clear game plan, all your basic food groups for a good deck. So why does it keep losing at your pods? It's because your deck needs this turbo button.
- That's why you need cards that only leave your hand when you need to lean in and get serious — when you need to slam that button and close the game out. Turbochargers are not combos or game-enders; they are cards that turbo charge our game plan.
- Turbochargers generally add or multiply the effect of our cards. For a burn deck, Torbran is an additive turbocharger, stapling a shock onto every burn card. Fiery Emancipation is a multiplying turbocharger, doubling the damage of all our red spells.
- For a creature deck, Glorious Anthem is an additive turbocharger, adding one power and toughness across the board, while Combat Historian is a multiplying turbocharger, doubling damage by giving creatures double strike. Even a one-mana Llanowar Elves can deal four damage.
- Most decks built on synergy play like they're simply additive: turn one card A, turn two card B making A stronger, turn three card C strengthened by B. At some point the game passes a threshold where everybody is spiking in power and you can't keep adding on — you need to double your effect because time is no longer on your side.
- When designing a new deck, make sure it has a means to turbocharge its plan to close the game. Knowing the game plan, you want a package of four to six cards dedicated to paying off or scaling your plan — cards that let you become the problem as soon as you play them.
- In a spell-slinger deck you might create artifact creature tokens through non-creature casts, play Lord effects like Chief of the Foundry to pump your creatures, then turbocharge with sorceries that give extra combat steps to double your damage while still fitting the spell-slinging theme.
- If you're playing a lands deck you can add Scapeshift and Valakut as a single-card turbo button to turn future Mountains into bolts, making your innocent ramp spells into damage spells. Adding turbo-button cards makes decks better and creates more engaging games — you control when to be chill and when to be the problem.
