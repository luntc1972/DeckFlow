## Summary

This video presents a two-phase goldfishing method for Commander deck design, framing every deck list as a hypothesis that must be tested through solo play rather than at the table. The core principle is proactivity: a deck must resolve a strong game plan and pose a real threat opponents must answer, regardless of how much removal or card draw it packs. Phase one, the rough, sketches roughly 100 cards using placeholder ramp (Arcane Signets, Swords to Plowshares, basics that tap for anything) so the builder focuses only on whether the game plan flows. Within about 27 plan-dedicated cards, pick enablers, payoffs, and pet cards, then play out up to six turns repeatedly to see how enablers tee up payoffs and where the commander fits. Commanders often play differently than they read. Phase two, the polish, replaces placeholders and mulligans repeatedly, tracking how many hands out of ten are keepable and why, tuning until six-or-seven keeps consistently deliver the plan. Goldfishing structurally cannot test interaction, so correct removal counts are built in via templates. The process repeats: rough, polish, rough again. The rough tests game-plan strength; the polish tests consistency.

## Key Clips

- A deck list is always just a guess. A hypothesis. The one thing you don't do, that you should be doing, is testing your deck more. But not at the table, and definitely not online at a blurry spell table match. But testing during design through goldfishing.
- The number one problem I see of players who goldfish during deck design is they are trying to solve too many problems at once. In design, it's incredibly difficult to address every problem at once when it's much easier to resolve key decisions, then move on to the rest.
- If your deck doesn't have a strong enough game plan, it doesn't matter how much removal it's packing or how much draw, because you're just ripping kingmaker card after kingmaker card and letting someone else win. Commander at any level is about resolving your game plan.
- The first phase of goldfishing is the rough. The goal of this process is to test the most important part of your deck, the game plan. Is the game plan good enough? Does your commander work the way you think it does? Commanders often read one way, but secretly play another way once you goldfish and experience it.
- I would throw in eight Arcane Signets and eight Swords to Plowshares as placeholder cards and just Islands that tap for everything because at this point of deck building, it doesn't matter what the ramp is, it matters how the deck is flowing. So once you quickly rough out 100 cards, you just mulligan into a hand that looks playable, then try it out. On average, I will recommend you to play up to six turns.
- The polish is where you're running hands back over and over again, but not playtesting them. In cEDH, we build for stronger mulligans because the game is so compressed. The most important thing you can do right now is mulligan again and again and see how many hands you can keep out of 10. And also note the hands that you can't keep.
- There's a drawback to this method. What about interaction? Goldfishing is a solo activity. There's no board wipes, no counter spells, and no pressure, which means it structurally cannot tell you whether you have enough removal or interaction. The easiest thing I have found is just integrating the right removal numbers through our template system.
- The rough test if your game plan is strong and the polish test if your deck is consistent. You keep polishing the deck until you're keeping six or sevens on average and they're consistently delivering on the game plan you want.
