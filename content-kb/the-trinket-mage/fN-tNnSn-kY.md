## Summary

This video argues that EDH/Commander deck brewing is largely "dead" as a necessity, driven by three changes: precons now release with nearly every set (versus once a year), aggregation sites like EDHrec hand you the common synergy pieces instantly, and new cards are explicitly designed for Commander so "every card is an EDH card." The result is easier, better-functioning decks but more homogenized lists that drift toward a metagame and feel like pseudo-precons—especially narrow types like shrines, curses, or vehicles where ~22 staples plus enchantment-focused ramp/draw/removal fill most slots. Made-for-Commander cards (The One Ring, Smothering Tithe, Bolas's Citadel) widen the power gap, so jank or unsupported themes (walls, color-hack, old Experiment Kraj lists) struggle to keep up, pushing players toward the strongest options. To keep brews unique, the creator suggests self-imposed restrictions: building from Scryfall searches without EDHrec rank, banning creatures or off-color staples, or thematic constraints (a lands deck avoiding landfall; a "feels blue" Rocco mill deck). It sponsors Commander Template, a deck-building site offering math-based land/ramp/removal/draw suggestions and Scryfall-tag filtering. Conclusion: brewing is now optional, which lowers the entry barrier but makes genuine custom brews stand out more.

That number is used for calculating the lands and ramp. But you can adjust this.
