## Summary

Tanuk Memorial Ensign is a free-mana GR commander built around a fetchland-heavy ramp shell. His landfall ability pings each opponent for 1 damage, and a second land entering each turn also draws a card. Fetchlands trigger twice (enter, then crack), so the deck draws and pings without needing extra ramp; Audrid's Charm and Crop Rotation fetch lands at instant speed to trigger on opponents' turns. Recursion engines—Azusa, Crucible of Worlds, Ramunap Excavator, World Shaper—replay lands repeatedly for free pings, draws, and ramp. The build is bracket 4, non-budget, with game-changers like Sol Ring and Glacial Chasm as a damage-prevention stonewall. Win lines: mass land reanimation (Aftermath Analyst, Lumra, Splendid Reclamation) returns all sacrificed lands for a flood of triggers, often lethal. It also runs mass land destruction (Decree of Annihilation, Jokulhaups, Bust, Impending Disaster), since the deck rebuilds faster than opponents. Keen Sense and Snake Umbra on Tanuk draw extra cards per damage, approaching infinite resources. Instant-speed removal handles threats and the risk of decking out. In play, Tanuk pinged opponents to death without needing MLD.

## Key Clips

- Tanuk Memorial Ensign is a free mana GR two-four kaboo pilot. He has a landfall ability that deals one damage to each opponent and if we trigger it for the second time each turn, we also draw a card. Tanuk turns all of our fetch lands and ramping into damage and card draw.
- Danuk wants us to have at least two lands enter each turn, rewarding us with card draw if we do so. The easiest way is through fetch lands. We can have a fetchland enter, ping once with Tanuk, then crack it to get another landfall trigger and draw a card. Using fetchlands we don't even need to ramp to guarantee our card draw.
- Fetch lands are core to our ramp engine, centered around recurring and replaying them multiple times each turn. Azusa, Lost but Seeking gives us two additional land drops, and Crucible of Worlds lets us play lands out of our graveyard. Pair these with a fetch land and we're searching free lands out of our deck every turn.
- Instant-speed sac-and-grab two-land options — Animist's restoration, Harrow, and Roaring Regrowth — let us trigger Tanuk on our opponent's turn to grab additional card draw, and they fill up our graveyard with lands for some of our other plans.
- That's where mass land reanimation comes into play. With some setup, we can sacrifice all of our lands and then play a card like Aftermath Analyst, Lumra Bellow of the Woods, or Splendid Reclamation to return them all to the battlefield. This results in a huge number of Tanuk triggers all at once and should often be enough to finish the game.
- This is a mass land destruction deck. Decree of Annihilation, Jokulhaups, Thoughts of Ruin, Boom/Bust, and Impending Disaster get rid of all lands on the battlefield, which is genuinely a win condition by itself. Most decks won't be playing our density of lands, ramp, and recursion, so if we hit them with MLD, they won't be able to rebuild anywhere near as quickly as we can.
- Keen Sense and Snake Umbra are both auras that make it so whenever the enchanted creature deals damage, we draw a card. Play a land, ping everybody, draw three cards. Do it again, draw four. Sticking one of these onto Tanuk gives us near-infinite resources — so many cards that it's a bit of a worry we might deck ourselves.
- This is comfortably a bracket four deck with a multitude of game changers and a very streamlined, aggressive game plan, including cards that shouldn't be played in lower brackets like Sol Ring. There's also a $50 budget version available on Patreon.
