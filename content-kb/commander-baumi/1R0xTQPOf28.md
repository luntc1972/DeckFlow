## Summary

A casual MTG Commander stream where the host plays community games on Spell Table while explaining deck-building choices. He opens with his "Six" deck — a basic-lands graveyard recursion deck that sacrifices and replays Six via Greater Good, ramps with cards like Nyxbloom Ancient and Cultivator Colossus, then wins by sacrificing every land and returning them with effects like Splendid Reclamation, Lumbra, and Endrek's land-bounce to fuel huge Mana. He rejects Groundskeeper as too expensive and adds Spelunking. He strongly endorses proxies, arguing cEDH is cheap because you just print cards. Next he plays an Aragorn, King of Gondor deck blending Voltron (Angelic Destiny–style auras, totem armor, beam-stick equipment) with clone effects (Saheeli's Artistry) to copy opponents' threats; Aragorn grants Monarch and unblockable attacks. Opponents pile dragons (Ur-Dragon, Dragon Tempest), Cleopatra counters, and a winter/Dusk hand-size deck. The final game features his Rocco/Norin deck using Norin's repeated flickering plus Witty Roastmaster, Genesis Chamber, and Kodama of the East Tree to chain permanents and ping opponents. It ends explosively when Over the Top dumps his board, enabling Impact Tremors, Boss's Chauffeur tokens, and Abdel Adrian-style drain for lethal. He prefers incidental lifegain over dedicated lifegain decks.

## Key Clips

- CEDH is the cheapest format because you just print the cards. None of this is real. It's not a cedh tournament if you're not allowing proxies.
- Groundskeeper would not be good in the six deck. It's just too expensive. If you're spending free mana to return a land to your hand that's just not good. It's one of those cards that's a trap — it makes every card you play more expensive by two mana, and ideally we don't even have lands in our graveyard because I bring them all back immediately.
- This is a basic-lands combo deck — every land is a basic land. The Scapeshift-style plan: I splendid reclamation all the lands back, discard so many lands into my graveyard, then Primeval Bloom makes each land tap for huge mana. With the aftermath analyst I return all lands into play and make six more Nyxbloom Ancients.
- I won't play life-gain decks, I don't like them, but I build decks that have a good amount of incidental life gain as part of their synergies — like Aragorn with lifelink. It makes for nice gameplay because you're always a bit safe. But getting too much life draws aggro, which is actually a problem.
- This Aragorn deck plays a weird mixture of Voltron and cloning. The idea is either I beat you to death with Aragorn, or if I don't, you probably have something useful on the board and I can clone it and beat you to death with whatever you've got. Aragorn is nice for that because he's a 4/4 with vigilance and lifelink you can do a lot of stuff with.
- Norin is the secret commander — Rocco gives us three colors but it's really a Norin deck. Whenever any player casts a spell or a creature attacks, Norin exiles and returns at end step, so with Witty Roastmaster and Genesis Chamber and enduring innocence, every flicker pings opponents and draws cards.
- On combo: it depends how easy it is. If you say I have an infinite and it's a two-card instant kill for free mana, that's a problem. I can play a different deck with no tutors. The big cutoff for me is — do you win during the infinite? If you just Torment of Hellfire and kill me the turn after, I don't care for it.
- It's always good to end on a good note. If you keep going until it's not fun anymore, you carry that negative experience with you. I want us to feel like we had fun today.
