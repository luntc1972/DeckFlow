## Summary

This EDHRECast episode analyzes Commander decks with unusual mana curves, using EDHREC data (commanders with 250+ decks) and Wasserstein distance from the average curve. The baseline deck runs ~10-11 one-drops, ~17 two-drops, ~14-15 three-drops, then tapers off. Key lessons: a commander that defines your early turns frees you to skew the rest of the deck. Examples covered: Gallia/Alena partners spike at six-drops because the commanders ramp through turns three and four; Yisan loads up on seven- and nine-drops he cheats into play; Lurrus and Squall front-load one- and two-drops due to mana-value caps, forcing careful late-game planning; Ulamog and Kozilek show flat curves because colorless decks lack cheap options and need many mana rocks; Edric and Shu Yun stack cheap cantrips to trigger draw/prowess; Ashling dips at four (evoke cost) then peaks at six-seven; Marvo builds a reverse bell curve around its eight-mana cap; mana-dork commanders (Ruby, Radha) jump at four. Watch for cost-deceptive cards (delve, X-spells, evoke) skewing curves. Finally, mana curves are identical across brackets one through four; only cEDH (bracket five) differs, peaking at zero- and one-drops for speed.

## Key Clips

- The average Commander deck contains about 10 or 11 one-drops, around 17 two-drops (the biggest density — mana rocks, ramp, cheap interaction), about 14-15 three-drops, nearly as many four-drops as one-drops (9 or 10), maybe five-plus five-drops, and around three six-drops, with numbers falling off sharply after that. The two- and three-drop slots are the highest concentration by far because they make good early plays and fill gaps later.
- Gallant and Alena (Gruul partners) produce a curve that starts at six: Gallant draws cards when you cast six-drops, and the three-mana commander into the five-mana partner means turns three and four are spoken for, so the deck spikes hugely at 36 six-drops and 10 seven-drops with almost nothing below.
- Yisan, the Wandering Bard has an up-and-down curve hitting only odd mana values, with an unusually high count of seven- and nine-drops because you cheat them into play with his activated ability rather than hard-casting them. Caring about a mana cost doesn't always make the curve extreme — Obosh as a commander stays close to average, but as a companion the deckbuilding restriction skews the data heavily.
- Lurrus of the Dream Den front-loads everything into one- and two-drops (about 20 one-drops, 32 two-drops) then drops off completely. A heavily front-loaded curve forces you to plan carefully how you close the game — turning a pile of one- and two-drops into something lethal by turn nine is an uphill climb.
- Colorless commanders like Ulamog and Kozilek produce remarkably flat curves with representation across nearly every mana value up to 11-12, because you must run big Eldrazi top-end plus a heavy package of mana rocks (Thran Dynamo, Gilded Lotus) and simply lack the color-based options to load up early turns the way colored decks do.
- Edric, Spymaster of Trest and Shu Yun, the Silent Tempest both spike at one-drops (Shu Yun ~29 one-drops) without their card text demanding it — Edric wants cheap evasive creatures to draw cards on hit, and Shu Yun wants cheap cantrips to trigger prowess and leave mana for the double-strike Voltron kill. Spellslinger and storm decks trend toward this distribution as they get more tuned.
- Marvo, Deep Operative shows a reverse bell curve — heavy ones and twos, a dip, then a spike back up to eight-drops — because clashing lets you cast a free spell of mana value eight or less when you win, so you stack cheap spells to set up the clash plus a dense top-end to cast for free.
- Mana-dork commanders (Ruby, Daring Tracker; Radha, Heart of Keld) and tap-for-mana commanders (Susan Foreman) cause a huge jump at the four-drop slot with almost nothing at one through three: when the command zone already fills your early-game ramp role, you don't spend deck slots duplicating it. Across all brackets the curves look identical except cEDH (bracket five), which peaks at the one-drop slot and runs more zero-drops (Chrome Mox) to operate a full turn or two earlier.
