## Summary

The creator wanted a Commander "bracket 2" deck that felt optimized rather than just weak, so they deliberately built a deck overloaded with subthemes—mimicking how precons feel unfocused—and tried to make it work. The deck is led by Glissa, Herald of Predation (green-black), which generates incubator/artifact tokens and provides a reliable wincon via counters and proliferate. Subthemes include: counters, artifact-token payoffs (Tarrian's Journal, Throne of Geth, Clock of Omens with counter-based mana rocks), a liquimetal package turning creatures into artifacts (Liquimetal Torque, Phyrexian Scriptures, Dollhouse of Horrors, Nexus of Becoming), token-copy populate, a graveyard package, constructs, and powerstones, plus an aristocrats-without-sac-outlets trick using populated incubators. It stays bracket 2 because nearly every win runs through Glissa producing two incubators per turn, capping power despite a niche infinite-mana combo and strong cards. To fight dead cards, the builder makes cards serve multiple roles, runs high ramp and draw counts, and limits tutors to roughly three. Ongoing problems: too few creatures and graveyard enablers for Dollhouse/God-Pharaoh's Gift, and weak standalone subtheme pieces. The deck wins ironically by swinging vanilla 6/6s.

## Key Clips

- **[02:11]** Preconstructed commander decks are typically unfocused by design—they feature slightly too many themes and elements because they're trying to give players a taste of different playstyles, and also give some easy paths to upgrade a given deck such as cutting a singular subtheme for some more relevant stuff.
- **[02:28]** What if I built a deck with way too many subthemes, like a truly irresponsible number of subthemes, and then tried my hardest to optimize it into a workable decklist?
- **[10:31]** I don't know if there's been an exact tutor limit given for bracket 2, but I've heard the number 3 floated around and that felt like a nice number to shoot for here that would force some creative workarounds without being an outright barrier against tutoring.
- **[10:53]** A big one is that I try pretty hard not to let a card perform only one task for the deck. There is no generic removal here—when I highlight the cards in my interaction suite that proliferate, use counters, or are lands, that excludes only five cards, and all of those either fit into another subtheme or have some sort of useful synergy.
- **[11:40]** Since there are single digit counts of setup and payoff for a number of different subthemes throughout the deck, it becomes very important to see more of the deck in order to piece together engines. I actually bought a couple of draw engines because I was noticing some light stallouts in the games I was playing, and sure enough that helped the deck out quite a bit.
- **[12:15]** Having a high count of ramp is also quite helpful since the deck has a relatively high mana commander that's relatively central to its gameplan, and always having the mana to replay her kinda lets me avoid needing to think too hard about how consistent my alternate wincons are.
- **[13:31]** There are two problems with the graveyard package currently—only 6 cards that put cards into the yard at the moment, and only 23 total creatures in the deck. If I've gone through 20 cards of my deck, I've only seen 4 or 5 creatures, and the number of those that have made their way to the graveyard is almost certainly less than that.
