## Summary

This video argues that several Commander precon commanders are far more powerful when rebuilt from scratch outside their precons, since Wizards prints simple decks around overpowered leaders. Four picks (ordered by release date): Elivia of the Wild Court, a 4-mana W creature who attaches self-growing Virtuous Roll auras that scale exponentially (+1/+1, then +4/+4, +9/+9, etc.) and draws on combat damage; she's a self-contained engine, so creature slots can go to utility, protection, ramp, with busted support like Umbra Mystic granting shield-like protection. Gonti, Canny Acquisitor (5-mana B) steals top cards on combat damage and discounts borrowed spells; legendary duplication (Spark Double, Sakashima) stacks Gontis for compounding card and mana advantage. Yuma, Proud Protector (RG) makes 4-power plant tokens whenever deserts hit the graveyard, enabling power-based green draw engines (Garruk's Uprising, Greater Good) and land-recursion loops. Satya, Aetheflux Genius (4-mana Jeskai 3/5 menace haste) copies a creature on attack, abusing ETBs, generates energy, and goes infinite with extra-combat creatures (Aggravated Assault, Breath of Fury, Lightning Runner). Weaknesses noted: Elivia needs to attack and is removal-vulnerable. Creator regrets omitting Zimone.

## Key Clips

- Precon commanders are often outrageously powerful because it lets Wizards print simpler decks with less specific cards that still function just because the commanders are so powerful. But if you take those commanders out of the precons and build a deck around them from scratch, they can become absolutely filthy.
- Elevia of the Wild Court is a four-mana Selesnya 4/4. When she enters or attacks, she creates a virtuous role token and attaches it to another creature, giving +1/+1 for each enchantment we control. She's a fully self-contained engine, leading to exponential power: +1/+1, then +4/+4, +9/+9, then 16, 25, 36, and so on.
- Because Elevia turns any board into a game-ending threat, she can focus creature selection entirely on utility, interaction, protection, and ramp. Manatots and beatdown duds become 5-power threats, and Selfless Spirit becomes amazing board protection no longer limited by being a small body.
- Gonti, Canny Acquisitor reduces the cost of spells we cast but don't own by one and steals the top card of a player's library on combat damage. The trick is legendary duplication—Spark Double, Irenicus's Vile Duplication, Sakashima—because Gonti stacks with itself: two Gontis steal six cards and save 12 mana, three steal nine and save 27.
- Yuma, Proud Protector's cost reduces by one per land in the graveyard, so in red-green he rarely costs more than three. Whenever a desert card hits our graveyard from anywhere, we make a 4/2 plant warrior with reach. Four power enables green's power-based draw engines like Garruk's Uprising, Elemental Bond, and Outcaster Trailblazer.
- Dune Enchanter turns all our lands into deserts no matter where they are, so we can throw anything into the graveyard and still build a board and draw cards. Greater Good is particularly nasty: sacrifice a four-power creature, draw four, discard three including deserts, make another four-power creature, and keep going.
- Satya, Aetherflux Genius is a four-mana Jeskai 3/5 with menace and haste. When he attacks he makes a tapped, attacking token copy of another creature with no limitations, fully using its ETBs, static abilities, and death triggers, plus generates two energy. Keep the token by paying energy equal to its mana cost.
- Satya goes infinite with any creature that creates additional combats on combat damage, like Port Razer. Port Razer can't attack the same player twice, but Satya's copies start counting from the beginning, making a new one each additional combat. He also goes infinite with Bloodthirster, Breath of Fury, and his own precon card Lightning Runner.
