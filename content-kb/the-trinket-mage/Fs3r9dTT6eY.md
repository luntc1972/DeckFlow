## Summary

This Magic Mirror podcast episode debates Commander deckbuilding philosophy with guest Travis Gafford. The central argument concerns tutors: Travis dislikes how they increase a deck's consistency and linearity, letting players fetch the same win condition every game and acting as the second-best card available in any moment. The hosts counter that tutors are not inherently the problem—linear decks are—and that narrow tutors (Trinket Mage, Crop Rotation, Court of Calling) reward deckbuilding effort by acting like build-your-own cards, with the cost paid during deck construction. They note that as Wizards prints more functionally identical effects, tutors become less necessary but enable complex multi-piece decks. A second debate covers Reliquary Tower: the hosts argue it is never free, worsens your manabase, competes with better colorless utility lands (Rogue's Passage, Homeward Path), and that discarding to hand size is often fine. Finally, they discuss the bracket system: game changers distract from synergy-based deckbuilding, bracket three is intentionally wide-open so players should run removal and card draw to handle anything, and most players simply build decks poorly. Travis argues bracket three is overloaded and labeling decks low/high three would help rule-zero conversations.

- **[18:50]** When you build a package of removal spells, you are doing so with intention during the deck building phase. All of the thought process and the deck building is where the cost is. It's kind of like a build your own charm. If you have a crop rotation, you're thinking, okay, I'm going to get a pit of offerings in my deck. That makes your mana slightly worse, but now you can exile graveyards off of your crop rotation.
- **[26:07]** If you want to create more consistency in your deck, you can just play a second copy of that singular card, which functionally does a lot more. As we get more and more cards that can increase the consistency in and of themselves, tutors become arguably more offensive because they're fulfilling a requirement for the deck that you don't need anymore.
- **[27:55]** You can turn up the complexity of your deck in terms of the number of moving parts. You can make it so that you need five cards to function a particular thing rather than four. You can add different synergy families that make it less and less likely that your deck would function without tutors.
- **[40:55]** Reliquary Tower is never free. The main thing I dislike is when people present it as free because it's probably making your mana base worse. And even if it isn't, you could be running a better colorless land in its place in a lot of decks. It's just good to acknowledge that you're paying a price.
- **[50:50]** Game changers are quite literally the biggest distraction of all time when it comes to building a bracket three deck. That's not how you should be thinking about a bracket three deck. What actually makes your deck your deck is the synergy. How consistent is it? How fast does it win? These are the questions that actually matter when determining the bracket, not how many game changers you're running.
