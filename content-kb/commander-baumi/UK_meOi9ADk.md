## Summary

This Secrets of Strixhaven Commander review praises the new turbulent lands: tapped dual lands that enter untapped only if opponents control eight or more lands, subtly aiding disadvantaged seats while staying playable in any seat. The creator cites 154 tracked games using a homebrew "lucky energy" rule (an emblem treasure for the last player), yielding near-even seat win rates (~25% each) versus the typical 30/25/25/20 skew. He criticizes bond/dual lands as unconditionally designed and favors conditional lands. Card evaluations follow. Honorable mentions: elder Dragons, two planeswalkers (Professor Dellian Fell, Ral Zarek), ramp-stapled spells, and Petrified Hamlet as possible cEDH land hate. Top picks: 9) increment creatures Bertha/Pensive Professor/Topiary Lecturer for +1/+1 counter decks; 8) Prismari Charm; 7) tie Emeritus of Truths (Swords to Plowshares, a deterrent like Seal of Doom) and Emeritus of Woe (Demonic Tutor); 6) Decorum Dissertation as a stronger Phyrexian Arena; 5) Muse Seeker looter; 4) Emeritus of Ideation (Ancestral Recall); 3) Augusta, Order Returned; 2) Flashback for spellslinger; 1) Grave Researcher (Reanimate), the best card for social Commander.

## Key Clips

- **[00:45]** The turbulence are especially good because even if you're in the first or second seat, they're still fine, perfectly playable. That's exactly what these kinds of cards should be, reasonable to play either way, but slightly better for players in disadvantageous seats.
- **[09:01]** Petrified Hamlet... is an untapped colorless land that lets us name a land card when it enters, and then activated abilities of those lands can't be activated unless they are mana abilities. So this doesn't turn off Gaea's Cradle, but it does hit Otowara and Boseiju... Crop Rotation can act as an emergency stifle with this land.
- **[09:01]** Embrace the Paradox is an instant speed Urban Evolution, which is actually a huge upgrade. I think that's even better than Lessons from Life. If you are in the Urban Evolution business, you are now definitely in the Embrace the Paradox business.
- **[17:10]** Once we have Seal of Doom in play, our opponents know that whoever puts something scary into play first is most likely to eat the seal. So they wait. They don't play their scary thing at all. Cards like this direct our opponent's actions in our favor. An Emeritus of Truth does the same.
- **[21:50]** Look at Emrakul as a six mana five five with flying and ward two that draws three free cards on ETB. Any flicker suddenly gets the modality of spending an additional mana to draw three free cards. Because ancestral recall is instant speed, we can find a nice timing for it, and it has inbuilt protection.
- **[25:03]** As soon as Augusta attacks, we can grow her significantly. This incidental graveyard hate will add up, dampening our opponent's chances. There are political aspects too. We can force our opponents into exiling specific cards in exchange for not smacking them with a flying creature we spent free mana on.
- **[27:04]** Flashback is a god tier spell in spell slinger lists. Think of it as a modal spell where the modes are all the instants and sorceries in our graveyard. This becomes especially powerful when combined with effects that create mana as we cast. Think Ashling Flame Dancer, Storm Kiln Artist, or Runaway Steamkin.
- **[27:54]** Grave researcher is the perfect prepared creature. It's cheap so we can get it down early. It fuels its own requirements, which once set up can be met every turn without fail, and its spell reanimate is very powerful. It's a low commitment card that always provides value with potentially game winning upside.
