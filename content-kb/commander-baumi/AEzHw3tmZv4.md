## Summary

A budget commander tournament host (strict $50 price cap, banning fast mana effectively and Sol Ring explicitly) profiles three of the strongest decks seen at his events. Magda, Brazen Outlaw: a two-mana red combo commander that taps cheap dwarves to make treasures, then loops Clock of Omens plus an artifact dwarf to generate infinite tapped treasures (which Magda can sacrifice without untapping), adding Academy Manufactor or Battered Golem for infinite mana, lock pieces like God-Pharaoh's Statue, and a Pyrite Spellbomb/Elixir of Immortality kill; cheap enough to leave budget for value and interaction, she reached many finals but never won because regulars built to hate her. Kutzal, Malamet Exemplar: a Rule of Law beatdown deck exploiting action-economy asymmetry (opponents can't cast on your turn), stacked with indestructibility/hexproof protection and +1/+1 counter generation plus overruns; won the sixth tournament. Ketis/Malcolm: a midrange-combo Izzet partner pair generating heavy treasures off combat damage spread by Ketis, enabling counters, removal, and Reckless Fire Weaver kills; won the third tournament. He praises budget commander's slower pace, diversity, and fairness.

## Key Clips

- I run budget commander tournaments. These tournaments are very strictly regulated to a 50 price point and have become a fixture in my local community with many talented and creative deck builders participating to show the best they can do given the restrictions.
- Magda, Brazen Outlaw, is a two-mana mono-red rare commander. Whenever a dwarf we control becomes tapped, we create a treasure token. We can also sacrifice five treasures to search our deck for an artifact or dragon card and put it directly onto the battlefield. Magda is a fast combo commander. Her goal is to gather 10 treasures.
- With Clock of Omens, an artifact dwarf and Magda on the battlefield, we tap the artifact dwarf and the clock of omens to untap our artifact dwarf. Because the dwarf is tapped during this process, Magda creates a new untapped treasure, entering into a loop that lets us create an infinite number of tapped treasures.
- Due to the budget restriction, fast mana is effectively banned from the format and only a tiny number of free spells are available. Sol Ring is just actually banned because I didn't want tournaments to be decided entirely by who gets to start with that nonsense in their opening hand. Fast mana and free spells are a core component of cEDH, and the lack of them creates a fundamentally different environment with less explosive turns.
- Rule of law is a two-mana white enchantment that simply says each player can't cast more than one spell each turn. This is one of the strongest effects available. Competitive decks are often built with lots of cheap and efficient spells, but that efficiency becomes useless when you can only play one spell each turn.
- Kutzil prevents our opponents from casting spells on our turn, which means we never have to worry about getting locked out by our own rule of law. We get four spells per turn rotation, but our opponents only get three. He also draws up to three cards each turn as long as we hit each opponent once.
- Mid-range combo decks follow a well-tuned value plan, usually centered around a commander that can fight well. They accumulate mana and card advantage with a variety of synergy pieces. Eventually they pivot into a combo that can require as few as two cards or as many as four or five. The trick is to structure the deck not around a single combo line, but rather many interconnected ones.
- Ketis is a two-mana red elemental lizard that makes it so whenever a commander we control deals combat damage to an opponent, it also deals that much damage to each other opponent. Malcolm creates a treasure token whenever one or more pirates we control deal damage to an opponent. We hit somebody with Malcolm while Ketis is out, which makes Malcolm deal damage to the other two opponents, generating a total of three treasures.
