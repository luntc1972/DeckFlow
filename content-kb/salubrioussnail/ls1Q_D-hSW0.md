## Summary

This video argues most decks don't need extra hand size and that running cards to avoid discarding to hand size often makes your mana base worse for no benefit. Discarding to hand size is only a real concern when three conditions hold: the deck runs heavy card draw (hand size naturally shrinks over time as you make land drops and cast spells, so you need draw to counteract it); the cards can't be deployed fast enough, either because they're high mana cost or reactive (a deck of one-to-three-drop permanents empties the hand before it clogs); and the cards are individually valuable enough to want to keep all of them (you may want every counterspell or tech piece, but not four lands and five generic six-drops). Even when all three are true, you may still be fine, because recursion, flashback, discard synergies, and graveyard-count effects exist, and you choose what to discard. The core takeaway: card selection beats card quantity by a wide margin unless an opponent has a Maro-style payoff. Don't fear drawing cards even if it forces a discard.
