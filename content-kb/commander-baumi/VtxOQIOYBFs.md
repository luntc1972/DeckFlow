## Summary

This video covers five Commander (EDH) commanders the creator considers stronger than they appear. Sergeant John Benton, a free-mana Selesnya 2/4 with trample and haste, draws cards equal to combat damage dealt; paired with green ramp and cheap pump spells like Giant Growth, he snowballs card advantage and lands while keeping nothing above four mana — kill him on sight, don't take the first hit. Patron of the Orochi, an eight-mana mono-green creature with snake offering (flash), taps to untap all forests and green creatures every turn, enabling repeated instant-speed effects; it benefits the mono-green pilot more than opponents despite untapping their permanents. Wort the Raid Mother grants conspire to red/green instants and sorceries via goblin tokens, doubling big ramp spells (Traverse the Outlands, Boundless Realms) — a kill-on-sight snowball. Alania the Virgin Storm copies the first instant, sorcery, and otter spell each turn (three copies), enabling otter synergies and extra-turn loops. Gev, Scaled Scourge, a cheap two-mana commander, was played in a budget cEDH list as an aggressive persist-combo value pile, looping persist creatures via plus-one counters to win — the creator's perception gap was widest here.

## Key Clips

- Sergeant John Benton is a free mana Selesnya 2/4 with trample and haste. Whenever he deals combat damage to a player, both us and that player draw that many cards. He plays much closer to a green-white spellslinger deck than traditional voltron, best paired with green ramp, giant grove, and combat tricks.
- John Benton game plan: get him out turn two (mulligan for mana dorks), swing for two with haste before anyone has blockers to draw two cards, then attack again with a cheap pump spell like Giant Growth. Haste plus inbuilt trample lets him grab momentum, while explorers, summer blooms, and lands-into-play ramp keep mana open for protection so he sticks around.
- If you ever face John Benton, kill him the moment he comes down. Don't get greedy, don't take the first hit to draw cards — that's how he builds momentum. If he's interrupted early he falls far behind, but if not the game spirals out of control rapidly.
- Patron of the Orochi is an eight-mana monogreen commander with snake offering — cast at instant speed by sacrificing a snake, reducing its cost by the offering's mana cost, so it can come down as early as turn four or five. Once per turn it taps itself to untap all forests and green creatures, including itself, so the ability is usable every turn. Playable snakes include Lotus Cobra and Ramunap Excavator.
- Wort the Raidmother is a six-mana Gruul goblin shaman that makes two 1/1 goblin tokens on entry and grants conspire to each red or green instant or sorcery — tap two creatures sharing color as you cast to duplicate the spell. Doubling ramp spells like Traverse the Outlands, Harvest Season, or two Boundless Realms goes insane. She's a kill-on-sight commander since untapping with her even once snowballs games.
- Alania the Vengeful Storm is a five-mana Izzet otter that copies the first instant, sorcery, or otter spell cast each turn — up to three spells per turn — so long as you gift an opponent a card. Support otters include Storm-Kiln Artist, Coruscation Mage with Offspring, and Valley Floodcaller, which gives non-creature spells flash so you can cast sorceries on opponents' turns and even duplicate extra turns.
- Gev, Scaled Scorch is a two-mana frog with ward-pay-life that gives creatures a +1/+1 counter for each opponent that lost life this turn, and deals one damage when you cast a lizard spell. The tournament list skipped lizards for an aggressive value pile with persist-creature combos: when a persist creature dies while Gev is out and damage was dealt, Gev overrides its -1/-1 with +1/+1 counters, creating an infinite sacrifice loop.
- These decks are built under a budget format — CEDH tournaments where decks must cost less than €50 in Moxfield updated to cheapest. The John Benton deck in particular is famous for being so cheap that €10 lists exist, but they see no play because they stomp through the casual community.
