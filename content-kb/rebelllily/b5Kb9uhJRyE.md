## Summary

A Commander Format Panel member and former cEDH competitor (known for "window theory") addresses community panic over potential bans. Thassa's Oracle won't be banned for cEDH reasons: he argues good decks (blue farm, Ragavan/Thrasios soups) win on the stack battle and resource accumulation, not on how the Oracle resolves, and it actually helps weaker low-color blue decks compete; only ~1 in 10 games is decided by an early jam. Any ban would be social/casual perception, but the bracket/game-changer system already locks it out of casual play, so he doubts it happens. Rhystic Study is the bigger problem: it's a time sink that drags out organized play, causes draws, and gives blue the best card-draw engine atop the best stack interaction. He personally would ban it, but ~50% of players love it and it was recently "dangled" via products (foils, Jumpstart), so banning after people paid requires shifting public perception first. On unbans, he favors Jeweled Lotus, opposes Dockside permanently, and finds Mana Crypt's turn-one high-rolls unfun. He stresses panel transparency and conservative, face-up decisions, argues against splitting cEDH off, suggests tournament-specific custom ban lists, and wants Hullbreacher unbanned.

## Key Clips

- As as like I personally do not believe that Thassa's Oracle will be banned for cEDH reasons. My understanding is that Thassa's Oracle doesn't super matter — yes it's a very visible wincon and endgame, but usually the good decks (blue farm, Ragthras, TnT, Nyla) win on how you get there: the stack battle, how you play your Rhystic Study or Esper Sentinel to build resources and draw interaction. The wincon is meaningless — even if Thassa doesn't exist, blue farm could just brain freeze everyone out.
- Even if we ban Thassa's Oracle, the pill to swallow is that deck's probably not good enough — banning it will not open up more space. The advantage of just jamming and playing proactively and winning versus playing reactively and trying to counter your opponents is just not competitive. Thassa's Oracle won't be banned for cEDH reasons; if it ever is, it's for casual/social reasons because it's too infamous and has too big a target on its back.
- The game changer system is working — Thassa's Oracle being put into the game changer system locks it out of the early casual parts of the bracket and restricts it to bracket three (three game changers) and four (unlimited). So the reason I don't think it'll get banned: first, not a problem, super clear; second, the bracket system locks it out of situations where it is problematic.
- Rhystic Study is a huge time sink. At a competitive level you can't handwave the triggers — multiple studies, multiple sentinels, the stack gets very complex, opponents react, counterplay, dealmake. It adds so much time that it is ruining the organized play environment. Games already slowed down with no mana crypt, no dockside, no jeweled lotus, and Rhystic Study is even better because games are getting longer. No one's finishing on time, a lot more draws.
- I am 100% personally banning this card. Rhystic Study wasn't designed for four-player, it was designed for two-player, and just gives the owner too much resources to win the game with afterwards. If I was arguing for deck diversity, I would insta-ban Rhystic Study for cEDH because blue not only has the best stack interaction but now they have the best card draw engine. Thassa's Oracle locks itself out; everyone wants to play Rhystic Study if they're in blue.
- Why we might not want to ban Rhystic Study: it's been dangled to players by recent products — confetti foil versions, jump start sets — they know it's a chase card. You can't dangle the carrot and then take it after people work for it. Even if 50% of the community says it's got to go, there's another strong 50% that loves it. The panel only takes action when there's very clear consensus, because banning cards takes money out of people's wallets.
- On dockside, jeweled lotus, and mana crypt: I'm very in favor of unbanning jeweled lotus. I don't think dockside should ever be unbanned — that card's a mistake. Mana crypt I think is a problem too; you high-roll into mana crypt turn one and you are just so far ahead. The big issue with how those bans happened was process — people are reasonably upset you took two-three grand out of their pocket without letting them know. The format panel wants to make decisions super face-up and transparent so bans aren't surprising.
- Breaking cEDH off as a separate format is a non-starter because it doesn't solve the problems and fractures the community. But we need to improve the tournament play experience. If there's a major circuit with a custom ban list — no partners, no Rhystic, no Rag rack, sizable prize pool — people will play it no matter what. It's technically still commander, a custom ban list on top, like Garry's mod. That's what the community actually needs to just try.
