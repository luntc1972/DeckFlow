## Summary

The video argues that mismatched power levels are usually not the real problem at an EDH table; the real culprits are too little interaction and too much variance. Decks should run enough removal and counterspells to trade one-for-one with whoever gets ahead, because otherwise even a small value advantage compounds unchecked. High variance comes from stuffing a deck with payoff cards and too few building blocks. The host splits payoffs into two types: amplification cards (that multiply tokens, counters, damage, or draw) and conversion cards (that turn one resource into another). Both are fine in moderation but ruinous in excess. Example: a Zaxara player who ran many doublers plus a single premium hydra, so one removal spell with no countermagic backup neutralized the whole board. As positive foils, the host cites two tuned $30 decks: a Dimir rogues list running six counterspells, six removal spells, two modal counter/removal cards, three stax cards, and 25 evasive creatures with self-sufficient payoffs like Bident of Thassa; and a Rakdos discard deck running ten removal cards plus the commander and 25 discard effects. Prioritizing consistency, resiliency, and interaction makes decks fit varied tables and reduces feel-bad games.
