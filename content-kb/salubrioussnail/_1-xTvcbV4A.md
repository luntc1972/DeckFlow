## Key Clips

- The boros deck is an artifact based deck that stays alive through Aristocrat style mid-range in the early and mid game and eventually launches into an egg style storm turn to close out the game. The most common way it won was by playing a lot of artifacts with a reckless fire Weaver in play in order to burn out its opponents.
- An egg is a low-cost artifact that you can crack for a card, often played alongside egg variants that give a card on entry or that can be sacrificed to give some other benefit. An eggs deck is generally one that looks to win in a single explosive turn looping a bunch of these eggs to generate cards, mana and other resources, done through cards like second Sunrise which can recur all of the artifacts a player sacrificed earlier in a turn.
- One card above all others bolsters the deck: Krark-Clan Ironworks. It's an artifact sack outlet which offers easy sacrificing of artifacts before a second sunrise, can mitigate the danger of certain types of removal by sending cards to the graveyard, and is an incredible mana engine that single-handedly guarantees the deck will have all the colorless mana it could ever need on its big turn. However, the card is out of the price range for a $25 deck.
- The commander is Gerrard, Weatherlight Hero, a niche commander from 2019 that triggers a second Sunrise effect when he dies. This is nice for adapting eggs to commander as there aren't that many second-Sunrise type cards. However the boros color identity poses problems: a lack of black and blue mostly excludes the deck from having access to tutors for easier access to critical cards.
- The deck ended up running three artifact sacrifice outlets plus creature sack options. Hans opted to supplement these with eight board wipes that could destroy both artifacts and creatures. Board wipes function as a single-use sack outlet for all nonland permanents, allowing a full refresh, and help address the lack of access to Krark-Clan Ironworks by letting the deck develop more permanents that generate mana before its loops can become mana positive.
- As the pool got more aggressive the deck struggled to keep its life total from getting chipped down before it could storm off. The solution: add additional creatures like Pilgrim's Eye and Wall of Omens as blockers, plus cards like Reveillark, Vesperlark and Sun Titan for additional blockers and a more robust grind factor, giving the deck a non-storm game plan powerful enough to compete and win on its own.
- As the resident combo player and the person who built the deck, Hans had a 28% win rate with it over the course of all the games, while all other players had a collective 19% win rate. Even once I knew the deck well enough to win with it I still slow played like crazy because the task of finding the way to a win is so inherently packed with difficulty even for an experienced pilot.
- A deck's complexity relates to two factors: how difficult it is for the deck to complete various tasks and how hard a deck needs to try to navigate around its opponents. $25 mid-range eggs is a far more layered, complex and challenging deck specifically because it lacks cards that effortlessly solve the problems between it and victory, forcing it to engage with what the rest of the table is doing and tackle problems in a more creative way.
