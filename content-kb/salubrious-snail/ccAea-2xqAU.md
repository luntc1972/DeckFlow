## Summary

The video argues that in EDH, card variation and power-level variance are two separate deckbuilding knobs, and defending one doesn't defend the other. It offers a four-stage "pipeline" model for classifying proactive power cards by how imminently they threaten opponents: early boosts like Sol Ring (only provides mana, low cost that falls off, easy to play around), mid-game card-advantage engines like The One Ring, late-game mana engines like Bolas's Citadel or Arcane Bombardment, and near-instant wins like Craterhoof Behemoth, Mizzix Mastery, or Rite of Replication. Variance feels sharper the further down the pipeline a card sits; removal mitigates it. High power-level variance wrecks threat assessment and makes fair matchups hard ("feast or famine" decks). It notes humans underestimate the odds of enablers and payoffs lining up, producing swingy decks. On tutors: in a deck with a few power cards, tutors reduce power-level variance but cut play variation. Examples: Krark's Thumb in Yusri (91% of decks), a creator cutting tutors from Vela the Night Clad after repeatedly grabbing Rite of Replication, and the host's Sharuum Slide deck tutoring Sol Ring, Fluctuator, Portal to Phyrexia, and Angel of the Ruins. Tutors also reveal which cards you gravitate toward, flagging power-level outliers.
