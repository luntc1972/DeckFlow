## Summary

This video argues Rhystic Study should be banned in EDH for two combined reasons: high power level and destructive social dynamics. The creator's ban framework requires both a frustrating play pattern and enough power to see wide play (citing Yuriko, Hullbreacher, Leovold, Primeval Titan, fast-mana bans). Rhystic Study qualifies on power: it's a top cEDH draw engine because opponents correctly choose not to pay the 1 when casting interaction, making pivotal plays, or lacking spare mana. It scales down imperfectly but still punishes weaker decks, higher mana-value engines, removal (which gets Rhystic-taxed), and players who stumble on land drops. The 'just pay the 1' defense is dismissed as a moral argument blaming less-skilled, unluckier players rather than a balance argument, compared to conservative status-quo economics. Socially, the card turns the basic act of casting a spell into a maze of pressure, breeds resentment, forges in-groups/out-groups, and can hand games to the Rhystic player—without the intentional design of true political cards (likened to Tergrid). Smothering Tithe is flagged as a similar candidate. Popularity and iconic-ness are rejected as ban criteria; what matters is a card's real impact on actual EDH games.

## Key Clips

- In an October 2025 announcement, Wizards of the Coast floated the idea of a change to the way hybrid mana is treated in the deckbuilding rules of EDH. However, that October 2025 announcement also featured some other things, one of which was proposing the banning of Rhystic Study, the powerful blue draw engine.
- That particular card has been in a perpetual state of low-level controversy for some years now, being both the third most widely played blue card but also the second most hated blue card according to salt polls conducted by EDHREC.
- So, these are typically the two things required to make a card truly problematic—a high power level, and a play pattern that is frustrating. For something to be worth banning in EDH, it needs to be frustrating in a way that creates bad experiences for people, and it also needs to be powerful enough to see wide play and make that bad experience commonplace.
- Rhystic Study is quite powerful even if all of your opponents are being tactical about paying the 1. If somebody wants to cast a piece of interaction that spends all their mana, they're not going to be paying the 1. There are all sorts of cases in which it is absolutely the correct choice not to pay the 1, and that makes Rhystic Study a pretty great card draw engine on base.
- Secondly, any removal spells cast on Rhystic Study are themselves Rhystic-taxed. Best case scenario it costs an opponent a mana, but if the removal isn't super mana-cheap there's a good chance the Rhystic Studying player will get a card as well, and that's all assuming that the blue player isn't prepared to play counterspells in defense of their copy of the most powerful draw engine in the format.
- The players most harmed by needing to pay an extra 1 for their spells will be, on average, the less experienced players piloting the less powerful decks and getting less lucky. 'Rhystic Study is fine, people just need to pay the 1' is not an argument about game balance, it is a moral argument about the people currently not paying the 1.
- So, this is my ultimate issue with the card. It applies to the most basic of game actions—casting a spell—and turns the game into a maze of social pressure. Instead of encouraging players to collaborate and make deals, it divides opponents and gets them to foster resentments amongst each other.
- All this to say, the popularity or iconicness of a card should not be our primary concerns when it comes to the EDH banlist. EDH games are the EDH format, and we should be concerned with how a card impacts real EDH games that millions of people play.
