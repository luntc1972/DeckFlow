## Summary

Core heuristic: decide what card types you need, by which turn, then run enough copies to reliably draw one by then—accounting for card draw. Worked example: to likely have one single-target removal spell by turn eight (assuming ~3 extra cards drawn), run six copies; to have two by then, run 11. Running fewer is fine, but make it a deliberate choice rather than accidentally landing on, say, four and being disappointed you never draw them. Apply the same math to any card category. Examples from his decks: the Sauron deck wins by flinging an orc army or attacking with evasion, so he runs five win conditions plus spell doublers as support, justified by a heavy card-draw game plan. His John Benton deck wants early protection—connecting once draws into more—so it runs 13 cheap protection spells to be almost certain to have one by turn three. Principle: know your game plan and its target turn, then size each card category so you aren't left short.
