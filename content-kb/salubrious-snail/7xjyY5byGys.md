## Summary

Using Sol Ring as anchor, the video argues cheap, powerful staples warp how players build decks. Because sites like EDHRec/Scryfall rank cards by popularity, players see top options (Counterspell for ~$1, Swords to Plowshares and Path to Exile under $2, Abrade/Vandalblast for artifacts, Mystic Remora/Mulldrifter/Treasure Cruise/Archmage Emeritus for draw) then a steep drop-off in power or budget, so they run only the 3-4 strongest of an effect where the deck actually wants 8-10. This under-does generalist ramp/draw/removal and over-does synergy pieces, creating a bimodal deck that either explodes or falls apart when a key piece is removed. Counterspell shouldn't be a power baseline; blue decks benefit from running weaker 3-mana counters. Recommendation: decide how many of each effect (ramp, draw, removal, counters) the deck needs before browsing cards. Also add cards based on the games you want, even free ones: he cut Sword of the Animist and Sword of Forge and Frontier from a Meria equipment deck, warns cheap infinite combos (Niv-Mizzet + Curiosity) change table dynamics, and notes graveyard decks must win fast or accept lower power against hate. Sometimes the strongest card (Cultivate, Torment of Hailfire) isn't the best fit.
