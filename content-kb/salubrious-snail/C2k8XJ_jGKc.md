## Summary

This video reframes EDH interaction away from a card-type checklist (creature/artifact/enchantment removal) toward a network of overlapping solutions to specific gamestates. A single threat like a reanimated Archon can be answered by creature removal, enchantment removal, a counterspell, or instant-speed graveyard hate, so cards like graveyard exile, stax, bounce, and defensive permanents (Ghostly Prison, propaganda) all count as removal depending on the deck. There's no fixed correct interaction count: counts range from the teens to 30+, because forms of removal overlap and vary in how much they hinder opponents. Higher life-total pressure (aggro, Voltron) permits fewer removal pieces, since threats matter less if opponents can be focused down. Deck archetype dictates focus: aggro clears blockers, combo buys time against imminent threats, control attacks resource engines and prefers multi-target efficiency. On staples: flexible spells like Beast Within and Chaos Warp shine in colors lacking hard removal; cheap 0–2 mana removal (Swords) wins on tempo but costs cards. Counterspell cost should match whether you have other uses for held-up mana—draw-go and treasure decks can run expensive modal counters. Ultimately, how you categorize cards should serve understanding your own deck and its tasks.

## Key Clips

- The best way to think about interaction is as a network of overlapping solutions to a number of specific gamestates. In EDH problems are often more complicated than one single problem permanent that needs to be removed.
- If an opponent uses Animate Dead to return an Archon of Cruelty, you can destroy the Archon with creature removal, destroy the Animate Dead with enchantment removal, counter the Animate Dead, or use instant-speed graveyard hate to exile the Archon in response. For our purposes, Abrade is not a removal spell, and Cremate is.
- Graveyard exiling is a form of removal, bounce spells are a form of removal, and defensive cards like Propaganda are a form of removal. Sources of graveyard exile, stax cards, and defensive permanents are generally extremely cheap per mana and stick around to hinder multiple opposing plays.
- Having a higher degree of life total pressure can often allow for a bit smaller of a removal count. This life total rule is most pertinent in aggro decks, and especially in Voltron decks, which look to knock players down one at a time with commander damage. Careful threat assessment and a few swings with a big commander can be worth countless pieces of removal.
- Beast Within and Chaos Warp are exceptional because they are hard removal in colors that don't have much hard removal for creatures. Generous Gift is functionally identical to Beast Within unless your opponent is playing an Elephant deck. In a green-black deck Beast Within won't feel as essential since you'll have other reliable ways to remove creatures.
- If your interaction suite is narrow, around 8-12 cards, there's a lot of utility in having each card be as flexible as possible. With 9 pieces of interaction, after your opening hand plus seven draws you've on average seen about one and a quarter; with 14 pieces around 2; with 18 pieces more than 2 and a half.
- Any time a deck holds up mana for instant-speed interaction, there's effectively a mana cost, since if the interaction isn't needed the mana goes unspent. 3, 4, and 5 mana counterspells aren't inherently bad — what matters is whether you have other things to do with that mana, like a draw-go control deck casting plotted spells or rolling over treasures between turns.
- Counterspells consistently embody multiple roles — they're a form of removal since they can nail pivotal spells, and useful for protection since they can counter opposing interaction. How you categorize your cards matters for understanding your own deck and making sure you can accomplish the different tasks you want to accomplish.
