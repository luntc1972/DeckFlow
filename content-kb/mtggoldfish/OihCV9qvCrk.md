## Summary

This video showcases a $10, four-rare Standard deck built around 28 six-mana creatures with cycling, all enabled by the single rare "Fix What's Broken." The strategy: cycle the janky six-drops (Canyon Crawler, Maarorrow, Soaring Sandwing, Sundering Brood Megan, Chitting Graves Stalker, Migrating Ketrad) into the graveyard on early turns, then cast Fix What's Broken (a four-mana sorcery costing six life) to reanimate every artifact/creature of that mana value at once. Cycling twice by turn four lets you reanimate two creatures on turn four, or four if you wait until turn five. The reanimated creatures gain life and make food, usually offsetting the six life paid. The deck runs only 20 lands because so many cards have land cycling. Support cards include Bitter Triumph and Reckquitting Hex for removal, Strategic Betrayal for graveyard hate, and Conduit Pylons (the lone nonbasic) to surveil and cast off-color spells. The deck's main weakness is graveyard hate like Rest in Peace, plus counterspells and sweepers; it's a glass cannon. It went 6-6 across 12 games, which the player considers a strong, possibly hot, result for such a cheap, repetitive budget brew.

## Key Clips

- Today we're playing a $10 four rare deck with 28 six drops in standard. The 28 six-mana creatures are super janky cards from recent limited sets that all have cycling — Canyon Crawler (a six-mana 6/6 deathtouch that makes food), Marorrow, soaring Sandwing, Shepherding Spirits, plus actual payoffs like Migrating Ketrad, Sundering Brood Megan, and Chitin Graves Stalker.
- The one rare in the deck is Fix What's Broken: a four-mana sorcery where you pay X life and reanimate each artifact or creature with mana value X from your graveyard. The plan is to cycle and swamp-cycle and plane-cycle turns two through five to dump six-drops into the graveyard, then cast Fix What's Broken to reanimate them all — making food, gaining life, getting flyers, and blowing up your stuff.
- The math: cycle once on turn two and again on turn three means casting Fix What's Broken on turn four reanimates two six-drops. Wait until turn five and double-cycle on turn four, and you reanimate four six-drops. Later games or if the opponent mills you, you reanimate even more.
- The mana base is as cheap and janky as possible — the only non-basic is Conduit Pylons because it surveils, letting you put something big into the graveyard. Because there are so many land cyclers, the deck runs just 20 lands even with 28 six-drops, and it still works fine. You do need two lands to start though, since every land cycler costs two mana, so you can't keep a one-lander.
- The deck's biggest weakness is graveyard hate. A Rest in Peace shuts the whole plan down and you can't kill it, leaving only the backup plan of hard-casting six-drops once you hit six mana. It's a glass-cannon deck — counters on Fix What's Broken, sweepers, and boards that let you reanimate then wrath you back are all problems. Fix What's Broken also costs six life, so against aggro you can get too low to ever cast it.
- Many of the random six-drops gain life or make food when they enter, so most of the time Fix What's Broken pays back all the life you spent, sometimes more. Even getting just two, three, or four janky common six-drops back is enough to win most games. Soaring Sandwing repeatedly became the hero of games just by existing as a 3/5 flyer left behind to rebuild around a wrath.
- Final record was 6-and-6 across 12 games — about a 50% win rate, which is better than expected and probably running hot. Would I recommend it? Sort of: it's a fun, repetitive, ultra-budget second or third deck at roughly $10-12 in paper and only four rares on Arena. It picks up wins but gets wrecked by graveyard hate, so not a deck to grind all day.
