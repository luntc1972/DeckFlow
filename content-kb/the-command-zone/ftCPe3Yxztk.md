## Summary

Command Zone's Rachel Weeks and Jimmy Wong explain Commander fundamentals with deckbuilding-relevant rules and heuristics. Core rules: 100-card singleton (one commander + 99, no duplicates except basics and cards like Dragon's Approach/Shadowborn Apostle that permit any number); commander sits in the command zone, recastable with +2 commander tax each time. Color identity is a deckbuilding restriction set by all mana pips in a card's cost AND rules text (hybrid mana counts as both colors; color words like "blue" or created-token colors don't count; fetch lands like Flooded Strand have no pips so are technically legal but do nothing). Front/back and partner cards combine both sides' pips. Gameplay: 40 life, free mulligan, and 21 combat commander damage from a single physical commander card (not stacked across sources/copies). Multiplayer deckbuilding heuristics: cards saying "each opponent"/"each upkeep" scale (Braids, Arisen Nightmare; Tender Shoot Dryad); higher mana-value spells and seven-drops are castable because games run long; ramp and card draw matter more; instants beat sorceries for flexibility; board wipes and multi-for-one removal are strong while one-for-one removal is saved for personal threats; pure aggro and mill are weaker. Commander Brackets (1–5) and the ~60-card game-changers list frame power level.

## Key Clips

- **[53:22]** Braids, Arisen Nightmare — in 1v1 it can only draw you one card, but in multiplayer you could draw up to three cards off a Braids trigger because it hits each opponent.
- **[54:24]** Higher mana-value cards are more likely to get cast in Commander because games run longer and attention is split; you can play seven-drops in your deck and reasonably cast them.
- **[54:56]** Mana ramp and card draw are a lot more important in Commander than in 1v1 because the early game is all about setup — three opponents at 40 life means 120 damage to get through.
- **[55:38]** Instants become a lot better with three opponents: keeping mana open, staying flexible, and reacting as the game develops matters much more than a sorcery.
- **[56:52]** Multiple-for-one removal like board wipes is really good in multiplayer, while one-for-one removal is still needed but should be saved to disrupt things affecting you personally or threatening to win.
- **[57:08]** Pure aggro decks aren't as good in multiplayer — 20 life is very different from 120 — and mill gets harder because decks are bigger and players team up on you.
- **[68:04]** Game-changers is a list of roughly 60 very powerful, unbanned cards; at bracket three you're limited to three of them, higher brackets any number, below that none — a soft restricted list.
