## Summary

A discussion of tribal deckbuilding in EDH/Commander. Tribal decks are praised as approachable onboarding tools for new players because synergies are visible (e.g. checking if a card is a Merfolk for Hakbal of the Surging Soul) and gameplans are simple. But the video argues they encourage lazy deckbuilding. Two claims: not all tribal cards are good in every tribal deck (Kargan Dragonrider in dragons), and cards good on paper aren't good in every deck. Using Miirym's EDHRec page: Crucible of Fire (22%) is an awkward anthem because dragons aren't go-wide; Acolyte of Bahamut (34%) is a slow ramp piece that isn't mana-positive until you untap twice with the 6-mana commander—a win-more card. The fix: build an identity and gameplan beyond "play [creature type]," starting from your core tribal cards and asking what they offer. Examples: a high-power pirates deck built around low-cost pirates and Malcolm's treasures; an Arahbo cats deck built around a single large cat (eminence), running lower creature count with equipment and power-checking draw spells, dipping into aggro/voltron; a rogue deck built from evasive creatures, curiosity effects, and interaction. The same applies to mechanic-based decks (ETB, cast-from-exile).
