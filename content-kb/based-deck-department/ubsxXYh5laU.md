## Summary

Argues for a dedicated aggro/Voltron/tempo/combo deck in rotation. Build with extreme focus: skip midrange norms — run little or no ramp, card draw, or removal (glass cannon). Core deckbuilding method: start with a desired end-game state, then sum total mana available across the game versus mana values of cards needed to win, and ensure they match. Bracket-3 example Vincent, the Vengeful Eternal: no ramp/removal/draw, wins ~turn 7 needing under 28 mana via multiplicative effects — Vincent damages all opponents, so pump, power-doubling, and double strike scale to every player. Liesa Voltron: 15 protection sources plus 43 card-draw sources so a single threat survives ganging-up; multiplicative via cost reduction, prowess, double strike using Cartouche of Knowledge, Rebellious Strike, Shalai Voice of Plenty, Shapers' Sanctuary. Bracket-2 Golos combo: assemble 8 artifacts (mana rocks, draw, token maker), Apocalypse Demon mill for 40+, ~30-35 mana. Key principles: prioritize 'explosiveness' (multiplicative not additive effects) and 'damage uniformity' (hit all players at once) — not every commander qualifies. Load protection to make threats connect. Cited aggro commanders: Krenko, Light Paws, Ur-Dragon, Adam Paqual, Kadira, Kaalia. Expect ~25% win rate; state your speed pre-game.

## Key Clips

- **[05:45]** If you're going to build a good aggro deck, your game plan is all about speed. The deck will require extreme focus and will not adhere to online templates because the deck will quite likely skip the commander norms that are built around the mid-range archetype. Depending on the deck and commander that you build, you'll likely run very little ramp, card draw, or removal, and perhaps none of the above.
- **[07:27]** Most of your deck will therefore be threats with some protection to make sure that those threats connect. This protection of your game plan is thus going to be critically important in any aggro deck because people can, should, and will try to stop you.
- **[07:27]** In a deck like my budget bracket three Leash Hex Voltron deck, I run 15 sources of protection because Leash is my only threat. The fact that I have 15 sources of protection means that I'm almost certain to have one in my opening hand, and the deck has 43 sources of card draw, so drawing into more is also incredibly likely.
- **[08:11]** When you start a deck build or review, you should start with a desired end game state in mind. Then take the entire mana pool you're going to have access to across that game, and then add up all the mana values of the cards that you likely need to win the game, and ensure that those match up.
- **[09:56]** Explosiveness, for lack of a better word, is when effects added together create a multiplicative effect rather than simply an additive one. With Vincent, he does damage to every other player when he does damage to one. Thus, a pump spell on Vincent isn't simply three extra damage to one player, it's three damage to every player for nine damage.
- **[10:42]** Let's say I play a Cartouche of Knowledge on her. She's now a 4/4 flying for one mana. Add Shalai, Voice of Plenty and Shapers' Sanctuary and she's an 11/7 and draws us another card. Because of her double strike, if I attack one of my opponents, she'll one-shot them all for three mana.
- **[12:47]** It's important to remember that just because we're playing an aggro deck, it doesn't change the fact that we should really only be winning about 25% of the time. There is a trap here that one can undersell the threat you pose to the table. Stand in the middle of the ring and point to the floor before the start of the game so everyone knows what you're about.
- **[14:51]** An aggro deck designed to win by turn nine will lose to a mid-range or control deck designed to win by turn nine almost every single time. For aggro decks specifically, being forced to win the game on the same timeline as a mid-range or control deck is inherently unfair and puts an advantage in the hands of those players whose decks are designed to excel in the late game.
