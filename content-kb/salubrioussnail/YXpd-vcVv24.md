## Summary

The creator shares thoughts on EDH/Commander deckbuilding, focusing on how a deck should be oriented relative to its commander. The conventional approach picks a cool commander, adds synergy cards, then fills gaps. A stronger option makes the commander do something tangential to the deck. The creator's favorite is having the commander do the exact opposite of the deck's plan: build a midrange deck with a deliberate weakness that the commander solves. Example one is Glissa, Sunslayer—a cheap, efficient blocker and draw engine—used not for counter-removal synergy (as EDHRec suggests) but to anchor a battlecruiser deck loaded with ramp, removal, and big cards, since Glissa covers early defense and card draw. Example two is Liesa, built not as a lifegain deck but as a hatebears deck: stuff the deck with disruptive small creatures to grind the board to a stalemate, then cast Liesa to break through. The formula: a commander that reliably covers one needed element lets you leave that element on the backburner and devote deck slots to what the deck does best, solving a known weakness in advance. The creator argues midrange decks, often dismissed as boring, can be fun built this way.
