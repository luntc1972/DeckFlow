## Summary

This video covers 17 ways to build incremental land ramp in mono-red Commander, an approach the creator prefers over mana rocks despite being slower (one land roughly every other turn). Core enablers are creatures and spells that fetch or place lands: Solemn Simulacrum, Scampering Surveyor, Skarrg/ Skar tiller, walking Atlas, and fetchlands like Myriad Landscape. Red's strength is duplication—cloning ETB creatures with Jaxis or Delina, and copying triggered abilities (return the favor, abstruse archaic) to double fetch-land searches or death triggers like Smaug's treasures. Indestructible lands (Darksteel Citadel, Cascading Cataracts), fetched via Expedition Map or Urza's Cave, combo with cleansing Wildfire, Geomancer's Gambit, and Sundering Eruption to ramp while drawing cards. Transform artifacts (Dowsing Device, Pendant of Prosperity, Dowsing Dagger, Explorer's Scope) and lands (terrain generator, Horizon of Progress) fit cheaply. Red's impulse-draw fills the hand with extra lands, making walking Atlas and play-from-hand effects unusually strong. Spell-copy effects (reverberate, flare of duplication) let red steal opponents' ramp like Cultivate. Other picks: brass's tunnel grinder, geological appraiser, formatic compass, there and Back Again.

## Key Clips

- To land ramp in red we want to play Solemn Simulacrum and Scampering Surveyor for obvious reasons — they are as straightforward as it gets, simply getting a land into play. Then we can double up on them using cards like Jaxis the Troublemaker or Delina, Wild Mage to clone them repeatedly, triggering their ETBs again.
- Another interesting use case for doubling triggered abilities is to double fetch lands. Cards like Maestros Theater or Evolving Wilds search lands out of our deck, and by copying those effects we can ramp. This is especially powerful on a trigger that searches multiple lands at once, like Myriad Landscape.
- Skarrg-style 'Scute' tiller is a creature that, when it becomes tapped, lets us either reanimate a land or put a land into play from our hand. It works particularly well in red because red cloning usually gives haste, letting us immediately attack to trigger its ability.
- Be honest about the level of ramp: we're not going to have huge bursts of mana like green or white. Instead we work incrementally, getting an additional land into play maybe every other turn, maybe every one and a half turns. It's slower and requires a larger package than just playing mana rocks.
- Darksteel Citadel and Cascading Cataracts are indestructible, which lets us use them with Cleansing Wildfire, Geomancer's Gambit, and Sundering Eruption. All three destroy a target land and let its controller search a land to replace it — cast on our own indestructible lands, this becomes ramp without the downside, and Cleansing Wildfire and Geomancer's Gambit also draw a card, effectively a better Cultivate.
- Brass's Tunnel-Grinder is a 2-mana artifact that lets us discard any number of cards then draw that many plus one. While on the board, if we descended that turn we put a counter on it, and at three counters it flips into Tecutlan the Searing Rift, a land that taps for red and discovers X when you cast a permanent spell. Fetchlands give an easy way to descend.
- Walking Atlas is a 2-mana creature that taps to put a land into play from your hand. Red's impulse-draw effects make you accumulate a handful of lands, so unlike other colors where these effects demand more lands than you have, in red you often have more lands than you can play — giving Walking Atlas real synergy. It's surprisingly better in mono-red than any other color.
- Red has the best access to spell-copy effects like Flare of Duplication, Reverberate, and Return the Favor. They aren't ramp by themselves, but they let us copy an opponent's ramp — if they play Cultivate, we Reverberate it. This only matters if there's green at the table, and if there isn't, ramping quickly matters less, so it's a fine trade-off.
