## Summary

This video announces winners of a deck-building challenge where builders chose among five underused commanders, prioritizing creativity, cohesion, and dynamic gameplay. For Senator Peacock, the winner is a modular Beatdown deck: modular artifact creatures want to die (drawing cards) and be unblockable, using both halves of the commander; it runs nine sub-four-mana modular creatures, nine proliferate cards, counter payoffs, restrained infect, plus balanced interaction, draw, and ramp. Shizuko's winner is a spell-land deck looping Talon Gates of Bandara to keep Shizuko phased out on opponents' untap steps, denying their mana while using a toolbox of utility lands and heavy recursion, winning via Dark Depths/Thespian's Stage, Field of the Dead, or Helix Pinnacle. Aomr's winner is a mono-red reanimator deck built around God-Pharaoh's Gift, exploiting extra combats for extra zombies, with Blade of Selves enabling a combo win. Egon's winner is a compact Voltron deck that integrates self-mill efficiently to feed Egon's exile cost while pumping his power. Yomiji's winner is a high-power combo deck looping legendary permanents through sack outlets and payoffs like grinding station and blind obedience, with redundant combos, ramp, tutors, and protection.

## Key Clips

- Choose a commander from among Aragorn Marshall of Rohan, Yomiji Who Bars the Way, Egon God of Death, Shizuko Caller of Autumn, and Senator Peacock and build a deck with it prioritizing creativity, cohesion, and likelihood to produce dynamic gameplay.
- Senator Peacock is a five-band commander who makes all your artifacts Clues — artifacts which can be sacrificed to draw a card — and who makes a creature unblockable whenever you sacrifice a clue. The winning deck was a modular Beatdown deck: artifact creatures that transfer their +1/+1 counters to other creatures when they die have both a clear reason to want to hit the yard and a clear reason to want to be unblockable, using both halves of the card.
- Shizuko Caller of Autumn is a three-mana 2/3 that produces three green mana for each player on their untap step. The winning deck loops Talon Gates of Madara to keep Shizuko phased out on opposing untap steps, preventing them from gaining her mana — a one-sided three-mana boost on a three-mana commander, plus it strengthens the deck's boardwipe resistance.
- Aragorn Marshall of Rohan is a four-mana 4/4 haste creature that grants an extra combat once per turn when another attacking legendary creature dies. The winning deck was a mono-red reanimator deck built around God Pharaoh's Gift; extra combats from Aragorn mean extra zombies, and Blade of Selves goes infinite with Aragorn by creating copies that die to the legend rule, giving additional combats until there are fewer than four players.
- Egon God of Death is dual-sided: the front is a three-mana 6/6 deathtouch sacrificed on upkeep unless its controller exiles two cards from their graveyard; the back is a one-mana artifact that mills a card each turn. The winning Voltron deck stood out for compactness — integrating the essential mill task (around 15-20 sources needed) into draw engines, removal, and a pile of fetch lands rather than dedicating space to pure graveyard fodder.
- Yomiji Who Bars the Way is a seven-mana 4/4 that returns any other legendary permanent put into a graveyard to its owner's hand. The winning high-power combo deck loops a legendary permanent with a sacrifice outlet and a payoff — cast Mox Amber, tap for mana, sacrifice it to give Thermal Navigator flying, Yomiji sends it back, repeat. Hope of Ghirapur, Mox Opal, and Mox Amber generate infinite mana with the deck's four artifact sack outlets.
- The Yomiji deck's win conditions: looping a creature like Hope of Ghirapur goes infinite with Glaring Fleshraker or Ornithopter Monument for infinite damage or infinite tokens; five draw cards can dump infinite mana to find a win; and blind obedience drains opponents. The structure makes it resilient — no singular piece is load-bearing and most combo pieces serve other purposes.
- The Yomiji deck features 23 ramp cards, 10 tutors, and 8 draw spells — good numbers given a seven-mana commander and a multi-piece combo — plus seven pieces of removal and 10 pieces of protection. It has plenty of interaction points but is resilient to setbacks, firmly pursuing its combo with the tutors and protection needed to consistently execute.
