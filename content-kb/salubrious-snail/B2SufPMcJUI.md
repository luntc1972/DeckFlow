## Summary

This is a conceptual video arguing for open pregame communication in casual EDH, not a deckbuilding tutorial, but it states several disclosure heuristics useful for deckbuilding. Core principle: give a 10-second pitch of what your deck does. Disclose two things—your strongest wincon (so opponents can decide whether to play against it and how to play around it) and your core gameplan; you need not reveal side synergies buried in the 99. Combo/one-trick decks (e.g., a Fiend Hunter ETB loop, or casting Rograkh repeatedly then Jeska-ing opponents) especially should disclose, ideally with timing ("combo win around turn 7"). The host cites his own decks as models: his Francisco/Malcolm deck he describes as fast with frequent turn-5 wins that usually wins via Time Sieve; his Sharuum deck he describes as a control deck built around Astral Slide with flickering permanents central. He notes one-trick/cascade-into-one-threat decks are often more fun to build than to play. Heuristic: if a synopsis of your deck ruins it, the deck wasn't interesting. Other cards referenced in passing: Ashnod's Altar (Teysa), Blood Artist, Second Harvest, Captivating Crew, Humility, Karn's Bastion.

## Key Clips

- **[12:32]** One-trick combo decks—like a deck built to cascade into a particular threat every game—end up more fun to build than to play; abruptly ending the game with a handful of cards isn't as fun in practice as it is to theorize.
- **[14:34]** If you're playing a combo deck, make it clear to opponents. Something as simple as 'I'm playing a deck with a combo win that frequently happens around turn 7' gives opponents the tools to decide if they want to be in that game.
- **[15:04]** With my most powerful deck, my Francisco/Malcolm deck, I'll tell opponents the deck is fast with frequent turn 5 wins and that it usually wins with Time Sieve—two facts that let them know what to watch out for.
- **[15:23]** With my Sharuum deck I tell opponents it's a control deck built around Astral Slide. That leaves dozens of interactions unmentioned but conveys the two key facts: it gains leverage the longer the game goes, and flickering permanents is central to the gameplan.
- **[17:10]** Flowchart for disclosure: if the part you're thinking about is a powerful wincon, disclose it; if it's your core gameplan in a way that helps opponents' decisions, disclose it; a cool side synergy deep in your 99 you don't need to reveal.
- **[21:54]** An alternative to keeping stax or combos out of a game is disclosing them up front, giving players time to prepare rather than getting blindsided later. Opponents should know your strongest wincon so they can decide how to play against you.
- **[20:04]** When I play a very impactful card I emphasize it, saying something like 'I attempt to cast Captivating Crew,' respectfully giving opponents who know my gameplan the chance to respond.
