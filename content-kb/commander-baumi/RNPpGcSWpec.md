## Summary

The creator argues against playing generic tutors (Vampiric Tutor, Demonic Tutor) in Commander, not for power reasons but because they homogenize gameplayΓÇöyou always fetch and play your single best card, making every game feel the same and defeating the variety the 100-card Singleton format is built to provide. Tutors also produce unsatisfying wins where opponents never get to play. The creator favors decks whose commanders aren't essential, enabling varied games. Instead of tutors, prioritize card draw: every card should replace itself to some degree. ## Key Clips

- I don't think you should be playing tutors in commander... I'm talking about stuff like Vampiric Tutor, generic tutors that can get a broad selection of cards. It's not because I think they are too strong, it's more that I think you're making your decks less fun by playing them.
- The cool thing about Commander is that we have 100 card decks with a Singleton rule. This means we are forced into variety. Every time you play your deck you have a different experience, so your decks feel fun and distinct. Tutors go against this: you can find the best card in your deck, but then you're always playing the best card, and it always plays out the same way.
- You don't want tutors, you want card draw. Against new players I notice they just run out of cards in hand. The trick is every card in your deck should draw cards to some extent.
- The trick isn't to just have big engines like Archmage Emeritus or Beast Whisperer, because those get destroyed. You want smaller pieces that casually replace themselves ΓÇö cards like Spirited Companion, Wall of Omens, Wall of Blossoms. You play them and draw a card, so you build incidental card advantage and survive a board wipe.
- Wall of Blossoms and Wall of Omens have multiple purposes. As a Defender, opponents won't attack into it, saving you life, and it replaces itself by drawing a card. I recently played Bane of Progress and people just died because they had no mana rocks left and not enough cards in hand to rebuild.
- If you're building a sacrifice deck, the best sacrifice target is a creature with a death trigger ΓÇö something that wants to die. World Shaper never dies in combat because opponents won't let it, so you sacrifice it yourself to a Viscera Seer: you draw and bring back lands. Play ETBs and death triggers, play Solemn Simulacrum, then sacrifice them once you've used their effect.
- Reassembling Skeleton is a trap. Sure you bring it back for two mana to sacrifice, but are your sac cards even good if you pay two extra mana? Village Rites is good as a one-mana draw two, but bringing in a skeleton first makes it three mana. You can get Sign in Blood for two ΓÇö it invites the idea of efficiency while being incredibly inefficient.
- The core takeaway: you don't need tutors, you want card draw. And stop sacrificing reassembling skeletons.
