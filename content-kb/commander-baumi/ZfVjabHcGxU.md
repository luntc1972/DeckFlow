## Summary

This video ranks the strongest precon (preconstructed deck) commanders, scoped to competitive cEDH viability. It first defines what counts as a precon commander: face commanders and alt/old commanders as tagged on Scryfall, allowing all partner pairings that share a precon, but excluding cards like Korvold and refusing Timna/Crown pairings since their colors never matched a single precon. Candidates discussed include Bloodpod's Tymna the Weaver + Thrasta/Tana pairing, Kraum/Tymna, Inalla (wizard combo, fragile, low tournament success), The Reveler/Reyhan-style untap engines, and notably Derevi Empyrial Tactician—an untap value engine that bypasses the command zone, making it nearly unkillable and combo-relevant. Newer threats: Stella Lee Wild Card (2024), a free spell-copy commander with one-card infinite combos at instant speed; Marneus Calgar (Warhammer 40k precon), an Esper token value engine and infinite-mana outlet; Dihada Binder of Wills (graveyard/Underworld Breach enabler with Doomsday potential); and Tayam Luminous Enigma (counters/reanimation grind deck). Judging by popularity, tournament performance, and card age, the host ranks third Tayam, second Stella Lee, and first Marneus Calgar as the strongest precon commander, while joking that Bloodpod fans may disagree—though results don't support it.

## Key Clips

- Any card that is tagged on Scryfall as face commander or alt commander is fair game. The decision is to stick to face commanders and alt commanders as defined by Scryfall, even if that means excluding Korvold.
- Commander 2016 introduced the partner mechanic and partner commanders like Tymna and Kraum. For a card to be an alternative commander of a precon, it needs to match the colors of that precon. Tymna and Kraum aren't in the same precon, so the executive decision is that they can't compete together.
- Tymna the Weaver and Tana the Bloodsower (the cEDH deck Bloodpod) are from the same precon. Bloodpod is a stax deck that uses Tymna as a card-advantage engine and Tana for colors. It was never top tier, mostly because it lacks blue, but it is a real cEDH list.
- Inalla, Archmage Ritualist leads an intricate combo deck that wins by casting Spellseeker and memorizing a 15-step flowchart. Her eminence ability copies a wizard for one mana whenever one enters. The deck is fragile, so it hasn't had the most tournament success.
- The Reaper King-style commander, the Ur-Dragon's Roon... actually 'the Rebbec' Imperial Tactician untaps a permanent whenever it enters or a creature deals combat damage. Untapping lands like Gaea's Cradle plus a four-mana command-zone-to-battlefield ability makes it essentially unkillable, and it's also a combo piece — absurdly broken.
- Stella Lee, Wild Card was the face commander of the Quick Draw deck from Outlaws of Thunder Junction. After casting three spells in a turn she taps to copy an instant or sorcery. With any spell that untaps her, she copies infinitely; cards like Ceta of the Wisps draw a card each cycle. The combo works at instant speed and can be restarted on the stack.
- Marneus Calgar, from the Warhammer 40k Forces of the Imperium precon, is a five-mana Esper commander that draws a card whenever one or more tokens enter, and for six mana makes two tokens. He's an infinite mana outlet in good colors and a value engine for token decks that excels in extended games.
- Final ranking: third place Tayam, Luminous Enigma (most popular, respectable results); second place Stella Lee, Wild Card (less popular but much newer); first place Marneus Calgar (most successful with the most high placements while still very popular and relatively recent).
