## Summary

Argues Commander decks should be built to a math-backed template around a specific game plan, so you draw lands, ramp, removal, or win conditions exactly when needed. Land math: ~42-43 lands guarantees 3 in opener and 5 mana by turn five with no ramp; author personally runs ~38 and calls 32 indefensible. Ramp: to reliably have a mana rock/dork by turn two you need ~11 copies; four ramp sources is effectively zero (need ~25 cards drawn), so run enough to be reliable or none. General copy-count guidelines to guarantee one by a given turn: 11 by turn two, 9 by turn five, 6 by turn eight (assuming some card draw); double the copies for two. Apply the same counts to removal, card advantage, and win conditions. Deck examples: Sauron wants heavy ramp + 5 win conditions; John Bunyan aggro wants 1-mana ramp and 13 cheap protection spells for turn-three consistency; Skullbriar/low-curve decks run near-zero ramp. Favor cards that do double duty (ramp/removal stapled to creatures, graveyard-fill as card advantage) and recursion synergy to hit high counts. Cut pet cards and alt win-cons that dilute the plan. cEDH templates differ. Trim a land per 2-3 ramp/draw sources; run the numbers via
