## Summary

A member of the Commander Format Panel reacts to a ban-list update, framing every ban as effectively taking $60 from players' collections, so changes demand strong justification and trust between the panel and players. The unbans of Bio Rhythm and Loot Tree, plus prior unbans like Coalition Victory, World Fire, and Sway the Stars, are low-impact and actually strengthen the list by clarifying what a ban-worthy card looks like. Rhystic Study stays legal despite real problems (eating clock time in cEDH, giving blue the strongest draw engine, indirect kingmaking in casual play) because it is iconic, expensive, promoted by Wizards as a chase rare, and softbanned by the bracket system. ## Key Clips

- The newly freed cards from this update are Bio Rhythm and Loot Tree. It's part of a wave of sensical unbans like Coalition Victory, World Fire, and Sway the Stars. Unbanning these cards makes the ban list stronger because the list becomes more and more clear what a ban-worthy card looks like.
- Rhystic Study is incredibly problematic in cEDH, especially in competitive events where all the triggers eat up a lot of time, and it creates stalemates that incentivize draws by exhausting a match clock. It gives blue the strongest draw engine alongside Mystic Remora, an incredible advantage.
- Rhystic Study shouldn't be banned because it's an expensive card Wizards has promoted as a chase rare specifically for this format. Banning it would have the same effect as banning Mana Crypt, Jeweled Lotus, or Dockside Extortionist. It's iconic, can be low impact in casual play, and it's expensive.
- Thassa's Oracle is the signpost broken wincon, difficult to answer outside a hard counterspell or a specific silver bullet like a Torpor Orb. But the bracket system fixes most of the problem with Thassa's Oracle in game experiences it doesn't belong in. There's not a strong enough consensus from the cEDH community that it's actually harmful to the meta.
- Hot take: I want to unban Griselbrand. He draws an incredible amount of cards in exchange for life, exacerbated by the higher life total in the format. The play patterns are very similar to Necropotence, but it's so iconic as a reanimator target it would be nice to bridge players from modern, legacy, or cube to higher bracket Commander.
- I don't want to unban Iona. It's not interesting to cheat in a threat that stops all casting of a mono-color deck when mono-color decks already have so much disadvantage. Iona specifically is a feel-bad that locks one person out, and monocolor decks would have to play colorless interaction to remove a rare Iona.
- Prime Time would instantly be a green staple the way Rhystic Study is a blue staple. Ramping by two on ETB and attacks would quickly put one player ahead. The biggest point against it: once unbanned, it's very difficult to reban, and rebanning damages integrity and trust from players in the format.
- Bans can't just be about power or loud discourse ΓÇö they have to be about trust. When we ban a card we reach into thousands of collections and take something players invested in. The strongest ban list is the one that bans the least. The bracket system does a lot of work soft-banning cards, giving powerful cards an appropriate space without impacting casual play.
