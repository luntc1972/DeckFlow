## Summary

Satya, Ehrcraft Genius is a four-mana 5/5 with Menace and Haste that, on attack, creates a tapped attacking token copy of another nontoken creature, grants two energy, and lets you pay the copy's mana cost at end step to keep it. The creator builds Satya for a 50-budget tournament, ignoring most energy cards and exploiting the free clone for ETB, combat-damage, and death triggers plus board pressure. Key creatures: Guide of Souls (turn-one energy/counter engine), Shoreline Looter and Loran's Escape for card flow, Esix-style evasion, Aesi alternatives, Copper Code Vanguard (human buff plus stacking Ward toward hexproof), and ETB pieces like Reflector Mage, Skyclave Apparition, and White Plume Adventurer that needn't stick. Higher drops include Bastion Protector, Crimson Caravan card draw, Solemn Simulacrum, and Eomer for extra combats. Win conditions revolve around extra combats and infinite loops via Lightning Runner, Aggravated Assault-style effects, and Breath of Fury. In a CEDH MTGO pod against a 'Six'/Najeela deck, Euro, and Grand Arbiter, repeated Reflector Mage bounces controlled the board until Lightning Runner copies via Satya delivered a near-infinite combat kill. The creator reports roughly seven wins in eight-to-nine test games.

## Key Clips

- Satya Ephlux Genius is a four-mana 5/5 with Menace and Haste. Whenever he attacks, he creates a tapped and attacking token copy of another non-token creature we control. He also gives us two energy, and at the beginning of our end step, we can pay as much energy as the token's mana cost to keep it around. Otherwise, we have to sacrifice it.
- I believe that Satya is so powerful that we can completely ignore the vast majority of energy cards and he's still worth playing. Creating an unrestricted clone every combat for free is just that powerful. We can utilize the creatures' ETBs, combat damage, and death triggers all while applying noteworthy onboard pressure.
- By far, our strongest turn one play is Guide of Souls. It's a one-mana 1/2 that gives us an energy and a life whenever another creature enters the battlefield under our control. It also lets us spend three energy whenever we attack to put two +1/+1 counters and a flying counter on an attacking creature. It gets especially nasty once we start duplicating it.
- My favorite is Copper Code Vanguard, a 2/2 Human Soldier that gives all of our other humans +1/+0 and Ward one. Things get silly once we start copying it. That damage bonus quickly adds up, and while ward one is annoying and ward two is difficult to get through, ward three might as well be hexproof. Since Satya is a human, Copper Code Vanguard also buffs him and opens the door to commander damage knockouts.
- Eomer, Marshal of Rohan, has a fun interaction with Satya. This is a four-mana 4/4 with haste that gives us an extra combat once per turn if another attacking legendary creature we control died. We can use Eomer for one extra combat per turn by duplicating him and then legend ruling away the copy. Since the copy enters tapped and attacking, Eomer will trigger and give us an extra combat step.
- Extra combats are very powerful with Satya, which is why our biggest finishers and infinite combos revolve around them. Lightning Runner, Port Razer, and Breath of Fury each let us take infinite combat steps and close out the game on the spot.
- With Satya, I would like to make a copy of the Lightning Runner. Then I would like to pay eight energy. My biggest worry now is that I might actually have dealt too much damage, because I need two more combats before my Lightning Runners are self-sufficient. It's not the cleanest infinite, but we don't need to go infinite. We just need to kill him.
- Out of the eight or nine test games I've played him in so far, Satya has won like seven. His performance has been through the roof. And not only that, I also feel like the deck I've built is nowhere near the optimal list. He's a broken magic card, obviously way out of line in power level, but because he originates from a very mechanically specific precon, many players just never gave him a second thought.
