## Summary

A deckbuilding-philosophy video on how to actually win EDH games. The host argues "wincon" is a near-useless category since anything can win; the better question is which cards *don't* win, and whether every card contributes to a cohesive plan. Don't just cram splashy bombs (Craterhoof Behemoth, Torment of Hailfire, Purphoros, Sanguine Bond)—too much splash starves you of building blocks. Decks are inputs→outputs systems; focus fails as unfocused inputs (dead draws, e.g. mixed artifact + facedown synergies) or unfocused outputs (value-tribal like Gavi, Nest Warden generating diverse but toothless payoffs). Midrange runs on "create parity" (lands, ramp, draw, protection, creatures) then "break parity" via overrun effects (Craterhoof, Finale of Devastation, Moonshaker Cavalry), goad, evasion (host calls Mass Calcify best), or removing blockers. Distinguish parity-breakers from win-more cards (Epic Struggle, Crucible of Fire): if a card is dead two-thirds of the time, the other third must be great. Aggro avoids parity; control creates it with wipes/stax then breaks it. Removal buys time at a cost—faster decks need less. Host runs 8+ counterspells in blue, more if the win plan is fragile. Combos should emerge from optimizing synergy, not be slammed in randomly.

## Key Clips

- **[02:26]** Don't ask which cards win the game—anything can win. Ask which cards in your deck are the ones that don't win. But this doesn't mean cram your deck with splashy bombs; too high a density of splash leaves too little room for the building blocks that actually lead to victory.
- **[03:05]** Most decks are a system of inputs and outputs. His Sharuum deck's inputs are Astral Slide and cycling cards, outputs are flickers that recur large artifacts. A deck can stumble with unfocused inputs (dead draws, no cohesive board) or unfocused outputs (value-tribal like Gavi that does a bit of everything but nothing in particular).
- **[05:46]** Ask what each card in your deck does, how often it will fail to perform its intended function, and whether it contributes toward a cohesive plan.
- **[08:20]** Lands, ramp, draw, protection, and a varied mixture of creatures are how you create parity. Breaking parity means letting creatures hit for a lot—overrun effects are most popular, but also targeted buffs, goad, extra combat, mass evasion, or disabling/removing blockers. He's on record that Mass Calcify is the best evasion spell.
- **[09:35]** Distinguish a card that helps you win from a win-more card. Epic Struggle wins if you have 20+ creatures, but tons of cards win off a board that massive. Crucible of Fire is an anthem for a creature type that's already big—most dragon decks are better served by another useful dragon. If a card is dead two-thirds of the time, that other third had better be really good.
- **[13:00]** Think of removal spells as something that buys you time, at a cost—each one subtracts from slots for your own gameplan. Faster decks won't need as many removal spells as slower decks, because opponents won't have time to fully develop threats before you're threatening lethal.
- **[15:05]** Counterspells can remove obstacles to your win plan, not just stop opponents—counter a board wipe or removal aimed at your key creature. He runs at least 8 counterspells in blue decks, and more if the win plan is fragile, such as slow board-based plans or a combo without backup.
- **[18:09]** Combo is synergy taken to the extreme, so combos should ideally emerge from optimizing your synergies—not from throwing Sanguine Bond and Exquisite Blood into a random black deck. Below high power, the best combo requires setup that fits your deck's plan and whose pieces have alternative uses.
