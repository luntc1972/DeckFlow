## Summary

This video reviews the four Magic: The Gathering Final Fantasy Commander precons and argues most are poorly built. Science and Spellcraft (Esper, Yishtolah) fails because it has 22 creatures, only 11 instants, and almost no way to trigger the commander's draw on opponents' turns—it can't support her instant-speed, high-mana-value noncreature spell plan and needs a ground-up rework. Revival Trance (Mardu, Terra, Herald of Hope) is a reanimator deck lacking reanimation targets: of 35 creatures, 13 have over three power (unreanimatable by Terra) and many others aren't worth bringing back, plus it skimps on card draw and interaction. Limit Break (Naya, Cloud) is fine but unfocused—any creatures plus any equipment work, though some picks like Skull Clamp don't fit big-creature themes; it's low on draw and ramp, making it Cloud-dependent. Counter Blitz (Bant, Tidus) is incredibly well-built and powerful—a +1/+1 counter and proliferate deck packed with synergy that would crush the others ~75% of the time, even running Bane of Progress, which wrecks the artifact-heavy rivals. The power imbalance undermines precons as beginner access points and bracket foundations.

## Key Clips

- The science and spellcraft deck is a white blue black list helmed by Yishtolah, Knights Blessed. She is a four-mana 2/4 with vigilance, and at the beginning of each end step, if a player lost four or more life this turn, we draw a card. She also drains each opponent for two life when we cast a non-creature spell with mana value three or greater.
- A deck built around Yishtolah wants to put emphasis on playing relatively high mana value non-creature spells as often as possible, as her life drain represents a necessary defensive line and can also enable us to force the card draws when our opponents aren't playing along. The colors she is in work great for this, as Esper excels at interaction and turning card draw into dangerous plays, particularly at instant speed.
- The bigger problem is that there are only 11 instants in the deck, with Sublime Epiphany and Mesmeric Glare as its sole counter spells. This deck doesn't have a good way to trigger Yishtolah on our opponent's turn. Even the new instant printed for this deck, Transpose, has rebound, which lets us recast it on our own upkeep, meaning we're not doing it on anyone else's turn.
- Terra, Herald of Hope is a four-mana 4/4. At the beginning of combat on our turn, we mill two cards and she gains flying until end of turn. Then whenever Terra deals combat damage to a player, we may pay two mana to reanimate a creature with power three or less. Her first ability enables her second by filling the graveyard, and her second ability lets her mana cheat by paying two mana for creatures that should cost much more.
- Out of the revival deck's 35 creatures, 13 just straight up have more than three power and hence can't be reanimated by Terra. Then there are 11 we can technically reanimate but that really suck to reanimate. Based on my assessment, this deck has around 11 creatures worth bringing back from the grave, and I'm being really generous there.
- Cloud, Ex-Soldier is a 4/4 that costs five mana and has haste. Whenever he enters, we can attach an equipment to him, and when he attacks, we draw a card for each equipped attacking creature we control. Then if Cloud has seven or more power, we create two treasure tokens. He prefers going wide, looking to attach equipments to multiple creatures instead of stacking them all on a single target.
- Counter Blitz is a green, white, and blue deck with Titus, Yuna's Guardian as its commander. At the beginning of our combat, he lets us move a counter from a creature we control to another. Then whenever a creature we control with a counter on it deals combat damage to a player, we draw a card and proliferate, but only once each turn. The deck is built to enable this, stacked with powerful counter-granting creatures and spells.
- Counter Blitz has Bane of Progress in the list. There's a card in here that will instantly win the game against the cloud deck. Yishtolah probably just dies as well since she plays a lot of enchantments on top of the artifacts. It's a disproportionately powerful card that already wins a ton of commander games by itself, but is specifically good against two of the three other decks. If we ran 100 pods of these four decks, Counter Blitz should win like 75 of them or even more.
