## Summary

This is a ranked, comedy-driven tier list of the ten spirit dragon commanders, judged on deckbuilding interest rather than raw power. Ranked 10th, Shiko and Narsh Unified rewards casting a second targeted spell each turn (copy it or draw), but Shiko adds only flying and vigilance. 9th, Teval the Balance Scale, is a Sultai value engine milling and spawning zombies—strong but boring 'good stuff.' 8th, Beza-style Vihaan toughness-matters drains life but grinds games out. 7th, Ureni the Song Unending, is a huge ETB ramp payoff. 6th, Vihaan Ancestors' Voice, balances life gain and life loss for counters and reanimation. 5th, Narsh Crackling Vanguard, cares about differently-named tokens and exiling cards to cast after attacking with any commander. 4th, Ureni of the Unwritten, cheats dragons into play on ETB/attack. 3rd, Teval Arbiter of Virtue, punishes you by losing life equal to spell mana value—high skill. 2nd, Narsh Heart of the Storm, doubles new-creature damage; weak but a glass-cannon kill engine. 1st, Shiko Paragon of the Way, exiles and copies a cheap spell on ETB—an open-ended flicker toolbox enabling proactive play.

## Key Clips

- At the bottom of our list are Shiko and Narset Unified, a Jeskai 4/4 with flying and vigilance. Whenever we cast our second spell each turn, we copy that spell if it targets a permanent or a player; if we don't copy a spell this way, we draw a card. It's ranked 10th because it's really just a Narset card — Shiko only contributes flying and vigilance.
- Number nine is Teval, the Balanced Scale, a Sultai four-mana 4/4 with flying. Whenever Teval attacks we mill three cards and may return a land from our graveyard to the battlefield tapped, and whenever cards leave our graveyard we make a 2/2 black Zombie Druid token. It's a strong, self-funding value commander, but it's the most definitive good-stuff commander — no deck-building challenge.
- At number eight, Beza-style Vaultborn — Bedevil... 'Bettor, kin to all,' an Abzan five-mana 5/7 with flying. At the beginning of our end step, if our creatures' total toughness is at least 10 we draw a card, at least 20 they untap, and at least 40 each opponent loses half their life rounded up. It's built around high-toughness creatures and untapping them for value, but it's a grind-the-game-to-a-halt commander.
- Number seven is Ureni, the Song Unending, a Temur eight-mana 10/10 with flying and protection from white and black. Its ETB distributes damage equal to the number of lands we control among opponents' creatures and planeswalkers. Best built as a disgusting ramp deck using cascade and discover to attach ramp to everything, then flicker it to re-trigger the ETB.
- At number six, Bedevil... 'Bettor, Ancestor's Voice,' an Abzan five-mana 3/5 with flying and lifelink. At the beginning of our end step we put +1/+1 counters on another creature equal to life gained this turn, then return a creature card with mana value up to life lost this turn from graveyard. Managing life gain and life loss at once makes a genuinely fascinating deck-building and gameplay experience.
- Number five is Neriv, Crackling Vanguard, a Mardu five-mana 4/4 with flying and deathtouch. It makes two 1/1 Goblin tokens on ETB, and when it attacks we exile cards from the top of our library equal to the number of differently named tokens we control. Caring about the number of different token names is a creative deck-building requirement, and we can play exiled cards on any turn we attacked with a commander — even a stolen one.
- Number three is Teval, Arbiter of Virtue, a Sultai five-mana 6/6 with flying and lifelink that gives our spells stealth. Whenever we cast a spell we lose life equal to its mana value. It punishes us for playing the game at all, demanding careful management of graveyard, life, and mana — powerful but limiting in all the right ways, though it risks an Altar of Dementia / Thassa's Oracle line.
- Our number one is Shiko, Paragon of the Way, a Jeskai five-mana 4/5 with flying and vigilance. On ETB we exile a target nonland card with mana value three or less from our graveyard, copy it, and may cast the copy for free. It turns the graveyard into a one-use toolbox across any card type, building a board and using removal/stax — an open-ended, proactive new way to play flicker.
