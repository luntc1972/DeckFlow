## Summary

This video argues that for casual/social Commander (bracket 2-3), consistency matters more than raw power, and recommends playing 40 lands rather than the commonly suggested 36-37 to avoid missing land drops, which makes ramp pointless and keeps you from participating. Commander's persistent access to your commander already softens flooding. To support a high land count without flooding, the creator advocates building the deck to convert excess lands into action. Key tools: looters like Shoreline Looter (unblockable, loots on combat damage, draws once at seven-plus cards in graveyard) and Malcolm that fix hand quality; colorless looters (Smuggler's Copter, Mask of Memory, The Ring tempts); red rummaging (Demand Answers, Cathartic Pyre) and retrace cards like Embrace the Unknown, Worm Harvest, and Formless Genesis that recast from the graveyard by discarding lands; MDFC spell/land cards; and one-mana cycling lands. Reanimating lands with Sun Titan adds value. Above all, the creator stresses loading the deck with broadly-defined card draw to improve hand qualityΓÇöthe real fix for flooding. The overarching goal is maximizing participation and minimizing low-rolls, not win rate.

## Key Clips

- Your highest priority in deck building for bracket 2, bracket three, social commander should be consistency. And one of the unique features of commander helps us there. We always have our commander, giving us a card to play even if we have too many lands. Drawing too many lands, getting flooded is inherently less of a problem.
- A high land count by itself isn't actually that good. You also need to build your deck in a way to support a high land count. So here are some tricks that allow you to play enough lands without it feeling like a hindrance.
- Shoreline Lutter is a blue two mana one that can't be blocked and lets us loot when it deals combat damage. And if there are seven or more cards in our graveyard, we no longer have to discard and instead simply draw a card. While Shoreline Lutter doesn't properly draw us cards, it fixes our hand. It makes it so that the cards we do have are good. It lets us play more lands because we can get rid of excess.
- It's worth playing cards whose whole purpose is to improve the quality of our hand. Red has rumaging instead. Cards like cool or demand answers, where we first discard and then draw. I've seen demand answers described as a card that doesn't actually do anything because we end up with the same number of cards in hand, but that's dismissing the quality of our cards, which is very important.
- Embrace the Unknown is a red three mana sorcery that exiles the top two cards of our library until the end of our next turn. We may play those cards. It also has retrace, meaning we can cast it out of our graveyard by discarding a land in addition to paying its other costs. If we then end up with too many lands, we can turn them into card draw with Embrace the Unknown. A simple inclusion like this makes flooding basically a non-issue.
- Another great way to ensure consistency are MDFCs, modal dual-faced cards. Specifically, the ones that have a spell on the front side and a land on the back side. But an important note with MDFCs is that you have to be ready and willing to play them as a land. That is their primary purpose. The backup utility of playing the spell is exactly that, a backup.
- I also include the one mana cycling lands in all of my decks. Secluded step, lonely sandbar, barren moor, forgotten cave, and tranquil thicket. That's why we cycle them away most of the time and draw a better card. But if you're not flooding, then a terrible land is still a million times better than missing a land drop. Also, once these are in the graveyard, you can reanimate them with Sun Titan.
- The best way to avoid getting flooded is to just draw so many cards that you're discarding excess lands to hand size. Your deck should be absolutely brimming with card draw. And don't be picky about what counts as card draw. Be broad in your card draw interpretation and then include a ton of it and you will see that your deck just plays smoothly.
