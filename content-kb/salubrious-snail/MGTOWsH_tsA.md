## Summary

Precon review of the "World Shaper" deck from Edge of Eternities, commander Hearthhull, the Worldseed—a 4-mana Jund Spacecraft. Once given 2 power it taps, pays 1, sacrifices a land to draw two cards and gain an extra land play; at 8 charge counters it becomes a 6/7 flyer that drains 2 life per land sacrificed. The reviewer frames the deck as sacrificing and replaying lands for value, with three payoff types: landfall (four cards plus Tireless Tracker), permanent-sacrifice payoffs (nine, including Korvold and Mayhem Devil), and lands-in-graveyard payoffs (four). It runs 42 lands, 10 budget fetches (tapped/Capenna fetches justified because the main land-recursion spells return lands tapped), tango and bicycle duals, and a strong removal suite (Windgrace's Judgement, Pest Infestation, Planetary Annihilation). Criticisms: weak early game and too much land-sacrifice/payoff given the commander already does both. Ten swaps proposed. Cuts: Loamcrafter Faun, Scouring Swarm, World Breaker, Sprouting Goblin, Groundskeeper, Uurg, Mazirek, Centaur Vinecrasher, plus Wastes and Dakmor Salvage. Adds: Spelunking (top priority, land-untapping is powerful), Lotus Cobra, Tireless Provisioner, Valakut Exploration, Deeproot Wayfinder, House Cartographer, Undergrowth Recon, Hugs, Twisted Landscape, Kessig Wolf Run.

## Key Clips

- **[01:42]** The biggest amplifier is things that can utilize lands in our graveyard—if we can get them back into play, the commander represents a fair amount of ramp potential on top of its value. Other amplifiers are landfall effects and payoffs for permanents being sacrificed.
- **[02:11]** The 8+ modality is less appealing to get to early, since it makes the commander more removable without putting on much pressure; it's better as a tool to turn on right before a big land-sacrifice play.
- **[04:15]** I was skeptical of the two tapped fetches and three Capenna fetches, but they make sense because the four most powerful land recursion spells return lands to play tapped, so tapped-entry isn't a downside for these fetches.
- **[09:07]** What I dislike: it lacks early game utility plays, and it has too much land sacrifice and sacrifice payoff, especially considering the commander is both of these things already.
- **[10:42]** For the cuts I'm choosing Mazirek, Kraul Death Priest and Centaur Vinecrasher—the deck is overloaded on higher-cost, trickier payoffs, so this is just rebalancing the ratios.
- **[11:03]** First and by orders of magnitude most importantly, Spelunking. Land untapping effects are disgustingly powerful in this deck, and the other options out there are quite pricey.
- **[11:19]** I cut some land-sacrifice payoffs and replaced with landfall: Lotus Cobra and Tireless Provisioner as mana engines, and Valakut Exploration as a value engine that doubles as a damage wincon, potentially one-shotting opponents alongside the commander.
- **[11:39]** I want higher-utility two-drops: Deeproot Wayfinder filters and ramps a bit early, and House Cartographer keeps a steady flow of lands to hand—the deck has late-game potential but is clunky and hungry for early smoothers.
