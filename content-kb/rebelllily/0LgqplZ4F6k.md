## Summary

The video argues that the most foundational part of building a good Commander deck is having a clear game plan, something most players overlook. Players typically have only a vague idea of what their deck does, then pull cards that fit a theme, synergize, or are generically good, producing a mediocre "7 out of 10" deck. The creator's first question when helping someone improve a deck is always "What do you want the deck to do?"—because card choices are impossible to guide without a defined win condition. Using cEDH as a clear example (Kraum/Tymna winning via Thassa's Oracle through Underworld Breach, Necropotence, etc.), he shows charting multiple pathways to victory as the game plan. He then applies this to a Lathril elves deck: win via Craterhoof, a big Shaman of the Pack drain, or Exsanguinate, working backwards to identify needed cards (token generators, mana dorks like Priest of Titania, reanimation like Hell's Caretaker). Knowing the plan clarifies every card's role, reveals what beats you (board wipes, Cyclonic Rift), and justifies protection like Heroic Intervention. Mapping desired outcomes and steps makes card selection easier and cuts unnecessary "fat" cards.

## Key Clips

- For some reason I never hear about the most foundational part about making a good commander deck: having a game plan. Most players have a vague concept of their game plan and start pulling cards from all the constructs we just talked about — a few that fit the theme, some that synergize, some generically good cards, cards that go with the commander, and a bit of interaction. And voila, another seven out of ten.
- When people ask me how to improve their decks, this is usually the first question I always ask: what do you want the deck to do? Because if we don't know what the deck really wants to do or how it wins, how are we supposed to ever make easier decisions? There's a bajillion cards to pick from — I can't tell you which one to play if you can't tell me what your game plan is.
- I'm going to use cEDH as the example because cEDH should have a clear idea of what their game plans are — after all, they're playing for money. Take Kraum and pair him with Tymna. What is the game plan of this deck? It wins with Thassa's Oracle. If that's the end point, let's draw a line down the middle to represent our pathway to this end state combo.
- How do we get to the game state of Thoracle? Sometimes we just have it and play the cards and win, but more often we play Underworld Breach with Lion's Eye Diamond and Brain Freeze to arrive at this state. We can also play cards like Necropotence — pay 30 life, draw 30 cards, resolve Borne Upon a Wind, and also get to Underworld Breach or Thoracle. We clearly have five pathways to victory, and these clear pathways are our game plan.
- We prioritize Swan Song or Dispel as our counterspell because a lot of our game plan revolves around resolving a single spell that will push us to victory, and winning that stack battle is really important. From here the way we design our deck can be hyperfocused and clear on how cards relate to what we want to do.
- Back to Lathril: I want to make a lot of elves and win with Craterhoof Behemoth. I also want to win with a really big Shaman of the Pack, and for fun let's say I also want to win with a massive Exsanguinate. Three pretty clear end points. Now let's work backwards — how do we arrive here?
- For lots of elves we play token generators and pump effects to make Lathril bigger, and because we want to Exsanguinate opponents, making a lot of mana is really important — which is why we'd play Priest of Titania, Wirewood Channeler, Circle of Dreams Druid. We also want Cryptolith Rite or Enduring Vitality to turn our creatures into mana. To get a Shaman of the Pack win we can loop it into the graveyard and reanimate it with Hell's Caretaker, Malevolent Awakening, or pick it back up with Marwyn.
- Now we're clear on the role of every card we play. We know we want to put down a lot of elves, so we also know that cards that beat us include board wipes — which we can protect against with Heroic Intervention or Rapid Vigor — and Cyclonic Rift, which I might play Sadistic Hypnotist to rip out of the blue player's hand. You don't need to do this exercise every time, but knowing the steps you want to take and the outcomes you want to achieve will help you pick cards and cut a lot of fat that general consensus taught you to play.
