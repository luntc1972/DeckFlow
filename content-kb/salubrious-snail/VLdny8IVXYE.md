## Summary

The creator examines whether Sol Ring should be banned in Commander and concludes it should stay legal. He places powerful cards on a "pipeline toward victory": Sol Ring sits earliest and is ephemeral—amazing on turns 1-2 but falling off by turns 4-5—unlike scalable mana (Smothering Tithe), draw engines (Rhystic Study, The One Ring), or endgame cards (Bolas's Citadel) that win immediately. Because Sol Ring acts early, the format's social tools work against it: opponents get many turns to gang up before a win threatens, so its blowouts (strongest deck draws it, plus other power, plus an opponent's weak start) are the exception, not the norm. Unlike Primeval Titan or Prophet of Kruphix, Sol Ring is humble and task-oriented—it lacks identity or scaling and fades into the background. He frames its value as increasing early-game variance, giving slower decks exciting games. Practically, a ban is a nonstarter: Sol Ring has appeared in every precon since 2011 and over 80% of decks, so banning it would muddy WotC's format messaging and never reach casual players. He distinguishes good practical reasons (precon inclusion) from weak ones (price, iconic-ness), and predicts Rhystic Study may eventually be banned.
