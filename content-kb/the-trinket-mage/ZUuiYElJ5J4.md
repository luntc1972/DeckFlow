## Summary

This Magic Mirror podcast debates the most flexible color in EDH, framed around multicolor decks since most popular commanders (Ur-Dragon, Edgar, Atraxa) are two-plus colors. The hosts argue green is the strongest standalone color, offering land ramp other colors lack and creature-tied removal for artifacts and enchantments, making it a strong primary but a redundant splash. For splash colors, one host favors white for its removal (Generous Gift), protection (Teferi's Protection), silence effects, and stax pieces. Another prefers red for haste (Arena of Glory), filtering, treasures, reach/fling damage, and powerful single-pip cards like Big Score, Deflecting Swat, and Jeska's Will, calling it the best small or tertiary splash despite weak removal. Blue is named the best light three-color splash due to cheap single-pip glue: counterspells and filtering. They note red and treasures favor being primary while splashing other colors, citing a red-heavy cEDH storm deck. Other topics: bracket placement of Overburden and discard, skepticism toward deck-ranking sites like Commander Salt and ChatGPT for deckbuilding (it doesn't read cards, gives mediocre regurgitated advice), shrine decks needing self-imposed restrictions to stay interesting, and favorite mono colors—white, black for aesthetics, and red for being charmingly bad.

## Key Clips

- **[05:44]** The most flexible color in terms of power level is green. It gives you land ramp that the other colors just really don't have access to. And green can always do anything it wants as long as it vaguely involves a creature — enchantment removal, artifact removal, creature removal — just tied to a creature, which is not a huge restriction.
- **[07:38]** For me, I think personally it's white that's the best splash color. Adding a little splash of white to any deck makes it a lot stronger, just because white has such good removal and slow-down tools and protection. Generous Gift is such good removal to add to any deck because it just hits anything, and then you get Teferi's Protection.
- **[08:27]** My favorite second color is probably red because getting access to haste is very good. Red also has a lot of filtering options — looting and other sources that let you see more of your deck — which makes up for a lot of the lack of protection you might have, and you still have your protection equipments if you want.
- **[10:13]** If you're splashing a color, you're not splashing just for removal. You're splashing for things that make your primary game plan a little better — a damage amplifier or a haste enabler on a land. Every other color blows up enchantments fine, so if you pair red with any other color you already have access to blow up enchantments.
- **[17:43]** For a small third-color splash it's probably blue — so many glue cards, stock-ups, Offer You Can't Refuse, Swan Song, cheap counterspells. It's just the glue that binds an otherwise red-white deck together, like Jeskai, without blue being a main part of the deck.
- **[19:42]** Red might be the best small splash color because there are random red cards that are some of the most powerful in the game — Deflecting Swat, Jeska's Will, Underworld Breach. If you're running just those as combo extenders and it's not a main part of your game plan, then maybe red's actually the most powerful tertiary color.
- **[21:54]** Big Score in storm decks is actually broken because you can use the treasures to filter for your other storm spells, especially since most rituals just make red mana. That single red is so nice to splash into multicolor decks compared to double-red Unexpected Windfall.
- **[32:58]** I would run Overburden in bracket three, but rule-zero it — mention it when you're getting into a game. At two mana, the ability to get it down really early and just completely wall so many players makes it a card I'd mention. You can break parity by playing non-creatures, or run it in a token deck since it doesn't affect tokens.
