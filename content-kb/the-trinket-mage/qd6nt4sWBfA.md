## Summary

This video argues that blue is Magic's most hated color, exemplified by the popular "Nope" counterspell shirt the creator dislikes. The core claim: counterspells aren't mechanically far from ordinary removalΓÇöcountering a commander resembles Doom Blading itΓÇöthough counters are more flexible, hit instants/sorceries, and stop ETB/death triggers. Counters have real weaknesses too: useless once a spell resolves, and "oops all counterspells" is weak in Commander because one-for-one interaction demands heavy card advantage to control a four-player table. The creator notes his blue control decks drew fewer complaints than an Athreos "all wraths" deck. To beat counters you must play differently: sandbag instants/sorceries, bait counters out at bad times, run cheaper threats to overcome blue's mana advantage, and use table politics to redirect counters. New players who play "normally" lose, making blue feel unbeatable. Blue's historical dominance (Power Nine, pre-Modern formats) reinforces this. Crucially, counterspells are often the last spell castΓÇöending gamesΓÇöand the ending of an experience dominates memory (per a Veritasium ice-water study), cementing frustration. The takeaway: blue's reputation is overstated; don't box colors or playstyles, since counterspell counts and fun are subjective.

## Key Clips

- A counter spell and other removal spells are in reality very close. If you cast your commander and I counter it or if you cast your commander and I Doom Blade it, those are pretty similar effects. Of course, a counter spell stops enter-the-battlefield effects and dies triggers, and can often hit instants and sorceries, which most removal can't.
- Counter spells have other weaknesses. Mostly that they are useless once a spell has already resolved. In commander specifically, counter spells are kind of mid. Just like any other one-for-one interaction, it requires a lot of card advantage to actually keep it up. This is why 'oops all counter spells' is just not a very powerful strategy in commander.
- I rarely get complaints from players when I play blue control decks because they feel it is beatable. They can always bait out the counter spells and then play their threats afterwards. Compared to when I played Athreos 'oops all wraths' ΓÇö it didn't matter when you cast your threat, it was getting wrathed away, and it got a bigger reaction than any counter-spell deck I've built.
- Most salt-inducing cards or strategies are that way because you can't beat them by playing normally. To beat counter spells compared to other removal, you need to play a bit different. A lot of playing around counter spells is forcing them out at inopportune times, since with spot removal you can often just cast your card with a death trigger and get 50% of the value regardless.
- In commander, using table politics to get your spell not countered is even better than getting it not removed. If you use politics and say someone else has something more threatening in hand, you can potentially dodge the counter spell. But that isn't super intuitive for new players.
- You want cheaper, lower-cost cards to defeat the mana advantage that counter spells provide. But a lot of casual players like their big, dumb, goofy cards, so they end up running their win-cons and value pieces into open mana without thinking, then get hit by a counter spell at the worst time.
- Counter spells are often the last spell cast in a game ΓÇö used to protect a win condition or stop someone from winning. The ending of an experience matters most in memory, so the final thing a player remembers is often a counter spell. That keeps the cycle going: players lose to counters and get frustrated, old players get joy out of them.
- You can play as many or as few counter spells as you want. Blue is often put into a box, but commander isn't just one thing. The adverse reaction to blue over other removal is rather silly.
