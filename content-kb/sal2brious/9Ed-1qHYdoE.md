## Summary

The creator shares unfinished thoughts on the problems with Cascade in EDH, organized around two points. First, Cascade adds variance: a six-mana Cascade card can hit anything from a one- to five-mana card. In their own Rata deck this is enjoyable because the deck is otherwise low-variance (two-mana dork commander, lots of four-mana ramp), but in EDHΓÇöalready high-variance with four players, 100-card singleton decks, and many moving partsΓÇöadded variance from cards like Apex Devastator or Maelstrom Wanderer may swing or fizzle. Second, Cascade discourages situational instant-speed cards (like counterspells) and encourages modal cards, lower alternate-cost cards, or effects stapled to creatures (e.g., Hans's Yidris deck running Spellskite, Unsubstantiate, Simic Charm). This deckbuilding limitation can be fun but may not yield interesting gameplay (cited: Connor's Volo deck), and players may simply run less interaction, pushing solitaire-style snowball decks. Discover partly fixes this by letting you put the card in hand to cast later, easing dead-card and variance issues. The creator was excited for Discover but frustrated Wizards keeps printing Cascade cards (Smoldering Stagecoach, Bloodbraid Challenger, Wild Seer). A 2025 update notes no new Cascade and two new Discover cards since.

## Key Clips

- The first major point is that cascade adds variance. Put simply, if you cast a six-mana cascade card, it can hit a five mana card and it can hit a one mana card. This is not a good or a bad thing necessarily. It's just a fact about the mechanic. It adds variance to the game.
- In the context of EDH as a whole, this is a little bit different because it is adding variance to a high variance format. EDH has four players. It has 100 card singleton decks. Most decks are very complex... And Cascade adding variance to that absolutely will have an impact.
- If you think about something like Apex Devastator or Maelstrom Wanderer, you drop one of these cards, they might win the game on the spot if you hit powerful cards, but they can also hit a ramp spell and a protection spell and then not do a whole lot.
- My second point... is the effect that cascade has on situational cards. Cascade discourages these. It discourages situational cards and it specifically discourages ones whose effect is instant speed and where that instant speed is relevant, such as a counter spell. It likewise encourages modal cards or cards with a lower alternate cost or cards which are cast in advance of their effect happening.
- My friend Hans built a Yidrris deck... It had stuff like spellkite, unsubstantiate, simic charm, malevolent hermit, things where if you hit it off of a cascade, even though the intended effect of the card is to do something at instant speed, it's not going to be dead. Either you'll have some different effect or you can save that protection for later.
- Is this just pushing people to run decks that are less interactive and just kind of do their own solitaire steamroll snowball type of thing? I think those type of decks are generally less interesting. And if Cascade promotes that sort of deck, I don't think that's good.
- I see Discover as being Wizards of the Coast attempt to fix some of the problems with Cascade because it allows you to put the card into hand rather than casting it immediately... it at least lets those cards not be dead if they're super situational. And in addition, I think it also helps out with the variance problem.
- The issue is Wizards of the Coast has kept printing Cascade cards. They printed Smoldering Stage Coach in Outlaws of Thunder Junction. They printed Bloodbraid Challenger in Modern Horizons 3... And they also printed Wild Seere Scouring Ma most recently in Bloomberg Commander. They also printed Monstrous Vortex in Modern Horizons 3, a discover card.
