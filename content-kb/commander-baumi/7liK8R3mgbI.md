## Summary

This video rebuts Snail's "lukewarm defense of Sol Ring," arguing Sol Ring makes Commander worse. The creator stresses Sol Ring is fast mana, not ramp: it immediately repays its cost, goes mana-positive, and acts as a ritual that leaps you ahead each turnΓÇöso it stays strong even late game, unlike a topdecked Rampant Growth. WotC itself admitted it qualifies as fast mana alongside Mana Vault but kept it off the game-changer list for sentimental, tradition-based reasons (one-per-deck since Sheldon Menery). At 84% EDHRec inclusion, it's the most-played card in the format. An early Sol Ring forces a table dilemma (no unified, cheap answer) and can warp games into a 1v3, producing non-games. The creator concedes Snail's best pointΓÇöSol Ring adds variance that can let weaker players shineΓÇöbut argues negative outcomes (widening gaps, sidelined players) outweigh positives, especially for casual bracket-two players who get few games. Removing Sol Ring from precons would be trivial (just swap or mulligan). Sol Ring is a relic of old high-cost decks; modern Commander builds engines and double-spells, making it far stronger now. Core thesis: Sol Ring should be a game-changer and cut from precons because it increases the chance of non-games.

## Key Clips

- Sol Ring is fast mana, not ramp. Unlike ramp, Sol Ring immediately repays its cost and goes mana positive. A Rampant Growth on turn two is just that, whereas a turn two Sol Ring can be followed up by a free mana play. It acts as a ritual, jumping you ahead not only in the moment but also afterwards.
- Wizards' own explanation admitted Sol Ring qualifies as fast mana and would otherwise be on the game-changer list next to cards like Mana Vault, but kept it off for being iconic and traditional, never arguing it is good for the game.
- An early Sol Ring creates a dilemma for the table: there is no unified way to get rid of it, so one player must waste a valuable early-game development turn to remove it and fall behind, while the others develop their board. Often the best plan is to let the Sol Ring player do their thing and try to catch up later.
- A very early Sol Ring turns the game into a one-versus-three (a 'one versus free') and has a much higher chance than any other card to turn the match into a non-game, which is a huge problem for casual players.
- Sol Ring is the single most-played card in Commander ΓÇö 84% inclusion according to EDHRec ΓÇö which undercuts the claim that it slips into the background and doesn't dominate the format.
- Snail's strongest argument is that Sol Ring increases variance, giving less-practiced players a chance to be the big bad and evening out experiences over time. The counter: a non-game doesn't get balanced by a later good match, especially for casual players who only get one or two games a week.
- Sol Ring is a relic from an era of expensive creatures and spells when it was a small head start. Modern Commander, even bracket two and modern precons, builds engines and tries to double and triple spell as soon as possible ΓÇö and being first to double-spell is game-winning, so Sol Ring has become an entirely different beast.
- The author's stance: Sol Ring should undeniably be a game changer and be removed from precons, though he is generally against banning. He will treat it as fast mana and only play it in decks meant for very high power levels, and encourages others to do the same.
