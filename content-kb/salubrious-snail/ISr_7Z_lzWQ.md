## Summary

Alex discusses building EDH/Commander decks that combine multiple subthemes rather than running one linear theme. The key tool is the "fulcrum card": a card that ties two archetypes together, usually a payoff from one theme that rewards another (e.g., Throne of Geth links artifact sacrifice and counters; Displacer Kitten links flicker and spellslinger; Professional Facebreaker, Quicksmith Genius, and Squee bridge red strategies). Evaluate fulcrum cards by their floor (how they perform in a subpar ~25th-percentile game) and their ceiling. Cards with low floors, like Astral Slide without cyclers or Rhet-Tomb Mystic in a deck already 40% cycling creatures, often waste a slot better used elsewhere. Consider deck space and how naturally two themes share DNAΓÇöartifacts plus ETB effects have abundant connectors, while pairings like cast-from-exile plus landfall require more effort. Combining disparate themes is fine if intentional: define the goal first. A competitive bracket-3 build should dial into cohesion with tutors and filtering to reduce variance; a relaxed bracket-2 build embraces mechanical messiness, runs filtering for fun, and can use higher-mana removal with added synergy utility (like Spread the Sickness) that feels at home even if not optimal.

## Key Clips

- A good place to start in expanding your deck's synergy universe is what I'll call fulcrum cards. This is a thing that naturally ties together multiple subthemes, and it typically comes in the form of a payoff from one theme that rewards another. Throne of Geth ties together artifact sacrifice and counters, Displacer Kitten ties together the flicker and spellslinger archetypes, and Strength from the Fallen ties together enchantments and creature-stuffed graveyards.
- An important consideration when thinking about this sort of card is the floor of the effect. Basically what we're wondering is how the card will feel in a subpar situation, where it's not living up to our wildest dreams. Professional Facebreaker will usually manage to generate at least a couple treasures, and Displacer Kitten can at least serve as protection if no juicy ETB creatures are present. For others, the floor might be a lot lower.

Once we do the floor/ceiling calculation it becomes clear that this little bird gets mogged by a Merfolk Looter in this particular deck.
- A major factor in the floor/ceiling discussion is the amount of deck space required to support a particular card. Treasures are just a naturally useful tool for basically all decks. This stands in contrast to something like a Doorkeeper in an Arcades the Strategist deckΓÇöhow do you make target player milling feel useful for a theme that usually wins through combat damage and doesn't traditionally utilize the graveyard very much?
- Another angle for thinking about deck space is to consider how closely interlinked two different themes are. Sometimes there will be a whole bunch of fulcrum cards for a set of two themesΓÇöartifacts and ETB effects: Thought Monitor, Conjurer's Closet, Myr Battlesphere, Mimic Vat. However, many other sets of two themes have dramatically less innate shared DNA, like cast-from-exile and landfall, where you'll have a harder time combining them.
- It's important to figure out the reason for building the deck, because that will affect the decisions you make. If you're trying to build a decent bracket 3 deck with a set of restrictions as a challenge, you'll want to dial in hard on the points of cohesion. Maybe you run partner commanders and add tutors like Enlightened Tutor and Muddle the Mixture so you can get your key card more often.
- Some people worry that tutors will homogenize the play experience of a deck, but this won't necessarily be trueΓÇöcard selection tools like filtering spells and conditional tutors reduce play pattern variance, but if you add them to a deck that would otherwise have extreme levels of play pattern variance due to having a lot of moving parts, you'll likely end up in a place that is still very fresh and dynamic to play with.
- Playing at a lower power table is a great place for a somewhat mechanically disjointed deck because incentives align on what removal you run. Lower power opponents won't require all the best removal, so you can run higher mana options with added utility for your synergy families.
