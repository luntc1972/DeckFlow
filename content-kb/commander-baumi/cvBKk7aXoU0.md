## Summary

This is a Commander deck built around Coalition Victory, an eight-mana sorcery that wins the game if you control a five-color creature plus lands of all five basic types. Jared Carthalion is chosen as commander because he makes a free all-color Kavu token that satisfies the creature condition, draws little removal attention, and as a planeswalker survives most sweepers. The strategy is a controlling shell: ramp heavily, draw cards, wait for opponents to overcommit, then wipe the board and use the rebuild window to assemble and cast Coalition Victory the fair way (no infinite combos). The mana base is intentionally janky but functional—many enters-tapped dual-type lands, fifteen basics (three of each type), and a slow fetch cycle—supported by Dryad of the Ilysian Grove and Leyline of the Guildpact for all-type fixing. Key payoffs and enablers include Bring to Light, Solve the Equation, Glissa, Council of War, Uro, and the Bringer cycle. Multicolor spells make it cost roughly ninety dollars. In the showcased game (deck five, game five), the pilot ramped, managed threats with sweepers, played Jared, then chained Bring to Light into Solve the Equation into Coalition Victory for the win.

## Key Clips

- Coalition Victory is an eight mana sorcery that wins us the game. If we control a creature of all colors and basic land types of all types, this card was banned for the longest time, but with the announcement of brackets, it's finally been released from the list.
- It's also important to keep in mind that coalition victory only checks the board as it resolves. We still need to be able to meet its condition even if our opponents respond to it.
- That then only leaves Jared Calfallion, a five mana planeswalker that can create a free kavu creature token that's all colors. Jared himself doesn't meet the coalition victory condition, but the token he makes does. Even if our opponents try to target down Jared, they'll almost certainly ignore the stinky little token he leaves behind.
- We can focus on a much more controlling shell that's centered around building up our resources while waiting for our opponents to overcommit. Then we hit them with a sweeper to force them into a state of rebuilding. And we use that as an opportunity to go for the win.
- We're not going to try to win through a combo. The easiest way of pulling off any silly win condition is to create infinite mana and infinite card draw, but to me that doesn't really count. In those moments, it's really the combo that wins the game, not coalition victory.
- Since we're going to need lands with all basic types, but didn't want to balloon the budget to a few hundred bucks, we played a whole bunch of enters-tapped dual lands with two basic types — Canyon Slough, Radiant Grove, and Wooded Ridgeline. We also play 15 basics, three of each type, and the slow fetch cycle.
- Dryad of the Ilysian Grove turns all of our lands into lands with each basic land type. And Leyline of the Guildpack does the same while also turning our creatures into all colors, making for a very easy coalition victory setup.
- An absolute standout card here is Bring to Light. Since we can spend upwards of five different colors of mana on bring to light, it lets us search out most of our deck, including sweepers, card draw, interaction, and solve the equation to find coalition victory.
