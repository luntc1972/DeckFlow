## Summary

Proposes a "unified theory" measuring lands, ramp, and draw in one shared unit: the baseline turn (draw 1 card, play 1 land) equals one "time walk" of value. Half a land or half a card = 0.5. So Rampant Growth = 0.5, Skyshroud Claim (two lands) = 1, Divination = 1, Opt = 0.5. Grizzly Bears has no relation. "Effective value" depends on context: ramp is worth most before your operational threshold (your commander/win-con's mana value and the turn you must cast it); draw's value climbs as the game goes long. Ramp and draw are roughly inverse, so you need both. Three failure modes (Liebig's law of the minimum): no lands (immediate, unrecoverable), no ramp (gradual ceiling), no draw (quiet, late). Fix priority: lands, then ramp, then draw. Recommended fixed numbers: at least 38 lands and a combined 24-slot ramp+draw budget, split by commander cost. 4-mana commanders (Teysa, Atraxa): 12/12. Higher-cost (e.g. Rosheen, up to 8): shift to 14 ramp/10 draw. 3-drop: 10/14. 1–2-drop (Yoshimaru): 8 ramp/16 draw. Also names Rhystic Study, Smothering Tithe, Propaganda, Dark Ritual, Talisman, Arcane Signet.

## Key Clips

- **[02:35]** Every turn in Magic the Gathering you draw one card and you get to play one land. That's the clock every player at every table is running on, regardless of deck, format, or number of opponents. That is one unit of value, the baseline.
- **[03:28]** Whenever a card helps us exceed the neutral output of one card and one land per turn, its value becomes additive. Rampant Growth is worth 0.5, Skyshroud Claim (two lands) is worth one whole point, and a Divination is also worth one point.
- **[08:43]** Ramp's effective value is highest before you hit your threshold — the mana value where your game plan becomes operational. After the threshold, the ramp came too late: turn seven Talisman, hot garbage.
- **[10:23]** Card draw runs the opposite direction from ramp. Early game your hand is full and draw is almost wasted, but as the game extends and your hand depletes, every draw spell multiplies your remaining options. Its effective value climbs the longer the game goes.
- **[13:53]** There are three distinct failure modes: no lands, you can't play spells; no ramp, you can't meet the table's pressure; no draw, you run out of steam after setup. This is Liebig's law of the minimum — yield is constrained by whichever resource is most scarce.
- **[17:04]** The priority order of failures to address is lands first, ramp second, draw third — ordered by how fast and how permanently each failure beats you. Fix them in the order you can least afford to ignore.
- **[18:43]** The combined ramp plus draw budget is always 24 slots, and you always have at least 38 lands based on the hot garbage model. What moves is how the 24 ramp-plus-draw budget splits, and your threshold tells you exactly where.
- **[19:19]** With four mana value commanders like Teysa or Atraxa, run 12 ramp and 12 draw. Higher-cost commanders shift toward 14 ramp and 10 draw; low mana value commanders like Yoshimaru shift to 8 ramp and 16 draw because their mana demands are already met.
