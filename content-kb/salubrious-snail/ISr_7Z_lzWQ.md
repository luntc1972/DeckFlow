## Summary

Alex explains how to add subthemes to EDH/Commander decks by combining synergy families. Start with "fulcrum cards" — cards that tie two subthemes together, where setup for one archetype becomes payoff for another. Examples: Throne of Geth (artifact sacrifice + counters), Displacer Kitten (flicker + spellslinger), Professional Facebreaker, Quicksmith Genius, Squee Dubious Monarch. Evaluate each candidate by its floor (how it performs in a subpar, ~25th-percentile game) versus ceiling, and the deck space it demands. Low-floor cards like noncreature spells (Astral Slide without cycling) or redundant picks (Rhet-Tomb Mystic in a deck already 40% cycling creatures) waste slots. Some theme pairs share lots of fulcrum cards (artifacts + ETB) and feel compact; others (cast-from-exile + landfall) have little shared DNA and are harder to fuse. Combining disparate themes is fine if intentional — match the plan to your goal. For a competitive bracket 3 build, lean into cohesion and add tutors/card selection. For a laid-back bracket 2 deck, embrace mechanical messiness, run enough filtering, and use higher-mana removal that doubles as synergy support (e.g. Spread the Sickness in his incubators deck).

## Key Clips

- **[01:08]** Assuming you're not trying to obliterate your deck's consistency, a good place to start in expanding your deck's synergy universe is going to be what I'll call fulcrum cards. This is a thing that naturally ties together multiple subthemes, but typically comes in the form of a payoff from one theme that rewards another.
- **[02:15]** An important consideration when thinking about this sort of card is the floor of the effect. What we're wondering is how the card will feel in a subpar situation, where it's not living up to our wildest dreams. For some cards the floor is pretty high; for noncreature spells it gets even worse.
- **[03:42]** At its floor a card like Rhet-Tomb Mystic will always consume a card slot that could have gone to an ETB creature or card draw, and how high is the ceiling in a deck where over 40% of the creatures already have cycling? Once we do the floor/ceiling calculation it gets mogged by a Merfolk Looter in this particular deck.
- **[04:10]** A major factor in the floor/ceiling discussion is the amount of deck space required to support a particular card. A lot of possible fulcrum cards might already be within your synergy family of choice, creating resources already useful to what you're doing — like treasures, a naturally useful tool for basically all decks.
- **[04:58]** Another angle for thinking about deck space is to consider how closely interlinked two different themes are. Sometimes there are a whole bunch of fulcrum cards for a set of two themes — artifacts and ETB effects have Thought Monitor, Conjurer's Closet, Myr Battlesphere, Mimic Vat — so your two-theme deck might feel decently compact. Many other sets of themes have dramatically less innate shared DNA.
- **[06:18]** It's important to figure out the reason for building the deck, because that will affect the decisions you make. If you're trying to build a decent bracket 3 deck, dial in hard on the points of cohesion and consider ways to get the different elements to come together more often — run partner commanders, pick colors with the most utility, add tutors like Enlightened Tutor to find your key card.
- **[07:26]** Card selection tools like filtering spells and conditional tutors will reduce play pattern variance, but if you add them to a deck that would otherwise have extreme play pattern variance due to having a lot of moving parts, you'll likely end up somewhere still very fresh and dynamic to play with.
- **[08:27]** Playing at a lower power table is a great place for a mechanically disjointed deck because you can run some of the higher mana removal options with added utility for your synergy families. My bracket 2 incubators deck has 16 interaction cards and literally all of them do something else for the deck — keeping a mechanically messy deck from feeling too bad.
