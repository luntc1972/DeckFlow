## Key Clips

- Card draw is the most important aspect of any commander deck. One of the main differentiating factors between new and experienced players is how much emphasis they put on having enough resources available. Experienced players will nearly always have at least a seven card hand.
- The biggest piece of deck building advice I always give new players is to include as much card draw as possible. So long as we have cards in hand, we are playing and that's what matters. Card draw can also compensate for deck building mistakes ΓÇö if we didn't include enough ramp, removal, or lands, we can focus on drawing cards and find them that way.
- Commander card draw is different from other formats as it needs to be sustained for a much larger number of turns. To achieve this, we want to build synergies across our deck so that nearly all of our cards can replace themselves in one capacity or another.
- Garrick's Uprising is a free-mana enchantment that gives all creatures trample and draws a card if we control a creature of power four or more when it enters, then again whenever such a creature enters. If we include it and choose only creatures with power four or more, everything has the potential to become card draw. It's the most exemplar card draw engine I can think of.
- A big reason green is considered the most threatening color in casual commander is its access to engine-based card draw ΓÇö Garrick's Uprising, Up the Beanstalk, Tribute to the World Tree, and Elemental Bond all follow the same idea. When they accompany each other, a green player can play a five-mana creature with power four or greater and draw four cards all at once.
- Instead of just slamming rampant growth, identify your specific card draw engines and choose cards that play into those. In a Golgari sacrifice deck, Morbid Opportunist becomes a natural fit, then double up by making ramp creature-based ΓÇö creatures that can sacrifice themselves like Sakura-Tribe Elder. This is what content creators mean when they say play the right cards for your deck.
- After building my engines, I slather my deck in tiny pieces of card advantage. My favorite counter spell is Dream Fracture ΓÇö a free-mana counter that lets both players draw a card. Less mana efficient than other counters, but replacing itself makes it easier to find an opportunity to cast, and making the opponent draw means we're less likely to make an enemy.
- Card advantage is literally anything that gives us a card to play. Helpful Hunter enters and draws. Undead Butler dies and returns a creature. Shoreline Looter attacks and loots. By looking at card advantage as a broad category and committing a much larger portion of our deck to it, we open ourselves to more thematic options while building a deck that's more consistent overall. This is why I highly value Cultivate and Kodama's Reach.
