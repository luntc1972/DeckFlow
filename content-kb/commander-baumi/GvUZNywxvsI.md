## Summary

The video argues Commander is too complicated. Every game has a finite "complexity budget"; lean core rules are valuable because they free that budget for richer card designs, which is why Wizards simplifies rules (damage off the stack, saddle/sneak replacing crew/ninjutsu). Commander, billed as casual, contradicts this: on a rules level it expands the base game with four complex additions. First, the command zone and commander tax raise unclear questions about cost reductions, zone changes, and phasing. Second, commander damage forces constant tracking of numbers that rarely matter. Third, color identity confuses players because identity and color differ; the creator says allowing off-color mana and limiting decks by actual card color would have been cleaner, and opposes the hybrid-card change. Fourth, multiplayer itself—four board states, life totals, 30 years of cards—overwhelms newcomers. The creator runs a Commander FNM and sees casual/irregular players (about half the base) struggle constantly; precons aren't beginner products. He wants a simple multiplayer beginner product (four monocolor decks, 20 life, no commanders). He notes Wizards minimizes ban/game-changer lists (Lutri's "banned as companion") to save complexity, and concludes a simplified Commander would be a better game, but subtraction is a hard sell.

## Key Clips

- Lesser rules complexity is valuable because it allows for higher card complexity, for more interesting and enjoyable cards. Excess core rule baggage consumes complexity that could otherwise be spent on card designs.
- Commander expands upon the base game rather than simplifying it. It has four different additional rules that are each quite complex: the command zone/commander tax, commander damage, color identity, and being a multiplayer format.
- Color identity is one of the most confusing elements for new players, because it doesn't make sense that color identity and color aren't the same thing. A better solution would have been to allow decks to produce mana outside their commander's color and limit decks by the actual color of their cards, with any deck allowed to run any land since lands have no color.
- Commander damage requires constant consideration—always tracking a set of additional numbers that are very unlikely to impact the game. Wizards briefly touched on commander damage because they understand it's too complicated for the value it brings.
- What new players really need is a multiplayer beginner product—think Jumpstart but for multiplayer, or a bundle of four simple precons tuned to play against each other. Precons these days are way too complex to be beginner products.
- Commander has two ban lists in effect: the regular ban list and the game changer list. Wizards are clearly working to minimize both, which is why Lutri was freed and why they keep proposing new cards to unban—they're trying to spend less complexity on rules.
- Commander is already way over its complexity budget. Common suggestions like banned-as-commander, additional bracket requirements, or extra lists can't happen because they'd consume complexity budget that isn't available. This is why Wizards take a conservative approach, changing as little as possible.
- At our LGS we have maybe 50% regulars and 50% people that circle in and out, playing once a month. That second 50% are the people who struggle, and that's a significant portion of the player base.
