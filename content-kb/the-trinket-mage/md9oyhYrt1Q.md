## Summary

The creator, a longtime cEDH player, argues competitive Commander is bigger than ever (estimating roughly 5% of players) but faces deeper structural problems. The core issue is tournament EDH (TEDH) versus casual cEDH. Tournament scoring—5 points for a win, 1 for a draw, 0 for a loss—incentivizes stalling, yapping, counter-spelling, and forcing draws rather than winning, plus king-making situations where proposing a draw is correct. Players aren't morally wrong; the system is flawed. Wizards can't fully fix cEDH: the format thrives precisely because it's grassroots and proxy-friendly (proxies make affordable meta decks possible), so official sanctioning and reserved-list cards won't work. The shared ban list also can't satisfy both casual and competitive players simultaneously (e.g., Timna, Rograkh are busted in cEDH but fine casually; unbanning Griselbrand would warp the format). Making a new 'Super Commander' format wouldn't change which deck is best. Seating/turn-order advantage exists but averages out over many games. Proposed fixes: hard time cutoffs, adjusted points (bonus for winning, removing draw points, assigning placement order), standardized multiplayer tournament rules, and bracket restrictions. The author's favored solution is removing prize support, since cEDH is best played casually for pride and fun.

## Key Clips

- CEDH is just playing strong Commander decks that are meta. This is regular bracket five. You can do this casually with your friends, like regular EDH. In a tournament setting, things do change for the worse. This is TEDH, tournament EDH, which is quite different from casual competitive EDH.
- One of the big problems are draws, as in a tie game. The way most events work, a win is five points, a loss is zero, and a draw is worth one point. Meaning, if you don't think you can win, the best thing to do is yap, play removal, stall, and hopefully either run out the clock or convince everyone to draw. For example, right now, Heartwood Storyteller is seeing some play as a card you play to force draws.
- You can also end up in king-making situations. Let's say player A has the win on the stack, but player B uses board upon a wind to put their win on the stack. Player C has no interaction, but you have one counter spell. If you stop one win con, the other player just wins. In both situations, you lose. So, you propose a draw. The system is the problem, not the players.
- Part of the reason CEDH is popular compared to a format like Legacy or Vintage is because it is not run by Wizards, which means proxies are allowed. You can get a proxy deck online for a dollar a card or less sometimes. If CEDH was not proxy-friendly like this, it would never get off the ground. The reserved list kills old formats.
- Because the ban list is shared with casual commander, you can't 100% satisfy the cEDH ban list and the casual EDH ban list. You can get close, but it won't ever be perfect. But you also can't make a new ban list, because if you make a new format called Super Commander, then cEDH literally doesn't change. Bracket five is just the best deck in the Commander format.
- If I were to change the ban list for cEDH specifically, Timna is the first thing on the chopping block. This is one of the most busted commanders ever printed. Card draw in the command zone is already good, but access to four colors and a second commander really pushes it over the edge. But Wizards can't ban Timna because it's fine in casual EDH.
- Another problem with cEDH is the seating problem, which is that your turn order makes a big difference in win rate. Player one winning more often because they get to untap with their turn one ramp before everyone else does. I'm actually not super concerned about table seating. It's literally random. If you just play enough, it's going to average out.
- There is one idea that I think will actually really help TEDH the most. And that is getting rid of prize support for major events. I went to LVO, the biggest Warhammer tournament. They played purely for pride. Games Workshop just cut the prize support and people enjoyed it a lot more. Competitive Commander is at its best when played casually with your friends.
