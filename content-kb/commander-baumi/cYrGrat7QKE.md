## Key Clips

- The wandering minstrel is a two-mana simic creature with a whole range of powerful abilities. His most noteworthy comes right at the start: lands we control enter untapped. It's actually one of the strongest lines of text that's ever been printed on a commander.
- The real strength of this kind of effect stems from using it with ramp spells. One of the main limiting factors on most ramp spells is that they make lands enter tapped next turn, forcing us to take turns off. The Wandering Minstrel accelerates the speed of all of our ramp by a turn.
- Bounce lands like Azorius Chancery and Simic Growth Chamber tap for two mana but bounce a land we control to our hand. They become exceptionally powerful here because we can immediately tap them for mana even before their own bounce trigger. We can use them as rituals, generating mana just by playing them and bouncing them back into our hand.
- These are the same tricks used in the Amulet Titan modern deck, which has a rich competitive history. A big portion of our deck is built around non-basic ramp so that we can find our bounce lands and towns: Omen Path Journey, Open the Way, Hour of Promise, and Clifftop Lookout, amongst others.
- For eight mana, including Wubrg, the wandering minstrel gives other creatures we control plus X plus X until end of turn, where X is the number of towns we control. I didn't include any finishers because this instant-speed repeatable overrun is all we need. We simply ramp, stick any creatures on the board, and threaten a kill with every attack.
- There are 23 towns printed in Final Fantasy and we play all of them. If at the start of combat we control five or more towns, we create a 2/2 elemental creature token that's all colors, supported with Weenie synergies plus Risen Reef and Omnath for elemental synergy.
