## Summary

This guide explains how to build powerful thematic Commander decks, defined as decks that make power concessions for the sake of an idea other than winning. The creator builds around a theme—not necessarily a commander—and includes cards that fit directly or indirectly, avoiding powerful cards that don't touch the theme. Themes can be Kindred, keyword mechanics, abstract ideas, or a single 'guideline card' deconstructed into concepts; self-imposed restrictions keep decks balanced and away from generic good-stuff piles. He optimizes for fun on both sides: limits board wipes and removal, avoids combos and lock pieces, and keeps turns short while pushing the game forward. The core deckbuilding tool is the Scryfall Tagger: look up a card's gameplay tags (impulse draw, ritual, ramp, removal, sweeper), combine otags to narrow searches, and dig no more than two steps from the centerpiece to find synergies. He uses four to five broad categories, emphasizes card advantage heavily (often running 40 lands), prefers land ramp over artifacts, values threat management via cheap bodies, treats plan B as a way back to plan A, and always includes a clear way to end the game. Round out with non-intrusive staples.

## Key Clips

- I would describe any deck that makes concessions in power level for the sake of an idea that isn't winning as thematic. This is a very broad definition and encompasses pretty much anything that isn't cedh.
- My general goal is to build my decks around an idea and then have every card fit into the idea in some capacity. This can be direct, like a kithkin in a kithkin deck, or indirect, like Tatyova's welcome in a kithkin deck because it makes it so that the kithkin draw cards when they enter. However I wouldn't play the One Ring in a kithkin deck because it really has nothing to do with them and they don't interact with it.
- The first thing I do is find my theme. This can be a commander but it doesn't have to be. A deck that depends on its commander for its game plan is often a fragile one, as the commander gets killed, recast, killed again and recast. I do think it's worthwhile to build every deck in a way that it can function without its commander.
- Another method for finding themes that I enjoy relies on guideline cards: find a card that gets you excited and then deconstruct it and use it as the thematic through line for your deck building. Use that card as a centerpiece of an interconnected web that spans around it. At the end of it the card shouldn't even be necessary anymore for the deck to feel like the card.
- I take those tags and do individual searches that combine them with the following: otag card advantage, otag ramp, otag removal, and otag sweeper. This usually lets me find a card draw, ramp and removal foundation for my deck that fits into the themes of it. The Scryfall tagger lets you combine tags, for example otag ramp and impulse draw, which really narrows it down.
- When digging into the tagger I wouldn't recommend going more than two steps removed from our starting point. Taking the second step can lead to really cool and unique ideas, small packages of two or three cards that only synergize with each other but still fit into the larger theme. I wouldn't recommend going a third step; at that point we often end up with loose disconnected pieces that feel like they aren't part of a whole.
- I usually stick to four to five broad categories per deck: card advantage, interaction (removal, counter spells, sweepers), mana (anything that ramps), and buff. By keeping the categories broad and simple I can clearly identify when a card doesn't contribute anything. Precise categories make it so that every card seems valuable, which makes it difficult to decide what to cut.
- I usually play 40 lands in my decks since I have a ton of card draw; I can just filter out the lands I don't need, and by playing a lot of lands I'm guaranteeing I'll hit my land drops. I put a particularly heavy emphasis on card advantage because when I play against most casual players, card draw tends to ultimately be the main difference why I succeed where they don't.
