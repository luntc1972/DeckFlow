## Summary

This video recounts the creator's experience building a budget "$25 deck pool" for Commander in 2020-2021, with one deck per guild rolled randomly before each game. Starting with two control decks (Orzhov Kambal, Azorius Noyan Dar) shaped the pool toward midrange and aristocrats archetypes. Over four iterations spanning ~170 tracked games, decks were swapped based on winrate and durability data. Orzhov control dominated early (54% winrate) via lifegain, suppression, and combos, but was later pressured out by a ruthless Selesnya cats aggro deck (42%). As aggro grew, slower decks added lifegain, board wipes, and blockers, creating a real metagame where lacking anti-aggression tools cost games. Combo decks (Lazav, Izzet) faltered once their play patterns were figured out and the pool turned aggressive. The Simic toolbox midrange deck proved most consistently strong by being well-rounded. The core lesson: build decks at a consistent power level that each present a specific, evocative threat demanding considerationΓÇöavoid generic midrange pilesΓÇöand be willing to dismantle a favorite deck if it's too weak or boring. This philosophy of presenting and answering specific problems makes multiplayer games more dynamic and inspired the creator's MTG content.

## Key Clips

- In 2019 a group of friends, none playing much Magic on a broke college budget, used a guild die from a D&D dice set to spawn an idea: rather than build one or two new decks each, build a low-cost deck for each guild and roll randomly to pick which deck to play in a given game. Thus began the $25 deck pool.
- The pool began with two control decks (Orzhov Kambal control and Azorius Noyan Dar control), and that influence shaped the rest: of five midrange decks, three were aristocrats and one was land-focused. More explosive combo decksΓÇögraveyard artifacts, big mana, and a counterspell deckΓÇöwere well-suited to handle control.
- After the first ~30 games, the Orzhov deck garnered a 54% winrate via lifegain, suppression, and combos, while other winrates ranged from 30% down to 0%. The 0% Niv-Mizzet combo deck was swapped for a Jhoira artifact combo deck, and flat, lifeless midrange decks (Selesnya, Tatyova lands) were replaced with cats aggro and a Vannifar toolbox.
- In the next 30 games, Orzhov won none of its 11 games as players pressured it down first, and the cats deck took over at a 42% winrateΓÇöa ruthless aggro deck applying pressure as early as turn 2 with enough draw to grind the lategame. Golgari and Rakdos aristocrats were swapped out for a Grismold tokens deck and a Malfegor discard deck, and slower decks added lifegain, board wipes, and blockers.
- Over a ~6-month period of 86 games (a sample large enough to be halfway reliable), winrates clustered into three groups. The Simic toolbox midrange deck topped out winning a third of its games due to being well-rounded; five decks sat in the high 20s; and the lowest cluster in the high teens held the Boros eggs, Rakdos discard, and both combo decks, whose play patterns had been figured out and which lacked blockers against a more aggressive pool.
- Factoring placement into the analysis (a durable deck that sticks around is more fun than one knocked out first), the Dimir Lazav deckΓÇöafter three slower Dimir iterations failedΓÇöwas replaced with a Sygg, River Cutthroat rogue aggro deck. With that and a new Izzet spellslinger deck, the pool reached its finished state around early 2021.
- The two deckbuilding rules were that decks should be a consistent power level and should do something unique other decks must consider, both during games and deckbuilding. Seven of the ten decks are extremely evocativeΓÇöRakdos renders the table hellbent by turn 6, Gruul explodes if left alive, Azorius oppresses, Grismold injects voltron, Orzhov dominates late games with board wipes and lifegain combosΓÇöprompting players to constantly ask: can this survive X, pressure Y, or fold to Z?
- The takeaway: if you build a set of decks, don't let any be overly weak or boring, and have the courage to dismantle a deck you like if it becomes either. Present a specific problem for your opponents and be prepared if they present one for youΓÇömultiplayer games are more fun when people think about their opponents rather than racing engines like a second-rate Eurogame.
