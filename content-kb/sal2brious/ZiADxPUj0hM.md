## Summary

This companion video presents a custom tool, built with the creator's brother Ian, that analyzes EDHREC commander pages and ranks commanders on several metrics. Entropy is a logarithmic measure of how unpopular the average card is, indicating whether a deck concentrates on one archetype or spreads across themes; average entropy is around 1.8. Synergy modifies EDHREC's synergy score to show how many times more popular cards are in a given commander versus its color identity overall. Hipster cards are those run in under 1% of decks; signature cards are run more in this commander than any other. Low-entropy commanders are typically cEDH partner pairs (Rograkh/Silas, Tymna/Kraum) or precon commanders with one definitive list, like the second Doctor/Ryan Sinclair, sometimes traceable to a single popular Moxfield list. Theme-locked precons (slivers, Felothar big-butts) and max-theme decks (D&D, Doctor Who) also score low. High-entropy commanders are generic or vanilla cards (Pir, Kaysa, vanilla 7/2 stat lines, support pieces like Raydan) offering no central build direction. Lowest synergy comes from colorless and four-color commanders that resemble their color average. The takeaway: strong central mechanics produce focused, distinctive decks; generic commanders produce diverse, average-resembling ones.

## Key Clips

- A tool built with the creator's brother Ian that lets you input a commander and ranks it on several EDHREC-derived metrics: entropy, synergy, hipster cards, and signature cards, so you can compare how commanders stack up against each other.
- Entropy is a data-science term for the variation and chaos within a data set ΓÇö here it measures how unpopular the average card is on a logarithmic scale: 1.0 means the average card is played at a 50% rate, 2.0 means a 25% rate. It captures whether a deck is concentrated on one archetype or spread across many themes. Average entropy is around 1.8 (Kenrith ~2.23, mono tribal ~1.45).
- Synergy is derived from EDHREC's synergy score (the rate a card runs in the deck versus its rate across all decks of the same color identity), then divided by the overall play rate. So synergy tells you these cards are, say, five times more popular here than in the colors overall. Kenrith ~1.428, Urza ~15x.
- Hipster cards are any card run in less than 1% of decks overall; the metric averages how many hipster cards a deck contains. Both hosts judged it the muddiest, least information-dense metric ΓÇö fun to analyze but the weakest signal.
