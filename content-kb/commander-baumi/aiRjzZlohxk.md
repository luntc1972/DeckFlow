## Summary

This is a deck tech for a budget (~$50) Commander deck nominally led by Rocco, Cabaretti Caterer, but actually built around Norin the Wary. Rocco (RWG) is cast for X=1 to tutor Norin directly onto the battlefield, then returns to the command zone as a defensive blocker. Norin repeatedly exiles and returns itself whenever any spell is cast or creature attacks, flickering up to four times per turn rotation across all players' turns. This fuels once-per-turn ETB engines without combo lines. Key card-draw pieces: Welcoming Vampire, Tocasia's Welcome, Enduring Innocence (each draws on low-power/low-MV creatures entering); plus Soul of the Harvest, Mentor of the Meek, Rumor Gatherer, Path of Discovery. Board and damage come from creatures that grow on Norin entering, token-makers like Blessed Sanctuary, and burn payoffs (Impact Tremors, Warstorm Surge, Torbran) that can deal 24 damage per rotation. Kodama of the East Tree cheats permanents/lands into play; Soulflayer-like Soul-Scryer doubles triggers; Soul Warden/Essence Warden gain huge life. Eleven removal and protection pieces guard Norin, since losing him collapses the deck. Highly consistent, hard to interact with, but frustrating for opponents—the author disassembled it for being too easy to win.

## Key Clips

- This is a deck built around Norin the Wary, even though Rocco, Cabaretti Caterer is technically the commander. Rocco sits in the command zone and is cast for X=1 to search the deck for a creature with mana value 1 or less and put it directly onto the battlefield — almost always grabbing Norin. Rocco adds a free mana to Norin but gives access to Cabaretti (red/white/green) colors for a much broader range of synergy effects.
- Norin is a one-mana 2/1 human warrior that exiles himself whenever somebody plays a spell or a creature attacks, then returns under our control at the end step. This constant self-flickering makes him nearly impossible to destroy, so he doesn't need commander protection — and he enters the battlefield on opponents' turns too, enabling up to four ETB triggers per turn rotation.
- The highest-priority engine pieces are card-draw effects that trigger when a low-power/low-mana-value creature enters: Welcoming Vampire, Tocasia's Welcome, and Enduring Innocence. Each is once-per-turn, but because Norin enters on opponents' turns as well, they trigger up to four times per turn rotation. These are the top Mulligan priorities; Tocasia's Welcome and Enduring Innocence are considered essential despite being pricier.
- Burn is the primary win route. Cards like Impact Tremors and Warstorm Surge ping opponents every time Norin enters. With Torbran, Thane of Red Fell added in, a board of Sarulf, Torbran, and Impact Tremors deals 6 damage per end step — 24 damage to each opponent in a single turn rotation — all without spending mana past the initial investment.
- Sword/Soul Skitter is a second Norin-like flickerer. Stacked with Norin so it returns first and exiles itself again, it effectively doubles engine effects like Path of Discovery, Soul of the Harvest, and Rumor Gatherer. Panharmonicon doubles all ETBs, and Gand of the Wide doubles tokens, pings, and value from the next batch of synergies.
- Kodama of the East Tree is probably the most powerful card in the deck: whenever another permanent enters under our control (not via Kodama), put a permanent of equal or lesser mana cost from hand directly into play. Since we regularly draw 4–12 cards per turn rotation, Kodama slams extra lands into play and cheats out four-or-less permanents for free using the four-mana Soul Skitter trigger.
- The deck runs 11 pieces of removal including instant-speed Generous Gift, Chaos Warp, and Beast Within, which double as a way to save Norin by triggering his flicker if he's targeted. Board wipes like Steel Command, Dusk // Dawn, and Blasphemous Act don't hit the self-exiling Norin, so the deck rebuilds faster than opponents after a wipe.
- The biggest weakness: if Norin is successfully removed, the rest of the deck becomes very mid. The main skill is holding instant-speed answers ready for the rare permanents that can stop Norin — containment priest, split second, or targeted-damage activated abilities. The deck is so consistent and frustrating to play against that the speaker disassembled it because it won too easily; budget around $50 (~$75 with shipping).
