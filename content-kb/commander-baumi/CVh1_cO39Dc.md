## Summary

The creator builds a gimmick Commander deck containing all 61 Game Changer cards plus only basic-mana lands (fetches, shocks, check lands), ending up with 45 lands and no synergy. Because no five-color Game Changer commander exists, they deliberately pick Progenitus—a weak commander—to avoid skewing games. The deck is technically Bracket 4 but terrible: 45 lands, no synergies, nonsensical inclusions like Natural Order with only three green creatures. Across several MTGO games, opponents frequently concede early or self-destruct (hermit druid combo killing itself, a confused Lumra cEDH player). A standout combo is imprinting Teferi's Protection into the recently-unbanned Panoptic Mirror, phasing out each upkeep to stall while building toward an 18-mana Coalition Victory win. The creator concludes Game Changers are poorly balanced relative to each other: cards like Rhystic Study, Smothering Tithe, and especially Glacial Chasm are extreme outliers you must answer or lose, while many others are weak. Bracket 4 felt unbalanced, identity-less, and confusing, worsened by WotC's vague 'optimized cEDH' redefinition. Despite this, they recommend trying a saner version of the deck to see the cards in action.

## Key Clips

- We just had a big update to the game changers list and a whole bunch of new cards were added to it. The game changer list is now up to 61 total cards. That's almost enough to build a deck made up of only game changers, but a bunch of them are lands. If we put all of them into a deck and fill the remaining slots with lands, we end up with a deck that has 45 lands.
- There is no game changer commander that's five colors. So for this deck to work, I had to pick a commander that isn't on the game changer list. I deliberately chose Progenitus. Progenitus isn't a very good card, and that's kind of the point. If I had chosen Najeela, every game would have been a Najeela game.
- As far as the lands are concerned, I am not playing any lands that have additional effects other than creating mana, which means no survey lands and no utility lands. I am playing entirely fetches, shocks, and check lands.
- I imprinted Teferi's Protection into Panoptic Mirror, a card that was just recently unbanned. At the beginning of my upkeep I phased out all of my stuff, so I couldn't do anything, but my opponents also couldn't deal damage to me. I could do the exact same thing in bracket 3 because that only took two game changers, and I think that's actually a problem.
- Overall, my experience with Bracket 4 was that they were some of the worst commander games I've ever played. It felt like there was no idea from anybody what Bracket 4 even really means. The decks were super unbalanced. Bracket 3 is too wide. Bracket 4 is too muddy.
- There are some game changers that are still just way above everything else. The obvious candidates like Rhystic Study and Smothering Tithe, but Glacial Chasm is just absurd. You need something to deal with it or you simply cannot win against it. That's not a card I would ever want to play against, so I don't want to play in Bracket 3.
- What I took away from this entire journey is that the game changers are not balanced within the game changers. I would agree that's fine if there were harder restrictions on when you can play them, since you are allowed three and there's such a broad range of what these cards do and how powerful they are.
- Would I recommend playing this deck? Weirdly, yeah. But don't do exactly what I did. Maybe play a couple more creatures, don't play literally every single game changer, don't play the worst possible commander, and remove a handful of lands. 45 is a bit much even for me.
