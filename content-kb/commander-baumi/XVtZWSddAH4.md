## Summary

This video critiques Edge of Eternities ("EPA/EOD drift") and centers on Speed Demon, a five-mana 5/5 trampler with the Start Your Engines mechanic. Speed is a per-player modifier raised once per turn by dealing combat damage to opponents, capping at four (reachable in three turns). At end of turn Speed Demon draws X cards and loses X life equal to your speed. The creator proxied a mono-black Speed Demon deck with no lifegain and no max-hand-size extenders, leaning into Madness and Flash creatures to exploit the reckless draw/discard. He proposes a "correct" redesign: remove the max-speed cap (speed unlimited, while other cards stay capped at four), add haste and flying, drop the Start Your Engines requirement so it accelerates regardlessΓÇöturning it into an all-out draw engine that wins or decks you out. He also praises Monument to Endurance, a free-cost artifact triggering draw/treasure/damage on discard (each once per turn), strong with looting and discard-to-hand-size, valuing midgame engines. He compliments the set's vivid special art and box-topper full-art lands.

## Key Clips

- The speed demon is a five mana 5/5 trampler that starts our engines. Starting our engines gives us speed, a modifier the player has that can be increased once per turn by dealing damage to opponents. Maximum speed is four, and speed can't be reduced except by one Turtle creature; otherwise it stays where it is for the rest of the game.
- Building up speed is not difficult: we start at one and can immediately trigger it the same turn to go to two, reaching maximum speed in only three turns after starting our engines, which is not much for Commander. All cards that start our engines also care about our speed, usually about max speed, at which point they get an additional effect.
- The speed demon's other ability: at the end of our turn we draw X cards and lose X life, where X is our speed. Playing it on turn five with max speed already set up immediately draws four cards. This often results in overdrawing, adding recklessness, accompanied by the corresponding life loss.
- Proposed redesign of the speed demon: give it haste, add 'your speed is unlimited' (with a clarifier that max speed for other cards remains four), drop the start-your-engines requirement, and keep the draw-X/lose-X-life ability. Only the speed demon breaks the speed limit, going faster every turn ΓÇö a draw machine that only knows victory or certain death.
- Critique of the mechanic name 'start your engines': it's awkward and limiting because it requires a set with engines, and exceptions like Glitch Ghost Surveyor show this. Neutral language implying a beginning, paired with 'speed', would have been better ΓÇö alternatives suggested include kick off, go go go, ignite, or get ready.
- Monument to Endurance is a free-mana artifact: whenever we discard a card we may draw a card, create a treasure token, or deal damage to all opponents, but each effect may only be chosen once per turn. It pairs well with looting effects, and discarding to hand size now comes with a free loot. The speaker plans to get two or three; calls it incredible and easy to fit into a wide range of decks.
- General deckbuilding principle: the most important aspect of any deck is its midgame plan and engines. Turning channeling or discarding-for-cost into replacement effects or mana is incredible. If you make those engines good and efficient, you can do whatever you want with the rest of the deck ΓÇö cards like Guest Grimoire and Monument to Endurance build this crucial momentum.
