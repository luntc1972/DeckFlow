## Summary

This is a deck tech for a budget Commander build using Disa (called "daa") as commander, themed around classic Jund (graveyard value, hand disruption, creature removal, then turn the corner). Disa cheats Go permanents onto the battlefield when they hit the graveyard from anywhere but the battlefield, so discard and mill become upside. Key enablers: Conspiracy, Maskwood Nexus, and Ashes of the Fallen set creature types; Buried Alive fetches three Gos (Pyro, Necro, Barago—Pyro best, since Parag turns Disa's Gos into direct-damage burn). Discard outlets (Tortured Existence, Chainer, Faithless-style effects) and Fos Shaman recur or find creatures and MDFC lands. Disa's second ability rewards connecting with creatures to make Go tokens: Brawn grants trample, Loyal Apprentice makes flyers, and token doublers (Doubling Season, Parallel Lives, Second Harvest) scale it—though those are outside the stated budget. The rest is nostalgic classic-Jund cards (Liliana of the Veil/Last Hope, Bloodbraid Elf, Bloodbraid references, Bonfire of the Damned, Grim Flayer, Traverse) that reference past Standard/Modern Jund. Mana base runs ramp, dorks, talismans, tapped duals, manlands, and many MH3 MDFCs—24 lands plus 10 MDFCs (~34 sources)—prioritizing reliably casting the commander by turn five over untapped early lands.

## Key Clips

- Disa cheats in go permanents if they hit the graveyard from anywhere but the battlefield. This makes cards like Conspiracy, Maskwood Nexus, and Ashes of the Fallen powerful, because any creature you discard or mill gets put onto the battlefield thanks to Disa.
- Buried Alive is incredibly powerful when Disa is in play: spend three mana to find three gos and put them all onto the battlefield. Find Pyro, Necro, and Barago, with Pyro being the best one. Parag turns all the gos from Disa's ability into direct damage burn spells, so you can destroy creatures, make more gos, or deal damage to opponents to close the game.
- Discard effects are good with Disa, since discarded go creatures get cheated in, negating the usual downside of discarding. Tortured Existence is a cheap enchantment that lets you discard creatures to bring back other creature cards to hand; Chainer, Adapt, and Faos Shaman work well for the same reason.
- For Disa's second ability there are two ways to build around it. First, make sure your creatures connect to make more go tokens — Braun gives all creatures trample if you control a forest, and Loyal Apprentice creates flying thopters that help you connect on multiple opponents, making it easy to create three go tokens a turn.
- The other way to synergize with the second ability is doubling the creature tokens you create from Disa, with staples like Doubling Season, Parallel Lives, or Second Harvest. These are outside my budget, but if you own them I'd recommend playing them too.
- Lilana of the Veil synergizes with Disa: you can break parity on table-wide discard since you can pitch a go to defend her. She's still about $16 so I didn't include her in the budget version, but as a Jund deck Lily is a must-have for my personal list.
- Because Disa is so central to the deck's plan, you want hands with all the mana sources for Disa. I play an assortment of mana dorks, rocks, and land ramp to cast the commander reliably, hopefully before turn five. Most haymakers come after turn four or five.
- The deck runs 24 lands plus 10 MDFC cards from Modern Horizons 3 for 34 land sources, excluding ramp, mana dorks, and talismans. I prefer tapped lands that provide multiple colors over untapped basics because casting demanding spells later matters more than playing untapped early.
