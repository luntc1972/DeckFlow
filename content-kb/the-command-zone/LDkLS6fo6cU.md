## Summary

Command Zone episode on building a disruption package. Bracket 3 template: about 12 targeted disruption spells plus 6 mass disruption. Run more (14-20) if the deck needs time to win, fewer in aggro or fast decks. Disruption buys time and slows opponents rather than ending their plan. Since you are behind about 75% of the time in Commander, plan for that. Exile beats destroy. Bounce and tucking are underrated. Lean on each color's strengths: white for any permanent, blue for stack and bounce, black or red for creatures, green for artifacts and enchantments. Use multicolor removal like Abrupt Decay, Assassin's Trophy and Terminate over Beast Within. Judge spells on efficiency, speed, flexibility and impact, including card parity. Draw-heavy decks need less flexibility. Early-weak decks want cheap removal such as Swords to Plowshares, Cut Down, Static Prison and Pick Your Poison. Aggro decks want late, high-impact removal such as Phyrexian Purge, Fire Covenant, Angel of the Ruins and Éowyn, Fearless Knight. ## Key Clips

- **[04:00]** On average at bracket three you want about 12 targeted disruption spells, and the template also calls for six mass disruption spells. Some decks want 10 targeted and more mass, and some want less overall.
- **[05:40]** If your deck needs more time to win the game, you want more disruption than average, closer to 14, 15 or even 20. Slower decks should disrupt early with efficient removal to slow opponents down to their level.
- **[12:53]** Exiling is generally the best form of removal. White is the best removal color for permanents because it has access to exile, so if you have white, lean on it more heavily for removal.
- **[22:20]** A bad disruption package leans on the wrong color, like playing Beast Within in white or black. Use black for cheap creature removal and green for artifact and enchantment removal, or play gold cards like Abrupt Decay, Assassin's Trophy or Terminate.
- **[35:35]** Four things to evaluate in a removal spell: efficiency (mana cost), speed (instant or flash), flexibility (card types it answers), and impact (multiple targets, card parity, extra upside). Also consider how it answers: bounce, exile, destroy or tuck.
- **[53:45]** If your deck is weakest early, such as control, enchantress or ramp, you want cheap early disruption like Cut Down, Static Prison or Pick Your Poison. It lets you advance your setup while removing a threat instead of spending your whole turn on removal.
- **[70:12]** For a 12-card removal suite: five or six creature-only answers, three artifact/enchantment answers, two flexible ones that hit multiple permanent types, and two that answer lands. Adjust based on how much of your mass disruption is creature-focused.
- **[77:41]** Good heuristic: taking damage is fine, losing cards is bad. Use removal at the last possible moment, such as when they declare an attack at you, when all doubt is gone that the thing is a problem.
