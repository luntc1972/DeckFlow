## Summary

The video reviews blue board wipes and card draw in Commander, arguing blue is structurally weaker than other colors at both. Blue lacks proper destruction sweepers and instead bounces permanents back to hand. Cyclonic Rift is best-in-class because its two-mana overload-free mode bounces a single thing, but competitors like Evacuation, AEtherize, and Nevinyrral's Disk fall shortΓÇöthey hit your own board, only respond to attacks, or ignore non-creature permanents. Nevinyrral's Disk is criticized for making its controller the table's only viable target until popped; the speaker disdains making it indestructible. Blue card draw is also weak: the best sources are engines, not one-shot cantrips designed for short 1v1 games, while Rhystic Study and Coastal Piracy are annoying or too conditional. The featured card is Consuming Tide, a four-mana sorcery: each player keeps one non-land permanent, all others bounce to hand, then you draw a card per opponent holding more cards than you. It becomes asymmetrical in decks built on land ramp (immune to the wipe) and individually strong permanents like Sphinx of the Second Sun, often netting three cards off a four-mana sweep.

## Key Clips

- A carefully chosen and well-positioned board wipe wins games. There are a variety of great options for all colors: Aetherize Predation clears out most boards while creating a massive one ourselves, Feast of Succession then handles the tokens, Chain Reaction holds the high score of most damage done by a single four-mana spell, and most devastating of all, Farewell.
- Blue is a bit different from the other colors. It doesn't have many proper destruction board wipes and instead tries to bounce everything, returning permanents back to their owner's hand. Cyclonic Rift is usually seen as best in class, and that makes sense, but seven mana is a lot, and casting it for two to bounce a single thing is a nice feature to have.
- Most of Cyclonic Rift's commonly used competition doesn't hit the same niche. Evacuation hits all creatures, even ours, and Aetherize needs to be used in response to an attack. Neither deals with non-creature permanents, which is a big deal ΓÇö it's not unusual for a non-creature to be the most threatening thing on the board.
- Never play Nevinyrral's Disk. By itself it seems like a reasonable colorless sweeper, but it has hidden text: as long as it's on the battlefield, all opponents will keep hitting us until we pop it. It creates a dilemma where playing more permanents doesn't make sense and attacking each other doesn't fix the problem, so we become the only viable target ΓÇö a situation we entirely caused ourselves.
- Consuming Tide is a four-mana blue sorcery: each player chooses a non-land permanent they control, returns all non-land permanents not chosen this way to their owners' hands, then you draw a card for each opponent who has more cards in hand than you. It's a particularly good board wipe because it hits all non-land permanents, often leaving players stranded for a couple of turns while they rebuild.
- Land ramp is a much safer option than artifact ramp because even though it's slower, it outperforms artifacts across most games ΓÇö it's not vulnerable to mass removal like Planar Cleansing and Consuming Tide. By putting heavy emphasis on land ramp, broad sweepers that hit all non-lands become somewhat one-sided and reward good deck building.
- Ideally you want to play Consuming Tide in a deck with individually strong permanents that are difficult to beat one-on-one. I play it in my Errol, Titan of Nature's Wrath deck because he's a huge lad that tends to be the individually strongest creature on the board. Blue has access to fantastic big creatures like Sphinx of the Second Sun, Archetype of Imagination, Hullbreaker Horror, Nezahal Primal Tide, and even non-creatures like Omniscience.
- Consuming Tide also draws cards. By bouncing our opponents' mana rocks while we have few or none, their hands fill up and we draw cards from that. More often than not when I cast this spell I get to draw three cards afterward, which is insane for a four-mana sweeper. In other words: get rid of opponents' stuff, keep our strongest permanent, and draw three.
