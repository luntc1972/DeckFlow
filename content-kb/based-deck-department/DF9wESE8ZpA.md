## Summary

The video is a framework for choosing mana acceleration in Commander decks. Core principle: how much ramp (dorks, rocks, ramp enchantments, free mana) you run should be dictated by your commander and game plan, not a fixed number. Examples cited: a Jon Benton aggro deck wants any 1-mana acceleration to land Jon by turn 2–3 with protection; a Sauron pseudo-control deck runs heavy ramp to cast Sauron by turn 4; a Skullbriar deck runs essentially no ramp because the commander costs two and the deck's average mana value is under two. Consistency math: to reliably guarantee a mana rock/ramp spell by turn two you need about 11 copies; running only four ramp sources means you'd draw roughly 25 cards deep to find one, so on average you hit ramp by turn 4 only every other game—four ramp pieces is effectively zero. Land-count heuristic: a normal midrange commander with no ramp wants about 42 lands; cut one land for every two-to-three cheap ramp or cheap card-draw sources you add. Takeaway: decide whether your deck wants ramp reliably or not, then commit enough copies to actually achieve that.
