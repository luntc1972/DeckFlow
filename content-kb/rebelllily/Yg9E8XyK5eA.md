## Summary

This video introduces Wizards of the Coast's official Commander bracket system, developed by the CFP/CIP to help players organize games by power level and intent. Rather than a numerical power scale, it uses five aspirational categories backed by a 'game changers' listΓÇöcards that dramatically warp gameplay (tutors, fast mana, free counters, combo pieces, certain commanders), serving as a watch list rather than a ban list. The five brackets: Bracket 1 (Exhibition) is ultra-casual theme decks with no game changers, no two-card combos, minimal tutors, no mass land destruction or extra-turn spells; Bracket 2 (Core) matches precon power with light extra-turn spells allowed but no looping; Bracket 3 (Upgraded) allows up to three game changers and slow combos to end long games; Bracket 4 (Optimized) is high-power with only the ban list as a restriction; Bracket 5 (cEDH) focuses on competitive metagames with expectations of competitive integrity and game-state maintenance. The narrator argues Brackets 1 and 2 are hard to distinguish and could merge into one casual bucket, and that the high-power/cEDH gap is best separated behaviorallyΓÇöby how a player pilots the deckΓÇörather than by decklist. He encourages players to try the system and share feedback.

## Key Clips

- The bracket system is a simple set of five categories that work off shorthand labels. This time we tried to move away from a numerical power scale and focus on something more aspirational, because at the end of the day deck composition is only half the story.
- This is why we have the game changer system: a list of cards the group decided dramatically warp how a game is played. This is not a ban list but a list of cards to watch out for. They include tutors, specific stax pieces, fast mana, strong value cards, combo cards, free counters, certain game-warping commanders and some others.
- Bracket 1, exhibition, is for ultra-casual theme-based decks with a very light focus on winning. No cards from the game changers list, no two-card combos, minimal tutors outside the list like Diabolic Tutor, no mass land destruction, and no extra-turn spells.
- Bracket 2, core, is for decks close to the power level of an average precon, with a balance of fun and winning. No game changers, no two-card combos or mass land destruction. Here is where you can start to play a little extra-turn spells but not to be looped; tutors are still minimal.
- Bracket 3, upgraded, is for decks stronger than the average precon. This bracket allows up to three cards from the game changer list, still no land destruction and minimal extra-turn spells. You can start playing combos here as long as it's not a fast one, so you're playing combos to help end a long game rather than something you gunned for from the very beginning.
- Bracket 4, optimized, is for high power decks with no restrictions other than the ban list. This might sound like cEDH but there's a difference between cEDH and optimized.
- Bracket 5, cEDH, we defined as having a focus on competitive metagames, but it also carries an expectation of competitive integrity and game state maintenance ΓÇö minimizing spite plays, salty speed concessions, all of that. Nothing fundamentally changes about how cEDH is played, so if you've been playing it, no changes.
- The best way for me to break high power and cEDH apart is behavioral. You could take a high power list and a cEDH list side by side and they could look similar, but it ultimately ends on how the player is piloting the deck ΓÇö how they're playing the game is truly what separates a high power player and a cEDH player.
