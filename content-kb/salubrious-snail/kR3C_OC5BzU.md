## Summary

This is a deck-building retrospective on a Mardu (Liesa, Forgotten Archangel) Commander deck built around making opponents fixate on their low life totals. The creator wanted an 'inevitability-aggro' feel: Liesa dodges command tax and drains opponents two life per spell cast, while a hatebear-heavy 99 slows the table and chips away at life. Early versions leaned too heavily into stax (Archon of Emeria, Hushbringer), which clashed with Liesa because rule-of-law effects reduce spellcasting and undercut her life-drain, plus the deck lacked card draw and felt boring. An initiative-mechanic rework added value but introduced fiddly props and cut damage. Swapping to Breena, the Demagogue as commander made it a front-loaded tempo/+1/+1-counter aggro deck that collapsed after board wipes—not the grindy inevitability experience wanted. The final fix: return to Liesa, cut heavy stax (keeping light taxes and Aven Mindcensor), and add real damage and dense draw (Ankh of Mishrah, Wound Reflection, Suture Priest, Hunted Horror, Enduring Innocence, Barrowgoyf). Result is a resilient bracket-3 group-slug aggro deck. Lesson: don't let powerful staple effects warp a deck's intended feel; experimenting with extreme command-zone choices clarifies what you actually enjoy.

## Key Clips

- **[02:08]** An aggressive gameplan can mean the ability to knock opponents out of the game early, but even before that happens, the first 20-30 damage dealt to an opponent already influences the way they play the game. It makes them play more conservatively, attack less confidently, and get substantially less comfort with the lifepay tools that are such a defining feature of commander.
- **[05:29]** How about in EDH, though? Hatebears are still useful for their effects, but the attached 2/2 or 3/3 body typically isn't nearly as helpful. This is where the command zone comes into play—my hatebears can whittle down life totals a little and generally gum up the works of the game, and then Liesa is my followup that can really start to cut down life totals in this slowed environment.
- **[06:07]** In retrospect the biggest one is that it felt more stax than it did Liesa—my opponents were being hindered, but more so by an Archon of Emeria than by their painfully low life total.
- **[07:32]** A commander deck can easily be this pretty compact, self-contained thing, and here I'd introduced an extra mechanic that required me to use this full sheet of paper prop, or else buy a replacement that's irritatingly small? It just felt like a pointless extra hassle to need to deal with, particularly given that my deck only ran 5 initiative cards and didn't draw any of them in some games.
- **[14:31]** The other reason for this choice was that stax in general, and rule-of-law effects in particular, have this weird clash with Liesa because they cause opponents to cast fewer spells, but Liesa wants opponents to be casting more spells, or at least wants the fact that spells cost 2 life to feel relevant.
- **[15:06]** When I'm making a deck to bask in the glory of having 20 lands in play, I don't want to run a bunch of high-quality finishers that will end the game as soon as I'm at 12 or 13. The same goes here—this is a deck intended to celebrate Liesa in specific and group-slug-aggro in general, and though a bit of stax can be nice to manage the pace of a game, it's a powerful tool whose ability to warp the playstyle of a deck needs to be respected.
- **[16:33]** Another way I honed my intentions here was being willing to accept a firmly bracket 3 status and adapt my playstyle to be more appropriate to that. The deck still punches above its weight class against decks that lean in a combo or storm sort of direction, but it's no longer the lumbering pile of stax that it was before.
- **[17:24]** Even if it's not meant to be long term, as it wasn't in this case, playing with more extreme command zone choices can be a great exercise in figuring out what you do or don't enjoy about your deck, and it's absolutely worth a try if you have a deck you're not happy with!
