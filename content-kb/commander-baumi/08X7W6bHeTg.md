## Key Clips

- The Word Shaper precon is a Jund deck centered on sacrificing lands for value ΓÇö not so much recurring lands, but sacrificing them and playing new ones through additional land drops. The commander, Halfhold the World Seed, is a four-mana spacecraft: once stationed for two, tap it by paying one mana and sacrificing a land to draw two cards and play an additional land this turn.
- Station 8 turns Halfhold into a 6/7 with flying, vigilance, and haste, and from then on whenever you sacrifice a land each opponent loses two life. With a Zuran Orb or similar repeatable sacrifice effect you can drain the table for 20-30 life at once, then reanimate the lands and do it again. The deck doesn't run Zuran Orb though ΓÇö it focuses on repeated individual land sacrifices that each generate value, keeping it slow and grindy, which is what bracket 2 should be.
- The biggest problem precons had for a long time is running out of steam too easily, folding to a single sweeper because they have no cards left in hand. World Shaper is filled with card advantage ΓÇö the commander draws, and new cards like Exploration Brew Ship, Umidian Wastewaker, and the vendor impulse-draw effects all generate cards. This deck can play through disruption and sustain itself in a long game.
- The most important thing about World Shaper is that it plays 42 lands. The common knowledge of 36-38 lands for a commander deck is objectively wrong. Missing a land drop is a devastating disadvantage ΓÇö every missed land drop is a Rampant Growth undone, an Arcane Signet unplayed. Commander is more often than not decided by who has the most mana.
- 40 lands should be the minimum. Frank Karsten's updated land-count article advises starting with 42 lands plus a Sol Ring, then cutting a land for every two or three additional mana rocks you add. Sam Black has said 40 is a good minimum. Any reduction below 40 needs a very strong justification, like infinite card draw or playing cEDH.
- To fix the frustration of drawing too many lands: cycle lands, MDFCs, and retrace spells turn lands into playable cards, while discard-as-a-cost and looting make them resources even in hand. First advice for new players ΓÇö go up to 40 lands in your precon, add a bit of card draw, and find ways to turn useless cards in hand into playable ones. This makes the deck much more resilient.
- The second precon, Counter Intelligence, is a Jeskai artifact deck centered on energy and charge counters that also plays 40 lands. The commander, In Spirit (flagship vessel), is a three-mana spacecraft with an easy station one that hands out a +1/+1 counter or two charge counters at the beginning of combat. Astral Cornucopia, Empowered Auto-Generator, and Everflowing Chalice produce tons of mana that feeds engine pieces, and eventually Lux Artillery drains the table for 10 every turn.
- At Command Fest, people were over-evaluating their decks constantly within the bracket system ΓÇö calling unmodified precons bracket 3, when they weren't powerful enough. Players relied a lot on powerful cards rather than synergies. The best games were ones where people just agreed up front: no, we're playing bracket 2.
