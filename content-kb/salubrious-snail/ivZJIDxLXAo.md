## Key Clips

- A pet card is generally thought of as cards that players have some sort of fondness toward, but pet cards can be pet cards for a variety of different reasonsΓÇöplayers might enjoy the art or flavor, they might find the card's effect cool and unique, and they also might just really enjoy what the card has to offer for their deck.
- For pet cards that are either generally bad, or so hyper-specific that it's difficult to find a deck where they truly feel at home, one good option is to build a deck with the understanding that it's here to fulfill the most primal of pet card desires. If it's a card you like for aesthetics, build a deck that fits that aesthetic. The deck likely won't be great, but that's fine because it's not here to be great.
- On the opposite end of the spectrum are the pet cards that are just good, or at least flexible enough to never feel badΓÇölike Dawn Charm, Swan Song, and Kambal, Consul of Allocation. You can put them in a deck of their colors and they will be almost guaranteed to feel at least decent in a casual EDH game.
- For a moderately potent card with a somewhat specific use case, Option A is to take one or two pet cards and build a specific deck for them. These sorts of cards won't reach their full potential if you're playing them in a deck they only tangentially connect to, and at worst you might gradually grow a resentment for them. Building a deck that a pet card fully belongs in is the greatest act of love you can show your favorite piece of cardboard.
- The other option for utilizing niche, moderately powerful pet cards is to put a bunch in one deck that is well-equipped to handle a number of different types of effects. One of these is midrange decks with simple goals and a solid core. If you want a pithy rule of thumb, the more generic a deck's general game plan is, the more niche and varied the specific card choices can be.
- Specific themes also lend themselves to running more niche cardsΓÇöproliferate, tokens, graveyard stuff, and synergies with a specific card type such as artifacts or enchantments. The key is that you need to figure out a solid core to the deckΓÇömake sure it has a way to get off the ground, usually by having enough lands, ramp, and draw, and then you can start to sprinkle in your spicier stuff.
- It's good to make sure you don't forget to run interactionΓÇöprotection for vulnerable elements of the deck and removal to make sure opponents don't run away with the game. How well-rounded a funny deck is may determine whether it ends up being a deck you keep wanting to play with, or whether it ends up getting set aside as that gimmick deck that was fun for a while.
