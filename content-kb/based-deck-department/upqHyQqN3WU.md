## Summary

Core thesis: ramp must serve a specific game plan, not autopilot. Ask what you're ramping toward, by which turn, and whether the deck needs ramp at all. Don't be greedy on speed or payoff. Target ~45-50 mana sources total; if running ramp, run ~12 sources (paired with 36-38 lands) to reliably see ramp by turn three. Ramp is binary: want it every game or not at all. Match ramp type to strategy: aggro/combo decks favor rituals/treasures (Seething Song, Unexpected Windfall) for one-turn bursts; creature-value decks need repeatable sources. Case study Pantlaza (bracket 3): instead of forcing turn-3 commander via 20 ramp cards and one-mana dorks, use explosive ramp (Bloom Tender, Fanatic of Rhonas, Selvala Heart of the Wilds) for turn-4 commander plus big turn-5 plays, freeing slots for haste/four-drops (Sneak Attack, Defense of the Heart). Sol Talisman preferred as sticky middle ground over Cultivate or Mana Geyser. Atla Palani avoids mana dorks (pollutes flip trigger), uses rocks. Marwyn wants sacrificial dorks for experience counters. Some decks (aggro Vincent) run zero ramp on purpose. Every inclusion forces a cut; bracket sets game length and total available mana.

## Key Clips

- **[01:23]** Turn your brain's autopilot off. Don't run all the same ramp sources in every single deck. Some decks want Birds of Paradise, some want Three Visits, some want Cultivate, and some want absolutely none at all. A deck running a three-cost Commander with nothing it wants to ramp towards shouldn't run a spell like Cultivate.
- **[02:27]** The average commander deck should run around 45 to 50 mana sources regardless of type or bracket. An elves deck running 30 lands also runs 15 or more mana-producing elves; a high-power cEDH deck runs free mana generation to similar effect. 90% of the time your deck is not the exception.
- **[03:25]** You only want ramp if it helps execute your game plan and play key cards earlier than if you simply hit your land drops. If you're playing multiple six-mana spells on turn eight, mathematically you need four more mana even hitting all your land drops, so you must have ramp. Ramp is maths, not vibes.
- **[08:28]** Running 20 ramp cards to reliably get Pantlaza out on turn three means less space for the other good stuff. You get Pantlaza out early but your hand has excess ramp or lands and no dinosaurs to spend the mana on — speed greed instead of good-stuff greed.
- **[08:58]** Rather than turn-three Pantlaza, cast explosive ramp like Bloom Tender, Fanatic of Rhonas, or Selvala to get a turn-four Pantlaza and untap with six or seven mana on turn five for a big dinosaur. This reclaims deck slots for haste enablers and four-mana cards like Sneak Attack or Defense of the Heart.
- **[11:52]** For Atla Palani I run mana rocks, not dorks, because her trigger looks for creatures in your deck when an egg dies — I only want to flip into the biggest dinos, so any mana dork would ruin the plan. In contrast, Marwyn wants one-to-two-cost dorks precisely so they die in the mid game and farm experience counters.
- **[13:18]** How much ramp is more mathematical: the answer is about 12. If you're ramping you want it by about turn three, and 12 sources reliably provide a ramp card in your first three turns. Ramp is binary — you either need it every time or not at all. Twelve ramp plus 36 to 38 lands puts you in the 40-to-50 mana-source band.
