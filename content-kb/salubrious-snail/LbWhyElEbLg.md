## Summary

This video argues against bolting an infinite combo onto a commander whose deck has a different primary gameplan. Building a combo deck where the commander is a core combo piece is fineΓÇöit compacts the combo and frees deck space. The problem is tacking a far more powerful combo onto an otherwise weaker synergy or midrange deck. Two issues result. First, the vibes issue: a deck that mostly does weak things but occasionally pivots to a wildly powerful line creates table whiplash and breaks opponents' ability to read and strategize against it. Second, the tangible issue: what your commander can do shapes how opponents treat it. Examples from EDHRecΓÇöZaxara with Freed from the Real (46%), Tivit with Time Sieve (72% overall, 63% of voting decks), and Atraxa with poison countersΓÇöshow that adding a combo makes the commander a priority removal or counter target, punishing the fair builds that lack the combo in hand. The author contrasts this with his own Radha and Malcolm decks, where roles either don't conflict or he deliberately builds counterspell protection around the known threat. The takeaway: know what kind of Magic you want, and calibrate expectations accordingly.

## Key Clips

- There's a specific deckbuilding choice I've noticed... when a player chooses to take their lovely synergy engine commander and turn it into a combo piece.
- If that combo is the deck's primary gameplan, that's actually a great way to build a deckΓÇöutilizing the commander in a combo generally makes that combo more compact, which gives additional deck space for interaction, support cards, and alternative gameplans.
- Where things become a problem is when the combo in question is one that is out of place and far more powerful than the deck's normal gameplan.
- According to EDHRec, 46% of Zaxara decks run Freed from the Real, a card that can produce infinite mana with Zaxara's ramp ability... If I know my opponent is among those 46%... that Zaxara immediately becomes a top priority for removal.
- What you do with your commander will inform how your opponents react to that commander.
- 63% of voting-oriented Tivit decks run Time Sieve. Statistically speaking, no shot that's resolving... a player could just be looking to do fun voting things, but they decided that grand experiment in democracy also needed a one-card infinite combo.
- The second you throw some poison counters on your opponents, the commander immediately reads 'each turn I get a poison counter' for all of your opponents, and their removal choices will reflect that... poison counter proliferation is about as visible and visceral as setup gets.
- In my strongest deck, Malcolm is both a combo piece and a mana engine... I account for this by running a massive pile of counterspells that I can cast using the treasures Malcolm generatesΓÇöI know exactly how my opponents are going to be feeling, and I'm prepared to say no if they send their removal spells in the most logical direction.
