## Summary

The creator argues that powerful Commander cards are often better played in the 99 of a deck than in the command zone. Many "must-kill" commanders—Euro, Magda, Siri, Slicer, Alexios—dominate or warp games and feel like pubstomping; demoting them to the 99 keeps their flashy payoff while letting opponents kill them once and move on, fitting casual tables better. The pattern also unlocks new strengths: value engines like Tatyova, Aesi, and Kodama of the East Tree double land drops in any Simic-style shell, while Cruel Claw works as an early aggressive value creature even outside a big-mana build. Self-contained engines such as Alivia (makes her own enchantments) and Minsc & Boo (fragile planeswalker, safer in the 99) replace themselves and avoid being obvious targets. Indestructible Theros gods (Heliod, Klothys, Karametra) and resilient creatures like Norin barely need the command zone, freeing it for another card. Partner pairs (Frodo/Sam) gain effective card draw, and backgrounds can slot into any matching deck. The payoff: dynamic decks that shift strategy when these cards appear, then revert when removed, adding gameplay variety.

## Key Clips

- Sometimes commanders seem cool on paper, but their play pattern doesn't work out as nicely in practice. To help me sleep at night, I started playing those commanders in the 99s of other decks. And while doing so, I quickly came to the conclusion that that's a really good strategy.
- Slicer hired muscle and Alexios, Daimos of Cosmos share the same concept. They are powerful attackers that can't be sacrificed and are either goaded or can't attack their owner. On every player's turn, that player is given control over these creatures and then they have to attack with them. This lets Slicer and Alexios attack four times per turn rotation, dealing 24 damage per turn rotation.
- When played as commanders, must-kill threats like Slicer and Alexios completely dominate and shift the play experience. But in the 99, they come in, change the pace for a bit, deal a bunch of damage, and then just peace out. Our opponents now actually have an opportunity to deal with them and then the game can move along.
- In general, I try to avoid playing must-kill commanders. Commanders like Yuriko the tiger's shadow, Magda Brazen Outlaw, or Zur the enchanter have effects so overwhelming that it becomes kill or be killed. The solution: put Yuriko into the 99 of a ninja deck. We still get to do the powerful flashy thing, but our opponents now actually have an opportunity to deal with her.
- Kodama of the East Tree is a value engine that doesn't need a deck centered around it. Every time a permanent enters the battlefield under our control, it lets us put one more into play with the same or lesser mana cost. It turns all of our land drops into two, especially when combined with Azusa and Tatyova.
- Self-contained engines work particularly well in the 99. Elivia of the Wild Court provides any board with power and replaces herself immediately through card draw. She herself provides the enchantments she needs to benefit from her card draw ability, so an enchantress shell is unnecessary.
- Planeswalker commanders like Minsc and Boo, Timeless Heroes can be difficult to make work because they are such obvious and easy targets, so don't play them as your commander. They become even better when treated simply as an engine in a deck that's centered around something less fragile.
- Resilient, hard-to-remove permanents like the indestructible Theros gods, Norin the Wary, or Ojer Taq are cast just a single time and don't benefit much from the command zone. That invulnerability goes both ways: they work incredibly well in the 99 because they don't need the command zone, freeing us up to play a different card. Partner pairs like Frodo and Sam staple a card draw onto each other when played in the 99, and backgrounds can be played in any deck that benefits without being in the command zone.
