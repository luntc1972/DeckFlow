## Summary

Day six of the host's "base deck diaries" argues that Commander players don't think about play patterns enough, and that many game-warping situations sit outside what the bracket system can manage. Two examples: a Voltron deck that kills on turn four is not automatically a "bracket four" deck, and an "oops all removal but no win conditions" deck that can't win before turn ten is not a "bracket one" deck. The host frames these as play-pattern/experience mismatches rather than power-level (bracket) mismatches, and calls aligning a group's untold expectations one of the most frustrating parts of Commander. His single practical tip: have better pre-game conversations. Stating up front that your deck aims to kill by turn three or four, or that it is all removal, lets opponents opt in, opt out, or pick a deck better suited to that game. He stresses the bracket system is a great communication tool but only a communication tool; to actually get the games you want, you have to talk it out beforehand. No specific card names or concrete deckbuilding includes/cuts are given.
