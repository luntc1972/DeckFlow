## Summary

This tutorial explains the "8x8 theory" (Cube Theory) framework for building Commander decks efficiently. The math: 35 lands plus 1 commander equals 36 cards, leaving 64 cards, which equals eight themes of eight cards each. The eight packages are: ramp (Mana acceleration), removal (remove other permanents), draw engines (more fuel), and protection (guard your commander)ΓÇöthese four form the foundation. The remaining four are two flexible Synergy theme packages tailored to your commander's strategy, a support package (catch-all cards augmenting your synergy themes, like recursion or tutors), and a Staples package of powerful on-color cards too good to ignore (including off-color picks like Roaming Throne). The presenter recommends using EDHREC tags to identify a commander's multiple themesΓÇöe.g., Belbe shows enchantress, vehicles, artifacts, and aggroΓÇöthen applying one direction down the package template. Crucially, the packages you run must fit your commander's game plan: skip ramp for low-Mana-value commanders like Phelia, or replace the draw package for commanders with built-in card draw like Tishana. The framework isn't a strict rule but a fast starting point for getting card variety at the right frequency.

## Key Clips

- The 8 by 8 theory is a mathematical way to build the commander deck. Simply put, 35 lands and a commander equals 36 cards. This leaves us with 64 cards remaining, and 8 times 8 is 64. This means all we need to do is pick eight themes each consisting of eight cards and we'll have a deck.
- Here is a very easy template of eight packages that I use to build any commander deck. First we have ramp, which are cards that accelerate your mana. Then we have removal, cards that remove other permanents so you don't lose the game. Then we have draw engines that give you more fuel to keep playing. Then we have protection cards that momentarily or permanently protect your commander. This is your foundation and half of our eight packages.
- We have two flexible packages for synergistic themes. Commanders these days work on multiple axes, so this lets you create multiple theme-based packages of eight cards each. So let's call it Synergy theme 1 and Synergy theme 2.
- For our seventh package, this is what I call support. Support is the catch-all cards that support your synergistic themes above. For example for Lathril I can have eight elf cards and eight tokens-matter cards, then for support I can add eight cards that augment either of these strategies ΓÇö creature or card recursion to bring back elf lords, or tutors to find my Doubling Season.
- For our last package, this is just the good cards, which are on-color staples that are too powerful to ignore. This also includes stuff like Roaming Throne which isn't on color but you get the idea.
- Let's take Bello, Bard of the Brambles. EDHREC shows us the tags for Bello include enchantress, vehicles, artifacts, unnatural and aggro. The easy way to apply Cube theory is to take a direction from one of these themes then apply it down the packages. We can build an enchantress deck with draw cards that synergize with enchantments, enchantment-based removal, enchantment protection, then for Synergy themes 1 and 2 it can be strong value enchantments and big mana.
- The important part, and this is key, is making sure the packages you're running fit your commander's game plan. For example, a low mana value commander like Finn maybe a ramp package doesn't make sense, or a commander with built-in draw effects like Tishana you can replace the draw package with an extra theme.
- The cube theory isn't a strict rule you must follow but it's a great framework to get started with to quickly assign the right amount of cards in your deck to get a good variety of cards at the right frequency. Applying themes over the cube theory will help you build decks faster and better.
