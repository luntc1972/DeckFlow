## Summary

Joey (EDHRECast) explains how to deliberately de-tune/deoptimize a Commander deck without making it garbage. Step 1: identify your motivation (pet cards, more variance, dropping a bracket). Step 2 'powerhouse pass': cut game changers, two-card infinite combos, free spells, fast mana, and tutors—swap tutors for more draw spells. Step 3: interrogate win conditions that always win the same way—e.g. Craterhoof Behemoth, Blightsteel Colossus, Torment of Hailfire, Walking Ballista (in his The Mimeoplasm deck), Syr Konrad (?); swap for less reliable wincons with obvious hurdles like Champions from Beyond (?) over Moonshaker Cavalry, keeping the same victory mode. Step 4 'be irrational': cut technically perfect cards that bring no joy (Palantir of Orthanc, a rogue he dislikes). Step 5: don't slash removal—he argues most decks need ~3 more removal and lands; cut only lockout pieces (Constant Mists, Rest in Peace) and prefer conditional removal (Dispatch, Extinction Event, Tragic Slip). Step 6: keep a strong mana base; only trim high-impact utility lands (cut Kor Haven, keep Mosswort Bridge, not Otawara). Step 7: reduce consistency-enabler redundancies (mana dorks, protection, haste providers, lands-recursion). Secret step: be prepared to lose more.

## Key Clips

- **[05:54]** Fast mana in your deck is a huge signal that you are trying to operate a turn ahead of your opponents, so that's a pretty easy thing to pull out to make the deck pace itself more appropriately.
- **[06:32]** If your deck has a bunch of tutors already, consider swapping them out for more draw spells. That will help you dig a bit more and maybe you'll find what you need that way instead.
- **[11:07]** Crater Hoof Behemoth, Blightsteel Colossus, Torment of Hailfire—these are the cards you could remove to defang your deck. They're so powerful at what they do that they rarely miss.
- **[12:08]** In my Mimeoplasm deck, one of the best targets for my commander to copy is Walking Ballista because I can instantly deal tons of damage to the table. If I were to de-tune that deck, that's the first card I'd cut.
- **[13:51]** If Moonshaker Cavalry basically always does the job and you want a lighter pace, swap it for another anthem like Champions from Beyond (?), which needs a threshold of creatures to provide its pump at all—giving opponents an identifiable way to stop you.
- **[14:52]** Identify the cards that are technically precise, perfect cards for the deck but which bring you absolutely no joy. They feel like they fulfill a mechanical obligation rather than making you excited—those are the ones to cut.
- **[19:40]** I actually sometimes recommend bringing your interaction count UP even when tuning a deck down. The average deck could stand to play about three more lands and three more removal spells—not ten more.
- **[20:55]** Scaling down my Baracos deck, I don't just cut Deadly Roll—I replace it with Dispatch, a removal spell that only works if I've made and kept treasures. I've gone from a best-in-class card to a situational one, which adds texture.
