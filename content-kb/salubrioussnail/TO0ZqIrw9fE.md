## Summary

This video reviews the Outlaws at Thunder Junction precon "Grand Larceny," led by Gonti, Canny Inquisitor, who reduces stolen-spell costs and steals an opponent's top card on combat damage. The reviewer asks what the deck does, how well, and how to improve it. Gonti is a soft payoff capping at 3 steals per turn, so the deck wants evasive creatures hitting opponents early plus a modest theft suite. Strengths: a solid ~$30 manabase (temples, painlands, check lands), decent removal, ramp, and value engines. Weaknesses: the deck splits between evasive beatdown and theft without cohesion, runs only 12 evasive creatures under 5 mana (too few for consistent triggers), includes flavor-driven theft cards with no mechanical synergy, and curves out poorly in 15 of 30 playtests. High-cost counterspells waste mana since the deck lacks instant-speed plays. The reviewer chooses to lean into combat damage, cutting weak theft cards (Chaos Wand, Villainous Wealth, Cunning Rhetoric, Stolen Goods) and adding low-cost evasive utility beaters (Malcolm, Looter Il-Kor, Suspicious Stowaway, Siren Stormtamer, Drana, Rankle, Kellan) plus minor manabase tweaks and added islands. Verdict: an unfocused but decently built precon that plays best as an evasive-creatures deck.

## Key Clips

- Gonti, Canny Inquisitor reduces the cost of your stolen spells and steals the top card of an opponent's library whenever you deal combat damage to them. For an extra mana and color over Ramirez DePietro, Gonti isn't restricted to pirates, can play opposing lands, and can spend mana of any type on stolen spells.
- Conditioning theft on dealing combat damage acts like bumpers on a bowling alley: it prevents the classic blunder of forgetting a win plan, since the unblocked creatures needed to do the theft are also the cards that win games.
- Gonti is a soft payoff. Cards stolen per turn caps at 3 and the cost discount on stolen spells is only 1 mana, so for a five-mana commander you need to be maxing advantage early ΓÇö meaning a good count of cheap evasive creatures to start thieving soon after Gonti hits, plus a modest number of other steal effects.
- The deck has 18 creatures with flying or hard evasion, but only 12 evasive creatures below 5 mana ΓÇö not a reliable count for such a pivotal card type. The deck wants three Gonti triggers a turn and the current selection won't consistently deliver that.
- The manabase is pretty good: colorless utility lands capped at 2, plus sets of temples, painlands, check lands, filters and two good filtering lands. WotC chose to give the precon a $30 manabase rather than one or two chase cards ΓÇö new players need good lands more than they need a Trouble in Pairs.
- Across 30 playtests the deck had some good curve-outs but in 15 of 30 games failed to launch a gameplan by turn 5-6 ΓÇö failing to spend mana on turns 2-4, playing low-impact cards, having disjointed card types, or not gaining enough mana. Many rollouts felt disjointed and awkward.
- High-cost counterspells with bonuses mostly work in draw-go control decks that play card draw at instant speed; this deck runs little at instant speed, so holding up 4-5 mana wastes it if you don't counter. Cut symbol-dense Plasm Capture for a cheaper counter like Negate; keep Smirking Spelljacker since it's also a 3/3 flyer.
- The recommended fix is to lean into combat damage over big theft: add low-cost evasive utility beaters like Malcolm, Looter il-Kor, Suspicious Stowaway, Siren Stormtamer, and Thousand-Faced Shadow, plus Drana, Rankle, and Kellan, while cutting unsynergistic theft cards (Doc Aurlock, Oblivion Sower, Villainous Wealth, Chaos Wand, Extract Brain) that don't help get Gonti triggers.
