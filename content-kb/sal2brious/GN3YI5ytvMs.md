## Key Clips

- Astral Slide fundamentally demands quite a lot out of your deck in order to be worth running over another flicker engine. I've consistently kept a high 30s count of cycling cards in my Astral Slide deck. Meanwhile, with less than half that number of cycling cards here, I think Astral Slide is going to be giving fewer triggers than a Teleportation Circle, and with significantly more mana spent to get there. Discrete engines like Soulherder, Teleportation Circle, and Thassa, Deep-Dwelling seem like much more reliable options for flickers.
- Treasures are uniquely potent in a couple of ways. They're artifacts, and given the already high count of mana rocks, that could be a neat synergy package. Another, probably more relevant feature is that they enable instant speed plays well, since they're stored mana that's never wasted. Replacing ETB removal creatures with flash options such as Ertai Resurrected or Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet, seems like it could keep the flicker synergies while allowing the deck to be a bit more spry.
- On the Tormod/Dargo deck: why is Dargo here? The deck runs very few red sources, and the only red card in the 99 is Squee. There are multiple black partners that seem like they could be really helpful if you were willing to cut red. Tevesh Szat packs a ton of utility in a token deck like this. Getting more use out of the command zone is a great first step toward powering this deck up and making it more reliable.
- A core aspect of control is the two decisions of what threats are worth targeting down, and when to deploy a big game-warping card like a board wipe. A big piece of them is trying to get away with not removing things for as long as possible in order to spend more time building resources and hit more things with an eventual board wipe. A core pillar of this Kykar deck is waiting as long as possible to cast the big reset card, so that as many cards as possible can be set up to assist with winning afterward.
- This toolbox artifact deck strikes me as a bit unfocused. It's running cards I would expect from a dedicated aggro deck and cards I would expect from a dedicated control deck. Cards like Sir Ginger and Mendicant Core don't have much of a place in a control deck. However, there are also cards like Filigree Angel, Salvaging Station, Vedalken Orrery, and Gilded Lotus that you really wouldn't want to draw in any halfway aggressive deck. Toolbox as a strategy naturally leans toward control.
- Combining damage amplifier demons with a controlling Marvo plan seems a little tricky, because the base level of damage being put out just isn't that high. Marvo is a one power creature. If Ancient Silver Spire plays are what you really enjoy, you have two options. Option one: make this a bracket three deck, which is honestly where the card realistically belongs. Option two: build a bracket four deck anyway, and slow down the pace of the game with more counterspells and stax like God-Pharaoh's Statue, Ward of Bones, Arcane Laboratory, or Jin-Gitaxias.
- On the Extus control deck: there are way too many defensive cards here, a whole 19. Defensive cards are delaying tools above all else, but it's unclear what this deck with a ton of delaying tools is delaying until. I'd start by replacing some of the defensive cards with card draw. This will make the deck more fluid to play, and replacing a plentiful type of card with more draw usually feels okay since draw ups your chances of finding that thing.
- On the Karlach aggro deck drawing too much heat: in principle this issue should be somewhat mitigated by the sheer brutality of the game plan. I probably wouldn't start going all in until I thought I had a solid chance of knocking a player out that turn, and if you start swinging too early, you could do a bunch of scary damage without meaningfully reducing your opponent's ability to gang up on you. More haste enablers like Hall of the Bandit Lord, Hanweir Battlements, and Lightning Greaves feel like things you'd basically always want.
