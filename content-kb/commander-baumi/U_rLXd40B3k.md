## Key Clips

- The three Titans of Pharaoh — Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath; Kroxa, Titan of Death's Hunger; and Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury — each follow the same core mechanics. They are 6/6 Elder Giants for a fairly low casting cost with a powerful effect whenever they enter the battlefield or attack. If they enter normally they immediately sacrifice themselves, so to keep one on the board they have to escape from the graveyard by exiling five other cards and paying colored mana.
- The presenter disassembled Kroxa and Phlage despite their power: Kroxa nuked opponents' hands so they never had cards to do anything, and Phlage's presence in the command zone prevented any commanders with three or less health from ever hitting the field. Both are very strong but don't make for interesting games.
- Uro focuses on our own progression rather than suppressing opponents. When he enters or attacks we gain three life, draw a card, and may put a land from hand into play. Think of his first cast as having a Growth Spiral in the command zone, adding huge consistency. Early game: mana dork turn one, Uro turn two, then five mana on turn three.
- Incorporate a small graveyard-filling shell with cards like Winding Way, Mulch, and fetchlands to feed Uro's escape. Affordable fetches — Evolving Wilds, Terramorphic Expanse, the new Capenna cycle, or the tap-fetchland cycle — all help. Since we always replay him from the graveyard, Uro always costs only four mana and five cards regardless of commander tax. Protect against exile removal with counterspells and hexproof equipment like Swiftfoot Boots and Lightning Greaves, which also grant haste to trigger both ETB and attack in one turn.
- Niv-Mizzet Reborn is a 6/6 Dragon Avatar with flying that costs WUBRG. When he enters, look at the top ten cards of your deck and for each color pairing put a card from among them into hand, easily drawing four to five cards. Because the card draw stems from an ETB, getting him removed is actually the good outcome — you just cast him again and use his ETB again.
- Niv-Mizzet requires WUBRG, but you can play it on a budget using gates. The Battle for Baldur's Gate cycle, Plaza of Heroes-style fixing, Gond Gate (untapped five-color land that makes all other gates enter untapped), and Baldur's Gate (two mana, then a mana for each other gate — going mana-positive at five gates) build a spectacular budget five-color mana base. Include Open the Gates, Gate Creeper Vine, District Guide, Sephuro's Route, and Crop Rotation to find them.
- Rasaad — actually Rsa/Krydle-style — Raphael... Rasaad ra Kingpin is a four-mana Human Rogue with deathtouch that gives the initiative when she enters. The initiative explores a dungeon; the final room lets you dig the top ten cards and put a creature into play with three +1/+1 counters and hexproof. Like the monarchy, the initiative is stolen by dealing combat damage, so stay low to the ground with creatures and interaction. Whenever you attack she gives an attacker deathtouch, and if you completed a dungeon, +5/+0 and first strike.
- The deckbuilding thesis: avoid the loop of playing a commander only to have it die repeatedly. Build with resilient or non-essential commanders — one that recurs itself (Uro), one that is only an ETB (Niv-Mizzet), and one that can wait until the late game (Rasaad). This style makes for much more consistent decks.
