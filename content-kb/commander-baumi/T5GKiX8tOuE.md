## Summary

This video presents five escalating tiers of white mana rampΓÇö"mana fraud"ΓÇöas an alternative to green ramp or mana rocks (which the host dislikes for vulnerability to Farewell). Level one is catch-up ramp: cards like Loyal Warhound and Knight of the White Orchid that put a land into play when opponents have more lands; delaying your land drop can force a trigger even without a green player. Level two uses bounce lands (Celestial Sanctuary, two-color and colorless ones like Arid Archway) to keep land count low so catch-up ramp stays live; Arid Archway is a Desert, enabling Sand Scout synergy. Level three uses Lotus lands (Lotus Vale, Lotus Field, Scorched Ruins) that sacrifice two lands, slashing land count to power Claim Jumper, Surveyor's Scope, Keeper of the Accord, and Aaron's map. Level four reanimates sacrificed lands with Broad Back, Sun Titan, and fetch lands for explosive turns rivaling green. Level five nukes the whole mana base (Zuran Orb, Aura Fracture) plus mass reanimation (Faith's Reward, Second Sunrise), even enabling an infinite Codex Shredder loop the host disavows. His thesis: every color can land ramp, and white excels.

## Key Clips

- Level one Mana fraud is catchup ramp: cards like loyal warhound or Knight of the white orchid enter, check if opponents have more lands than us, and if they do we ramp a land directly into play. Loyal warhound is best seen as a free-mana creature ΓÇö if we aren't the starting player, take a free-mana turn, tap two lands, play the warhound to ramp out the land, then play our land for turn, delaying our natural land drop so opponents have more lands than us.
- Level two is bounce lands like Celestial Sanctuary that tap for two but return a land to hand on entry ΓÇö this keeps our land count low so catch-up ramp stays effective later. Mono-white can't run the two-color cycle, but Kabira (returns a Plains) works, and the colorless bounce lands Guildless Commons and especially Arid Archway fit any deck. Arid Archway is a Desert, enabling synergies like Sand Scout, a two-mana catchup creature that searches a Desert while keeping land count low.
- Level three is Lotus lands ΓÇö Lotus Vale, Lotus Field, and Scorched Ruins enter and require sacrificing two other lands, then tap for three or four mana. They slash total land count, supercharging catch-up ramp: play Lotus Vale, sacrifice two Plains, tap it for a Claim Jumper that sees opponents far ahead and ramps two lands from your deck. Lotus Field has hexproof but enters tapped; the best payoff is Surveyor's Scope, which exiles to search for X basic lands where X is the number of players with at least two more lands than you.
- Keeper of the Accord triggers at the end of every opponent's turn, so a decimated land count means ramping free lands every turn. Archaeomancer's Map searches two basics to hand on entry, then whenever an opponent with more lands plays a land you put a land from hand into play ΓÇö and since you're always nuking your mana base you always qualify. Bounce lands and cards like Tithe and Land Tax keep your hand stocked to feed the Map.
- Level four resurrects sacrificed lands. Play Lotus Field on turn three, tap two lands first, then cast Brought Back (instant, two mana, returns up to two permanents put into the graveyard from the battlefield this turn) to return the two lands you sacrificed ΓÇö a six-mana turn on turn four. The dream line: tap two basics, play Lotus Field sacrificing them, play Surveyor's Scope, search three basics into play untapped, then Brought Back the two sacrificed lands for eight mana. Sun Titan, Sevinne's Reclamation, and Renewed Faith reanimate low-value permanents, and fetch lands like Krosan Verge feed the graveyard.
- Level five nukes your entire mana base with Zuran Orb, Renounce, or Aura Fracture. Tap all lands for mana, ideally with Gauntlet of Power, sacrifice every land, play catch-up ramp like Claim Jumpers and loyal warhounds, sacrifice those too, then cast mass reanimation ΓÇö Faith's Reward or Second Sunrise ΓÇö to return all permanents that died this turn untapped and tap the lands again. With a Codex Shredder and Faith's Reward you can loop for infinite mana, but it's a non-interactive solitaire combo the speaker warns against playing at the table.
- Overall thesis: white is astonishingly good at getting lands into play and its ramp at level four is genuinely on par with green ΓÇö slightly less consistent but it also puts big creatures like Sun Titan into play. Every color can land-ramp, not just green; white and blue are both excellent. The speaker plays a mono-white Aesi/landfall list using sacrifice-and-reanimate to trigger landfall en masse, and warns that relying on Arcane Signet over land ramp leaves you exposed to a Cleansing wiping your mana base.
