## Summary

A deck-review YouTuber tallies the five most common EDH/Commander deckbuilding mistakes from two years of reviews. (#5) Macro-level unfocus: building around a synergy pillar (landfall, lifegain, counters) but adding payoffs shooting in too many directions; fix with restraint and specificity, excluding cards that only thinly synergize. (#4) Vulnerability to hate: glass-cannon or fragile gameplans; mitigate durability via dual-purpose protection like counterspells and flicker, haste, instant-speed enablers (Leyline of Anticipation), and ramp rather than dead protection spells. (#3) Not proactive enough: control/goad/theft decks over-invest in managing opponents and lack their own wincon; goad and theft are defensive side-benefits, not reliable win plans, so categorize cards by actual role. (#2) Not reactive enough: under-investing in interaction; ~20 pieces is typical, but ruthlessly proactive fast decks can run fewer. Match interaction type to deck speed—cheap 1-for-1s for fast decks, grindier or synergy-tied removal for slow ones. (#1) Micro-level unfocus / dead cards: thin synergies and amplifier cards (token doublers, Fiery Emancipation) that do nothing unless something's already happening; run fewer pure amplifiers when the enabling state is fragile, more flexible enablers.

## Key Clips

- Mistake #5: Your deck is unfocused on the macro level. Players build around a central synergy pillar (lifegain, +1/+1 counters, self-discard) but add payoffs shooting off in every direction with no rhyme or reason. Adding a damaging card like Tunneling Geopede or lifegain like Grazing Gladehart to a landfall token-beatdown deck dilutes the gameplan. The lesson is restraint: know when to exclude a card even when it theoretically has a bit of synergy.
- Crank up the specificity. If you cruise through Scryfall on a synergy search term and add endlessly until deck space fills, you get a deck that does something. The trick is turning a soup of generic payoffs into a deck that knows what it is and where it wants to go.
- Mistake #4: Your deck is vulnerable to opposing hate. Glass-cannon decks can be powerful but easily kneecapped. The most popular fix is protection spells, but you can increase durability without them: counterspells, flicker spells like Ghostway for ETB value, protection-from as evasion, and boot equipments granting a keyword plus haste.
- On durability without protection spells: Haste prevents a creature eating dirt before doing anything. Blue players who can't run Anger or Arena of Glory can run Leyline of Anticipation or Alchemist's Refuge — saving pivotal setup plays for an opposing end step instead of your main phase dramatically increases the odds you get to use them. And ramp lets you bounce back after a setback and avoid playing synergy pieces a turn apart.
- Mistake #3: Your deck isn't proactive enough. Common with political/pillowfort or control decks that manage opponents well but spend too much space on it and never build their own gameplan. 'Goad decks' are the clearest example — a goad card's effectiveness is inversely proportional to the number of goad cards already in your deck. Treat goad as a defensive early-mid game tool, not a wincon. Pretend your goad cards are fogs; a deck with 12 fogs looks suspect.
- Categorize cards by the role they play, not the mechanic. A category of seven goad cards in Archidekt breeds subconscious bias and false comfort. Theft commanders like Admiral Beckett Brass and Gonti, Canny Acquisitor nudge you toward proactivity, but making theft the main plan and assuming wincons arrive naturally is unreliable — theft is good for finding removal, ramp, or draw, not for closing a game.
- Mistake #2: Your deck isn't reactive enough. Most of his decks run 20+ pieces of interaction, but 8, 7, or even 5 is fine for decks that are tight on space and ruthlessly proactive with a fast, coordinated plan. The reason he so often recommends more interaction: standard midrangy decks that win on an average timeline need ways to slow opponents or variance knocks them out. Faster decks want efficient 1-for-1s (Path to Exile, Nature's Claim, Bitter Triumph); slower grindier decks want card-efficient removal tied into synergy packages.
- Mistake #1 (given for 21 decks): Your deck is unfocused on the micro level — you have dead cards. Thin synergies leave payoffs feeling useless unless good on their own (e.g. God-Pharaoh's Gift needs high creature count plus graveyard filling). Amplifier cards like Fiery Emancipation, token doublers, and Traverse the Outlands require something already happening. Check how consistently and resiliently the thing you're amplifying is present; if it's fragile, show restraint with pure amplifiers and run more flexible cards that get the gameplan going.
