## Summary

This video walks through building a budget cEDH-adjacent "bracket 50" Commander deck around Omniscience. Omniscience wins the game once in play but costs 10 mana, so the deck cheats it out via Polymorph effects. The author rejects reanimation (graveyard-vulnerable to Rest in Peace) and Iluna (exiles win-cons permanently, restricts permanents) in favor of three enchantment-finding Polymorphs: Reality Scramble, Chaotic Transformation, and Reweave. The needed sacrificial enchantment is supplied by a cheap Background, Guild Artisan, paired with a primary commander. Guild Artisan generates two treasures on attack, enabling a guaranteed turn-four six mana to Polymorph into Omniscience or God-Pharaoh's Statue (backup). Volo, Itinerant Scholar is chosen over Gale for its larger body and powerful card-draw engine via Volo's Journal, synergizing with Omniscience and untappers. The deck is a value pile with multiple two-card combos (Dualcaster Mage + Electrodominance, Storm-Kiln Artist + Haze of Rage), backup payoffs (Hullbreaker Horror, Niv-Mizzet), and low-commitment win attempts. Key lessons: obsession drives testing, low-commitment win attempts (like Silence) preserve chances, winning is easier than stopping three opponents, and staying active beats hoarding combo pieces. Runs 38 lands.

## Key Clips

- The biggest lesson I took away from my obsession with Sammy is that I draw a lot of value out of my obsessions. They keep me interested in what I'm doing, and that lets me spend a lot of energy on both deck building and even more importantly, testing the deck. You cannot get a good deck if you do not test it.
- Omniscience is a blue enchantment with a fairly simple ability: we may cast spells from our hand without paying their mana cost. Once this thing is in play, we win the game. We don't need to worry about howΓÇöwe can freely pick and choose our approach. But it costs 10 mana, so casting it is out of the question; we need to cheat it into play.
- My fix was Silence, a spell that stops our opponents from casting spells for the turn. Silence let me poke for a win instead of committing. It forced my opponents to either counter the SilenceΓÇöand then I could judge their reactionsΓÇöor if they didn't counter it, then I was good to go. They are low commitment win attempts that kept my chances open when it didn't work, and could also function as interaction in emergencies.
- Polymorph effects destroy a permanent and then flip through the deck until they find a card with the same card type and put it into play. There are only three that can find an enchantment: Reality Scramble, Chaotic Transformation, and Reweave. That immediately tells us we're at least Izzet.
- Guild Artisan has our commander create two treasure tokens when it attacks the opponent with the most life. We can play Guild Artisan, then our commander, and then attack the following turn. This generates two treasures and gives us a guaranteed six mana on turn fourΓÇöenough to cast Reweave or Chaotic Transformation, or even better, cast Reality Scramble with Counterspell backup. This is a one-card combo: the other two pieces are in the command zone.
- Volo, Itinerant Scholar is a 2/3, which makes him much more likely to trade when blocked. When he enters, he creates Volo's Journal, a hexproof artifact that, whenever we cast a creature spell, lets us write down a creature type we haven't written before. Volo can then tap for two mana to draw a card for each noted type. In long matches, Volo regularly draws me five or more cards per turn, which is absurd and game-winning.
- I've chosen to include a backup plan in the form of God-Pharaoh's Statue, a six-mana artifact that makes all spells our opponents cast cost two more mana and drains them for one life at the end of our turn. We can polymorph a treasure to find it. Chaotic Transformation actually lets us polymorph multiple things, so we can find both Omniscience and God-Pharaoh's Statue all at once.
- There's a greater lesson here: it's much easier to win in Commander than it is to stop your opponents from winning. There are three opponentsΓÇöyou can't stop all of them by yourself. Focusing on your own strategy is better than trying too hard to interact. Blasting interactive pieces without explicit purpose will often lead to not being able to protect your own win.
