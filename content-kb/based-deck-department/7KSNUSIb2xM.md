## Summary

The host argues about board wipes in casual Commander. He admits he loves them, calling himself a control player whose all-time favorite deck is Psychatog, culminating in an Upheaval combo win. He says wipes are an equalizer against players who go too fast, too greedy, or run strong Ward creatures. Downsides: multiple back-to-back wipes make games run two to three times longer, cause collateral damage to slow-starting players, and produce feels-bad resets. He notes the commonly recommended 3-4 board wipes per deck tends to yield about 3-4 wipes per average game. His fixes: don't use a wipe to solve a single problem (an air strike when you need a sniper rifle); weigh collateral damage since the target may recover fastest; communicate with the table so someone else may handle the threat; cast wipes to put yourself ahead or close out the game rather than to reset or as a panic button; if a wipe only stops someone else winning, reconsider casting it. Aim for one to two necessary wipes per game. He prefers one-sided, flexible, or thematic wipes, and pseudo-wipes that tie players down or stop interaction, like Sudden Spoiling (?) clearing an attacking board.
