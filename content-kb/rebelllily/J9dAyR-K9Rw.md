## Summary

This video lays out three fundamentals for building a powerful $25 budget Commander deck using cEDH principles. First, Mana: more mana sooner enables more and higher-quality actions ahead of opponents. The pilot pairs Malcolm and Kitesa (cheap commanders) to generate up to three Treasures per swing, ramping ~3 mana per turn, especially with Guild Artisan; Treasures also fix colors, easing the budget's lack of cheap fixing lands. Second, a strong finisher: lacking budget tutors and efficient combos, the deck leans on big mana sinksΓÇöEldrazi like Bane of Bala Ged, Desolation Twin, Ulamog's Crusher, plus Aurelia's/Aali Primal StormΓÇöa Pirates Kindred subtheme (Storm Fleet Sprinter, Fathom Fleet Swordjack, Lightning-Rig Crew) for more Treasures and damage, payoffs like Toski and Leovold, and combos like Niv-Mizzet with Curiosity or Tandem Lookout. Third, interaction: Treasure mana offsets the cost of cheaper counters (Cancel, Refute, Supreme Will, Spell Burst with buyback) and bounce/removal (Divide by Zero, Bolt-style burn) since premium interaction is too pricey. Card filtering (Collector's Vault, Izzet Charm, Anticipate, Prying Eyes) digs for answers. Focusing on mana, finishers, and interaction yields a strong, exciting budget deck.

## Key Clips

- The first rule to building a powerful commander deck is focusing on our Mana. Taking a look at some of the top cEDH decks like Kinnan and Urza, we know that having more Mana sooner means you get to take more actions and take higher quality actions ahead of your opponents.
- This is why I settled on Malcolm and Tymna ΓÇö they're both cheap commanders we could get out quickly, and together the pair can generate up to three Treasures per swing, with Malcolm ramping you by three Mana per turn when paired with something like Guild Artisan.
- Good Mana fixing lands are pricey, which means it's harder to play high color decks efficiently within our budget without relying on green or dedicating more slots to fixing with ramp spells. Malcolm and Tymna also do a good job fixing any color we need with treasures.
- The second fundamental is having a strong finisher. cEDH is all about getting to the finish line with efficient combos and tutors, but at $25 we're not getting any good tutors. Our game plan is relatively simple: we play Malcolm and Tymna, swing, make mana, and try to resolve as many big threats as we can.
- This is where I just pulled out any big Mana spell within my budget, which happened to be a lot of Eldrazi like Bane of Bala Ged, Desolation Twin, and Ulamog's Crusher. You can also get an Emrakul's Primal storm for really cheap.
- We have a second game plan of just playing into the Pirates theme of the deck with Kindred synergies to use them as ways to get more Treasures, like having an unblockable Storm Fleet Sprinter, or Fathom Fleet Swordjack to deal direct damage to players equal to the number of artifacts we control.
- The third fundamental is interaction. Good interaction is expensive, especially made-for-Commander cards like Deflecting Swat or Fierce Guardianship. This is why having a commander who generates a lot of Mana helps compensate for a budget and quality problem ΓÇö we can play spells like Cancel, Refute, and Supreme Will and feel less of the higher Mana costs thanks to treasure generation.
- By focusing on Mana generation, impactful finishers, and strategic interaction, we can construct a powerful commander deck that's both exciting and strong even at such a budget.
