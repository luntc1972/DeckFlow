## Summary

This video answers how many lands a Commander deck should run. Using Frank Karsten's hypergeometric work (17 lands in 40-card limited), the creator derives a land ratio of 0.425, scaling it to 100 cards to reach a 'golden number' of 43 lands. Commander's play pattern fits control/mid-range, where 43 sits, while EDHRec's average of 36 lands falls in aggro territory. Deck-builder Sam Black independently targets 43 (under 15% to miss land drops vs. 18% at 41). The creator argues against cutting lands for mana rocks or cantrips, since hitting land drops should precede counting mana sources, and rocks/ramp are removable and effectively delayed land drops. Acknowledging nobody wants 43, he recommends a practical scale of 36-43, never crossing 36, and settles on 40 lands (80% chance to see four lands by turn four, given average commander mana value ~3.5). To hedge the gap, he suggests land cyclers, MDFC spells from Modern Horizons 3 and Zendikar Rising, cycling lands and deserts (cap tapped lands at ~10), and lands that generate valueΓÇöclue lands, Eldraine lands, Fountain of Youth-style utility lands, and manlands. Final recommendation: 40 lands, balancing mana screw against flood through varied, action-providing lands.

## Key Clips

- Land bases are the foundation of a great deck and we don't spend nearly enough effort designing them when that's the real differentiator between a good deck and a great deck.
- Frank Karsten proved that 17 lands is a good number for balancing the probability of having enough lands to hit your first few land drops and play your spells and reduce mana flooding. Dividing 17 by 40 gives a land ratio of 0.425; multiplying 0.425 by 100 puts us at 43 lands, the golden number for a Commander deck.
- 43 lands lands exactly within the realm of mid-range or the border of control, which fits Commander's play pattern. The average land count of EDHRec decks is 36 lands, which puts you within the range of an aggro deck.
- To validate 43, deck builder Sam Black: 'You play 41 closer to the floor... you're still like 18% to miss. 43 is closer to what I target often; that brings you down to below 15% to miss, that feels a lot better to me.' Frank Karsten updated his formula in 2024 to account for MDFC lands, the companion rule, the free Mulligan, and drawing on the play, and 43 is still the correct number.
- Making sure you can hit your land drops always needs to precede deciding how many man rocks you play, because rocks can be removed by your opponents. Mana rocks are paid land drops: if you had two lands and a Talisman and didn't draw the third land, it's the same effect as a board generating three mana, except you spent your second turn on it and artifacts are easier to remove.
- Create a scale of 36 to 43; 36 is the border we should never cross. Setting that by turn four we want to have seen four lands (a sample size of 12 cards), at 36 lands the failure rate is more than a quarter of our Mulligans. I picked 40 because it's easy to remember and puts us at 80% success in seeing four lands.
- Three ways to optimize the land base at 40: replace the missing lands with land cyclers (Lord of the Rings, Tarkir, Scourge); max out on MDFC spells from Modern Horizon 3 and Zendikar Rising, cutting about two lands per color; and play cycling lands, deserts, and duals ΓÇö but no more than 10 lands in your entire base should come into play tapped.
- The last trick is playing lands that actually do things, common in contemporary Standard and Pioneer mid-range decks. Clue lands that investigate give pseudo card draw to sink mana into, plus Eldraine lands, Fountainport from Bloomburrow, the Ravnica lands with abilities, and manlands. The recommendation is playing 40 lands; designing the quantity is a careful balance of mana-screw risk against mana flood.
