## Summary

A streamer rebuilds a Reddit Izzet (blue/red) deck led by Togo, Ironforged Champion, whose ability makes a mana-rock Equipment whenever a land enters, and the equipped rock can be sacrificed to throw it. The core loop is landfall: enablers like Storm Cauldron, Trade Routes, Crucible of Worlds, walking-Atlas-style extra-land effects, and bounce lands generate repeated land drops, each spawning rocks. The payoff is turning those rocks into threats via Cyber Drive Awakener, Brudiclad, Hellkite Tyrant, and Rise and Shine. Card selection (Quicksmith Genius) digs aggressively, and a graveyard package (Scrap Mastery, Goblin Welder/Engineer, Krark-Clan Ironworks, Underworld Breach) recurs artifacts for extra value. The breakthrough discovery is Sakashima copying Togo to double every rock trigger, accelerating into game-ending turns. Counterspells and a few removal pieces provide interaction. The builder repeatedly cuts weak cards (Glacian, Emry initially, pingers) in favor of high-impact payoffs, stressing that finicky synergy decks need a clear, strong payoff, not just interlocking parts. The original $10-per-card budget is abandoned to include stronger pieces like Krark-Clan Ironworks and Mox Opal. Target power level is roughly six-to-seven. Lands run ~33, split toward red early with double-blue interaction drawn later.

## Key Clips

- I found a cool deck on Reddit... this person wanted to do Togo landfall and also use the artifacts. There was a restriction of like $10, no card should be over $10. Togo is whenever a land etbs you make a rock equipment, and the equip creature you can just toss the rock, which is really funny.
- This next part of this deck is super finicky, which makes it a lot harder to build well. Finicky decks need a lot of things to go well and a lot of intricate details. The engine is complex and the outcome is complex, or the outcome is not strong. So if I'm going to rebuild this I think I have to clean this up first.
- There needs to be a strong direction from synergy to payoff. Your payoff needs to be good. You can Patron the Moon and bounce two lands and get two rocks, so what? When we're doing payoffs I also like to bring in cards I'm excited to play. It's not about how good it is, it's about what I'm excited to do, because if that's the thing I want to do the most then I can build the deck around it and have more fun.
- I love using tags. For engines it's important to tag enabler and tag payoff. Life gain decks are very much like this where you have cards that gain life and cards that do things when you gain life. If you don't have that balanced you can gain a lot of life and do nothing, or have a lot of payoffs but no life gain.
- I'm huge on Crucible. I think every deck should be playing Crucible Worlds. I'm so into hitting land drops that Crucible just seems like a super free card that not enough people are playing.
- This is how I research a lot of cards: I just go to different themes and find cards, because generally that's good enough. It doesn't find you the really niche stuff, but most of the time I don't need the niche stuff. Sorted by EDHRec rank, so I find the good cards first then the worst cards. Part of the challenge is making sure you don't cut all the fun cards to play all the good cards.
- The real answer if we're being legitimate here, just play Togo Malgam and now you've got a really good deck. The Togos Sakashima plan is actually just nuts — now we get double rocks and then next turn we can Cyber Drive. That was the missing piece, literally the missing piece.
- What felt good about that: we turned the rocks into a resource. Shimmer Dragon's really good, the Aether Grid was fine, the Storm Cauldron's really good. I believe this deck now.
