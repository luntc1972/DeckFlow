## Summary

EDHRec collects deck data across the internet and builds per-commander pages showing commonly played cards. The video defends EDHRec as a useful deckbuilding tool when used correctly, rather than relying on it for everything or avoiding it entirely. Two main usage windows: very early to get a lay of the landΓÇöseeing popular, powerful, or flashy plays (Fists of Flame on Zada, Parhelion II on Greasefang) and gauging how a commander is typically built (e.g., Animar as combo). The most important technique is filters: the base page mixes all archetypes and becomes 'soup' for diverse commanders. - Whereas the initial usage of EDHRec was to get a lay of the land and see a handful of basic build paths, in this later stage of EDHRec usage I am looking for specific cards. Scryfall is a lovely tool, but there are some things it just won't hit in any reasonable number of searches. This is where EDHRec is helpfulΓÇöit can help you fill in the gaps with popular cards that scryfall searches might have missed.
- The base page sucks if you're looking for card choices for your specific deck, because it includes every deck for a commander, even plenty that look nothing like the one you're trying to build. Decks with a given commander can vary dramatically from one another, and the more diverse a given commander's decks are, the less helpful the base EDHRec page will be. The answer is filters.
- The most useful type of card will be what I like to call 'indicator cards,' which are basically just cards that fit very cleanly in or out of a given archetype. For example, Map the FrontierΓÇömana ramp past two or three mana stuff will typically only be included in a deck if it fits with a deck's synergies, so its presence will generally be a good sign that a deck has a big mana gameplan.
- For an anti-indicator card, with a Kardur control deck the way to start sculpting a helpful EDHRec page is to exclude the cards that do the same thing as the commander, such as Disrupt Decorum and Geode Rager. After that the page suddenly starts looking a lot more helpfulΓÇöVengeful Ancestor is only in 12% of the remaining decks after this filtering, down from 43% on the base page.
- You can combine includes and excludes. With a Liesa hatebears deck, what works best is the combined approach; if I filter for decks that include Thalia and exclude Soul Warden, lifegain is basically obliterated from the page. You can do something similar with price if you're looking for a budget deckΓÇöexclude an expensive card and include something cheap like a solid budget land.
- Getting the best usage out of EDHRec requires some level of detachment from the website. The stuff at the top of an EDHRec page is simply the stuff that a bunch of people decided should go in their deck. Even just taking a bit to think about why a given card is present at a certain position on the page and what sorts of decks it might be played in is a really helpful exercise.
