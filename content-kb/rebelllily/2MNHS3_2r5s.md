## Summary

Live stream building Commander decks for the four spoiled Final Fantasy precon face commanders: Titus (Esper counters/proliferate), Terra (Mardu, an Alesha-like reanimator with flying and mill), an Esper cat-girl (life-loss/draw payoff that deals 2 damage on noncreature spells costing 3+), and Cloud (Naya equipment-attacker). Two decks get built. Titus becomes a +1/+1 counter deck resembling the Brokers precon: the builder adds counter generators (Ironscale Hydra/Ozolith, Branching Evolution, Bristly Bill, Evolution Sage), card-draw payoffs (Fathom Mage, Galadriel), eight counterspells, removal, and ETB token-makers to go wide, noting the deck lacked counter sources and beaters early before token bodies fixed it. The Esper cat-girl becomes a Punisher/control deck protecting the commander with hexproof equipment (Silver Shroud Costume, New steel suit), medallions for cost reduction, many counterspells, Punisher cards (Painful Quandary, Polluted Bonds), and card-draw engines (Curiosity, Crucible of Worlds, Black Market). Necropotence is added but flagged as possibly too strong; the deck stays bracket 2-3. Win condition is grinding via repeated draws and life-loss triggers, plus big beaters and Wound Reflection. Terra and Cloud were discussed but not built due to time; builder felt Terra is just Alesha reprinted.

## Key Clips

- They spoiled the four face commanders from the precon, so the plan is to build all four: Titus (an Esper counters commander that moves counters around), Tirra Herald of Hope (a mardu Alesha-like reanimator with flying), the Esper cat girl Isthola, and Cloud (a Naya equipment commander). The build order ends up Catgirl, Cloud, Titus, Tirra — Tirra is basically just Alesha with flying, so any Alesha deck can swap the commander.
- Titus: at the beginning of combat on your turn you may move a counter from one creature you control onto a second, and whenever one or more of your creatures with counters deal combat damage to a player you proliferate and draw a card. The deck is a plus-one-plus-one / hardened-scales build — easy to assemble because there's so much counter support.
- Key pivot for the Titus deck: it has tons of synergy but doesn't punch hard, so add ETB token-makers (Trav the Golem-maker, Eternal Witness-style pickups, soldier/Golem generators) so there are more bodies to hold counters. Cards like Branching Evolution, Doubling Season, Hardened Scales, Conclave Mentor and Ozolith double counters, and Fathom Mage / Bristly Bill / Evolution Sage provide draw and counter sources. The deck plays best as a modular plus-one-plus-one engine, not as one big Voltron threat.
- Isthola the cat girl: at the beginning of each end step, if a player lost four or more life this turn you draw a card; and whenever you cast a noncreature spell with mana value three or greater she deals two to each opponent and you gain life. Crucial reading — the draw trigger says 'a player,' not 'an opponent,' so cards like Wound Reflection and Punisher effects that drain everyone (including yourself) still turn on the card draw. Build is Esper control/Punisher.
- The Esper Punisher shell leans on cards like Painful Quandary, Polluted Bonds, Omen Machine effects, Wound Reflection, Blood Chief Ascension, and Fate Unraveler to grind every opponent's life total while triggering Isthola's end-step draw up to once per turn. The deck wants cheap counterspells (Insidious Will, Rewind, Misdirection) plus cost reducers (medallions) to protect the commander and keep mana open.
- Because Isthola dies instantly to removal, the whole deck is built around making her untouchable: equip hexproof gear like Silver Shroud Costume (Fallout, equips at instant speed), Giant's Amulet, and Champion's Helm, plus Lightning Greaves / Diplomatic Immunity for a repeatable shroud. The aura/equipment-on-commander plan is the engine — once she's safe, the table struggles to interact with the deck.
- Final assessment: Necropotence makes the Esper Isthola deck too strong (likely bracket 3+), so it's left in mostly because people will ask. The important play pattern is that you don't need to rush the commander — there's enough card draw (Curiosity-style on the commander, black card advantage) to sit back, hold counters, and grind. Both finished lists (Titus counters and Isthola Esper Punisher) will be posted in the video description; Cloud and Tirra are deferred to next time.
