## Summary

Argues for deck consistency, defined not as doing the same thing every game but as consistently doing something whose outcomes don't vary much in power level; cites his $25 Rakdos, Meria (equipment aggro), and Glissa (ramp into big threats, board dominance) decks as examples. Advocates "rollout consistency"—checking via solo playtesting that the deck does something good in the first few turns. Offers probability benchmarks: need ~10 copies of an effect to see it in an opening hand over half the time; 15 ramp spells gives 79% chance of ramp by turn 2. Warns that leftover deck slots invite weakening filler; better to fight for space. Recommends categorizing the deck into Ramp, Draw, Removal, Protection; run 8–14 removal (more for slower decks), variable protection (0 to 12), and question whether a card (e.g. Gilded Lotus) truly counts as ramp. Note two Swiftfoot Boots/Lightning Greaves give under 23% protection in five draws. For situational consistency, use tutors and toolbox shells—Birthing Pod, Yisan, Sunforger (Vadrok), Astral Slide (Sharuum). Defends tutors over luck-based combo draws.

## Key Clips

- **[02:54]** One of the easier forms of consistency to nail is "rollout consistency"—it asks the question, do I do what I want to do on the first several turns of the game? You won't play a turn 1 Sol Ring every game, but check that your deck is generally doing at least something that feels good.
- **[04:49]** A simple benchmark you can reference is that you need 10 of a card in a deck to have it show up in a given hand more than half the time.
- **[05:36]** A lot of people underestimate how many cards 100 actually is, which tends to result in putting in fewer cards with a particular effect than would be ideal. When slots feel plentiful, filler that weakens the deck works its way in—cards with slight synergies that are probably there in place of better-suited ones.
- **[06:30]** Categorize your deck. Four supporting categories I usually have are Ramp, Draw, Removal, and Protection—those last two sometimes combined for decks whose interaction is mainly counterspells. Separating them out and making sure you have enough of each ensures you spend slots on things that actually make the deck better.
- **[06:59]** Within a category, ask if a card truly belongs. Is Gilded Lotus ramp? If you're accelerating to ten or more mana to play big threats, yes. If you're running it just to hit a 6 mana commander consistently, no it's not ramp.
- **[07:40]** For removal I'd recommend starting between 8 and 14, and adjust from there—slower, lower-velocity decks will usually want more removal than faster, higher-velocity decks.
- **[08:14]** If you only run the two boots for protection, your odds of drawing protection in your first five draws of the game are a hair under 23%. That's not great, if your commander staying alive matters a lot to the deck.
- **[10:00]** A couple quintessential toolbox cards are Birthing Pod and Yisan, the Wanderer Bard, which work well in decks with a lot of card diversity at each mana cost. The best way to give a deck easy access to situational cards without destroying its consistency is tutors.
