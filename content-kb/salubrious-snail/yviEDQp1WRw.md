## Summary

Argues Commander players should run more removal/interaction, and offers two methods to fit it in without feeling like wasted deck space. Method one: densify the removal you already run. Instead of single-target removal (one slot per threat), use multi-removal—Hull Breach, Soul Shatter, Windgrace's Judgment, Decimate, Casualties of War—that hits multiple current and future problems, or removal engines that kill over time: Aura Shards, Royal Assassin, Trygon Predator, plus multi-card engines (Astral Slide recycling board-wipe ETBs, Containment Priest + flicker, Muldrotha + Fleshbag Marauder). These cost more mana but free space for counters, tech, and draw. Warns against a fourth/fifth board wipe since decks rebound fast. Method two: widen the suite with removal that blends into deck synergies—by card type, mechanic (Drown in Ichor for counters, Trygon Predator for evasion, Lethal Scheme for go-wide), or modal/MDFC cards (Valorous Stance, Silverback Elder, Gix's Command, Angel of the Ruins). Cites Hans's Lilah 'Izzet Bizzet' and his own Radha cascade deck. Finally: match removal to what your deck needs—lean proactive combo decks (Francisco/Malcolm) run 2-mana-or-less staples; grindier decks run engines. Instant vs sorcery speed tracks power level.

## Key Clips

- **[01:26]** First method: make the slots you're already spending on removal do more. Most popular EDH removal is single-target—an entire deck slot spent removing one card. Two compact alternatives: multi-removal and removal engines.
- **[02:23]** Windgrace's Judgment can destroy each opponent's most valuable nonland permanent, and both Decimate and Casualties of War can blast cards of several types at once—hitting current problems plus things that might cause problems later.
- **[02:58]** Removal engines destroy things over time. Aura Shards is one of the most powerful; creatures like Royal Assassin and Trygon Predator remove one permanent per turn. In toolbox control, a few engines free space for counterspells, tech, and draw.
- **[04:55]** If you're considering your fourth or fifth board wipe, consider other options—a lot of decks bounce back shockingly quickly from a board wipe, or mitigate it entirely.
- **[05:24]** Second method: widen your suite but pick removal that blends into what your deck is doing—align with synergies like card type, counters, tokens, or graveyards, so it feels less space-gobbling even as removal count stays high.
- **[06:21]** Modal cards—Valorous Stance, Silverback Elder, Gix's Command—work for removal and non-removal. So do effectively-modal landcyclers like Angel of the Ruins and MDFCs; they can be lands, draw, ramp, or protection in addition to removal.
- **[08:57]** In my lean, proactive Francisco/Malcolm combo deck, removal exists to kill creatures threatening my turn 5-6 win, so all dedicated removal is 2 mana or less to weave into a turn; I don't care what's on board two turns from now.
- **[10:24]** Instant vs sorcery speed comes down to power level. At higher powers with sudden combo attempts, especially without blue counterspells, instant-speed removal is nearly a must—and hold-up removal should cost as little as possible.
