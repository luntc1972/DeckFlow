## Summary

This video reviews the 21 legendary vehicles and spacecraft that can now be played as commanders without partner/background-style text. The creator sorts them into four tiers. "Trash" picks (Bessie, Heart of Kiran, Prima Vista, Valor's Flagship, Grunt) are near-vanilla beaters. A "bad mostly because colorless" group (Adrestea, Gordian Argosy, Sky Sovereign, Console Flagship, Regalia) suffers from limited color access. The "cool" tier includes token engines (Esika's Chariot copying big green tokens, Parhelion II making angels, Pridian Steel Flagship producing knights), card-draw payoffs (The Last Ride at 13-or-less life), RMS Titanic for monored treasure bursts, and Falcon Airship for repeatable reanimation. The top three the creator wants to build: Weatherlight Completed, a colorless sacrifice commander rewarding creature deaths with scry/draw; The Indomitable, a blue 6/6 granting Coastal Piracy card draw to enable an aggressive evasive blue archetype; and Jack Door, a two-color artifact commander that wheels into massive draws off artifact count. Key insight: vehicles dodge creature-only board wipes since they aren't creatures on opponents' turns, but remain vulnerable to artifact removal. The creator frames these decks as a ship's crew and welcomes the rule change.

## Key Clips

- Wizards just announced that legendary vehicles and the upcoming spacecraft may now be played as your commander even without the Shorai text. There are a total of 21 cards that now have access to the command zone.
- It's also worth noting that vehicles have a bit of an additional layer of protection since they're not usually creatures on our opponent's turns. That means they dodge Blasphemous Act, Wrath of God, or any other creature-centered sweepers. Of course, they are still open to Planar Cleansing or simply artifact removal.
- Esika's Chariot is a four-mana monogreen 4/4 with crew 4. When it enters we create two 2/2 cats and whenever it attacks we create a token that's a copy of target token we control. Unlike similar cards, the Chariot isn't limited to only creating copies of creatures, that opens the door to many fascinating possibilities.
- RMS Titanic, a four-mana red 7/1 with flying, trample and crew 3 which sacrifices itself as soon as it deals combat damage to a player and then creates as many treasure tokens as it dealt damage. I think it's reasonable to expect this commander to create seven treasures at least once per game. And in mono-red that can set up an explosive, potentially game-ending turn.
- The Indomitable is a four-mana blue 6/6 with trample and crew 3. Whenever a creature we control deals combat damage to a player, we draw a card. Coastal piracy is such a powerful effect that entire decks can be built around it. These decks had a problem: they desperately need coastal piracy in play, and there was just no good way to guarantee that. The best color for these decks is blue. And now we got the Indomitable as a commander.
- The Falcon Airship Restored is a black three-mana 4/3 with flying and crew 2. Whenever it deals combat damage to a player, we may sacrifice it to return target creature card from our graveyard to the battlefield. The Falcon is a repeatable reanimation engine. It's not particularly fast or efficient, but it is very resilient. None of the cards our commander interacts with have any limitations placed upon them.
- Of these 21 cards there are actually only three cars and 13 boats. It's mostly boats. Lots of people have been joking about how cars can be commanders now, but the rule change is mostly boats.
- Jack Door is a three-mana 4/4 with crew 3. Whenever it deals combat damage to a player, we may discard our hand and draw a card for each artifact we control. There's only one other boat that's two colors; everything else is either mono or colorless. That alone makes the Jack Door much easier to build around.
