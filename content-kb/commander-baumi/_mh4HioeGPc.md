## Summary

Owl/Ao the Dawn Sky is a mono-white Commander deck (~$55 budget, Bracket 2) centered on landfall and death-trigger recursion. Ao, a 5-mana 5/4 flyer with vigilance, has a death trigger that either cheats four-mana-value-or-less permanents into play from the top seven cards, or pumps the team with +1/+1 counters; vigilance plus the value-on-death make him hard to attack into or block. The deck runs white catch-up ramp (Claim Jumper, Sand Scout, cartographers) that requires being behind on lands, so the pilot deliberately shrinks land count via Aura Fracture, Refracture, bounce/Lotus lands, and sacrifice. Lands in the graveyard fuel reanimation (Sun Titan, Redemption Choir, Restoration of Eiganjo) and instant-speed mass returns (Second Sunrise, Faith's Reward) paired with sac outlets (Claws of Gix) to loop permanents for value. Landfall payoffs are thin in whiteΓÇöFelidar Retreat is best, with Retreat to Emeria, Ardenvale, and Emeria Shepherd as backups. Sweepers and Planar Cleansing act as one-shot sacrifice. Finishers: Jazal Goldmane overrun, Blade of Selves myriad for extra death triggers, Lotus Bloom for mana. Recursion is limited, so timing matters.

## Key Clips

- Ao the Dawn Sky is a white five-mana 5/4 with flying and vigilance who gives us a choice when he dies. We can either look at the top seven cards of our library and put any number of non-land permanents with total mana value four or less onto the battlefield, or put two +1/+1 counters on all our creatures and vehicles.
- Felidar Retreat is a four-mana enchantment with a landfall ability that lets us either create a 2/2 cat or put a +1/+1 counter on each creature we control alongside giving them vigilance until end of turn. This is a really powerful card that's white, probably for balance reasons.
- White has access to some very good ramp. Cards like Claim Jumper, Sand Scout, and Cartographer let us ramp lands as long as we have less lands than an opponent. By actively managing our land count, including being willing to reduce it, we can reliably use catch-up ramp for the entire game. This also places lots of lands in our graveyard, fantastic for reanimation.
- Sun Titan, Redemption Choir, and the Restoration of Eiganjo let us return non-specific permanents from our graveyard, including lands. Add Second Sunrise and Faith's Reward ΓÇö instant-speed cards that return all our permanents that died this turn. Pair those with sacrifice outlets like Claws of Gix and now we can cycle lands in and out of our graveyard while protecting our board and generating mana and card advantage.
- This is a recursion deck that can only recur a limited number of times. So we need to be very careful and precise with the opportunities that we find and use. Sweepers give us death triggers and the deck is resilient to them; in some weird way Planar Cleansing could be considered a sacrifice outlet that also sacrifices all of our opponents' stuff.
- I added Blade of Selves to give our creatures Myriad, which is very powerful because the tokens enter and immediately die to the legend rule, giving us two death triggers. With those we might even want to occasionally just buff our board instead of expanding it.
- Jazal Goldmane is a four-mana 4/4 with first strike and an activated ability that for five mana gives our attacking creatures power and toughness equal to the number of attacking creatures. It's an overrun we can find with Ao and pump lots of mana into, which works great with our blink shenanigans since we tend to have a lot of mana.
- This is my mono-white landfall deck that I actually have in real life and absolutely adore. It's centered around very specific ideas that require lots of work to accomplish but take us on a really interesting journey of trying to accumulate the right pieces and finding the right moments. The deck is around 55 bucks, very much in the budget range.
