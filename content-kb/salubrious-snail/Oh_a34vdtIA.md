## Summary

A deck reviewer counts down the five most common Commander/EDH deckbuilding mistakes from two years of supporter reviews. #5 (7 reviews): macro-level lack of focus—piling generic synergy payoffs that shoot in different directions; fix by showing restraint and excluding cards that don't serve the gameplan. #4 (8): fragile gameplans; rather than dedicated protection spells (which he dislikes when behind), add counterspells, flicker, haste, flash enablers like Leyline of Anticipation, and ramp. #3 (10): not proactive—decks like goad-piles or theft decks over-invest in using opponents' tools without building their own wincon; goad's value drops with each copy, so treat it as defense and categorize cards by role, not mechanic. #2 (13): not reactive—midrange decks on average timelines need ~20 pieces of interaction, though ruthlessly proactive decks can run as few as 5; tie removal into synergy packages and match efficiency to deck speed. #1 (21): micro-level dead cards—thin synergies and amplifier cards (token doublers, Fiery Emancipation) that do nothing without a board; only run them when the thing amplified is common and resilient.

## Key Clips

- **[02:42]** A necessary lesson here is in restraint: knowing when to exclude a card even when it theoretically has a bit of synergy with what you're building.
- **[02:58]** The trick is learning to crank up the specificity, and turning a soup of generic payoffs into a deck that knows what it is and where it wants to be going.
- **[05:00]** Saving your deck's pivotal setup plays for an opposing endstep rather than trotting them out on your main phase dramatically increases the odds that you'll actually get to use them, and effectively increases the amount of mana you get to spend uninterrupted by opposing main phases.
- **[06:42]** If you have a creature you want to attack with or activate a tap ability of, giving it haste in some form or other fully prevents your creature eating dirt before it could do anything at all.
- **[07:02]** If you're adding a goad card to your deck, its effectiveness will be inversely proportional to the number of goad cards already in your deck.
- **[08:35]** Categorize cards based on what role they're playing instead of giving an effect like goad its own category, so you don't develop subconscious bias that causes you to feel overly comfortable looking at a category of seven goad cards.
- **[12:06]** If you've built a standard midrangy thing that wins on a very average timeline, you need some ways to slow opposing plans or your chances of winning go down substantially.
