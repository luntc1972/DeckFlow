## Summary

This video is an opinion essay, not a deck guide. The creator responds to Jeffrey White's viral "pigs eat slop" post, which many read as criticizing Universes Beyond but actually targeted Commander. The creator, a Commander-only player, asks why so many legendary creatures Wizards seemingly designs for Commander (Vile Smasher, Baron Bertram Greywater, odd Final Fantasy and Spider-Man legends) are neither unique, interesting, nor powerful, and rarely get built (under 500 Vile Smasher decks per EDHREC). His theory: these stinky legendaries aren't made for Commander players at all. Drawing parallels to gaming's two-decade shift toward RPG-style individual expressionΓÇöWarcraft 3 heroes, DotA, League, Hearthstone, OverwatchΓÇöand to card games trending singleton (Hearthstone Highlander, Yu-Gi-Oh Genesis), he argues 60-card formats feel restrictive (four-ofs, few unique cards). Legendaries carry a real downside in 60-card decks since only one can be on the battlefield, pushing builders toward high card diversity and self-expression. He illustrates with a legendary-heavy lifegain/cleric deck spanning Bloomburrow, Foundations, Final Fantasy, and Spider-Man. Conclusion: Wizards prints these legendaries to make 60-card formats more Commander-like, not to serve Commander. He admits this may be pattern-seeking and dislikes the post's hostile language.

## Key Clips

- The number of legendary creatures released has been on a steady increase. This makes sense when you consider that Commander has been rapidly growing in popularity and is now Magic's biggest format. Since commander decks are singleton, they don't care if they have to play legendary creatures. Wizards of the Coast are printing tons of commanders to pander to their now largest demographic.
- The bar for a powerful commander is quite high, so the cards printed for that role have a good chance to be too much for the smaller formats to handle. Nadu, Winged Wisdom was a card that Wizards admitted to being intended for commander ΓÇö a blatant design mistake by any metric.
- Cards like Baron Bertram Greywater, Fortune Loyal Steed, Gerald the Fleshwright, or Vile Smasher Gleeful Grenadier are neither unique nor interesting, and they are not powerful either. According to EDHREC, fewer than 500 Vile Smashers have been built. This card wasn't made to be a commander, but it still has all the trappings of one.
- Modern game development places emphasis on making the player feel like an important individual. RPG elements have become more prominent across all genres ΓÇö Warcraft 3 heroes, DotA, League of Legends, Hearthstone, Overwatch hero shooters. Commander fits well into that zeitgeist better than traditional magic, and so it blew up. But it or something similar coming into existence was inevitable.
- In competitive 60-card formats customization is actually quite restrictive. Izzet Prowess in Modern plays only three different creatures and 11 unique spells total. In Standard, Izzet Cauldron runs only 12 spells and Mono-Red aggro is down to 10. If we go back 20 years, games like Magic offered comparatively huge customization. Then Commander shows up and force-feeds us 60 unique spells.
- My theory is that cards like Vile Smasher don't exist for commander players. Rather, they are made to make 60-card formats more similar to commander. In 60-card formats, legendary creatures come with a disadvantage ΓÇö you don't want to play too many because you can only have one on the battlefield. So a deck built around them ends up with high card diversity, since you wouldn't want four copies of each legend.
- A lifegain archetype example: Essence Channeler enabled by Soraline, Cosmos Caller, plus Exemplar of Light, Ajani's Pridemate, Hinterland Sanctifier, Lynde the Steadfast Queen, then Final Fantasy's Araf Gainsborough, Minwu White Mage, and the Wind Crystal ΓÇö many of them clerics. Then Halia Guided by Light, and Spider-Man's Aunt May, Silver Sable, Spider-UK. These cards were designed with each other in mind, giving high card diversity and lots of deckbuilding expression.
- Wizards are printing lots of stinky legendary creatures that are slightly better or have different utility than similar regular creatures. These aren't cards printed into 60-card formats for the benefit of commander players. They are printed to make 60-card formats more like commander, to fit the individualistic, self-expressive gaming culture that has formed over the past two decades. It's not slop for pigs, it's slop for you.
