## Summary

The creator runs budget ($50) Commander tournaments that play like competitive cEDH. After abandoning a controlling Shorai Genesis engine deck (Isochron Scepter + Dramatic Reversal) because it was boring, they built a mono-green Six deck in two days and won the tournament, partly because opponents couldn't identify its combo pieces. Six is a free 2/2 with reach that mills three on attack, drops a land to hand, and casts nonland permanents from the graveyard via retrace by discarding lands. The deck runs 47 lands (46 Forest, 1 Sunscorch Desert), mana doublers (Gauntlet of Power, Mana Reflection), and self-mill engines (Cultivator Colossus, World Shaper). Life from the Loam is the key card, dredging to refill lands. The primary win: with Six, a mana doubler, Spelunking, Life from the Loam, and a repeatable one-card draw (Insidious Fungus), sacrifice all lands and reanimate them via Claws of Gix/Sakashima orb + World Shaper/aftermath analyst for infinite mana, then mill the deck and ping opponents to death via Sunscorch Desert loops. Backup lines use Feldon green shell, Springheart Nuko, and Endrek forerunners. Weaknesses: graveyard hate and being targeted once opponents understand it.

- I run these 50 euro old budget Commander tournaments ΓÇö this is basically cEDH but with a budget restriction of 50. It's quite popular and incredibly competitive. Deck lists are public; I make them public after everybody has submitted, so players research their opponents' lists and figure out how to play around them.
- I originally prepared a Shorikai Genesis Engine deck ΓÇö an incredibly controlling list all about stalling the game and eventually winning with an Isochron Scepter + Dramatic Reversal combo. It worked, but it wasn't fun to play. After four or five practice matches I got so bored I scrapped it, leaving two days to build a new deck and one evening to test it.
- The centerpiece is our commander Six: a Mana 2/2 with reach who, whenever he attacks, mills three and lets us put a land from the milled cards into hand. During our turn we can play nonland permanents from our graveyard with retrace ΓÇö discarding a land in addition to the card's regular cost. The deck only plays two non-permanents: Beast Within and Life from the Loam.
- It's a mono-green deck running 47 lands ΓÇö 46 Forests and one Sunscorch Desert. We need to discard lands to play cards from the graveyard, and the heavy land count helped a lot with the budget restriction.
- Two prerequisites while playing Six: mill ourselves to access cards, and make enough mana to use them. Mana doublers like Gauntlet of Power, Zendikar Resurgent, or Mana Reflection make lands tap for extra mana. Self-mill comes from cards like Perpetual Timepiece and Branchwood Prowler ΓÇö the best versions also add lands to hand so we can replay what we milled.
- Life from the Loam is the most important and most powerful card in the deck ΓÇö a two-mana sorcery returning up to three target land cards from graveyard to hand, with dredge three. It effectively draws three cards a turn during the setup phases.
- The primary win line is a 7-8 card combo: tap all lands for mana, sacrifice the lands, then sacrifice Aftermath Analyst or World Shaper (via Claws of Gix) to return all lands; Spelunking makes them enter untapped so we re-tap for infinite mana. Life from the Loam refuels lands in hand to keep recasting, and we replace each card draw with a dredge so we never deck out. Once the whole deck is milled, Sunscorch Desert deals one damage per loop to kill the table.
- I won the tournament ΓÇö three of four matches, the most dominant victory of anyone. But I mostly won because nobody knew what to do against the deck; multiple top local competitors came up and straight told me they didn't know how it worked. It won't work a second time: people will play Rest in Peace and just target me down.
