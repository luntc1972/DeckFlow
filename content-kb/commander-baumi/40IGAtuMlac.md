## Summary

The video argues that ramp and interaction are not exclusive to green; red can compete by being creative. The creator highlights spell-copy effects (Fork, Reiterate) that duplicate opponents' ramp like Open the Way, counter countermagic, or mirror one-sided board wipes, and spell-redirection cards (Bolt Bend, Veil's Reversal, Deflecting Swat) that change a spell's target—making red strong against counterspells and removal. The core pick is Return the Favor, an Outlaws at Thunder Junction uncommon with Spree: for one mana each, copy an instant/sorcery/activated/triggered ability (choosing new targets), and/or redirect a single-target spell or ability. Its flexibility means it is rarely dead: copy and redirect ETB fight triggers (Kogla), steal fetchland activations (Myriad Landscape) for free ramp, protect against counters/removal, or steal card draw. A standout line is holding a fetchland open, cracking it in response to an opponent's targeted spell, then copying the fetch and redirecting their spell for a huge tempo swing. The creator rates it as reliable as Cultivate, Generous Gift, and Counterspell, and recommends trying it.

## Key Clips

- Land ramp is often seen as the sole domain of green, with white maybe dabbling a little bit in it. I disagree with this. While green undeniably has the easiest access to ramp and the other colors have to put in a bit more work, so long as we are open-minded and get creative with it we can get some really strong results.
- One of my favorite strategies is to copy spells like Fork to duplicate my opponent's ramp and use it for myself. They might open the way for six, and then I do the same for two mana. This of course requires a green player at the table, but if there is no green player then it's also not as important that we are ramping a lot.
- Having cheap duplication can be incredibly valuable. If we're getting countered then we can copy the counter spell and use it to counter itself, or we can duplicate one-sided board wipes and ensure that everybody is getting hit equally, or duplicate spells that have additional costs like Unexpected Windfall.
- Red has access to spell redirection cards like Bolt Bend, Wild Ricochet's reversal, and Deflecting Swat. They all share a core idea: they let us change the target of a spell or ability. We can take a Swords to Plowshares aimed at us and send it right back at its owner, or redirect a counter spell. Red is really good at dealing with counter spells.
- The main issue with cards like Fork and Bolt Bend is that, while great in the right circumstances, it's also not unusual that they are just dead in our hand. That's why I'd introduce Return the Favor — an inconspicuous uncommon from Outlaws of Thunder Junction.
- Return the Favor has the Spree ability: both abilities cost one mana. The first lets us copy an instant, sorcery, activated, or triggered ability and choose new targets for the copy. The second lets us change the target of a spell or ability with a single target. It staples both effects onto one card, giving much-needed flexibility — while Fork or Bolt Bend can be dead in hand, it's rare for both to be.
- Because it can copy activated or triggered abilities, it can copy almost anything — loyalty abilities, ETBs, and attack triggers. We can wait until an opponent plays Kogla and Yidaro, then both copy and redirect the ETB fight effect to clear out two creatures the other opponent controls.
- One of the coolest tricks is keeping a fetch land open until an opponent casts a spell with a target we want to redirect. We leave that spell on the stack, crack our fetch land putting that trigger on the stack, then cast Return the Favor: copy our fetchland activation and redirect their spell, creating a huge swing in momentum that should last multiple turns if not the entire game.
