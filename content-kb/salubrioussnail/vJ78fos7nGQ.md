## Summary

This video explains slot compression: fitting more of each card category (finishers, draw, removal) into a deck by choosing cards that do multiple things. For midrange decks, run well-statted creatures that also draw cards or force discard; even finishers like blood artist effects double as board-wipe deterrents. High-curve decks like Glissa pay ~20 slots for ramp but reward it with removal and draw stapled to large creatures, winning without dedicated wincon slots. Hate bears use stax effects that are also creatures, oppressing opponents while keeping a board to block and pressure life totals (e.g., Liesa). Combo decks gain space by picking pieces that stand on their own—altars in artifact-synergy decks, sac outlets that buff, or generically good cards like Fanatical Devotion and Greater Good; a mid-power Najeela deck runs low-cost warriors that fuel both beats and combo. Synergy decks built around a card type, creature type, or attribute (equipment, auras, Goreclaw's power-4+ creatures) pack more power and let conditional tutors fetch much of the deck. For lands, use MDFCs and utility lands to push counts to 35–40, mitigating screw and flood. Caveat: compression and smoothness don't replace a real wincon—decks still need a way to actually end the game.
