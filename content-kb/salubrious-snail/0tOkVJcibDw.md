## Summary

A deckbuilder is challenged to build a Commander deck around three random cards: The Sibsig Ceremony, Aether Vial, and Birthing Ritual. The Sibsig Ceremony reduces creature costs but destroys cast creatures on entry, replacing them with zombies; the other two cards deploy creatures without casting, sidestepping that downside. All three reward high creature counts and lowish mana values. The chosen commander is Iname as One, which dodges Sibsig's destruction by wanting to die: when cast from hand it tutors a spirit on ETB and recurs one on death, chaining Iname, Death Aspect and Iname, Life Aspect to load the graveyard and rebuy spirits. Sacrifice outlets pair well with Sibsig since creatures die anyway. The deck is a patchwork rather than a forced combo: spirits midrange (Myojin, Kodama, Infernal Kirin), an Aether Vial niche, and an organic lands package (Urborg, Command Beacon, Field of the Dead, Dark Depths). Setup is demanding—12 mana, commander must reach hand via Command Beacon/Campfire/etc.—so the deck leans on heavy ramp, ~14 draw cards, and four tutors, at the cost of interaction. Five other creators built alternative decks (Codie donate, Bhaal, Myrkul, Tayam, Teval) from the same prompt.

## Key Clips

- **[01:22]** Birthing Ritual is probably the lowest-commitment card here—it has a low mana cost, relatively flexible criteria for podding things, and you get to see all your options before sacrificing. Really the only thing this needs to work well is a moderately large creature count, though it can also help to have a decent spread between mana values to allow stairstepping up in mana costs.
- **[01:36]** Aether Vial is traditionally played in heavily-creature-based decks with relatively low average mana values—typically you drop it early, get it up to two or three counters, and then pop out extra dudes at instant speed. Like Birthing Ritual, this card is going to be looking for a high creature count to be useful, and preferably a relatively low average mana cost among those creatures.
- **[02:59]** Indestructible works since Sibsig destroys the creatures rather than making you sacrifice them, and this leaves a handful of options such as Sapling of Colfenor, Kotis, most Gods, and any partner pair that includes Anara. The Mimeoplasm can copy a creature with indestructible, and Indoraptor will likewise be indestructible if you have an indestructible creature to discard.
- **[04:53]** Sacrifice outlets are actually pretty sweet with The Sibsig Ceremony in general, because it destroys the creatures. So you've got all these creatures that are going to die anyway, and you may as well sacrifice them. There are even some that care about power or mana value, and for those, sacrificing Iname as One itself can be a fantastic power burst.
- **[06:13]** We need to get the commander into our hand before playing it—the ETB trigger doesn't happen if we cast it from the command zone. In this deck there are four cards that allow getting our commander into the command zone—Campfire, Netherborn Alter, Road of Return, and Command Beacon—and getting one of these will naturally be a requirement for any sort of commander based gameplay.
- **[09:44]** One thing this deck doesn't have a lot of is extra space to work with. I'm already spending a bunch of slots just making my questionable commander even functional, and adding more draw and ramp would inevitably end up eating into something else. The only way to make more space would be to cut spirits, which would weaken my spirit synergy cards.
