## Summary

Tutorial on building Commander decks as engines: repeatable systems converting one resource into another at scale, via enablers (inputs) and payoffs (outputs). Example commander Zor-Line, Cosmos Caller (?) — bats, life gain, graveyard return. Enablers should be cheap in mana value, self-sufficient (work alone), and consistent/repeatable. Cited enablers: Awakening Zone (tokens+mana) paired with Skullclamp (creatures→cards) or Soul Warden (life); Ajani's Mantra; from a Resistance Allies deck, Invasion Reinforcements (cheap, not repeatable), Celestial Eulogist (repeatable, not self-sufficient), Retreat to Emeria (4 mana, also a payoff). Bad enablers named: Rox Faithmender, Sunscorch Regent, Zhur-Taa Ancient, Wolverine Riders — too expensive or conditional; Nykthos Paragon belongs in the payoff slot. Payoffs should close games: Archangel of Thune, Vito Thorn of the Dusk Rose, Angelic Accord over card-draw-only Dawn of Hope and Well of Lost Springs. Play fewer payoffs than enablers. Recommended counts: 12 inputs, 6 outputs, plus 3 synergy/multiplier cards (e.g. Parallel Lives). Commander shifts the ratio: Thrasios as output needs fewer payoffs; a cheap enabler-commander lets you add more scaling payoffs. Method: start with your intended payoff and work backwards, then goldfish.

## Key Clips

- **[02:00]** An engine is a repeatable system that converts one resource into another one at scale — kind of like a factory. For example, you can play an Awakening Zone to create token creatures that are also mana, then convert that resource of creatures into card draw with the classic Skullclamp, or pair Awakening Zone with a Soul Warden to gain life off the token creatures.
- **[03:34]** The key to picking the right enablers: you want them cheap in mana value, because if your whole engine depends on this resource you don't want to wait till later to get going. You want them self-sufficient so they work by themselves without needing other cards. And you want them consistent — repeatable in how they generate the resource, like an upkeep trigger or an activated ability.
- **[05:04]** Using my Resistance Allies deck: it wants Ally tokens, so I play 13 cards that create Ally tokens. Invasion Reinforcements is cheap at only two mana and self-sufficient because it always creates a token on ETB, but it's not repeatable — and I'm happy with that exchange because it fits the theme, does the job, and fits the mana curve.
- **[06:48]** A Rox Faithmender will rot in your hand or on the battlefield if you don't have a way to trigger it, and Sunscorch Regent competes for the same area of the curve where you should be slamming down a Nykthos Paragon to close the game instead. Wolverine Riders creates elf tokens every upkeep and gains life, but it's so expensive it can't be an early enabler.
- **[08:41]** Dawn of Hope and Well of Lost Springs convert life gain into card draw to maintain momentum, but the big problem is they don't close the game. Outputs should focus more on threats or game enders like Archangel of Thune, Vito Thorn of the Dusk Rose, or Angelic Accord to convert your life gain triggers into threats.
- **[09:23]** You should play fewer payoffs than enablers, because most payoffs are useless until there's an enabler to support them — they're the cards you're happier to draw later in the game when they're more useful, rather than opening with a hand of expensive payoffs waiting for a cheap life gain source.
- **[10:37]** Using the cube theory model I like brewing with, I always start with at least eight inputs and eight outputs because that's easier to remember, but it really should be at least 12 inputs and six outputs.
- **[14:53]** When designing the engine — the game plan of your deck — start with some of the payoffs you want to win with and work in reverse, so you can pick the right backbone to support it. This way you pick everything to elevate the payoff, because that's how you win the game.
