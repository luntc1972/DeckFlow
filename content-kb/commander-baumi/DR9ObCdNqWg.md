## Summary

Deck tech for Six, a mono-green Commander built on a graveyard toolbox for around a 50-euro budget. Six costs three mana, is a 2/4 with reach, mills three on attack (returning one land to hand) and grants nonland permanents in the graveyard retrace during your turn. Game plan: self-mill to fill the graveyard, then replay permanents by discarding lands. Runs 46 lands (45 Forest + Demolition Field), seven mana dorks plus Elvish Spirit Guide for a fast turn-two Six, and aggressive mulligans. Key support: self-mill ramp creatures (Aftermath Analyst, Cavalier of Thorns, World Shaper), land-return effects (Splendid Reclamation, Ramunap Excavator, Life from the Loam with dredge as the most synergistic card), and four protection auras/equipment for Six. Wins through five mana doublers, five overrun effects (Endless Ranks favored for vigilance), and Springheart Nantuko, which copies enchanted creatures on land ETB for near-loops with Cavalier or Fungal green Shell. Weaknesses: depends on Six, no instant-speed interaction, and total-graveyard hate or exile board wipes (Farewell, Leyline of the Void) hurt; pivot to casting creatures from hand. Praised as a fair, interactive, resilient casual deck.

## Key Clips

- Six costs three mana to play and is a 2/4 with reach, a pretty good stat line as he can attack early. Whenever he attacks you mill three cards and then you may put a land card from among the milled cards into your hand. He also gives nonland permanent cards in your graveyard retrace during your turn — retrace lets you play those cards by discarding a land in addition to paying their normal costs.
- Play 46 lands — 45 Forest and a Demolition Field. Less than 45 lands is not enough to ensure you draw one with Six every time he attacks, which matters because every land in hand lets you retrace a permanent. Don't add tapped lands; play mana efficiently to maintain momentum.
- Get Six out quickly: play seven mana dorks plus an Elvish Spirit Guide for about a 73% chance to land Six on turn two if you mulligan for it. Mulligan aggressively for mana dorks — a hand of six lands and a mana dork is fine because it gets Six out fast and then fills the graveyard.
- Run around 8 to 10 cards with strong self-mill. Aftermath Analyst, Cavalier of Thorns, and World Shaper are great because they double as both mill and ramp. Splendid Reclamation returns all lands from your graveyard to the battlefield, one of the strongest things you can do since you constantly mill and discard lands.
- Life from the Loam is the most powerful and essential card in the deck — a two-mana sorcery that returns up to three lands from your graveyard to hand, with dredge three so it returns itself when you'd draw. It's the only sorcery that doesn't mind being milled. Elvish Visionary, Soul-Guide Lantern, and Kenrith's Transformation provide redundancy to dredge it again.
- Springheart Nantuko is the second essential card. Enchant it onto a creature and whenever a land enters you can pay two mana to copy the enchanted creature. On Cavalier of Thorns each copy triggers its ETB, putting a land into play and refilling graveyard; with a mana doubler you can loop the entire deck since the Cavalier's lands enter untapped.
- Targeted graveyard hate isn't a big problem because retracing spells can't be removed in response — retrace doesn't create a separate trigger or require a target, it places the spell directly on the stack, so cards like Scavenging Ooze can't respond. Graveyard hate that exiles the whole graveyard, like Leyline of the Void, is the real threat.
- Win condition is creature-based combat: five mana-doubling effects for explosive turns and five overrun effects (End-Raze Forerunners preferred for the +2/+2, trample, and vigilance). The author notes Six isn't good for cEDH because the deck lacks instant-speed interaction and can't stop combos.
