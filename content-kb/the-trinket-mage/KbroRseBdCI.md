## Summary

This Magic Mirror podcast episode features a deck-building challenge where hosts spin wheels for three constraints: budget, a required "spicy" card, and a restriction. Alex builds an "unspeakably bad reanimator" deck ($1,200 budget) around The Unspeakable with 30+ Kamigawa-block cards, leaning on arcane spells, Goryo's Vengeance/Living Death reanimation, Unhallowed Cathar to copy arcane spells, and Cunning Bandit to steal creatures. Trinket builds a $60 Athreos, Shroud-Veiled deck around Sibsig Ceremony, using indestructibility, Myojins, ETB/death triggers, sad-robot ramp, and tutors, hitting a 50-subtype requirement via diverse creature types. Tomer builds a four-color Yidris cascade deck ($1,200) around Living End, cycling creatures into graveyards then mass-reanimating via cascade, padded with treasure-token discard synergies to meet a 20-token requirement. Khan builds a $60 Zaher deck around Axis of Mortality and 25 Tempest Hawks, aggroing opponents then comboing Axis of Mortality with Tainted Remedy to swap life totals for a lethal nuke. The episode closes with listener questions covering boba orders, niche mechanics players have tried, cards they'd grant "run any number" text, handling rules disputes, cards to reprint cheaply, and game-store economics around prereleases and Universes Beyond pricing.

## Key Clips

- We decided to spin wheels that would give us traits that we had to apply to a deck that we had to build. Now it's more you have to have a specific card as opposed to just like overarching themes, and we cut the color combination. The first wheel was deck cost, the second was a spicy card you'd have to build around, and the third was a restriction.
- Alex's spins: a $1,200 deck, The Unspeakable as a core card, and 30 or more cards from a single block. He chose Kamigawa block to get all the arcane spells. The Unspeakable is a 6/7 flying trample for nine mana that cares about arcane spells, and the deck does reanimator: fill the graveyard, then Living Death after a big Buried Alive.
- Cunning Bandit from Kamigawa: when it re-casts a spirit or arcane spell you may put a key counter on it, and at end of turn if there are two or more key counters you may flip it into Azamuki, Treachery Incarnate, which lets you remove a key counter to steal an opponent's creature. Then cast an arcane spell and sack their creature with Unhallowed to casualty it.
- Alex's second deck: $60 budget, Sibsig Ceremony, and 50 or more subtypes. Sibsig Ceremony is a three-mana enchantment that makes creature spells cost two less; when a creature you cast enters, it's destroyed and replaced with a 2/2 black zombie. The commander is Athreos, Shroud-Veiled, who stays indestructible, and the deck runs all four Myojins plus ETB and death effects.
- Tomer's deck: $1,200 budget, Living End, and 20 different tokens. Living End is a suspend sorcery that removes all creature cards from graveyards, sacrifices all creatures, then puts the removed cards into play. He went four colors with Yidris as a cascade commander, cycling and discarding creatures to mass-reanimate them. New Perspectives is the best card since with a full hand all cycling costs become zero.
- Khan's deck: $60 budget, Axis of Mortality, and seven or more copies of a non-land card. He used 25 Tempest Hawks (80 cents each) since any number are allowed and each finds another on combat damage. Commander is Zahur, Glorious Past, using Start Your Engines and the flyers. The combo finish is Axis of Mortality plus Tainted Remedy: swap life totals so the opponent's life gain becomes life loss.
- On rulings disputes in casual EDH: life is too short, just don't play with people you don't like. People say they can't play with anyone other than their playgroup, but make new friends. If someone insists past you looking it up and citing a judge, they're unreasonable and you shouldn't be jamming games with them. On Spell Table, collect the people you like onto Discord and get off as soon as you can.
- On The One Ring: they did test it but a weaker, different version, then changed the design and didn't test it again. It's a flavor fail in 60-card formats where you play one Ring, draw, then play your other Ring and draw again. They also wanted it to be a multi-format all-star, and it's a box-topper RNG loot-box reprint now, which is loot boxes invading box toppers.
