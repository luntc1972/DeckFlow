## Summary

This video analyzes every Commander precon ever released, categorizing cards by function (ramp, removal, draw, recursion, protection) using EDHREC and Archidekt data. It identifies three design eras. The beginning era (2011-2019) ran ~39 lands, 14.6 removal, 9.3 ramp, 9 drawΓÇöcontrol-style decks that lacked acceleration. The expansion era (2020-2022) modernized to 37.6 lands, more ramp (12.6) and draw (11), less removal (13.4). The modern era (2023-present) slims lands further, pushes ramp to 12.8 and adds token generation (5.8 spells) while cutting removal to 12, recursion to 3, protection to 3.13. Overall trend: less waiting, more doing. Wizards designers explain the shiftsΓÇöramp and draw make games more consistent and let players recast commanders and recover after board wipes, while excess removal stagnates games and is least flavorful. The creator distills this into a memorable '10/10/38 blueprint': 10 draw, 10 ramp, 10 removal, 38 lands. Tips: vary card roles within each category (cantrips, engines, burst, refill; staggered ramp costs) for more interesting games, and optimize for enjoyment over pure power. A second host adds that cheap precons upgrade well with a few strong cards.

## Key Clips

- Commander precon designs move through three distinct eras. First, the beginning era from 2011 to 2019 established the baseline with averages of 39 lands, 14.6 removal spells, 9.3 ramp spells, nine card draw spells, 4.05 recursion spells and 3.95 protection spells. These decks were built like control decks that played the long game but didn't accelerate into their endgame quickly.
- The expansion era from 2020 to 2022 is when commander precons exploded from a limited release to 15 decks per year, with a modernized land count of 37.6 lands, a reduction in removal to 13.4, more card draw at 11 spells, and a substantial increase in ramp at 12.6.
- The current modern era from 2023 to present has a slimmer land base, even more reduction of removal at 12 spells, strong card draw at 10.7, even more ramp at 12.8, reduction in recursion to three spells and protection to 3.13, plus a new pattern of token generation across all archetypes at 5.8 token generation spells on average.
- A Wizards designer explained the trend: Ramp is fun. Mana generation is important for commander and for supporting the splashy expensive cards we want precon enjoyers to be excited for. We also want players to realistically be able to recast their commanders more than once in a game.
- On lowering removal, a designer said: Overindexing on removal leaves less room for fun, proactive cards. It also means commanders stick around less often. We embrace a bit more of the battle cruisery style in precons where removal should be used on the most threatening pieces, but games should still proceed forward. Too much removal stagnates them.
- Across more than a decade of decks, the average precon has 10.6 card draw spells, 12.1 ramp spells, and 13 removal spells. This becomes the 10-10-10-38 blueprint: 10 draw, 10 ramp, and 10 removal with 38 lands. Easy to remember, statistically sound, and a great starting point for building decks that play well.
- A tip on filling categories: for card draw, have some great early cantrips like Faithless Looting, some engines, some that give a burst of cards when you have no board like Painful Truths, and some that refill later like X cost draw spells. For ramp, have two mana ramp, a couple three mana ramp, and one or two bigger ramp cards. Variation of role per category makes games more varied and interesting.
- If you ever feel stuck between optimizing and playing what you love, optimize for enjoyment, tell a cool story with your deck and share something about yourself.
