## Summary

This video defends the Eminence keyword, which lets one line of a commander's text work from the command zone, then pairs it with a stronger on-field ability once cast. The creator argues Eminence is a fine, interesting mechanic whose bad reputation stems from specific design mistakes on cards like Edgar Markov and The Ur-Dragon. Both became top commanders by being five-color or three-color "champions" for iconic tribes (vampires, dragons), crowding out lower-color alternatives and commanding high prices. Problems identified: too many colors, generic uninteractible payoffs (free tokens, mana discounts), and creature forms not worth casting. The creator proposes four design rules: stick to two colors or fewer when overshadowing risk exists; raise specificity of trigger or payoff when one is generic (Arahbo's sorcery-speed cat buff is praised as interactable and specific); never directly grant cards or mana from the eminence line; and make the castable commander genuinely desirable. He highlights aggro as Eminence's best fit, since it boosts a deck's floor early while the cast commander compounds advantage later, and sketches custom designs (equipment counters, noncombat-damage boosts, goad, downside abilities) showing the mechanic's untapped potential.

## Key Clips

- **[04:52]** If Ur Dragon had the same eminence text but was mono red or even red blue, suddenly that's an actual niche. But if you instead make a 5 color option that's dramatically better than the others, and if that power doesn't even require casting the card, you destroy a lot of the reason to consider other options. I would lean toward two colors or less on eminence commanders as a rule, especially if they're in a niche where they risk overshadowing other options.
- **[07:50]** Getting an effect without paying for it is notable, and it's important that this effect is specific so as to contain how out of hand it can get. This is true for both the trigger of the eminence ability and the payoff for it—ideally it should be a specific trigger that provides a specific output, but if you're going to make one a bit more general, you really need to be careful to crank up the specificity of the other.
- **[12:15]** The difficulty and specificity of the setup needs to match the power of the payoff. If triggering an eminence ability is easy, the payoff should be less generic and require more ingenuity to take advantage of, as is the case with Oloro. If an eminence ability has a stronger and more generic payoff, it should have a triggering condition that is more specific or more preventable, as is true of Arahbo. When in doubt, designers should go more specific with both.
- **[14:18]** The design rule I'd set here is simple—eminence abilities should not directly give cards or mana. Indirectly is fine—all of the eminence commander abilities are frequently used to generate cards, mana, or both, but there needs to be an extra step involved to increase the specificity and interactivity. Giving a deck these resources totally for free with no extra steps gets you into really dangerous territory for a deck feeling healthy and fitting in.
- **[16:50]** Eminence commanders should be desirable to actually cast. The issue with Edgar and Inalla is that the text on the creature just isn't that great of an amplification of the eminence effect. This is the commander format, so you should be casting your commander. It gives an opportunity for the command zone to offer multiple tiers of benefit, one before your commander is in play and one after, but this can't happen if you never have a reason to ascend past the first tier of power.
- **[18:15]** Aggro is the biggest area of opportunity I see for eminence. There are fast deck styles propped up by the presence of a somewhat fragile commander, leading to glass cannon decks capable of crushing players but falling apart to two removal spells. What eminence does is allow you to split the difference, with the eminence effect boosting a deck's early game and overall floor, and the commander creature offering a way to compound that advantage later in the game.
- **[20:45]** Of the three most popular commanders of the past year, it's an easy to trigger card draw engine, another easy to trigger card draw engine, and a massive mana-granter. WotC should be a little extra careful when it comes to printing things that give direct resources aimed at commander players, whether Eminence is involved or otherwise.
