## Summary

The video argues against the common belief that Wizards of the Coast prints weak, oddball legendary creatures (like Vile Smasher Gleeful Grenadier or Final Fantasy's minor legends) specifically for Commander players. Many such legends see almost no play—under 500 Vile Smasher decks on EDHREC—and aren't unique, powerful, or clearly designed as commanders. The creator's theory: these legends exist instead to make 60-card formats more like Commander. He traces a two-decade cultural shift in gaming toward individual self-expression and RPG-style named heroes—Warcraft 3 heroes, DotA, League, Hearthstone, Overwatch—paralleling Commander's rise. Competitive 60-card Magic offers limited customization (four-of decks, few unique cards), while Commander's singleton format forces high card diversity. By printing many interchangeable legendary creatures that share synergies (e.g., a lifegain/clerics archetype spanning Bloomburrow, Foundations, Final Fantasy, and Spider-Man), Wizards lets 60-card deckbuilders run one-of legends instead of playsets, boosting diversity and self-expression. For deckbuilding: legendary tribal/lifegain synergy pieces increasingly span multiple recent sets and are designed to combine. The creator concedes the pattern could be coincidence or poor design, and criticizes the original 'pigs eat slop' post as needlessly hostile.
