## Summary

This video distinguishes two separate "knobs" in EDH deckbuilding: card/play variation (more different cards drawn game-to-game, which the creator enjoys) versus power-level variance (how widely a deck's power swings moment-to-moment and game-to-game). Defending one does not defend the other. A four-point pipeline model categorizes proactive cards by how imminently they threaten opponents: early-game boosts like Sol Ring (low felt power, easy to play around), mid-game card-advantage engines like The One Ring, late-game mana engines like Bolas's Citadel or Arcane Bombardment, and game-ending combos like Craterhoof Behemoth. Power variance feels sharper further down the pipeline because the table has less time to respond, wrecking threat assessment and turning Commander into a luck game. High-variance "feast or famine" decks arise because humans underestimate the low odds of multiple synergy pieces lining up, and because players treat commander-synergy power cards as a "gift" (e.g., 91% of Yusri decks run Krark's Thumb). On tutors: running them for power cards reduces power-level variance but also reduces play variation. Tutors also reveal which cards you gravitate toward, letting you decide whether a card belongs. Both choices are valid personal preferences.

## Key Clips

- If you take nothing else away from this video, understand that a game with a lot of card variation is different from a game with a lot of power level varianceΓÇöyou can have one without the other, and a defense of one of these things isn't necessarily a defense of the other.
- The power level variance of a deck basically refers to how widely a deck's power level differs moment-to-moment and game-to-game. If a deck has access to a powerful card like Sol Ring or Skull Clamp, it's going to be having a better time on average than if it instead had access to Mind Stone or Rogue's Gloves.
- This is the four point model I like to go withΓÇöyou've got your early game stuff that is more of a temporary boost than anything, you've got your mid-game engines that can start to push out of control if given time, you've got your late-game engines that signal an imminent threat to your opponents, and you've got your truly game-ending cards at the very end. Sol Ring falls right near the beginning of that pipeline; combos and combo-adjacent cards fall at the opposite end.
- The issue with decks that have a high power level variance is that they make threat assessment almost impossible. This information is unknowable if the power of a deck's gameplan is determined by raw chance, and the end result is that commander turns into a luck gameΓÇöWizard-flavored Yahtzee or Dragon-themed Bingo.
- Humans are exceptionally bad at thinking about chance. With six enabler cards and four payoff cards, there's about a 64% chance you'll have one of the enablers, a 49% chance you'll have one of the payoffs, and only a 30% chance you'll have both in the top 15 cardsΓÇöand south of 25% once you account for having the mana to cast them. This is why running cards mainly only good alongside other cards produces a high-variance, feast-or-famine deck.
- Krark's Thumb is run in 91% of Yusri decks. With five coins flipped per turn, average cards drawn goes from 2.5 to 3.75, average damage taken goes from 5 down to 2.5, and the odds of an Omniscience turn are almost eightfold improvedΓÇönow just below a 24% chance. The 91% run rate demonstrates the universality of the good-cards-are-a-gift mentality, even in a casual format.
- In a deck with a handful of more powerful cards, including tutors will likely reduce the power level variance and lead to a smoother game-to-game experience, but it will also reduce card and play variation, making the deck more samey. Not including tutors does the oppositeΓÇögreater variety of plays, but sudden power level spikes when a powerful card is drawn.
- Tutors let you see what cards you're gravitating toward, and they give you extra opportunities to decide how you feel about a card. If you're tutoring for a card a lot, you can ask 'have I been enjoying having this card more often?'
