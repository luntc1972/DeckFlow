## Summary

This deck is built around Baeloth Barrityl, Entertainer paired with the Noble Heritage background, in a red/white goad-and-politics shell. Baeloth forces opponents' creatures with power less than his to attack (goad), and makes treasures when goaded attackers/blockers die. Noble Heritage grows Baeloth via +1/+1 counters each upkeep while granting protection from opponents who add counters, creating an arms race that widens his goad threshold turn over turn. The plan is to force constant combat so opponents fight each other, then finish via a buffed BaelothΓÇöSunforger, Rogue's Passage (unblockable), Silver Shroud Costume, and equipment recursion (Pure Steel Paladin) rebuild him after removal. Heavy protection (Pariah Shield, Deflecting Palm, Comeuppance, indestructible effects) covers incidental board wipes since recasting a 5-mana commander is costly. Karmic redirection finishers (Brash Taunter, Boros Reckoner) and goad-payoff cards (Take the Bait, Hot Pursuit, Agitator Ant, Prisoner's Dilemma) reinforce the theme; Reign of Riches turns treasures into cascade card advantage to offset red/white's weak draw (supplemented by Esper Sentinel, Nelly Borca). Card draw and getting Baeloth recast are the deck's main weaknesses. Rated roughly bracket 2, no game changersΓÇöprioritizing theme, politics, and fun over reliable wins.

## Key Clips

- Baloth Entertainer is five mana for a 2/5 elf shaman. Creatures your opponents control with power less than Baloth's are goaded. As long as Baloth is on the battlefield, any creature your opponents play, even with haste, has to check whether it meets the threshold and is forced to attack. Right off the bat, anything with power one has to fight, so all those 1/1 tokens have to be used immediately.
- Noble Heritage: when this creature enters and at the beginning of your upkeep, each player may put two +1/+1 counters on a creature they control, and for each opponent who does, I gain protection from them until my next turn. I always put Noble Heritage on Baloth, so the goad threshold climbs from power one to power three, then five, seven. Opponents put counters on the commander or value piece they want to keep out of combat, which means I'm protected from them. Their stuff is goaded by me and I'm protected from them, so I come out on top either way.
- Goad is a control effect to some, but I disagree, because this makes you play the game. Battlecruiser magic stalls when everyone just holds up blockers and nobody attacks. Baloth forces it: we're going to combat, life totals drop, people make deals to team up against me. A counterspell pile is no one playing the game except me; at least with goad everyone's swinging at each other.
- Prisoner's Dilemma is five mana: each opponent secretly chooses Silence or Snitch. If all chose Silence it deals four to each; if all chose Snitch it deals eight to each; otherwise it deals 12 to each opponent who chose Silence. It has flashback so you can do it twice. In one game two low-life opponents both expected to choose Silence, but one snitched, so the Silence player died while the snitcher took no damage and then killed me.
- I'd peg this deck at bracket two with no game changers. It started as a roughly $100 proxy build because I didn't know if I wanted to play Magic, printed on printer paper behind lands. It's more thematic about politics and goading than a serious win condition. The only real win condition is Rogue's Passage, since 'target creature can't be blocked' lets Baloth get in.
- Propaganda is the bane of this deck, more than removal. There's a rules interaction where if you have to pay a cost to attack, you don't actually have to attack, so goad gets shut off. Angel of the Dire Hour and similar 'pay to attack' effects counter the deck the same way. Game changers and brackets aside, those are the cards that hurt most.
- Baloth gets removed often, so I run a Voltron package and equipment to rebuild him. Pure Steel Paladin makes equip cost zero so I can re-equip everything onto him after a recast. Silver Shroud Costume is one of the best cards because it's equipment that gives shroud the turn he comes down and makes him unblockable, acting like a protection spell plus evasion. Sunforger makes him four stronger and lets you unattach to search for Deflecting Palm or Comeuppance.
- Reign of Riches says the first spell you cast each turn paid for with treasure mana has cascade. Since Baloth makes treasures, this is effectively extra draw ΓÇö most of the time you hit a low-cost artifact or creature like Esper Sentinel and become card positive. People call it five mana, but it's really three because the treasures pay for it, and even less across multiple turns.
