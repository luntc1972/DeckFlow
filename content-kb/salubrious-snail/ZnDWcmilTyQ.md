## Summary

A precon review of the Bloomburrow group hug deck led by Ms. Bumbleflower, whose ability draws a card for a target opponent on each spell cast but rewards the pilot when triggered a second time per turn, pushing a two-spells-per-turn blue plan. The reviewer reads the deck as a politics-heavy +1/+1 counter beatdown that hands cards to the weakest opponent while growing evasive threats; Bumbleflower grants counters and flying. Strengths: solid card quality, several strong counter payoffs (Rishkar, Kalonian Hydra), 14 ramp and 15 draw cards, a robust, well-fixed manabase (though roughly a third taplands), and decent interaction (2 counterspells, 3 wipes, 5 removal). Main criticism: the deck is unfocused, padded with generic group-hug cards that don't serve Bumbleflower's selective-gift, counter-beatdown identity, and is slightly too high-curved. The reviewer makes 20 budget swaps (nothing over $3): cutting symmetrical resource-givers (Rites of Flourishing, Kwain, Selvala), overcosted or weak cards, and adding cheap counter synergies (Conclave Mentor, Kami of the Whispered Hopes, Dreamtide Whale, Incubation Druid) plus interaction (Negate, Brokers Confluence) and lifegain (Envoy of the Ancestors) to win evasive races. He praises selective hugging for creating more dynamic, equal games.

## Key Clips

- The commander for this deck is the honorable Ms. Bumbleflower. Her effect is quite cool and unique. The line "Whenever you cast a spell, target opponent draws a card" is wild. If you don't cast exactly two spells per turn, she gives your opponents more cards than she gives to you. The second spell clause gives you cards when the ability triggers for the second time each turn, which hones in the blue identity of this card.
- In the decklist there are a fair number of decently chunky beaters, most of which would enjoy being given +1/+1 counters and flying. There are 5 cards that utilize Bloomburrow's "gift" mechanic, 11 other cards I'd describe as huggy, and 6 cards that are vaguely political. This deck looks to gradually develop beaters and value engines while currying favor with opponents, right up until it starts to snowball out of control.
- The +1/+1 counter package has some real all-stars: Rishkar Peema Renegade is a sweet ramp tool and Kalonian Hydra is a counter-pumping bomb that will run away with games if left unchecked. There are 14 cards I'd categorize as ramp and 15 cards I'd categorize as draw.
- The average curve out this deck manages is far better than in either of the other precons I've looked at. Of the 38 lands, 9 always enter tapped and 7 sometimes do, averaging out to roughly a one-third tapland quantity. The manabase is similar to the Outlaws at Thunder Junction precon, with a healthy land count and robust color-fixing.
- This deck has two counterspells, three board wipes, five single target removal spells, and a smattering of other cards that slow opponents, defend against major attacks, and protect key pieces. The deck's plan of building up large, evasive threats means life total races won't be uncommon, so having a fog around might be quite helpful.
