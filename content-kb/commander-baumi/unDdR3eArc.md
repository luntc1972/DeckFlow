## Summary

This video argues that Rhystic Study—and similarly powerful staples like Sol Ring, Mana Crypt, Vampiric Tutor, and Smothering Tithe—are poor Commander inclusions despite their raw strength, because strongest doesn't equal best in a social format. The presenter reframes deckbuilding with a flavor scale (1 = optimizing purely to win, 10 = full thematic commitment with no regard for winning) plus an 'optimization constant' placed around 7. This creates two zones: power-side decks grow stronger as you optimize them, while theme-side decks grow weaker as you lean into flavor. He illustrates with two mono-green decks: a Saw commander deck optimized purely for power, and a Gargos Hydra deck that becomes weaker but more thematic as he adds bad Hydras. Powerful 'auto-include' staples create friction by shoving otherwise theme-side decks briefly into power territory without justification. He values justification, playing to win honestly within a deck's theme, and avoiding feel-bad starts like turn-one Mana Crypt. His advice: players should clearly declare which side they're on, and theme players should stay consistently on the theme side. He rates his own decks around 7 on the flavor scale.

## Key Clips

- I don't think Rhystic Study is a good card for Commander. Rhystic Study is incredibly powerful, it's one of the strongest cards in the game, but strongest isn't necessarily the best criteria for how good a card is for Commander. Commander is a social format and Rhystic Study introduces a lot of social friction.
- What you will often see when discussing power levels in Commander is a very simple graph where we're just optimizing to win, and the higher our optimization to win, the more powerful the deck becomes. Most rankings start at power level five, which is generally precons, because you can just go buy a precon and play at a precon power level. As we go past that we reach high power, until we eventually reach cEDH where the decks are all about winning.
- I think this logic is quite flawed. It's not inherently wrong, it just leaves out an incredibly important aspect of Commander, which is flavor. Most players are trying to build flavorful decks and optimize their decks for flavor reasons. So the new assumption is that everybody is always trying to maximize the optimization of their deck, but instead of optimizing just for win percentage, players are optimizing for flavor.
- We define one on the flavor scale as no theme and optimizing to win. As we move along the flavor scale, winning becomes increasingly less important, so once we reach a 10 winning isn't a priority whatsoever. A 10 might be a deck made up of only men with beards — a deck that cares entirely about its theme, not about winning.
- I've got two monogreen decks. My Six deck is optimized for power because it doesn't really have any flavor other than I want to use my commander well, and that's easy because Six is very powerful. My Galos Vicious Watcher deck is a Hydra deck — I play like 24 hydras, which is high because hydras are terrible. The more hydras I put into it, the weaker it becomes, but the more strongly emphasized on its theme. So if a deck rests on the power side, optimizing for flavor makes it more powerful; if it rests on the theme side, optimizing for flavor makes it weaker.
- Cards like Rhystic Study create pain points because they're often included without consideration for flavor, which pushes decks that should be comfortably within the theme side into the power side, even just for a moment, and that can be frustrating. You can play Rhystic Study in a tax theme deck and nobody would complain, but if you just include it in an otherwise thematic deck, that feels worse, like you've included this power thing without proper justification.
- I don't play with Sol Ring, and this is exactly why. Sol Ring is a power card, the most powerful card in the format, that is just randomly included in all decks even in strongly thematic decks. I cannot understand why Sol Ring is in every precon when it creates negative experiences — precons are incredibly thematic decks and Sol Ring doesn't fit the theme.
- I want us all to play garbage and then play to win as hard as we can. When I had 10 Gates and Maze's End on the field and could win at any time but said this is not Maze's End to keep the game going, that's not fun — nobody wants a win handed to them. Decks that play on the theme side need to stay consistently on the theme side, otherwise you create frustrations. Players who want to play on the power side should clearly declare that, and players who play on the theme side should clearly declare that too.
