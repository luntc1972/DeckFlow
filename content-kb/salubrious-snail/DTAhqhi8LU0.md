## Summary

This video argues Commander players should treat lands as a real part of the deck and be intentional about land count, which is often higher than internet advice suggests. It presents Frank Karsten's format-agnostic land-count regression formula (built from 60-card data, factoring mana curve, cheap draw, and cheap ramp) as a starting point, then Karsten's EDH-specific simulation model, which recommends running at least 37 and up to 42 lands to curve out. Key principles: you can run fewer lands if your commander gives consistent early card draw/filtering (e.g. a Rona deck, a Yasharn deck); but if your commander costs 4+ and draws cards, or the deck is heavy on expensive draw, run MORE lands, since big draw cuts through extra lands and reduces screw. Colors and symbol density also matter: without fetches/shocks you must play fewer colors or more lands. Karsten's 89+M% symbol standard should be strict for pivotal on-curve cards (commander, ramp) but relaxed for later cards (finishers, combo pieces, removal). Shorthand: ~30 symbols great access, 25 good, 20 decent, 15 unreliable. Examples: Meria (37 lands), Glissa (41 lands). Use taplands, utility lands, and MDFCs to raise land count.
