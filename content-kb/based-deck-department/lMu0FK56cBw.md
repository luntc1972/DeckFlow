## Summary

Commander: Sylvok/"EMTT" (Sulk of the Third Seat), a Dimir graveyard spellslinger that flashes back instants/sorceries from graveyard when an opponent loses life, reduces their cost (3-drops become 1), and lets sorceries be cast at instant speed. Build method: envision endgame board state and work backward; use a character-sheet with 5-6 buckets and the 33-card technique. Cut ramp (kept Mind Stone, Bonder's Ornament, Sol Ring, Cryptolith Fragment) since the commander self-ramps and you don't want him early. Cut all mill (removed Millikin, Deranged Assistant) — milling win-cons is disastrous; use surveil/loot instead. Counters that synergize with graveyard: Circular Logic, Countervailing Winds, Swallowed by the Lava (single-pip 3-drops). Draw engines added late: Tibalt's Judgment, Fang Dragon/Secrets of the Dead, Enhanced Surveillance. Life-loss sources: prefer controllable single-trigger (Blight Speaker, Cryptolith Fragment, Vhal) over conditional triggers — control matters. Looting via Forbidden Alchemy, Thirst for Discovery, Ledger Shredder, Likeness Looter. Removal plus wraths (Wash Out, Consuming Tide, Evacuation). Ensure 11+ proactive 2-drops for agency. Win-cons weakest: Fanatic Daniel + wrath, Rise of the Tide + Wonder, Lord of the Nazgul, Shark Typhoon, Talrand. Bracket 2-3. Upgrades: Demonic Tutor, Fierce Guardianship, Force of Will, Gifts Ungiven, Deadly Rollick, Soul Shatter, Fell the Profane, Leela.

## Key Clips

- **[01:39]** When his ability triggers from our opponents losing life, it overrides general timing rules and allows us to play sorceries at instant speed. Card advantage and cost reduction are great, but when it comes to whimsy, this part of his ability really stands out.
- **[01:55]** Whenever I'm brewing a new deck like this, I like to start the process by looking at the commander and envisaging the endgame board state or how I'm hoping to win and then working backwards. Everything in my deck should in some way facilitate that.
- **[04:06]** I didn't really care about casting EMTT until I had a bunch of stuff in my graveyard, which typically was after turn five or six, by which point I had more than enough mana. Pair that with his built-in ramp once he's in play, and the case for ramp in this deck is pretty weak. So I cut the ramp.
- **[04:33]** How much mill absolutely sucked compared to surveil or drawing and discarding. I had a test game where I milled every win condition in the first few turns. This made me remove all instances of mill and find other solutions for getting cards into the graveyard.
- **[05:58]** Circular logic, counterveiling winds, and swallowed by Leviathan all cost three mana, which when reduced if cast from a graveyard is now one mana. This is a sweet spot for many of our spells.
- **[06:46]** I hate lands that come into play tapped in most cases, but we have almost no turn one play, so the opportunity cost for an opening hand with a tapped land here is pretty minimal.
- **[07:17]** Many of our life loss sources like Blight Speaker or Cryptolith Fragment only get us a single trigger per turn cycle, but critically they let us control and decide exactly when that trigger is. Cards that trigger on opponents' creatures dying lack that control — we're a control deck and I prefer having control of our game plan.
- **[10:32]** The lack of ramp meant I needed proactive plays. Feed the swarm or gate weren't going to do much when the table goes mana rock pass. I had to go back and ensure I had 11 or more two-cost spells I could cast proactively so it didn't feel like my game started on turn three.
