## Summary

The creator dissects using repetitive board wipes as a commander gameplan, using a proposed Child of Alara tribal Gates deck (55 lands, ramp, draw, Maze's End, Crackling Perimeter, beatdown, big lifegain, only 5 interaction slots, 2 counterspells) as the cautionary example. Core claim: board wipes are "asymmetric" — nominally symmetrical but hitting decks very unevenly. They wreck low-to-mid power synergistic midrange decks but do nothing against combo, aggro abusing your life total, high land counts, or instant/sorcery value engines, so a wipe-reliant deck stomps casuals yet flails and loses to everything else. He extends this to his Glissa deck (homage to a mono-black Sheoldred deck) which runs 8 wipes; he says a better build would run 3–5 wipes plus tutors, but he avoids unconditional tutors as bland. Key heuristic: be deliberate about your interaction package — holes should be intentional, not incidental (his Meria equipment aggro deck intentionally skips hard removal for big creatures, planning to kill the player; fast blue decks intentionally run non-creature counters). Final principle: stax effects are a better bulk include than board wipes because stax chokes stronger, resource-planned decks (Archon of Emeria) while wipes only punish weaker ones (Plague Wind).

## Key Clips

- **[01:31]** In commander, board wipes are an "asymmetric" effect — literally symmetrical, but they impact different players massively differently. A low-mid power synergistic midrange deck feels them brutally, but a lot of deck types just won't care as much.
- **[01:59]** Board wipes won't stop combos, they won't prevent aggro decks from abusing your life total, they won't slow down large land counts, and they won't shut down opponents generating a lot of instant and sorcery-based value.
- **[04:36]** A better version of my Glissa deck would probably run 3–5 board wipes instead of 8, with tutors to bridge the gap — but years as a mono-black player taught me unconditional tutors are bland, so I don't play them.
- **[05:06]** The real point: be mindful constructing your deck. Think about your interaction package and what it's good or bad against. Ideally it's well-balanced, and if there are holes they should be intentional rather than incidental.
- **[05:24]** My Meria equipment deck has very little hard removal for big creatures — a calculated hole. It's an aggro deck aiming for early board presence, so its answer to later threats is to deal with the player or politic around the threat.
- **[06:36]** Running a load of stax effects is a better idea than running a load of board wipes: the decks shut down by an Archon of Emeria are generally stronger and more carefully built than those shut down by a Plague Wind.
- **[06:53]** You can build a deck with a bunch of stax effects and a win plan that outruns a midrange deck, but you can't build a deck full of board wipes with a win plan that outruns a combo deck.
