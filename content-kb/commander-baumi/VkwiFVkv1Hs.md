## Summary

This video presents an Aragorn, the King of Gondor commander deck built around the monarchy mechanic and an aggressive, low-to-the-ground creature strategy with a flavorful Spirit kindred theme (Aragorn leading an army of the dead). Aragorn (4-mana 4/4, vigilance, lifelink, Jeskai) establishes the monarchy once on entry; it then sticks for the game, so few monarchy-referencing cards are needed. Aragorn also disables a blocker on attack and makes himself unblockable while monarch. The deck prioritizes card draw to hit land drops every turn, leaning on Coastal Piracy and its clones (combat-damage draw) plus evasive flyers. Board protection comes from Selfless Spirit, Drogskol Captain, Guardian of Faith, Spectral Adversary, and Rattlechains, with recursion from Mentor of the Meek-style threats and Storm of Souls. The build favors counterspells (Mwonvuli/Mausoleum Wanderer, Spell Queller, Malevolent Hermit) over removal, using them only against board wipes and unbeatable threats to avoid drawing aggression. Wins come from Lords and anthems (Patron of the Valiant-style effects, Bennie/Banner of Kinship) building incremental board power, not single big threats. Minimal ramp; catch-up ramp via Surveyor's Scope. Budget ~Γé¼60, mostly blue/white. Emphasizes flavor-driven, non-template Commander deckbuilding.

## Key Clips

- The monarchy is the best mechanic in Commander. It encourages active and aggressive play while being easy to understand and simple to track. Using it well is one of the benefits of playing aggressive, low-to-the-ground creature decks.
- Aragorn is a four-mana 4/4 human Noble in Jeskai colors. He has vigilance and lifelink, and when he enters we become the Monarch. While we are the Monarch, at the end of our turn we draw a card; opponents can steal the monarchy by dealing combat damage to us. There's no way to get rid of the monarchy once introduced ΓÇö it sticks around for the rest of the game, so we only need to cast Aragorn once to establish it.
- When Aragorn attacks, up to one target creature can't block, so he can disable problematic creatures protecting the current Monarch. If he attacks while we are the Monarch, nothing can block, giving us complete control over combat and an unmatched ability to push for damage.
- We only play very little ramp since during the first few turns we want to establish an aggressive board, and ramping would take too many cards out of our hand. It's essential to play a land every turn ΓÇö missing even a single land drop is a game-ending disadvantage ΓÇö so card draw is needed just to keep playing creatures and hitting land drops.
- Coastal piracy is a four-mana enchantment that lets us draw a card whenever one of our creatures deals combat damage to an opponent. Even with a small board of three to four creatures, it draws three to four extra cards each turn. To use it we need creatures that can hit opponents, so we choose creatures with built-in evasion ΓÇö luckily a lot of spirits have flying.
- Several spirits protect our board: Selfless Spirit can be sacrificed to give all creatures indestructible; Drogskol Captain is a Lord that also grants hexproof; Guardian of Faith flashes in to phase out our creatures in response to a board wipe; and Rattlechains lets us play Spirits at instant speed so we can flash them in after sorcery-speed removal has passed.
- We kill opponents with Lords and anthems rather than big standalone creatures, which give opponents easy removal targets and draw aggressive counterplay. Start with small utility creatures and incrementally increase the power of the entire board. Banner of Kinship can be a game-ender since it buffs based on the number of our creatures.
- Surveyor's Scope is a two-mana artifact that taps and exiles to search for a basic land for each opponent that controls at least two more lands than us. The lands enter untapped and it can be activated on an opponent's turn, so we can use it to catch someone off guard with counterspells. Because we play bounce lands and don't ramp ourselves, those opportunities aren't rare.
