## Summary

The video reviews three Magic: The Gathering Tarkir: Dragonstorm cards from a Commander player's perspective. Ugin, Eye of the Storms is a seven-mana colorless planeswalker whose cast trigger exiles a colored permanent (hard to counter—needs a stifle on the cast trigger), repeats on every colorless spell cast, and offers card draw/lifegain, free colorless mana, and a minus-11 ultimate that casts your whole colorless deck for free. The speaker rates Ugin as likely the best of a weak planeswalker group but worries it needs a dedicated colorless/mana-rock deck and probably still requires a preceding board wipe to function; at seven mana it underperforms unless set up. The standout is Teval, the Balanced Scale, a four-mana 4/4 flyer (from the Sultai precon) that mills, returns a land from graveyard, and—critically—creates a 2/2 black Zombie Druid token whenever cards leave your graveyard, with no restrictions. It synergizes with dredge (life from the loam), reanimation, Titan's Nest, decaying soil, and Druid payoffs, making it a premier Sultai value commander. The speaker calls Teval the set's best card. Tevesh-like delve commander mentioned is dismissed as bad.

## Key Clips

- Ugin, Eye of the Storms is a seven mana colorless planeswalker with seven loyalty that when he's cast exiles up to one target permanent that's one or more colors. It's a cast trigger exile removal making it incredibly difficult to interact with; a counter spell that only hits Ugin doesn't do anything—it would need to be able to stifle the cast trigger itself.
- Once Ugin hits the board, whenever we cast a colorless spell we get to do this again. In a colorless deck that has access to a lot of cheap mana rocks or some eggs, triggering this repeatedly is not difficult at all, and that lets us clear out most problematic permanents our opponents might have.
- His ultimate is a minus 11 that lets us search our deck for any number of colorless non-lands and cast them without paying their mana cost. If Ugin is still alive at that point, then each of those also triggers his passive, guaranteeing a victory.
- There's this interesting phenomenon where in Commander you have 40 life, so when you deal a little bit of damage to a player it doesn't feel like it matters that much, but when you deal a little bit of damage to a planeswalker it dies. So players want to attack planeswalkers because it feels like they're making more progress.
- In practice I worry that Ugin needs a very specific deck to function. We need colorless spells in hand to trigger him, the most common being mana rocks. But Ugin is also seven mana—we need to play the mana rocks to even cast him at a reasonable time. I think it's likely Ugin will still require a preceding board wipe to be properly utilized.
- Teval, the Balanced Scale is a four mana 4/4 with flying. Whenever he attacks we mill four cards and then get to return a land from our graveyard directly to the battlefield tapped. Commanders that generate land advantage are quite resilient to removal because the lands they ramp pay for the recast, at which point they start ramping more lands again.
- Teval also says that whenever one or more cards leave our graveyard we create a 2/2 black Zombie Druid creature token. The zombie creation doesn't have any restrictions and doesn't specify the cards have to be exiled—they just have to leave the graveyard, so reanimation and hand or deck recursion both work. Cards like Life from the Loam, Titan's Nest, and decaying soil all generate zombies through their graveyard interactions.
- The tokens Teval creates have relevant creature types. Zombie enables grave crawler loops. Druid is surprisingly relevant as well—Gilt-Leaf Archdruid and Seton, Krosan Protector are usually limited because Druid isn't a supported creature type, but with Teval making Druids everywhere those payoffs come online. I sincerely believe Teval will be a new premier Sultai good-stuff commander.
