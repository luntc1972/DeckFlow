## Summary

This deck tech covers the creator's Liesa, Forgotten Archangel commander deck, built April 2023 to make opponents fixate on their dwindling life total. Liesa dodges command tax and drains opponents whenever they cast spells, pairing with hatebears that gum up the game and chip damage so a low life total constrains opponents' lifepay choices. Early versions failed: the original felt more stax than Liesa and lacked card draw; a 2024 rework added the initiative mechanic plus value engines but cut damage tools and required an awkward Undercity prop. Swapping to Breena, the Demagogue made the deck a front-loaded tempo/aggro build reliant on creatures sticking, losing the inevitability feel after board wipes. The successful current version returns to Liesa: adds real damage (Ankh of Mishra, Wound Reflection, Suture Priest, Hunted Horror) and dense draw (Enduring Innocence, Barrowgoyf), and cuts heavy rule-of-law hatebears like Hushbringer and Archon of Emeria because they reduce opponents' spellcasting, undermining Liesa's life tax. Accepting a bracket-3 identity sharpened the deck. Lesson: experimenting with extreme command-zone choices like Breena helps clarify what you actually want from a deck.

## Key Clips

- I wanted to make people think about their life total a lot. An aggressive gameplan can mean the ability to knock opponents out of the game early, but even before that happens, the first 20-30 damage dealt to an opponent already influences the way they play the game. It makes them play more conservatively, attack less confidently, and get substantially less comfort with the lifepay tools that are such a defining feature of commander.
- A low life total especially does that if I can keep around a creature that drains opponents whenever they cast spells. My command zone being a command-tax-dodger who drains anybody who casts spells. Liesa is my followup that can really start to cut down life totals in this slowed environment.
- In 60 card formats, hatebears are a relatively common sight. Death and Taxes is a type of legacy deck specifically built around this sort of creature. In EDH, hatebears are still useful for their effects, but the attached 2/2 or 3/3 body typically isn't nearly as helpful. This is where the command zone comes into play—my hatebears can whittle down life totals a little and gum up the works, and then Liesa is my followup.
- The initiative is a mechanic that causes a player to travel through the Undercity. It's like Monarch, but needlessly complicated for entirely aesthetic purposes. I didn't have an undercity token, so I drew one on a full-sized piece of paper. A commander deck can easily be this pretty compact, self-contained thing, and here I'd introduced an extra mechanic that required me to use this full sheet of paper prop. My deck only ran 5 initiative cards and didn't draw any of them in some games.
- The nature of a Breena command zone is dramatically different from the nature of a Liesa command zone. On some level Liesa is an inevitability commander, an unstoppable life total tax that always represents a way to grind the game toward a single-digit-hitpoint slogfest. By contrast, Breena is very much a tempo commander. The profile of that damage is front-loaded and reliant on creatures sticking around on board. If some of my creatures got removed, or if there was a board wipe, all of that damage momentum would slow to a stall.
- Step one was going back to Liesa. A big piece of this was the beatdown suite—an issue with previous iterations was that the 99 didn't actually do a good job getting life totals low enough for Liesa to matter. I picked out a bunch of new damage options. Ankh of Mishra, Wound Reflection, Suture Priest, and iconic political aggro tool Hunted Horror—it's effectively 13/13 in stats for 2 mana. Enduring Innocence, Assemble the Players, Elegy Acolyte, and Barrowgoyf.
- The biggest area I tweaked was dead-simple: I cut a lot of hate-bears. Stax in general, and rule-of-law effects in particular, have this weird clash with Liesa because they cause opponents to cast fewer spells, but Liesa wants opponents to be casting more spells. This is similar to lessons with my other decks—when I'm making a deck to celebrate Astral Slide value engines, I don't want to run more generic sources of draw that step on the toes of what I'm celebrating.
- The year with Breena in the command zone was a really important part of figuring out what I wanted. Playing with more extreme command zone choices can be a great exercise in figuring out what you do or don't enjoy about your deck, and it's absolutely worth a try if you have a deck you're not happy with.
