## Summary

This is a thought-experiment deck centered on Pantlaza, Sun-Favored, a Naya 4/4 dinosaur that, when it or another dinosaur enters, lets you Discover X equal to its toughness. Discover exiles cards until hitting one with mana value X or less, which you cast free or take to hand. The trick: by playing no other cards at the discovered cost (four) or below, you reliably hit specific combo pieces. The deck runs 79 lands plus terrible enter-tapped, depletion, and legendary-only lands as makeshift ramp, since normal ramp is banned by the constraint. Plan: ramp Pantlaza out by turn four (rarely three), then discover into flicker creatures (Felidar Guardian, Icewind Stalwart, Restoration Angel) to re-trigger Pantlaza, find Eldritch Evolution, sacrifice Pantlaza for Kiki-Jiki, and execute a Kiki flicker combo for infinite hasty creatures. Trumpeting Carnosaur (discovers five) backs up the line. The combo is fragile: any counterspell, removal, fog, or stax breaks it, and drawing key pieces early bricks the deck. Across bracket-four and cEDH games on MTGO, it won nearly every game except where one opponent recognized and focused it down. The creator stresses it's not fun to play against and should never be built.

## Key Clips

- Today we are playing Pantlaza, Sun-Favored. Pantlaza is a Naya five-mana 4/4 dinosaur that, once a turn when it or another dinosaur enters the battlefield, lets us discover X where X is that creature's toughness. Discover means we exile cards from the top of our library until we hit a card with mana value X or less, and then we get to either cast it for free or put it into our hand.
- Discover and cascade — but we're using discover today — is a really interesting keyword because it lets us target specific cards in our deck to always discover into. All we need to do is play no other cards at our discovered mana value or below. Since Pantlaza discovers for four on ETB, we can't play any other cards that cost four or less mana.
- Step one is ramping Pantlaza into play as quickly as possible to limit our opponents' resources. We can't play normal ramp, but we have lands that tap for two like Ancient Tomb and City of Traitors, plus Untaidake the Cloud Keeper which adds two colorless only for legendary spells, and depletion lands that enter tapped and can only be used twice. We do still play 79 lands.
- Split cards like Road to Ruin have individually low mana costs but a combined high mana value, meaning we will discover right past them. Adventures, rooms, channeling — there are not a lot of cards we can play, but some exist. Relying on those and cost-reduction effects we can even fit interaction like Blasphemous Act and Hour of Revelation.
- Here's the combo. We play Felidar Guardian, Icewind Stalwart, and Restoration Angel — the same creature three times for redundancy. When they enter, they exile another creature we control and immediately return it. We discover one of these, cast it, and flicker Pantlaza, which lets us discover again and look for Eldritch Evolution.
- Eldritch Evolution has us sacrifice a creature to tutor a creature onto the battlefield that costs up to two mana more. We sacrifice Pantlaza and search out Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker. Kiki-Jiki has haste and can tap to create a hasted copy of a creature. We use this on a flicker creature, then use the copy's ETB to flicker Kiki, resetting him — creating an arbitrary amount of haste creatures to kill our opponents. Standard Kiki cheeky combo.
- With just these cards it can fail, because if we discover Eldritch Evolution first we won't have a flicker creature on the board. That's why we also play Trumpeting Carnosaur, a six-mana creature that discovers for five. If we hit Eldritch Evolution first, we sacrifice Pantlaza, grab the Carnosaur and discover five, hitting either Kiki or a flicker creature, then dig out the remaining pieces.
- If not interacted with, this is a guaranteed victory, but it's trivially easy to interrupt while being clearly telegraphed. Any counterspell at any point just kills us dead, our creatures can be removed to break the combo, and this is a combo that folds to fog. We can only combo a single time, and if Kiki ever gets removed we have no way to win anymore.
