## Summary

The video argues that the more commander-centric a Commander deck is, the more fragile it becomes, forcing unfun "protect the mage" gameplayΓÇöillustrated by a Vivi storm deck that uses cheap spells to ramp Vivi, wins off Dragon Storm, and spends the rest protecting him. The proposed fix is to "mid-rangeify" the deck: shift from going all-in on one role to playing permanents and flexible cards that each serve multiple roles (attacker, removal, card advantage), letting the deck flex aggressive, controlling, or grindy as needed. Examples cited include Blood Tithe Harvester, Graveyard Trespasser, Immersturm Predator-style draw-on-damage flyers, and a Sword Coast Serpent that bounces then returns as a threat. The method: add a pivot synergizing with your win condition (here, more dragons since Dragon Storm finds them), organize the deck into ramp/draw/removal/synergy/payoff packages, then convert about half of each package into multi-role permanents. The pilot cuts one-shot cantrips (Ponder, Faithless Looting, Preordain, Frantic Search) for sticky card draw (Kindred Discovery, Frost Cliff Siege), and swaps temporary mana (Rite of Flame, Mana Vault) for sustained ramp (Oza's Incubator, Carnelian Orb, Galazeth Prismari) plus more lands. Result: a deck with flexible threats and fewer feel-bad turns.

## Key Clips

- The more commander centric a deck is, the more vulnerable your strategy becomes. A Vivv deck will generally have some cheap spells to trigger Vivv to make a ton of mana, then a great payoff to end the game like Dragon Storm, while the rest of the deck is dedicated to protecting Vivv because the deck is so reliant on Vivv sticking around.
- This leads to the classic protect the king gameplay that is often unfun as the pilot's forced to play setup and find a window and end the game abruptly while everyone else is pressured to throw everything they have to stop that player at any moment.
- Mid-range decks are defined by their ability to flex between an aggressive role against control, a controlling role against aggro, and a value attrition game in the mirror. And every card plays multiple roles.
- To mid-rangeify your deck is to shift your deck from going all in on doing the thing or just committing to one role to playing more permanence and spells that allow you to threaten the table in multiple ways. It's about adding pivot game plans that we can flex to.
- We know there are classic buckets of cards in commander that make a deck run well: ramp, draw, removal, and your synergy cards to actually do the thing. The easiest way to give your deck a pivot and mid-rangeify it is we find permanents that do more than one thing.
- Organize your current deck into our core categories: ramp, draw, removal, synergy, payoffs. And once you've done that, go through each category and try to convert half the package into our mid-range flexible cards.
- I cut spells like Ponder, Faithless Looting, Preordain, and Frantic Search, all of which help me dig for the right card but don't give me more material to play with. I replaced them with cards like Kindred Discovery and Frost Cliff Siege because they're cards that stick around and draw more than once.
- For some of my mana cards, I cut temporary mana like Rite of Flame and added Oza's incubator, Carnelian Orb of Dragonkind, and Galazeth Prismari. I also slightly increased my land count, letting me scale mana over time instead of relying on burst turns.
