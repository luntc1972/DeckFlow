## Summary

A reviewer lists the five most common EDH deckbuilding mistakes from years of supporter deck reviews. #5 Unfocused macro goals: decks built around a synergy pillar (landfall, lifegain, +1/+1 counters, self-discard) but with payoffs scattered in every direction; practice restraint and cut off-plan cards—e.g., in a landfall token-beatdown deck, incremental-damage cards like Tunneling Geopede or lifegain like Grazing Gladehart dilute the plan. #4 Vulnerable to hate: add durability without dedicated protection spells via counterspells, flicker (Ghostway), protection-from as evasion, boots granting protection+haste, haste sources (Anger, Arena of Glory; blue uses Leyline of Anticipation or Alchemist's Refuge to play at instant speed), and ramp. #3 Not proactive enough: control/goad/theft decks over-invest in managing opponents; goad's value drops with each added copy—treat goad as defense, not a wincon; theft (Admiral Beckett Brass, Gonti) isn't a reliable closer. Categorize cards by role (Archidekt) to avoid subconscious bias (e.g., Kardur under removal). #2 Not reactive enough: slower midrange decks need ~20 interaction pieces; hyper-proactive decks can run 5-8; tie removal to synergy (Sharuum ETB removal). #1 Dead cards/micro-unfocus: thin synergies and amplifiers (God-Pharaoh's Gift, Traverse the Outlands, Fiery Emancipation) need a reliable, resilient base.

## Key Clips

- **[02:20]** Adding a lifegain card like Grazing Gladehart to a landfall beatdown deck just adds fluff that doesn't serve the deck's goals, even though lifegain has some theoretical synergy.
- **[02:44]** A necessary lesson is restraint: knowing when to exclude a card even when it theoretically has a bit of synergy with what you're building.
- **[05:00]** The worst feeling is a creature eating dirt before doing anything; if you want to attack or use a tap ability, giving it haste fully prevents that.
- **[05:15]** A blue player who can't run Anger can run Leyline of Anticipation or Alchemist's Refuge—saving pivotal setup plays for an opposing end step dramatically increases the odds you get to use them.
- **[07:07]** If you add a goad card, its effectiveness is inversely proportional to the number of goad cards already in your deck.
- **[08:17]** Categorize cards by the role they're playing rather than by mechanic; a category of seven goad cards can give you subconscious bias that makes you overly comfortable with the state of your deck.
- **[12:41]** A faster midrange deck can run mana-efficient 1-for-1 removal like Path to Exile, Nature's Claim, and Bitter Triumph, since it may only need to cut one or two threats to stay in control.
- **[15:25]** The key for amplifier cards is how consistently the thing you're amplifying will be present and sticking around—if it's fragile or uncommon, show restraint and run fewer pure amplifiers.
