## Summary

The video reframes EDH interaction away from a card-type checklist toward a network of overlapping solutions to specific gamestates. A single problem (e.g., Animate Dead returning Archon of Cruelty) can be answered many ways: creature removal, enchantment removal, counterspell, or instant-speed graveyard exile. Removal should be defined broadly—bounce, graveyard hate, stax, and defensive permanents like Ghostly Prison all count, and these stick-around pieces are often cheaper per mana than single-target removal or wipes. There is no fixed interaction count; counts range from the teens to 30+ depending on deck and metagame. Life-total pressure substitutes for removal—aggro and Voltron decks need less, and cards like Magister Sphinx act as escape valves. Removal purpose varies: aggro clears blockers, combo answers obstacles to buy time, control attacks resource engines. Flexible staples (Beast Within, Chaos Warp) matter most in colors lacking hard removal and in narrow (8-12 card) suites; Generous Gift is weaker as a Beast Within clone. Cheap 0-2 mana removal wins on tempo and instant-speed mana efficiency, but single-target spells fall behind on cards. High-cost counterspells are fine when mana is held anyway (draw-go, treasures, modality like Three Steps Ahead). Categorization should serve deck understanding.

## Key Clips

- **[01:43]** Instead of this sort of checklist approach, the best way to think about interaction is as a network of overlapping solutions to a number of specific gamestates.
- **[04:12]** As you get more acquainted with common play patterns of decks, and which ones you tend to have a problem with, it becomes much more useful to focus on situations that need to be remedied. An interaction suite that does a good job handling the threats of one table might not do as good a job at a different table—you need to know the environment you're stepping into.
- **[05:26]** There's no way to know exactly how many removal spells or overall interaction spells you need because different forms of removal will have overlap in the profile of what they can answer, and different interaction spells will have different degrees in how much they hinder opposing plays.
- **[06:27]** Having a higher degree of life total pressure can often allow for a bit smaller of a removal count. All sorts of late game permanents that would generally be good candidates for removal become a lot less of a concern if their controller is in range of being rapidly focused down. Most pertinent in aggro and especially Voltron decks.
- **[07:37]** Different decks will utilize removal for very different purposes. Creature-based aggressive decks focus on clearing blockers and avoid conventional board wipes unless one-sided; combo decks focus interaction on obstacles to the combo and spend removal only on imminent threats; slower controlling decks focus removal on knocking out resource generation like card draw engines.
- **[10:41]** If your interaction suite is relatively narrow, somewhere in the ballpark of 8-12 cards, there will be a lot of utility in having each of those cards be as flexible as possible. If that average is lower, running more versatile staples like Beast Within and Chaos Warp will likely be a good idea.
- **[13:19]** Any time a deck is holding up mana to use interaction at instant speed, there is effectively a mana cost associated with that, since if the interaction wasn't needed the mana goes unspent. This is why cheap instant-speed kill spells and low-cost counterspells are especially highly prioritized in EDH.
- **[14:29]** 3 mana counterspells aren't inherently bad—what matters is whether you have other things to do with that mana you hold up. Higher-costed counterspells make sense in draw-go control decks that play reactively, or when unspent mana rolls over via treasures, storage lands, or Kruphix-style commanders, or with modal counterspells like Three Steps Ahead.
