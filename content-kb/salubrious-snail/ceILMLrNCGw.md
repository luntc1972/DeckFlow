## Summary

The creator builds a Radha, Heir to Keld battlecruiser/midrange deck to capture a "big bad at the table" vibe less brutal than his Glissa deck. Radha is a 2-drop mana dork always available from the command zone, covering low-cost ramp so the 99 can run zero cards below 4 mana. He contrasts this with a past Goreclaw deck (16 low-cost ramp) that won fast or ran out of steam. Principle: coordinate ramp-to-other and low-cost-to-high-cost ramp ratios; 6+ mana creatures (Kogla, Etali Primal Storm) stay relevant as topdecks where 4-5 drops disappoint. Ramp suite: all 10 Explosive Vegetation-type effects, Blighted Woodland, Mina and Denn, Unexpected Windfall treasures; 19 turn-3 ramp cards give a 79% opening-hand chance. Running nothing under 4 mana makes cascade/discover predictable, yielding an effective high-20s ramp count. Threats target 6-7 mana on a $50 budget, nothing over $4: Screamer Killer, Emergent Woodwurm, Tyrant's Familiar, Thunderfoot Baloth (lieutenant works because Radha is expendable). Many creatures carry removal. Draw is light—7 engines, 2 one-shots. Weaknesses: almost no instant-speed interaction (4 removal, 1 protection), predictable curve, boring early turns, aggressive mulligans, board wipes.

## Key Clips

- **[01:18]** Any ramp deck worth its salt needs to coordinate the ratio of ramp to other stuff, and also the ratio of low cost ramp to high cost ramp. Running a lot of low-cost ramp leads to beautiful curve-outs but runs out of cards faster and worsens later topdecks.
- **[02:51]** Dropping a high-quality four-drop on turn 3 like Forgotten Ancient or Atsushi is less impressive than dropping a Kogla or Etali, Primal Storm on turn 4; six-plus mana creatures stay relevant as topdecks whereas 4-5 mana creatures are often disappointing to draw late.
- **[03:51]** Green stompy decks struggle most with board wipes. Mana dorks accelerate faster but get eaten by wipes alongside the big threats, so the ramp suite was chosen to play through hate and keep scaling into the late game.
- **[04:16]** The ramp suite focuses on Explosive Vegetation—I run all 10 of these effects that exist—plus Blighted Woodland, an untapped land that can serve as turn 3 ramp if activated during combat after attacking with Radha.
- **[04:48]** Since I want some sort of ramp on turn 3 every game to play a six drop on turn 4, I opted for 19 total cards that offer turn 3 ramp, giving a 79% chance of drawing one in each opening hand.
- **[05:20]** I decided on literally no low-cost cards, for one key reason: cascade. If you don't run any cards costing less than 4, you're guaranteed a decent minimum value from your cascade trigger, making the deck effectively run a high-20s number of ramp spells.
- **[07:43]** Tyrant's Familiar and Thunderfoot Baloth are powerful with a commander in play, but most decks can't exploit them since opponents remove the commander before attacks. My commander is a cheap mana dork I don't care about past turn 4-5, so opponents are less eager to remove her and she's cheap to replay.
- **[11:00]** It lacks basically any instant-speed interaction—generously, four instant-speed removal cards and one instant-speed protection card. This is a product of being a cascade deck running no cards below 4 mana, where desirable instant-speed options are slim.
