## Summary

Builder constructs an unconventional mono-white Commander deck themed around landfall, using Ao, the Dawn Sky as commander chosen mainly because it's an acceptable creature to sacrifice and reanimate. The engine: tap lands for mana, sacrifice all lands to Zuran Orb (for life and as a sacrifice outlet), play catch-up ramp like Claim Jumper or Knight of the White Orchid, then reanimate the sacrificed lands with Second Sunrise or Faith's Reward to flood lands back into play and fire repeated landfall triggers. Mono-white offers few landfall payoffs (mostly Felidar Retreat variants), so triggers are sparse. Support cards include Trove Warden, Redemption Choir, Balancing Act, board wipes (Devastating Mastery), Soul Warden-style lifegain, and Welcoming Vampire. Across two MTGO games the deck lost both, hampered by drawing too many lands, running out of basics to reanimate, and too few Second Sunrise-style reanimation effects. The builder concludes the ramp/land package works well (consistently out-ramping opponents) and Ao is the correct commander, but the deck needs more reanimation, faster sacrifice outlets, possibly a flicker package, and ways to return lands from hand. Treated as an early, fun-but-unfinished project deck requiring significant tuning.

## Key Clips

- The deck is mono white landfall reanimator. The plan: use Saurin/Zuran orb to sacrifice all your lands for mana, then play catch-up ramp like Claim Jumper or Knight of the White Orchid, then play something like Faith's Reward or Second Sunrise to bring all the lands back at once, triggering a bunch of landfall effects.
- There are only about five or six landfall triggers worth using in mono white, mostly Felidar Retreat variants, so the deck is limited on actual landfall payoffs and tries to do many things at once instead.
- AO, the Dawn Sky is the commander specifically because it's a good sacrifice target and reanimation target — you can sacrifice AO into random sac outlets, and its death trigger gives value. Built deck-first, then found AO as a fitting white commander.
- Four mana value is an important benchmark for the deck because AO can't bring back permanents with low mana value well; the deck wants four-mana and two-mana cards, with three-mana valued a lot less.
- Balancing-effect cards like Balancing Act work because most opponents sacrifice creatures down to the lowest permanent count, while this deck can sacrifice its lands and then reanimate them all back.
- Card-evaluation principle stated: avoid cards that play against you — ones that, when drawn, actively hurt you or do nothing (e.g. Charitable Levy with a non-creature-heavy list, or cards that help opponents ramp like Oath of Lieges).
- Post-game assessment: the land/ramp package worked well — consistently had the most lands in play. The weakness was reanimation: too few Second Sunrise / Faith's Reward effects, and the deck struggled to find a useful landfall payoff while having drawn every basic land out of the deck into play.
- Identified follow-up improvements: add a small flicker package, more reanimation, and better ways to get lands from hand into play (deck is good at lands from graveyard and deck, weak from hand). Amulet of Vigor noted as a strong-but-expensive fit.
