## Summary

Video argues cutting cards is mostly emotional (FOMO, sunk cost, pet cards) and becomes simple once the deck's game plan is clear. Before cutting, answer how the deck wins (specific cards/combos, worked backward turn by turn) and which card types and counts support it. Guideline: about 8 copies to see one by turn six (e.g. win condition); 12 or more to see one in the opening hand (mana rock, cheap draw like Brainstorm). Tag cards by role, cut redundant categories, and ask whether you are excited to draw each card. Bracket 3 aims to win by turn 7-8, so slow cards are cut. Examples: Frodo/Sam food deck cut ramp, Entomb, Reanimate, Blind Obedience, Gandalf the White, and a third to half of its win conditions, adding cheap draw, spot removal and sacrifice spells like Viscerid Deepwalker(?). Aragorn deck cut Mana Flare, Heartbeat of Spring, Moria's Wake, Monologue Tax, Harvest Season, Songs of Freyalise for Tamiyo's Safekeeping and Heroic Intervention. Wilson Voltron cut excess protection (Clever Concealment, Mithril Coat, Swiftfoot Boots), Seasoned Dungeoneer, The Great Henge. Smaug deck wants 11 two-mana four-cost rocks (Thran Dynamo, Ring of the Lucii) over Arcane Signet, Mana Geyser, and cuts win-more like Kavu Hoard Dragon(?),

## Key Clips

- **[03:56]** The core reason you're struggling to cut cards is because you're not clear enough about what your game plan is and what cards you need to support it. If you were, it would be very easy to spot which cards you want and which you need to cut.
- **[04:23]** First, how does your deck win? Get very specific: which individual cards, combinations or interactions allow you to win? Work backwards from that point, what you play each turn all the way back to your first land drop.
- **[05:18]** To see one of a card by around turn six, like a win condition, you need about eight of them in your deck. If you want one in your opening hand, like a mana rock or cheap card draw such as Brainstorm, you need 12 or more.
- **[08:41]** Vibes-based gut check: think about which cards you're excited to see when you draw them. Cards that get a neutral or negative response are the first to take out, provided the deckbuilding fundamentals are covered.
- **[12:42]** Entomb and reanimation spells seem out of place in the food deck; just because a card is generally very good doesn't mean it's good in your specific deck. Blind Obedience is a lone stax piece that doesn't fill a core role.
- **[15:15]** In bracket three we don't have time to bounce back, so slow cards like Moria's Wake, Monologue Tax, Harvest Season and Songs of Freyalise get cut for protection like Tamiyo's Safekeeping or Heroic Intervention.
- **[16:57]** In the Wilson Voltron deck, cut protection down to about 15 or 16 cards; Clever Concealment and Mithril Coat are too slow, and Seasoned Dungeoneer and The Great Henge don't fit a deck that wants Wilson big early.
- **[20:28]** Smaug is a seven-mana commander, so want 11 ramp sources that each ramp two mana, like Pirate's Pillage, Thran Dynamo, Ring of the Lucii, Stonespeaker Crystal, over Arcane Signet or Mana Geyser. Cut win-more X-spells like Torment of Hailfire, Crackle with Power and Exsanguinate.
