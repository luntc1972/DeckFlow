## Summary

The video advocates pushing Commander ramp to its practical limit using Cascade (and Discover) as a delivery vehicle for endless ramp. Rather than relying on single powerful spells like Boundless Realms or Traverse the OutlandsΓÇöwhich invite counterspells, stack interaction, and getting punished for greedΓÇöthe speaker fills the three-to-four mana slots almost entirely with ramp so that Cascade cards (mostly four-to-six mana) reliably cascade into ramp spells. This attaches ramp to every play: Shardless Agent, Bloodbraid Elf, Geological Appraiser, and Throes of Chaos become value engines because they cascade into Cultivate, Kodama's Reach, Explosive Vegetation, etc. The thesis rejects the usual 'ramp only early' advice; never stop ramping to build a massive mana advantage, even casting Cultivate on turn 10. Above five mana, powerful payoffs (Bane of Progress, Ulvenwald, removal like Let the Galaxy Burn) become easy to cast. The deck is built around Orni/Te'ou (a Temur lands-matter commander). Downsides: running out of lands (decking yourself), and weak removal density. The strategy suits casual social Commander, not high-power fast tables. A smaller cascade package can also be grafted onto any mana-hungry deck.

## Key Clips

- There are a whole bunch of cards like Open the Way, Traverse the Outlands, and Boundless Realms that let us ramp an enormous number of lands all at once. We can then double up on these cards by using cloning effects or graveyard recursion. This gets all lands out of our deck into play very quickly, but it also leaves us completely open to being attacked, and a smart table knows that they should punish us for that endless greed.
- Cascade is a fairly complex keyword that makes it so that whenever we cast a spell with Cascade, we get to reveal cards from the top of our deck until we find a spell with mana value less than the mana value of the cascade card. And then we get to cast that spell for free. There's also discover, which is really just cascade, but slightly different.
- Most cascade cards are in the four to six mana range, which means if we fill our three to four mana slots entirely with ramp, we can attach ramp to those spells. The true way to have a real massive mana advantage is to just never stop ramping. I have no shame playing cultivate on turn 10.
- It's an Orni the Song Unending deck which is a Temur commander that cares about having a lot of lands in play. Temur in particular is a great fit because a lot of the cascade cards either in blue or in red and also summon green. And then of course we need green to be able to have enough ramp in our deck so we can cascade into it.
- Shardless agent comes with a free Visit or Sakura Tribe Elder attached because we're always cascading into ramp. At four mana we've got Bloodbraid Elf, Geological Appraiser, and Throes of Chaos. A four mana 3-2 is not impressive, but a four mana 3-2 that has a cultivate attached to it is pretty damn good.
- As we head into five mana, we find less ramp and just raw cascade ΓÇö Bloodbraid Challenger, Forceful Denial, Immerturf, Celebrant of Bounty. All of these cards are super mid. However, because when we are casting them, we're very likely to also cast a Vastwood Surge or an Unexpected Windfall, they suddenly become quite okay. This is very much a quantity over quality approach.
- If we're slamming three mediocre spells a turn, then it's good if some of them kill a few things that our opponents got so that our mass mediocrity can outweigh their quality. Let the Galaxy Burn and Noise Marine are incredible cards since they can act as removal, and removal is key in a deck like this.
- If I'm building a deck that needs a lot of mana to get its game plan started, I genuinely like incorporating a small cascade package with an appropriately altered low-cost card distribution. I did so with a Wort the Raidmother deck ΓÇö my inclusions were Violent Outburst, Bloodbraid Elf, Throes of Chaos, and Geological Appraiser. I then tailored the mana costs of my utility pieces like sweepers, fogs, and recursion to be outside of my cascade range.
