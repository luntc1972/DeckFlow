## Key Clips

- Reaper King decks can manifest a couple different ways. One option is to make them a slower, more controlling deck, waiting for Reaper King to be out and then playing a number of Scarecrows afterward. The other option is to play Scarecrows right from the start, using Reaper King's anthem effect to swing in for damage.
- My opinion is that this deck would benefit from deciding where it wants to focus its attention and then committing to thatΓÇöboth a later game plan that focuses more heavily on Reaper King triggers and an earlier game plan that focuses more on artifact beats are valid plans, and choosing one of these to focus more heavily on will improve the deck.
- As with other Aikido-style decks, there certainly could be a question of what this deck's proactive plan isΓÇöopponents can simply choose not to play a creature with a high enough power to take a large chunk out of their life total.
- Storm decks generally tend to run lower land counts to prevent losing momentum, and extra land play cards can more accurately be thought of as a Flubs downside mitigation tactic here. The deck is likewise relatively low on removal and counterspells, and that's also to be expectedΓÇöthis is a very proactive deck that is looking to steamroll ahead with its own gameplan.
- The best way to be resilient is to spread out stored resources between different zones and card typesΓÇöin this deck's case it's mostly spreading resources between board and graveyard. This likely means an okay level of resiliency, since neither creature wipes nor graveyard hate will totally blow out the deck, but enchantment wipes will be pretty brutal and a bigger focus on keeping cards in hand could help mitigate this.
- A voltron deck is a great way to get opponents to target your stuff, since they'll be terrified about their life totals. Of the deck's 22 cards that provide protection of some sort, about half are reactive, and these will allow the deck to draw cards off of Rayne without needing to lose her.
- Lure-based creature wipes are inherently the sort of thing that will be very strong against decks with a handful of large creatures, but weaker against a lot of other types of deck, so spending too much deck space on them will naturally leave a deck without the resources needed to deal with decks that are less dependent on key creatures.
- A land count of 33 with a curve this high just isn't going to be reliableΓÇöthe deck's 6 or higher cost cards will end up stuck in hand in some games. I think it's appropriate to run at least 37 in a deck like this, and these can be utility lands.
