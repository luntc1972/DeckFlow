## Summary

The creator entered a local cEDH tournament and refused to net-deck or dismantle their beloved Derevi budget aggro deck, instead building an original Bant deck from scratch around the partners Kodama of the East Tree and Ishai, Ojutai Dragonspeaker. Ishai applies passive pressure—growing large from +1/+1 counters and forcing opponents to spend removal—while Kodama serves as the combo engine, generating near-infinite value by recurring permanents. Key combos: Kodama plus Sakashima enables endless permanent chains; Cloudstone Curio loops lands (e.g., Lush Oasis pings); Maloku plus Sunscorch Desert loops tokens for damage; Tireless Provisioner plus bounce-lands (Boseiju-style Palace) makes infinite mana, food, and landfall; Mana Breach becomes a combo enabler rather than a Stax piece. The builder leaned into a patient 'second-to-go-for-the-win' philosophy, white's strong interaction suite, and the deck's unknown identity. They cut interaction, added lands and card draw to favor grindy metas, accepting weakness to turbo decks. Results: a draw, a clean win off Mystic Remora plus Exploration, a loss to Najela turbo, then a finals win using Chord of Calling for X=6 to fetch the five-mana Maloku, exploiting opponents' unfamiliarity with the deck.

## Key Clips

- My local community ran a cEDH tournament and I signed up even though I knew very little about cEDH. I had played some cEDH before but I didn't really enjoy it, mostly because I was playing a deck I hadn't built myself — the Elsha 'To Infinity and Beyond' list I found online. It felt very reliant on its commander and the combo wins just aren't that interesting.
- There are just not that many Bant commanders and even fewer that are cEDH viable — really just Derevi and Tymna/Thrasios-style choan that have seen play. So I started looking around for partners, because if I can't have a Bant commander maybe I can just assemble one. Kodama of the East Tree and Ishai, Ojutai Dragonspeaker — the last one actually doesn't sound that bad.
- The idea is Kodama plus Ishai: use Kodama as our main combo tool and Ishai to be a nuisance. We get to play Kodama in a deck with access to white, we start with an extra card because unlike Sakashima we can actually play Ishai, the deck is more aggressive, and nobody knows what this deck does because Kodama/Ishai doesn't exist — it's not a known entity.
- Ishai turned out to be amazing. Play Ishai, wait until it comes back around, and Ishai is often going to have seven or eight plus-one-plus-one counters. The important thing is this is a problem our opponents have to deal with — we can just play Ishai and wait and it will eventually kill somebody, which means Ishai very frequently eats removal spells that then weren't ready for Kodama.
- Kodama is a six-mana 6/6 with reach: whenever another permanent enters under your control, if it wasn't put onto the battlefield with this ability, you may put a permanent card with equal or lesser mana cost from your hand onto the battlefield. The basic combo is Kodama plus Sakashima — Sakashima lets you have two Kodamas, then a Cloudstone Curio lets you bounce and replay lands endlessly for infinite mana and infinite land ETBs.
- Mana Breach is a free enchantment that says whenever a player plays a spell they return a land they control to hand. For most decks it's just a stax piece, but with Kodama we bounce lands, replay them with Kodama triggers, and tap them for mana again — so it's a stax piece for opponents and a combo line for us.
- With the tournament coming up I made one more decision: I was going to be greedy and play for the second win attempt. I removed a lot of interaction, added more lands and more card draw, and intentionally slowed the deck down. I'll be weak to turbo decks, but I had a feeling this would be a grindy meta where I win the long game.
- In the finals I tapped out and cast Chord of Calling for X equals six. Opponents debated but didn't know my list, so they let it slide. The trick: you don't have to get a creature for six mana just because you paid six — I went and got Mokotaur, a five-mana creature, and won on the spot. I won our first cEDH tournament with a deck I fully built myself from scratch.
