## Summary

Deck tech updating a Temur (Rograkh/Thrasios) cEDH combo deck nicknamed "wet paper tiger" after the Mana Crypt and Dockside Extortionist bans. The pilot argues the meta is now slower, eliminating tier-three decks dependent on Dockside and Mana Crypt, but this deck is untouched because it never relied on those cards—it makes huge mana on turns one and two and stays ahead of the curve. Thrasios is only an infinite-mana outlet, not a grind engine; Rograkh enables Mox Amber, Paradise Mantle, Spring Leaf Drum and curio lines. Its strength is flexible combos: you dump cards onto the battlefield and assemble wins many ways, so disruption barely matters. Listed lines include Machine God's Effigy + Devoted Druid, Curio + Cloud of Fairies + Gaea's Cradle, Magda + Curio + Relic of Legends, Shrieking Drake + Wild Growth/Fertile Ground, Kinnan + Basalt Monolith, and Eternal Witness + Snap. New additions: Magda, Malevolent Rumble, Clement, Dosan, Apple of Eden, Lily Splash Mentor, Sowing Mycospawn, Seedborn Muse. Big mana breaks Temur's tutor inefficiency to keep parity with Grixis. Pilot goldfishes several hands showing fast, flexible combo wins and recommends the deck for competition.

## Key Clips

- A lot of people have been asking me to update this deck especially after the bans and how I think it's positioned. What I'm going to go through this video is first how things are positioned, then the core combo lines and what this deck is, then lastly the new adds based on my performance and the bans.
- Losing Mana Crypt and Mox Opal eliminates a lot of the tier three decks that were dependent on it, and Dual Lotus as well. A lot of decks are going to be slower and not able to power out the bigger mana value spells. Overall sentiment is the meta is slower.
- This deck was one of the fastest, most explosive, powerful decks in the format and it is. It was not relying on Mana Crypt or Mox Opal, which is one of the big misconceptions. The point is this deck's incredibly flexible, has a lot of different ways to win, and it's very explosive. It just makes mana right off the bat.
- It's called Wet Paper Tiger, a joke because Matt Sperling called Rograkh the paper tiger, so this is a wet one — it's Thasos instead. This is a very flexible combo deck that features Rograkh and Thasos. You are only playing Thasos as an infinite mana combo outlet. This is not a grindy deck.
- Grixis is hyper efficient — Demonic Tutor costs two, Vampiric Tutor costs one, Dark Ritual. Thasos does not have that efficiency. The fact that we're able to make a lot of mana breaks that inefficiency and keeps us at parity with Grixis. We make more mana so we stay at parity with their efficiency.
- You make a lot of mana, dump all your cards on the battlefield, and even if your opponent removes it you don't care because you have like 10 different plans, and all your plans have three different ways to assemble a combo. So it's very difficult for your opponent to disrupt you, and after you dump your cards you use Thasos to keep drawing.
- Devoted Druid plus Machine God's Effigy: you have Devoted Druid, MGE copies the Druid, and now it can put minus-one counters on itself, untap infinitely for blue or green, infinitely play your Thassa's Oracle, draw your deck, win. The caveat is you need to be very creative with this deck and figure out your game plan on the fly.
- I added Magda since the event because it extended a lot of the combos we had — Magda tap, Relic of Legends make a red, make a treasure, then Rograkh picks her up for infinite red or infinite treasures. I also swapped to Dosan instead of Veil of Summer after losing a game to Mindbreak Trap when I preemptively fired off Veil like a Silence.
