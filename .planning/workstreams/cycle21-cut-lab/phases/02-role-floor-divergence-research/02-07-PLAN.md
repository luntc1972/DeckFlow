# Phase 2 Plan 07 — Lands as the calibration control, a go/no-go willing to be negative, and the protection disclosure

## Release Posture

**This phase ships NO user-visible change and is NOT a release.** No CalVer bump, no tag.

## Why This Plan Exists

Three ROADMAP criteria remain, and all three are about **honesty in the output** rather than about
computing anything new. Each has a specific way of failing quietly.

### Criterion 9 — the lands verdict must be stated against the prior

> 9. **Lands and ramp are measured** (D-C), and the lands verdict is stated explicitly against the
>    2026-07-16 prior as "reproduces" / "contradicts" / "insufficient data". A lands result is never
>    presented as novel without that comparison.

Plan `02-04` put `lands` and `ramp` into `TargetRoles`, so they are measured. Nothing yet compares
lands to the prior.

The prior is `.planning/archive/2026-cycles/research/2026-07-16-edhrec-bracket-land-data.md`. Its
verdict, in its own words:

> **Power level (bracket) is THE driver.** Monotonic: 36 → 36 → 35 → 34.5 → **30** (cEDH −5 to −6).
> **Commander CMC ≈ no effect on land count.** … **Commander abilities → little land effect, with ONE
> exception** … Vivi (commander that taps for mana) is consistently ~2–3 lands lower.

Its measured numbers, which this plan embeds as constants:

| Bracket | B1 exhibition | B2 core | B3 upgraded | B4 optimized | B5 cEDH |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| between-commander mean lands | 36.3 | 35.7 | 35.2 | 34.5 | 30.0 |
| between-commander SD | 2.24 | 1.19 | 1.25 | 1.20 | 2.14 |

Overall mean 34.9, SD 1.45. Every commander-ability manabase adjustment was **rejected** on that
evidence.

The ROADMAP is explicit about why lands is re-run anyway, and it is not because anyone expects a
different answer:

> Lands therefore double as a **calibration control**. If the new methodology reproduces the known
> no-go on lands, that is evidence the harness is trustworthy on the roles where the answer is not
> already known. If it *contradicts* the prior, that is a finding about the methodology and must be
> reported as such before it is treated as a finding about decks.

**The quiet failure mode this plan prevents:** the harness produces a lands number, someone reads it
as a fresh result, and a known-rejected commander-ability land adjustment gets rebuilt. The
comparison must be **computed and emitted by the harness**, not written by hand by whoever reads the
output.

### The casual-bias prior — the findings must confront it, not route around it

`.planning/archive/2026-cycles/quick/260718-nip-investigate-usefulness-of-edhrec-dump-fo/` investigated
whether the EDHREC dump was useful and concluded that **EDHREC averages are casual-dominated**. The
codebase then acted on that conclusion twice, independently of this phase:

- `ManabaseAnalysisService.cs:603-605` restricts the EDHREC commander cell to brackets 2-3, with the
  shipped comment *"dump means are bracket-agnostic and the EDHREC population is casual-dominated, so
  a popular commander's mean would drown the optimized/cEDH bracket signal."*
- cEDH got its own tournament-sourced corpus (`ICedhLandBaselineProvider`) rather than being served
  from EDHREC at all — see `CutLabFloorDefaults.ResolveLandsDefault`, which routes bracket 5 to the
  cEDH baseline before it ever consults the EDHREC bracket row.

That report also made an argument aimed squarely at **this phase's premise**, not merely at lands:

> a stax deck and a swarm deck under the same commander are both correct with wildly different mixes,
> so distance-from-average is noise for the serious-builder audience

If that is right, per-commander role-floor divergence is measuring within-commander archetype spread
and calling it commander identity — which would make a `go` verdict actively misleading for Cut Lab's
audience.

**This plan does NOT rebut that argument, and must not.** It requires the findings to **engage** it
explicitly, in the same way and with the same standing as the lands prior: state the argument, state
what this run's evidence does or does not say about it, and do not let a `go` verdict be published
without the engagement. Rebutting it in a plan, before any data exists, would be exactly the
pre-committed conclusion this phase is built to avoid.

### Criterion 10 — the go/no-go must be able to be negative

> 10. The go/no-go is willing to be negative. If no role clears the bar, the findings say so plainly
>     and Phase 3 becomes a documented no-op — a null result is a valid deliverable for this phase,

## Design Decisions

### D-01 — the lands verdict is a computed enum, not prose

Emitting a paragraph and hoping a human fills in the verdict reintroduces exactly the manual step
that lets a lands result be presented as novel. The harness computes one of three values and prints
it as a heading.

Decision rule, stated so it is auditable. **The breadth bar is `BreadthMinimum = 3` distinct
qualifying commanders clearing the bar** — the same constant `BuildGoNoGo` (`:579`) uses to award a
role a Phase-3 `go`, and the same number stated in the methodology text at `:646`:

| Condition | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Fewer than `LandsCalibrationMinCommanders` (3) commanders qualify for `lands`, OR the corpus lands baseline is zero | **insufficient data** |
| `lands` is `no-go` in `BuildGoNoGo` — no commander clears the P25 bar for lands | **reproduces** |
| `lands` is `signal-present` — 1 or 2 commanders clear, i.e. FEWER than `BreadthMinimum` (3) | **insufficient data** (D-06) |
| `lands` is `go` — at least `BreadthMinimum` (3) distinct qualifying commanders clear the bar | **contradicts** |

The mapping deserves its rationale spelled out in the emitted text: the prior concluded commander
identity barely moves land count. A no-go on lands is therefore **agreement** with the prior, and a
go is **disagreement**. This inversion is counter-intuitive on a first read and will be misread if
left implicit.

### D-06 — "signal present but insufficient breadth" is INSUFFICIENT DATA, never CONTRADICTS

*(Numbered out of sequence deliberately: it amends D-01's table, so it is placed beside it rather
than after D-05.)*

An earlier draft of this plan mapped `signal-present` — 1 or 2 commanders clearing, below
`BreadthMinimum` — onto **contradicts**. That is wrong in the one direction this phase cannot afford
to be wrong in, and it is wrong on the single role chosen as the calibration control.

`signal-present` is, by the harness's own words at `:595`, *"signal present but insufficient
breadth"*. It is an explicit statement that the evidence does not reach the bar. Converting an
explicitly-insufficient result into a claimed **contradiction of a committed prior** would:

- assert, from one or two commanders, that a study over ~50 commanders x 5 brackets was wrong;
- trip D-01's "this is a finding about the methodology" escalation on evidence that by construction
  does not support any finding; and
- do it on `lands`, whose entire purpose here is to tell us whether the harness is trustworthy — so a
  false contradiction here would poison the reading of every other role's verdict.

Rule, binding: **a lands result contradicts the prior only when `lands` reaches the SAME breadth bar
a role must reach to earn a Phase 3 `go` — `BreadthMinimum` = 3 distinct qualifying commanders
clearing the bar.** Below that bar the verdict is `insufficient data`, and the emitted text must say
how many commanders cleared and how many `BreadthMinimum` requires.

The bar must be stated **explicitly and identically** in both places it appears: this decision table,
and the emitted `## Lands Calibration Control` text (Task 1 section C.5). Do not paraphrase it in one
and hard-code it in the other; read `BreadthMinimum` in the code and name the same constant in both.

Note the asymmetry is deliberate and honest: a lands **no-go is AGREEMENT** with the 2026-07-16 prior
(the prior found commander identity barely moves land count, so finding no per-commander divergence
reproduces it), while `insufficient data` is a statement about this run, not about the prior. Neither
is a disappointment — per the inversion above, a lands `no-go` is the OUTCOME THAT VALIDATES THE
HARNESS.

**When the verdict is "contradicts", the emitted text must state the ROADMAP's rule directly:** this
is a finding about the **methodology** and must be reported as such **before** it is treated as a
finding about decks. Include a concrete first suspect list — the P25-vs-point-estimate difference,
the EDHREC land self-check deltas from plan `02-06`, and the Postgres corpus's category-tag coverage
gaps already listed in the "Known gaps" block.

### D-02 — also emit the raw side-by-side, and lean on the EDHREC bracket data

The verdict is a summary. The findings must also show the numbers it came from, because a reader who
disagrees with the classification rule needs to check it.

Emit a comparison table carrying **all three reference sets** beside the run's own lands figures:

| Column | Source | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Prior mean / SD, B1-B5 | `2026-07-16-edhrec-bracket-land-data.md` | EDHREC average-decks, ~337k decks, >=400/cell |
| Live shipped `avgLands`, B2-B5 | `DeckFlow.Web/Data/manabase-baseline/latest.json` | `averages.csv` via `EdhrecAveragesConverter`; **no B1 row exists** |
| Fresh measured mean, B2-B5 | this phase's `_edhrec-brackets/` corpus | qualifying cells only; B1 `NOT REPORTED` per plan `02-06` D-06 |
| Run Postgres lands mean / SD / P25 | this run's Postgres corpus | within-commander distribution |

For reference, the first three sets as they stand today:

| Bracket | Prior (2026-07-16) | Live `latest.json` | Fresh measured (2026-07-27) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| B1 exhibition | 36.3 | *(no row)* | *(1 qualifying cell — NOT REPORTED)* |
| B2 core | 35.7 | 35.9 | 36.0 |
| B3 upgraded | 35.2 | 35.5 | 35.4 |
| B4 optimized | 34.5 | 34.5 | 34.3 |
| B5 cedh | 30.0 | 30.5 | 28.7 (THIN — 40 qualifying cells) |

**The shapes look close. Do not conclude from that, and do not let the plan conclude it either.** The
plan's job is to require the comparison and the agreement statement; the verdict is `02-07`'s own gate
(D-01/D-06) applied to the run's actual output. **No verdict is pre-committed here.** In particular,
"the three reference sets agree with each other" is a statement about the three EDHREC-derived
baselines, not about this run's P25 result, which measures something none of them can measure.

**Required output:** an explicit `Do the three reference sets agree?` line, computed — state the
maximum absolute spread across the three per bracket, name any bracket where they diverge by more than
1.0 lands, and say which set the run's own figure sits closest to. A divergence among the three is a
data-drift or ingestion finding worth stating before any verdict about decks.

The EDHREC half is the closer comparison: the prior was EDHREC average-decks, so an EDHREC-sourced
per-bracket lands mean from this run is measuring the same thing the prior measured, roughly nine
months later. A large divergence there is a data-drift or ingestion finding, not a methodology one,
and is worth separating from the P25 question.

**The table must keep the two sources in separate columns with their source named**, per plan
`02-05`'s criterion-8 discipline. Never average a Postgres figure with an EDHREC figure.

### D-03 — ramp gets a stated verdict too, but with no prior to compare against

D-C's third justification: *"Ramp was never measured this way at all — the rejected work was
commander-ability-driven land adjustment, which is a different question from whether ramp counts vary
by commander."*

So ramp has **no prior**. Emit a short ramp block stating exactly that: this is the first measurement
of per-commander ramp variation, there is no earlier result to reproduce or contradict, and the
verdict stands on its own. Saying so prevents the opposite error — a reader assuming the 2026-07-16
land study covered ramp because it mentions ramp counts in passing.

### D-04 — the protection disclosure is unconditional

Emit it whether or not `protection` clears the bar, whether or not any commander qualifies, and
whether or not `--edhrec-data` was supplied. It is not a caveat attached to a result; it is a
standing limitation of the run.

Place it in **two** places: once in the "Known gaps" list, and once directly inside the `## Go/No-Go`
section adjacent to the `protection` line, because that is where a reader forms a decision. Also add
it to the JSON payload as a structured `protectionUnderDetection` object with `affectedRole`,
`needles`, `knownMissedCards`, `consequence` and `blockedBy` fields, so a downstream reader is not
required to parse prose.

### D-05 — the no-go template is a committed artifact, written before the run

Criterion 10 asks the go/no-go to be *willing* to be negative. A template written **after** a
disappointing run is written under pressure to soften it. Written now, before any data exists, it is
written honestly.

`NO-GO-TEMPLATE.md` is a planning artifact in the phase folder, not something the harness emits. Its
job is to make the negative outcome's shape concrete and legitimate in advance: what Phase 3 becomes,
what is still worth keeping from the run, and what would have to change to revisit the question.

### D-07 — the casual-bias prior is a required engagement, with the same standing as the lands prior

The findings must contain a `### The casual-bias objection` block, emitted **unconditionally**, that:

1. States the archived conclusion in its own terms — EDHREC averages are casual-dominated
   (`.planning/archive/2026-cycles/quick/260718-nip-investigate-usefulness-of-edhrec-dump-fo/`).
2. Names the two places the codebase already acted on it:
   `ManabaseAnalysisService.cs:603-605` (EDHREC commander cell restricted to brackets 2-3) and
   `CutLabFloorDefaults.ResolveLandsDefault` (bracket 5 routed to the cEDH tournament corpus instead).
3. States the argument aimed at this phase's premise verbatim in substance: a stax deck and a swarm
   deck under the same commander are both correct with wildly different mixes, so distance-from-average
   is noise for the serious-builder audience.
4. States what THIS run's evidence does and does not say about it, using the run's own numbers — for
   example, whether the within-commander spread the Postgres corpus shows is large relative to the
   between-commander spread, which is the quantity the objection is really about.

**Do not rebut it in the plan.** Point 4 is filled from the run, not from this document. If the run
cannot speak to it, the block says so.

Note what the objection does NOT cover: it was made about *distance-from-average*. Decision D-B moved
the statistic to a **25th percentile over real per-deck distributions**, which is a different quantity
and is the one thing the 2026-07-16 methodology structurally could not compute. That is a reason the
question is worth re-asking — it is not a rebuttal, and the findings must not present it as one.

### D-08 — corpus hygiene is a stated LIMITATION, not a gate

**Developer decision, 2026-07-27: Phase 2 is NOT gated on corpus hygiene.** Phase 5 stays independent
and non-gating. The measured numbers below are recorded as stated limitations in the findings, in the
same pattern as the protection under-detection disclosure — disclosure, not repair.

Measured 2026-07-27. **State the sample sizes; do not inflate them.**

| Fact | Value | Sample |
|---|---|---|
| Postgres corpus size | 397,063 decks, 151,202 processed, 4,003 commanders with processed decks | full corpus |
| Depth | 847 commanders >=40 decks; 346 >=100; 88 >=250; 17 >=500; deepest 917 | full corpus |
| Commander-format share | **286/287 live decks are `deckFormat` 3 (Commander) — 99.65%**; 1 was format 7 | random n=300 processed decks drawn across commanders clearing the >=40 gate |
| Dead deck ids | **13/300 (4.3%)** are 404 / private / deleted | same n=300 |
| Theorycrafted | **7/287 (2.4%)** | same n=300 live decks |
| Created-year spread | 213x 2026, 62x 2025, 10x 2024, 1x 2023, 1x 2021 | same n=300 |
| Oversized decks | a handful carry 216-475 total cards across all boards | same n=300 |

What the importer does and does not capture: `ArchidektApiDeckImporter` parses `cards[]` **only**.
`deckFormat`, `theorycrafted`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `viewCount`, `points` and `edhBracket` are
all unparsed and unstored. **Commander-ness is inferred from a card categorized "Commander", never
from the deck's declared format.**

**Why Phase 5 stays non-gating — and this is arithmetic, not judgement.** `edhBracket` IS present on
the Archidekt payload the harvester already fetches, at roughly 25% fill, so capturing it is free. But
the deepest commander in the corpus has **917 decks**. 917 x 25% / 5 brackets is roughly **46 decks
per cell**, against EDHREC's **400** floor. **Archidekt bracket capture therefore cannot fill a
bracket cell for ANY commander — not now, and not after a full backfill.** That does not weaken
decision D-A; it strengthens it, because it shows the hybrid corpus is not a convenience but the only
way to get bracket at usable depth.

**This plan adds no harvester change, no format filter, and no Phase 5 dependency.** The developer
un-gated this deliberately. Record the numbers; change nothing.
