## Summary

Guide on adding subthemes to EDH decks. Core tool: "fulcrum cards"—cards where one theme's setup becomes another's payoff. Examples: Throne of Geth (artifact sacrifice + counters), Displacer Kitten (flicker + spellslinger), Strength from the Fallen (enchantments + graveyard), Professional Facebreaker (evasive creatures → treasure), Quicksmith Genius (artifacts → rummage/graveyard), Squee, Dubious Monarch (graveyard → tokens), Dross Scorpion (artifact creatures + untap). Evaluate each by its floor (~25th-percentile game): Professional Facebreaker's floor is high; Astral Slide without cyclers is a textless 3-mana enchantment. Assess deck space needed—Rhet-Tomb Mystic is redundant in a deck already 40%+ cycling creatures, losing to Merfolk Looter there. Treasures suit any deck; milling (Doorkeeper) is hard to leverage in Arcades. Some theme pairs share lots of fulcrums (artifacts + ETB: Thought Monitor, Conjurer's Closet, Myr Battlesphere, Mimic Vat); others don't. Decide goal first: bracket 3 wants tight cohesion plus tutors (Enlightened Tutor, Muddle the Mixture for Dungeon Delver); filtering/tutors reduce variance in high-variance decks. Bracket 2 tolerates messier synergy—run enough card filtering, and pick removal with added synergy utility (Spread the Sickness) since low-power tables don't demand top-tier interaction.

## Key Clips

- **[01:17]** A good place to start in expanding your deck's synergy universe is what I'll call fulcrum cards—a card that naturally ties together multiple subthemes, typically a payoff from one theme that rewards another. Throne of Geth ties together artifact sacrifice and counters, Displacer Kitten ties together flicker and spellslinger, and Strength from the Fallen ties together enchantments and creature-stuffed graveyards.
- **[02:15]** An important consideration is the floor of the effect—how the card will feel in a subpar situation where it's not living up to your wildest dreams. For some cards the floor is high: Professional Facebreaker will usually generate at least a couple treasures, and Displacer Kitten can at least serve as protection if no juicy ETB creatures are present.
- **[03:02]** It gets even worse for noncreature spells. A supporter was trying to integrate a small Astral Slide flicker package, and without any cycling cards in hand that card is just a three mana textless enchantment. Figure out what works as a minor part of a deck versus what has no business being outside a deck that heavily supports it, due to its low floor.
- **[03:27]** People suggest Rhet-Tomb Mystic for my Astral Slide deck—it gives creatures cycling and it's a Wizard. But at its floor it always consumes a card slot that could have gone to an ETB creature or card draw, and how high is the ceiling in a deck where over 40% of the creatures already have cycling? Once you do the floor/ceiling calculation it gets mogged by a Merfolk Looter in this deck.
- **[04:59]** Consider how closely interlinked two themes are. Sometimes there are a whole bunch of fulcrum cards for a set of two themes—artifacts and ETB effects: Thought Monitor, Conjurer's Closet, Myr Battlesphere, Mimic Vat. If you're playing a bunch of these, your two-theme deck experience might feel decently compact despite its mechanically broad nature.
- **[06:38]** It's important to figure out the reason for building the deck, because that will affect your decisions. If you're building a bracket 3 deck as a challenge, dial in hard on the points of cohesion. If Dungeon Delver is a key card, add tutors like Enlightened Tutor and Muddle the Mixture so you can get that card more often, and pair that with other cards that fit the same tutoring restrictions.
- **[07:22]** Some people worry tutors homogenize the play experience, but that isn't necessarily true. Filtering spells and conditional tutors reduce play-pattern variance, but if you add them to a deck that would otherwise have extreme variance due to a lot of moving parts, you'll likely end up in a place that is still very fresh and dynamic to play with.
- **[08:37]** Playing at a lower power table is a great place for a mechanically disjointed deck because lower power opponents don't require all the best removal, so you can run higher mana options with added utility for your synergy families. My bracket 2 incubators deck has 16 interaction cards and every one of them does something else for the deck. Is Spread the Sickness the best removal? No, but it feels immensely at home here.
