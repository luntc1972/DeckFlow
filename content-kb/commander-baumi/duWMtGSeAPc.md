## Summary

Casual Commander stream where the host plays personal decks and discusses format philosophy. The featured deck is an Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath "commander deck" gimmick built entirely from cards legal as Simic commanders, swapping Uro as a default general; the host notes Simic value-piles generate card advantage but struggle to actually kill opponents. Players test a house rule replacing standard commander damage with doubled combat damage from commanders. The host argues lifegain is overrated as a threat since real decks deal enough damage, and that infinite-life combos should simply prompt a concession. The host considers splitting gameplay and deck-building into separate streams.

## Key Clips

- The whole joke of this deck is that I call it my commander deck ΓÇö I just play a bunch of cards that can be played as simic commanders, and I use Uro as my default commander but in theory I could swap it out for any other commander. The problem it had is that it was difficult to actually kill people with it, because a bunch of simic commanders just create a lot of value but they don't really kill anything.
- I used to be pretty positive about Explore but as I've gotten a greater understanding for the game I've stopped liking it. More often than not it's just two mana do nothing. I'm taking it out and putting in an overrun-style card instead.
- An infinite-life combo is frustrating because people don't conceptualize it as a combo kill. If I have a combo that casts infinite lightning bolts everybody understands they're dead, but if I have a combo that gains infinite life people think they should still be able to win ΓÇö and they can't. If life gain is really that much of an issue, why not just limit how much life a player can have, like cap it at 100, instead of using commander damage?
- Our house commander-damage rule: if a commander deals combat damage to a player, that player loses that much life again afterward ΓÇö so commanders just hit twice as hard. Triggers don't trigger twice. You immediately notice commanders become really scary.
- For cheap proxies use MTGprint.net ΓÇö paste your list, get a PDF, print it at home or at a copy shop on thicker paper, and slide it in front of a bulk card in a sleeve. For high-quality proxies use MPCFill.com which sends to makeplayingcards.com: don't go below 800 DPI (1200 is better), pick a 'proxy' background since a real Magic back would make it a counterfeit, and it runs about 25-30 cents per card (down to ~15 cents on large 500+ orders), roughly 30-40 bucks for a whole deck.
- The problem with group hug / fog decks is they slow down the game by speeding it up. Everybody ends up with infinite mana and infinite card draw but nobody is actually on low life or able to finish the game, so every turn takes forever and the fog deck gets way more resources than it ever should have. It's totally fine to kill people ΓÇö just don't be a weird nuisance that wastes everyone's time.
- There's a distinction in stax. I don't mind stax that limit how much you can do in a turn, like 'play only one of these each turn' ΓÇö that keeps pressure up and still leaves outs. What I don't like is stax like Back to Basics or Solitary Confinement that just stop you from playing the game entirely, because if you've already used or didn't draw your removal you literally can't participate. People say 'just run more removal,' but a smart opponent won't play into removal, and that advice pushes everyone toward five board wipes per deck and a removal death loop.
- I run 42 lands here. I'd rather play more lands than fewer ΓÇö if you build your deck properly you have a ton of card draw, so who cares about a few extra lands. One of the worst things that can happen is missing your land drop, so it's always something to avoid. With five-color decks I don't build the colors evenly; I lean into one color even when it's technically three or five colors.
