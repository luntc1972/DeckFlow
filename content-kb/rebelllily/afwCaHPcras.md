## Summary

A Commander Format Panel member explains the recent bracket/ban-list update and the reasoning behind contested cards. Two cards were unbanned (Bio Rhythm and Loot Tree), part of a wave of low-impact unbans (Coalition Victory, Worldfire, Sway the Stars) meant to clarify what a ban-worthy card actually looks like and build player trust. Rhystic Study stays legal despite being problematic in cEDH (eats clock time, refuels hands, favors blue) and casual play (indirect kingmaking) because it is iconic, low-impact in low-power pods, and a ~$60 chase rare Wizards has repeatedly promoted; banning it would burn money like Mana Crypt, Jeweled Lotus, and Dockside did. Thassa's Oracle stays because the bracket system already keeps it out of casual pods, and removing it wouldn't help low-color decks. Hot takes: he'd consider unbanning Griselbrand (plays like Necropotence, enables reanimator diversity) but not Iona (locks a player out, unfun) or Prime Time (instant green staple, hard to reban). Core thesis: bans must be about trust and stability, not raw power or loud online discourse; unbans should strengthen the list, and the strongest ban list bans the least, with the bracket system soft-banning powerful cards into appropriate pods.
