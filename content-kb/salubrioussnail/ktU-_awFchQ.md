## Summary

The creator builds an EDH deck chasing a low-to-the-ground, cutthroat "cage fight" feeling rather than typical high-resource Commander games. The strategy uses 20 discard spells to drag the whole table to hellbent, where scrappy 2-3 drop creatures can compete. Key design principle is "compactness": cards should serve multiple roles, so most draw (11 of 14), discard (9 of 20), and removal (5 of 14) spells are creatures, keeping pressure high while filling utility slots. Malfegor is a "Counterweight Commander"—instead of synergizing directly with discard, he subverts the deck's weaknesses by blasting blockers and punishing opponents who hoard or overcommit creatures. The deck prioritizes resilience via discrete card-advantage engines, graveyard recursion, madness, and discard payoffs to survive its own self-discard. The builder contrasts this intentional, role-driven use of discard against engine commanders like Tergrid, where discard becomes frivolous fodder. Main weakness: non-random discard is weak against opponents with full hands or graveyard synergies, risking a kingmaker scenario. Mitigations include graveyard hate and single-target discard, though Rakdos lacks strong enchantment hate. Overall the deck succeeds at evoking reckless, grindy, fly-under-the-radar gameplay.

## Key Clips

- So, what if instead I went a different direction—what if I played a pile of scrappy little dudes, and then dragged the rest of the table down to their level? This is where discard comes in—discard doesn't necessarily say yes or no—rather, it strips away resources, forcing my opponents to pick and choose what they want to keep in hand, and disproportionately hindering decks doing more synergistic or complicated things.
- When I went to finally update the 2020 version of this deck this summer, I started with a full 20 discard spells—the goal is to get the table hellbent, and you need a lot of discard spells to do that consistently. Two spells ends up being a good number to shoot for, and a count of 20 allows me to draw that within the first 5 draws of the game approximately 75% of the time.
- This brings me to a key feature of the deck—"compactness". On a basic level it means that decks should use their slots as efficiently as possible so as not to overcommit resources to accomplish tasks. The compactness can be seen when I sort my categories by card type on Archidekt—11 of the deck's 14 draw spells are creatures, as are 9 of its 20 discard spells and 5 of its 14 removal spells.
- In this way Malfegor is an example of what I'll call a "Counterweight Commander"—he subverts the weaknesses of a discard deck full of grindy creatures, thereby making the deck better-rounded. Instead of doing the same thing as the deck, he does basically the opposite, and the resulting tension created by this allows for almost a call and response-esque play pattern.
- What that means is that a deck can recover from setbacks readily. Of the deck's 20 discard spells, more than a third affect me as well, and the deck's commander literally makes you discard your hand. The natural followup is that this deck is specifically engineered to be unusually good at playing under exactly those circumstances.
- All of this brings me to one of my least favorite engine commanders—Tergrid, God of Fright. The issue with using sacrifice and discard effects as engine fodder is that these unpleasant tools become frivolous and impassive. Instead of deploying these tools in a way that is targeted and strategic, the Tergrid player creates a bland engine powered entirely by the suffering of their opponents.
- The biggest weakness of the deck is that certain opponents will be much less harmed by my discard spells than others. The power of non-random discard is inversely proportional to the number of cards in somebody's hand up until the point at which they've been hellbent. The worst case scenario is that I basically act as a kingmaker, all but handing the win to the person who is best-equipped to handle discard.
- I had a game recently where I played Malfegor discarding my 6 card hand, then activated Spymaster's Vault to cycle through some more cards and put four +1/+1 counters on Malfegor, then swung 15 at an opponent since I now had an Anger in my graveyard from all that discarding, and during all of this I was on 5 life with two Dark Confidant effects on board threatening to end me on each of my upkeeps.
