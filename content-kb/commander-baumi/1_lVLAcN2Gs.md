## Summary

The video is mostly a spoiler review of an upcoming Magic set built around vehicles and speed mechanics. The streamer evaluates many cards using the new keywords: "start your engines"/Max Speed (ramping a speed counter by attacking, then unlocking effects), exhaust (once-per-game style activations), saddle, mounts, and pilots. He rates cheap engine-starters like Nesting Bot, praises card-advantage attackers (e.g. the saddle two-drop that draws), and flags must-kill threats such as Samut and Zihiba/"zud" as un-fun. He is bullish on Subzunon/"sub lunan" (odd/even counter draw-and-attack), Loot, and an Evos-Spark planeswalker that attaches to a creature, snowballs loyalty off combat damage, and repeatedly minus-fives to draw two—best paired with Aurok/Ao the Dawn Sky. He likes the start-your-engines lands (Amat Raceway, Mar Ganda Raceway). Later he plays cEDH one-versus-one with a mono-green Six combo deck, assembling infinite mana via Life from the Loam, Spelunking, Aftermath Analyst, mana doublers (Caged Sun), and End-Raze Forerunners, using Beast Within and Kogla as utility, and grinds through a counterspell-heavy Baral opponent to win. He also prototypes a tabletop auto-battler shop economy between games.

## Key Clips

- Glory Heeling: a two-mana 2/2 with lifelink that says 'whenever this creature attacks while saddled, search your library for a Plains card, reveal it, put it into your hand and shuffle.' Saddling two is not that difficult — play it on turn two, play something on turn three, saddle it turn four, attack, draw a land. It's also under three mana so you can use it to trigger your other card-draw effects.
- Agonasaurus Rex: a five-mana 8/8 with trample — a good rate on its own — but its primary mode is cycling. When you cycle the card, put two +1/+1 counters on a target creature or vehicle and give it trample and indestructible until end of turn, plus a card draw. Usually you cycle it to make something indestructible with trample; occasionally you play it as the 8/8.
- Evo Spark planeswalker (four mana): as long as it's attached to a creature it can't be attacked, and when that creature deals combat damage during your turn you put that many loyalty counters on it. +1 attaches it and puts a +1/+1 counter; -5 draws two cards; -10 adds 10 mana of any color. The power is the second mode — attach to something like Atraxa the Dawn Sky, swing each turn, gain loyalty, and -5 to draw two cards nearly every turn.
- Subzunon (five-mana 6/6, reach/trample/indestructible): can't attack or block unless it has an even number of counters on it. At the beginning of your first main phase put a +1/+1 counter, then if it has an odd number of counters draw two cards. Optimal line: place an extra counter before combat so you draw two, attack, then draw two again next turn — effectively a big body plus repeatable card draw.
- Criticism of the Saheeli (Ceda) card: surprised she's Simic when the character read as Boros red/white. Exhaust puts X counters on her, then makes each other creature's base power/toughness equal to hers until end of turn — but it gives no trample, so it fails as an overrun (overrun requires evasion). Called the worst-designed card in the set because it doesn't represent the character.
- On Samud and 'must-kill' cards: dislikes cards that simply must be removed from the board because the game becomes 'hate you out of the game or lose.' Samud is just a beater with mana-cost reduction — uninteresting — so the must-kill quality is frustrating. The Evo Spark gets a pass because it's a unique, interesting card despite also being must-kill.
- Mono-green Six combo line played in 1v1 Commander (40 life): Six is a free 2/2 that mills/returns lands. Combine Spelunking, Life from the Loam, mana doublers (Cached Sun), and repeatable draw (Sendian/Cycle Resurgent, aftermath analyst) to loop endlessly. Beast Within can convert a permanent into a needed piece (e.g. into a second Resurgent). Win via Insidious Fungus loops and Endless Forerunners with mana doublers.
- Board-game auto-battler prototype using Magic cards: cards are sorted by mana cost (each mana cost = one unit, each color = one tier). Shop economy adapted from the auto-chess shop: shuffle the tier pile, everyone draws five into hand, place cards face-down to buy, reveal and pay, leftovers shuffle back, plus one 'hold' slot. Unit strength scored by purchase cost; four-player free-for-all, lose a health on a loss, shop auto-upgrades every other round.
