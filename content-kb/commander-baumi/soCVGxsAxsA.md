## Summary

The creator builds an aggressive Commander deck around Choco, Seeker of Paradise, a four-mana 3/5 bird with landfall that pumps and grants repeatable card draw and ramp whenever you attack with birds. The strategy embraces constant attacking despite Commander threat-management norms, flooding the board with cheap, evasive birds (39 of 41 creatures). The deck runs 41 lands so Choco's attack trigger reliably hits lands, entering them tapped for free ramp; no other ramp sources are needed. Cards are chosen by four categories: card advantage, mana, interaction, finishers. Key pieces include bird lords (Imperial Eagle, Kangji's Lieutenant), token makers (Murmuration, Chocobo Racetrack, Perch Protection, Side Quest: Raise a Chocobo), anthem finishers (Banner of Kinship, Steel Plume Marshall), and recursion via Jackdaw Savior, which reanimates flyers and exploits Choco milling cards into the graveyard. Protection spells (Perch Protection's Teferi's Protection mode, Unbreakable Formation) keep the board alive through sweepers, turning opponents' wipes one-sided. Four 'coastal piracy' effects supply card draw on combat damage. The deck costs ~€55. In gameplay, the deck applied heavy early pressure but lost after an opponent forced a discard, denying a lethal reanimation.

## Key Clips

- Threat management is a real thing in Commander. To maximize our chances to win, we often want to play it a bit like a classic MMO. Poke a bit here and there, mostly focusing on defense, and then finish the job all at once.
- Choco, seeker of paradise, is a four mana 3/5 bird without flying. Instead, Choco has landfall, gaining plus one attack whenever we have a land enter. Also, whenever we attack with one or more birds, we look at that many cards from the top of our deck, put one into our hand, put any lands from among them onto the battlefield tapped, and the rest into our graveyard.
- I really would recommend not going lower than 40 lands with Choco simply because we're going to be digging past them anyways. And having a high quantity of lands guarantees we'll be ramping every turn. That is so long as we are attacking with birds. The deck plays 41 lands.
- When choosing creatures for a Kindred deck like this, I almost exclusively choose cards that fit into my four major deck building categories: card advantage, mana, interaction, and finishes. If a bird can't be classified into one of these categories, then it's probably not worth playing. The important thing here is to shoot for quantity over quality.
- Jack Doll Savior is a three mana white 1/1 with flying from Bloomburrow that whenever it or another creature we control with flying dies, lets us return another target creature card with lesser mana value from our graveyard to the battlefield. Since most creatures in our deck have flying, this represents fantastic sweeper protection. And it gets even better when considering that Choco places excess cards into our graveyard.
- Sweepers are super good in Commander, but if we assume they'll be coming and we play around them, we can turn our opponent's sweeper to our advantage. Since we are likely to be the problem and have our opponent's attention, we don't really need sweepers ourselves. And if we protect our creatures, then we are making those sweepers one-sided.
- Side Quest Raise a Chocobo is a two mana green enchantment that creates a 2/2 chocobo bird token when it enters. Then if at the beginning of our first main phase we control four or more birds, we complete the quest and the enchantment transforms into a black chocobo, and we get to search our deck for any land card and put it straight into play, triggering landfall to buff all birds we control.
- They correctly assessed that I was the threat and they managed to suppress us and put us into a position where we didn't really have any winning plays. The thing about commander, you can't win every game. But that's not what matters. Did we have fun? Hell yeah.
