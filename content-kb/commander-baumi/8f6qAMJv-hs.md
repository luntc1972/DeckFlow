## Summary

This video presents anti-Voltron tech for Commander, framed as humorous, hostile counterplay against decks that build one enormous creature via auras, equipment, and counters (usually the commander). Best answer is killing the Voltron target directly; Tower of the Magistrate grants protection from artifacts to unequip equipment, even on opponents' creatures. When the creature can't be targeted, non-targeting answers bypass hexproof/shroud/protection: edicts that hit the highest-power creature (Crackling Doom, GS's Command, Consumed by Greed), mass edicts (Blasphemous Edict), and Sadistic Shell Game. Restrictive cards (Ruxa Halo naming a commander, Promise of Loyalty, goad effects) stop attacks. Board wipes like End Hostilities destroy creatures plus attached permanents; Out of Time phases out all creatures, dragging commanders along since phasing isn't a zone change. Leadership Vacuum is the standout: a one-mana blue instant returning a player's commanders to the command zone, ignoring all protection, replacing itself, hitting partners. Damage-reducers (Force Field, Vine Presence) prevent kills. Finish via non-targeted steal effects like Reins of Power. The creator stresses this is comedic showcase, not a recommended deck focus, prompted by tournament prep against Light-Paws, and mentions developing an auto-battler board game.

## Key Clips

- Voltron is a strategy that rewards creating a single enormous creature by attaching auras, enchantments, counters, and other buffs to it, usually the deck's commander. Voltron decks tend to prioritize protection from cards like Swiftfoot Boots or the swords cycle before going into power.
- The best and easiest way of dealing with a Voltron deck is to just kill the Voltron target anytime it shows up. But if you can't find removal in time, a card like Beast Within can no longer hit the buffed creature.
- Tower of the Magistrate is a land that taps for colorless, but for one mana you may give a creature protection from artifacts until end of turn. Used on an opponent's creature it prevents attaching equipment and unequips any already attached; activate it in their upkeep to fully prevent the Voltron player from equipping.
- Edicts that hit the highest-power creature — Crackling Doom, Garna's command-style effects, and Consumed by Greed — are nearly unavoidable precision strikes against decks that rely on a single powerful creature. Mass edicts like Blasphemous Edict also work. Sadistic shell-game effects bypass hexproof and shroud because each player chooses a creature you don't control, then those creatures are destroyed without targeting.
- End Hostilities is a sweeper that destroys all creatures and all permanents attached to creatures, so even if the Voltron creature survives you lose all auras and equipment. Out of Time phases out all creatures until it leaves; phasing removes commanders without letting them go to the command zone, and it neither targets nor destroys, so protection doesn't help.
- Leadership Vacuum is a blue instant: target player returns each commander they control from the battlefield to the command zone, and you draw a card. It bypasses creature protection because it targets the player, ignores indestructible, and hits all commanders a player controls — strong against partner commanders. Can be used on yourself in response to Out of Time.
- Force Field is an artifact that for one mana lets you choose an unblocked creature and reduce all damage it would deal to one; it doesn't target, so hexproof or protection does nothing. The Vine Presence is a cheaper enchantment that reduces all damage to zero. Both slow Voltron to where it can't get a kill.
- Rune-Tail's Halo lets you name a card on enter and gives protection from it; name a player whose commander is the threat and it can't deal damage to you. Promise of Loyalty has each player put a vow counter on a creature and sacrifice the rest; creatures with vow counters can't attack you for the rest of the game, and it comes attached to a board wipe.
