## Summary

This video examines whether board wipes have ruined casual Commander. The host argues the problem is more nuanced than blaming wraths alone. Board wipes slow games by resetting players' progress toward lethal, sometimes creating "casual Commander purgatory" where no win conditions remain. One-sided wipes like Wave Goodbye, Winds of Wrath, or Cyclonic Rift mitigate this by letting you push toward a win, though opponents can respond in kind. Full board wipes that exile all creatures, artifacts, and enchantments (Farewell, Game Over, Withering Curse, Ultima) are the real culprits—far more powerful, cheaper, and more common than ever, killing former staples like Merciless Eviction. This is power creep: threats got faster and more threatening (Adeline vs. old Avenger of Zendikar), so wraths, removal, and protection all escalated. The host advises against playing zero wipes since decks need a catch-up mechanic, and recommends adjustments instead: sandbag cards rather than emptying your hand, run more card draw and value engines, play burst damage to turn the corner faster, and use alternatives like goad, pillow fort effects (Ensnaring Bridge, Ghostly Prison), and counterweight commanders (Fblade/Boros control) to redirect damage and progress without clearing the board.

## Key Clips

- **[01:57]** This can be mitigated by playing one-sided board wipes. Something like a Wave Goodbye or Winds of Wrath can be put into a deck in a way that it only clears your opponent's board and not yours. This way they don't prolong the game as long as other board wipes do, since you can use them to clear the board and attack, potentially even setting up for a win.
- **[02:57]** Maybe you're simply behind on board, which is actually when you want to cast a board wipe the most. If you got nothing on board and your opponents have tons of stuff, then that's when you get the most value out of a board wipe. And when you're losing on board, the more types of cards the board wipe hits, the better. It is simply the best way to catch up to three players ahead of you.
- **[10:59]** Even in an aggro deck, you probably want some amount of wraths. I think playing literally zero is just a bad idea... With the increased power of enchantments and artifacts these days, it is more important than ever to have a way to actually clear those.
- **[17:39]** If everyone is on these wraths that clear everything, the real solution is sandbagging. Simply don't empty your hand. Easily one of the most common ways players lose Commander games is dumping their hand, getting hit by board wipe, and having nothing to do for the rest of the game. Instead, if you simply hold an extra piece in hand or don't cast your commander immediately, you can oftentimes bait out the first board wipe and recover a lot faster.
- **[17:58]** Another thing to consider is card draw. If board wipes are more prevalent and powerful, then you need more cards to counter that. Card advantage will help you get past top decking and back to building your board.
- **[18:27]** I'm taking a page out of Snail's book here and playing a counterweight commander. One of the weaknesses of control decks is casual commander purgatory... Fladge takes care of that. This commander does real damage. When partnered with a Slayer's Stronghold, you can easily wrath and then bring the commander back via escape and swing in that turn.
- **[22:00]** You don't actually need a wrath specifically. Goad is one of the best ways to get around a big board without clearing it. Take the bait, taunt from the ramparts, and illusionist gambit are all cards that can be easily played to buy you a turn or two without actually clearing the board. Pillow fort cards work similarly. A ghostly prison, crawlspace, or Kazul can make people think twice about attacking you.
- **[25:26]** Players just need to adjust. Play more burst damage so you can turn the corner after a board wipe faster. Sandbag a bit more so these wipes don't take out all your resources. Play some of the other new things Wizards has added like Goad or forcing damage elsewhere with Pillowfort effects, allowing you to slow damage to you without slowing damage to the whole table.
