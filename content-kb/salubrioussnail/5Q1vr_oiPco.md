## Summary

Tutors earn their place in EDH by adding consistency, which the speaker values over raw variance. The common ideal that a deck should do something different every game is criticized: if you draw a threat far stronger than your other options, you effectively win a die roll that can decide the game, which the speaker finds bland—closer to Yahtzee or bingo than Magic. Consistency also serves Commander as a social format. Players arrive with expectations about the decks they face; inconsistent decks are hard to conceptualize and play around, while a consistent deck lets opponents engage with what it actually does, producing more dynamic gameplay. Tutors are not the only path to consistency. You can simply run many cards that do similar things, an approach the speaker enjoys for its simplicity. But some effects don't exist in seven copies, or are too situational to run redundantly. In those cases tutors become necessary to maintain consistency—so the speaker is willing to spend four deck slots on ways to tutor for a key card like Astral Slide.

## Key Clips
