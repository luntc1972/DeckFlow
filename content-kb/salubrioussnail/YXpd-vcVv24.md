## Summary

Discusses ways to orient a Commander deck relative to its commander. Conventional approach: pick a cool commander, add cards that synergize, then fill gaps with what the deck needs. Warns that commander and deck can end up doing the same thing (example: Krenko, Mob Boss in a goblins deck both just play goblins). The speaker's preferred approach: build the commander to do the opposite of the rest of the deck — build a midrange deck with a glaring weakness that the commander solves. Example: Glissa Sunslayer, a strong 3-mana blocker, draw engine, and enchantment punisher; instead of the EDHRec counter-removal builds, he built a battlecruiser deck jammed with ramp, removal, and big cards, since Glissa covers early blocking and draw. Example: Liesa; instead of the common lifegain build, he built a hatebears deck that chips damage and hinders opponents, then plays Liesa (5 mana) to cut through the resulting board — a deck with a "Liesa-shaped hole." Principle: a commander that covers one element lets you put that element on the backburner and focus deck slots on what the deck does well, solving a known weakness ahead of time. Endorses midrange as underrated.
