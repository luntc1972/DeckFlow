## Summary

This video uses EDHRec as a case study in how scraped, aggregated data can develop self-reinforcing feedback loops. EDHRec (launched 2015) scrapes Commander deck lists from sites like Archidekt and MTGGoldfish, organizes them by commander, and ranks cards by how often they appear. The problem: decks built directly off EDHRec recommendations get posted back and re-scraped, so the site increasingly ingests a regurgitation of its own data. Over time this tanks lower-ranked cards, makes recommendations sluggish to change, and artificially bolsters already-popular cards regardless of true value. This amplifies a common deckbuilding mistakeΓÇöoverrating "win-more" cards that are only strong when your commander sticks around (examples cited: Junk Winder and Second Harvest in Volo decks, shown in ~47-48% of lists). The tool reports what cards see play but not why, leading inexperienced builders to fill decks with synergy-dependent cards while underrunning pressure (attackers) and removal (e.g., killing a commander on cast). The fix when facing such decks: apply pressure and removal to punish them before they snowball. The takeaway is not to avoid EDHRec, but to treat its suggestions with the same criticality as any source and remember it omits cards it isn't showing you.

## Key Clips

- Magic The Gathering was released in 1993 and followed the template of a lot of card games: there was an opponent to defeat, and defeating them requires some little dudes that do things as well as other types of cards. It's a game whose play formats are mostly designed for two players, which always brings up the natural question of what your other one-to-four friends do.
- Enter Elder Dragon Highlander, known more commonly now simply as EDH or Commander. EDH is a format with four players where each person picks one of their little guys to be a commander that they can play multiple times at escalating cost. With origins in Alaska in the late 1990s, the format gradually gained popularity over time, with Wizards of the Coast finally printing a set specifically for the format in 2011.
- Looking at individual decks is relatively slow since each individual deck has the biases of its deck builder. What if we could look at all similar decks at the same time, seeing cards based on how often other people choose to run them? This is where EDHRec comes in. A website that aggregates deck lists is going to allow sorting through a ton of potential card choices very efficiently ΓÇö we aren't trusting the card analysis of just a single person, but rather the analysis of the collective, thereby reducing the risk of bias.
- EDHRec scrapes deck lists from websites such as Archidekt and MTG Goldfish where players post their deck lists to receive feedback. These deck lists are then organized by Commander as well as by other categories like price and sub-themes, and then within each tab cards are displayed by the frequency with which they appear in decks.
- Instead of getting data from independently operating deck builders, EDHRec is receiving a regurgitation of its own aggregated data. Since all posted decks are treated equally, and decks made heavily using EDHRec are much quicker and easier to build than decks built from the ground up, the proportion of decks built mainly with EDHRec naturally balloons over time. Cards that didn't quite make it into the EDHRec lists get artificially tanked further, making the suggestions sluggish to changes, while cards already high up are bolstered regardless of how useful they actually are.
- When I think of the archetypal overplayed card in the format, it's one that is questionable on its own but is good in a game where the deck's Commander sticks on the battlefield untouched by other players. You could also call this a win-more card ΓÇö a card that's bad when a player is behind or when a game is tight, but strong when a player is already ahead. EDHRec compounds this as these questionable synergy cards are pushed high up in the results.
- A general trend I've noticed is that most casual EDH decks don't run nearly enough of two key things: pressure and removal. Pressure is just things that make your opponent's life totals go down, usually creatures that can attack them. Removal is similar ΓÇö if you know your opponent is running a deck loaded up with cards that do nothing when their Commander doesn't stick around, then all you have to do to keep that player in check is make sure a doomblade gets tossed at their Commander whenever it's played.
- EDHRec isn't a magical website ΓÇö it's a website with all the advantages and all the flaws of anything in our modern age that exists through the wonders of sifting through data slurry. It's a good resource as long as you treat the cards that it shows you with the same criticality as cards you find anywhere else, and also recognize that there are cards it's not showing you.
