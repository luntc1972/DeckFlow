## Summary

The creator wanted a Bracket 2 Commander deck and chose to build one by deliberately overloading it with subthemes, then optimizingΓÇömirroring how precons are intentionally unfocused. The deck is anchored by Glissa, Herald of Predation (5-mana 3/5), whose beginning-of-combat options Incubate, flip incubators, or grant first strike/deathtouch. Subthemes include counters, artifact tokens (Clock of Omens, Throne of Geth, counter-based mana rocks), a liquimetal package (Liquimetal Torque, Phyrexian Scriptures) to turn creatures into artifacts, token-copy populate (Nexus of Becoming, Dollhouse of Horrors copied by Esika's Chariot), a graveyard package, a constructs package, and powerstone producers. It typically ramps Glissa out turn 4, accumulates incubators, builds an engine, proliferates counters, then swings with vanilla 6/6sΓÇöan ironic win for so complex a deck, and what keeps it Bracket 2. Power is capped because every win relies on Glissa; tutors are limited to two (targeting ~3). To fight dead cards, nearly every card serves multiple rolesΓÇöminimal generic removal, ramp, or draw. Ongoing challenges: weak standalone subtheme cards, insufficient graveyard fuel, low creature count (23). Goals: add creatures, a third tutor, flesh out packages. There's also a niche three-card infinite-mana combo.

## Key Clips

- The bracket system was rolled out in commander, and I noticed I didn't have any bracket 2 decksΓÇöI had bracket 3 decks from battlecruiser to aggro to control to staxy midrange, but nothing I'd feel comfortable consistently pulling out against three precons without playing a 1v3 archenemy role.
- Precons are unfocused by designΓÇötoo many themes to give players a taste of different playstyles. So what if I did the same thing but on overdrive: build a deck with a truly irresponsible number of subthemes, then try my hardest to optimize it into a workable decklist? Thus began Phyrexian-flavored Subtheme Soup.
- The commander is Glissa, Herald of PredationΓÇöa 5 mana 3/5 offering a choice at the beginning of combat: Incubate 2 twice, flip all incubator tokens, or give Phyrexians first strike and deathtouch. Incubate creates a noncreature artifact token with +1/+1 counters that can flip into a Phyrexian artifact creature for two mana.
- The subthemes: counters (subtheme #1), artifact tokens (subtheme #2, with Clock of Omens as the most powerful card tapping two artifacts to untap another), a liquimetal package turning creatures into artifacts, token copy populate via Nexus of Becoming and Dollhouse of Horrors, and a late-added graveyard subtheme. Plus minor packages: constructs and powerstones.
- The deck stays bracket 2 because despite an infinite mana combo (Empowered Autogenerator + Nexus of Becoming + Voltaic Construct) and powerful cards, I've never won without heavily involving Glissa. She gives two incubators per turnΓÇöthat's allΓÇöso I win by swinging in with predictable, mostly-vanilla 6/6s, a juicy morsel of irony for such a complex deck.
- To mitigate dead cards I try hard not to let a card do only one taskΓÇöthere's no generic removal, and only Golgari Signet and Talisman of Resilience are the most generic of 19 ramp cards. High ramp and draw counts are essential since each subtheme has single-digit setup/payoff, so you need to see more of the deck to piece together engines.
- Ongoing challenges: the graveyard package has only 6 cards that put cards into the yard and only 23 total creatures, so Dollhouse runs out of targets. Current goals are increasing creature count and finding a third tutorΓÇöI've heard a 3-tutor limit floated for bracket 2, which forces creative workarounds without being an outright barrier.
- A sensible player would cut most of the graveyard support, two populate cards, and all token copy cards aside from Nexus. But that's the joyΓÇöI shouldn't be doing this if I wanted a deck that plays smoothly, and doing it anyway is what makes it a bracket 2 deck, the same reason it includes kinda-not-great proliferate cards like Plaguemaw Beast that feel acceptable here.
