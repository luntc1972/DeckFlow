## Summary

This video documents building a high-power, budget cEDH-adjacent Commander deck around partner commanders Malcolm, Keen-Eyed Navigator and Francisco, Fowl Marauder (Dimir pirates). Malcolm makes treasures on combat damage; Francisco filters and grows. The plan: turn-1 one-cost pirate, Francisco turn 2, Malcolm turn 3, giving a treasure/filter engine by turn 3. The wincon is Time Sieve—Malcolm's three treasures plus extra artifact producers (Academy Manufactor, Storm Fleet Negotiator, Worldwalker Helm, Oaken Siren) reach five artifacts per turn for infinite turns. Artifact and generic tutors (Fabricate, Reshape, Whir of Invention, Lim-Dul's Vault, Ringsight, three transmute cards) assemble the combo, typically winning around turn 5. The deck is extremely consistent, following a five-turn map with turn 4 as flexible setup. It runs 13 counterspells—justified because treasures double as stored mana and Time Sieve fuel—plus removal, draw (all four Bident-of-Thassa effects, delve cards), and an alternate beatdown wincon (Haunted One). Manabase is 35 lands, all able to cast a turn-1 pirate. Weaknesses: no early interaction for Malcolm, reliance on connecting in combat, and poor recovery from setbacks due to low card quality. Core lesson: efficient command-zone-driven gameplan plus high interaction density enables power on a budget.

- **[04:23]** I can present what this deck is trying to do as a five-turn map. Turns 1-3 consist of a one-cost pirate turn 1, Francisco turn 2, Malcolm turn 3. Turn 4 is the setup turn. Turn 5 is the combo turn, where I tutor for Time Sieve if needed, play it, and begin a series of extra turns.
- **[06:20]** There's one type of draw that stands out above all others in this deck — cards that let me draw when I hit people. This deck runs all four Bident of Thassa effects that exist, and this is for good reason. Landing one on turn 4 smooths out any issues I might have comboing off.
- **[07:04]** Another type of card I develop on my setup turn is mana rocks — since all of these tap for mana immediately, the two-mana ones effectively cost a single colorless. Weaving them into a turn 4 offers a burst of mana for early combo turns and also an artifact to sacrifice to Time Sieve.
- **[08:55]** My deck runs 13 counterspells. This works because of treasures — a great thing about treasures is they're never wasted mana due to their one-time-use nature. They serve a dual purpose as stored mana and as artifact tokens for Time Sieve, so I can tap out every turn and still have mana for counterspells if needed.
- **[10:00]** This deck's manabase follows a simple principle — I want untapped, colored mana as often as possible. Every single land can theoretically play a pirate turn 1, and though this means running spicy lands like Gemstone Mine that see diminishing returns later, these are worth it for guaranteeing I set up my engine properly.
- **[14:39]** The best way to build a strong deck is to take a fundamentally broken magic card and put it into your command zone.
