## Summary

The creator explains how to build a "hidden commander" deck around a card in the 99, using The Sibsig Ceremony (three-mana enchantment: creature spells cost 2 less; a cast creature entering is destroyed and replaced with a 2/2 zombie) as the case study. He derives two principles: (1) run analogues for the central card, and (2) minimize the footprint of cards that synergize with it, since you won't always have the central card. Commander chosen is Athreos, Shroud-Veiled, picked as a low-footprint fit; supported by indestructible/pseudo-indestructible creatures (Erebos Bleak-Hearted, Yahenni, Breathkeeper Seraph, the four in-color Myojins). For ramp analogues to Sibsig's mana: Heartless Summoning, ETB ramp creatures (Solemn Simulacrum, Scampering Surveyor, Kor Cartographer), Crowded Crypt, Sol Ring, Myr Convert, plus mana rocks. Creatures wanting to die (Junji, Ao) and death-draw engines (Erebos, Dark Prophecy) pair with sacrifice analogues (Season of Loss, Fanatical Devotion). Nineteen ETB creatures, five recursion cards, and Mirror of Life Trapping (exiles cast creatures, an underplayed synergy) round it out. It's a ~$60, 50+ subtype, bracket-3 deck. He notes both principles also help ordinary command-zone decks, especially minimizing footprint via flexible, multi-purpose cards.

## Key Clips

- **[02:56]** The two principles: (1) find analogues for your central card, and (2) minimize the footprint of cards that synergize with your central card—both stemming from the fact that you won't always have access to your hidden commander card.
- **[06:10]** This is how I landed on Athreos, Shroud-Veiled. It's 6 mana, indestructible when fully online via devotion, and works well alongside ETBs and on-death effects—if I get it into play turn 4 off a turn 3 Ceremony and start throwing coins on opposing creatures, it's effectively a Worn Powerstone for this function.
- **[07:46]** The Sibsig Ceremony provides a lot of mana, so the first order of business in making the deck work without it is adding a lot of other ramp—finding analogs. Heartless Summoning is the easiest choice as a similar 2 mana creature discount plus downside, and ramp-on-ETB creatures like Solemn Simulacrum, Scampering Surveyor, and Kor Cartographer.
- **[09:48]** Take Junji the Midnight Sky—both the discard and the reanimate modes of its death trigger are solid three-mana effects, and with the Sibsig Ceremony you get that plus a zombie and a death trigger and a card in your graveyard for other purposes.
- **[09:48]** This deck integrates a number of ways to sacrifice our creatures—analogs for Sibsig Ceremony's destroy effect. Season of Loss is a flexible removal tool that can also draw, and Fanatical Devotion is a sac outlet that can also save creatures. You want the best possible spread of situational usefulness, not just the card that's best paired with a hidden commander.
- **[11:52]** Mirror of Life Trapping exiles creatures that enter if they were cast, then returns them when the next cast creature enters. With Sibsig Ceremony you can order triggers so creatures get sucked into the mirror instead of destroyed. It's crazy this isn't run more—Panharmonicon is in 8% of Sibsig decks, yet Mirror doesn't even cross the 2% threshold on EDHRec.
- **[16:17]** Principle 2—minimize the footprint of synergizing cards—is the more broadly applicable one. If you add cards specifically to synergize with your commander and that's all they do, you're forced into replaying your commander to make them useful. Cards that do multiple things give you more freedom in how to play the game.
