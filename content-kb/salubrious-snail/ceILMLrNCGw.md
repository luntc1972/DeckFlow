## Summary

The creator builds a Radha, Heir to Keld battlecruiser Commander deck as a less oppressive alternative to their removal-heavy Glissa deck. The core idea: Radha is a turn-2 mana dork available from the command zone, covering low-cost ramp so the 99 can skip cards under 4 mana entirely. This makes cascade and discover triggers reliably hit rampΓÇöthe deck effectively runs a high-20s count of ramp spells, anchored by all ten Explosive Vegetation-style effects plus Blighted Woodland, totaling 19 turn-3 ramp sources (79% to open with one). Ramping into 6-7 mana creatures gives strong turn-4 plays that stay relevant as topdecks. Running no low-cost ramp avoids dead late-game draws. A ~$50 budget forces creative picks like Screamer Killer, Emergent Woodwurm, Tyrant's Familiar and Thunderfoot Baloth (safe since Radha is expendable). Many big creatures carry removal. Only 7 draw engines plus 2 spells are needed because high-cost threats stockpile in hand. The deck's strength is flexibilityΓÇöstockpiling board, hand, and lands simultaneously, and playing through board wipes well. Downsides: almost no instant-speed interaction, predictable curve, boring early turns, and a required aggressive mulligan policy. Ultimately the build chases a midrange battlecruiser vibe.

## Key Clips

- My Glissa deck is a battlecruiser deck that uses its command zone to give it a consistent, high-quality early creature, thereby allowing the cards in the 99 to be a pile of greedy, high-cost nonsense. This sort of massive-nonsense-tribal deck is really fun for when I don't wanna play a deck that's particularly challenging.
- Any ramp deck worth its salt needs to coordinate the ratio of ramp to other stuff, and also the ratio of low cost ramp to high cost ramp. Running a lot of low-cost ramp leads to beautiful curve-outs and buttery-smooth early-midgames, but it also leads to running out of cards faster and worsens the quality of later game topdecks.
- What if there was a way to do the inverse of what was done in the Goreclaw deckΓÇöto have the commander cover the low-cost ramp, and then run a massive pile of 4-cost ramp? This is where Radha comes back in as a two-drop mana dork that's available at all times.
- Green stompy decks tend to struggle with one type of card above all othersΓÇöboard wipes. While mana dorks often allow for faster acceleration than other types of ramp, they have the downside that they generally get eaten by board wipes alongside the big threats that prompted the wipe. The ramp suite I ended up landing on focused on a classicΓÇöExplosive Vegetation. I run all 10 of these effects that exist.
- I opted for 19 total cards that offer turn 3 ramp. That gives a 79% chance of drawing one in each opening hand, a figure that is high enough to leave some room for needing to mulligan for lands.
- I eventually decided on literally none at all, and this is for one key reason: cascade. If you simply don't run any cards that cost less than 4, you're guaranteed a pretty decent minimum level of value from your cascade trigger. Cascading for a four-or-less-cost card yields an 85% chance of finding a card with some sort of ramp attached.
- Tyrant's Familiar and Thunderfoot Baloth are quite powerful with a commander in play, but most decks can't take full advantage of them because opponents can simply remove their commander before attacks. However, since my commander is a mana dork that I don't really care about past turn 4 or 5, my opponents are far less eager to remove her, and she's pretty cheap to replay to get the lieutenant ability back online if they do remove her.
- The deck's biggest strength is its flexibility. Against faster decks it can dig in its heels and focus on staying alive until it has the resources to stabilize, knowing it'll easily outscale them later in the game, whereas against decks that go bigger than it in the late game it can advance a bunch of large creatures and turn them sideways to get that deck down to a manageable life total.
