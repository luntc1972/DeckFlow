## Summary

This video is a critical review of the Magic: The Gathering Spider-Man set, not a deck strategy guide, so it offers little competitive or deckbuilding insight. The reviewer argues the set fails because it references Spider-Man characters (Madame Webb, Spider-Punk, Ezekiel Sims) without providing narrative context, unlike the Final Fantasy set which taught the story through supporting cards. He also criticizes the gameplay designs as uninspired functional reprints: Iron Spider is essentially Steel Overseer, Miles Morales mimics a counter-doubling hydra, and Spectacular Spider-Man copies Selfless Spirit. The set crams 79 legendary creatures into under 200 cards, forcing reprints. He identifies only six or seven genuinely interesting cards: Behold the Sinister Six (mass reanimation), Norman Osborn (cost-reduction deck-building payoff), Peter Parker's Camera (limited resources), Imposter Syndrome (creature duplication on combat damage), Lady Octopus (one-mana card-draw counters into cheating things into play, called potentially insane), and the dual-color lands Oscorp Industries and Urban Retreat with alternate play options.

## Key Clips

- This reliance on old tropes and trite ideas was ultimately the main point of failure of Outlaws at Thunder Junction and Murder at Karlov Manor. And the lack of that is part of why I think Edge of Eternities and Tarkir Dragonstorm and Bloomburrow worked so well. They focused on what they wanted to say and showcase.
- This is a problem of information density. Many of the spider people and villains are too specific for me to be able to understand anything about them. But the cards that should usually provide the necessary context are too vague to do so.
- Ezekiel Sims, Spider Totem is a card with such a loaded name that it's completely incomprehensible, but its ability is plus two plus two to a spider once per turn. Expecting me to Google it to learn about it is a complete failure of game design.
- In my opinion, the best Universes Beyond set was Lord of the Rings. A big reason why Wizards were able to do such a great job is because Lord of the Rings is a fairly straightforward story. They showed us Frodo's environment, his struggles, and his journey. They didn't just point out he exists and then move on to Frodo Punk and Frodo UK.
- There was no need to try to showcase all of Spider-Man. They should have kept the scope smaller and more grounded. Make it a slice of Spider-Man, a specific story that really showcases what the character and his universe are about.
- Iron Spider, Stark Upgrade, a three mana colorless two-three with vigilance that taps to put a plus one plus one counter on each artifact creature and/or vehicle we control. It also gives the option to pay two mana and remove two counters from our artifacts to draw a card. This is just a Steel Overseer.
- These legendary named characters are functional reprints and they have to be because there are 79 of them in a set with not even 200 cards total. Compare that to Final Fantasy, which had 100 legendary creatures, but also 310 cards. It had a lesser legendary density and a much higher density of interesting legendaries.
- Going through the entirety of Spider-Man, there are only six or seven cards that I find genuinely interesting. Behold the Sinister Six is finally a mass reanimation spell that doesn't make me instantly concede. Lady Octopus, Inspired Inventor spooks me because she gets counters as we draw cards and can tap to put things into play, but she only costs one mana. Very strong.
