## Key Clips

- Interaction is one of the first things I check in my decks. I want to make sure I can deal with a threat of any type. So, can I remove an artifact, enchantment, creature, or land? All of these removal slots can be rolled up into one single card. Anything that destroys target permanent will cover almost all your bases.
- Chaos Warp is an awesome card here because it ticks every single box I have mentioned. It hits every type and gets around indestructible, making it a must-have in a lot of my casual decks. Lastly, you want cards to remove pieces that have protection ΓÇö hexproof, shroud, or some annoying ward cost. This can easily be covered by any board wipe.
- A lot of commander decks I build have cards in them that get there ΓÇö cards that can take over a game if they have the space to do so, usually with incremental advantage. Inevitability can be found in many places: Mind's Dilation, Portal to Phyrexia, Crucible of Worlds, Urza's Saga. These value engines mean your deck has staying power in the later portions of the game.
- This is why X spells are so good in Commander. A Genesis Wave for five can be a great value play. A Genesis Wave for 10 can be a game ender. Making sure you have some kind of X spell or mana sink will help you win games that go long.
- What all decks need is defense ΓÇö not for your life total necessarily, but for your strategy. Players fall into the idea of my deck does A, B, then C, but forget to ask: what if someone attacks me first? What if they remove your equipment? Ways to dodge board wipes, redirect interaction, or sneak past blockers can be great ways to add protection for your game plan.
- In casual commander, flexibility is king. I think slightly less mana efficient removal can be worth playing if the flexibility in what it removes goes up. Letting you see more cards via card draw means you can more likely find what card you actually need.
- MDFCs like Bala Ged Recovery help you protect your game plan by getting key cards back. All of these cards don't even take real slots in your deck since they can be lands if needed. I count MDFCs as lands when I'm building my decks. Combine these with flexible removal and card draw and you should, on average, find a piece of flexible removal sometime during the game.
- Building a deck that can win any game means you always have an out to play to. When things go wrong, you know there is something in your deck that can save you. One single Expropriate can flip that whole board state on its head. Play to this out ΓÇö chump block to get an extra turn, bounce a creature to save one more turn until you find that out.
