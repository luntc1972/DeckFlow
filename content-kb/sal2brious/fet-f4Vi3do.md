## Summary

This compilation gathers six months of patron deck reviews, each breaking down a Commander deck's gameplan and suggesting upgrades. Recurring evaluation themes: assess whether a deck's payoffs and enablers line up, flag unfocused or win-more packages, and check ramp/draw/land counts against the curve. Ghired EnchanTokens needs more ramp and draw and fewer weak token payoffs. Neheb Burn is commander-dependent but saved by strong ramp and filtering. Sisay plays as a secret Jodah deck wanting more ramp and removal. Toggo artifact-tokens runs odd payoffs (Academy Manufactor, Brass's Bounty) better replaced with land ramp. Living End is a fast graveyard deck wanting graveyard hate. Across reviews, the reviewer repeatedly recommends: cut cards that don't advance the core plan, add interaction (especially instant-speed removal and counterspells), protect key commanders, raise filtering/draw to avoid stall-outs, and balance fast-mana versus board-wipe vulnerability. Combo decks (Braids, Slimefoot) need more instant-speed interaction and fast mana. Aggro/voltron decks (shadow tribal, Tomb Raider) want more evasion and lifegain. The consistent verdict: tighten synergy, align every card to the gameplan, and add resilience without overloading on win-more escalation cards.

## Key Clips

- This deck does end up feeling a bit unfocused to me. First of all, the deck's payoffs don't always line up correctly. You've got token producers like Hallowed Haunting and Sigil of the Empty Throne that are creating big enough creatures to win all on their own, but you've also got ones like Felidar Retreat and Ajani's Chosen whose tokens likely won't do much without other payoff cards. My suggestion: yoink out some of the payoff, probably the small token creature stuff since it works least well with populate, and fill that space with ramp and draw.
- The enabler and payoff framework is entirely contingent on the board presence of a 5 mana commander. This seems like the deck's biggest weaknessΓÇöif the commander gets removed, things get a lot more awkward. The deck runs 6 protection spells, but this isn't nearly enough. However, it might be less severe than it seems, because of the deck's quantities of ramp and filteringΓÇöthe ramp can replay Neheb after he dies, and the filtering can get rid of dead payoff cards through looting effects.
- Sisay as a commander very much comes off as a grindy utility choice, but my guess is this is basically a secret Jodah deck, since that's the tutor choice with Sisay that immediately turns a bunch of 3-4 cost legendary creatures into a terrifying threat. I think shoehorning a bit more ramp into the deck will really pay off in the case where Jodah ends up getting removedΓÇöthe commander is still a mana sink, after all. I would also add a bit more interaction; its current removal suite is not very impressive.
- Academy Manufactor is here, but the deck only has 6 cards that produce treasures, clues, or foods, which isn't enough to make Manufactor a useful tech piece. Brass's Bounty seems a lot worse than just running a big piece of land ramp for purposes of getting a bunch of artifact tokens out. The cards that allow artifacts to tap for mana also seem great, since Thrasios can turn excesses of mana into card advantage.
- I playtested the Living End deck 10 times, and on the faster playtests it ended up with about 20 power on board on turn 4, which is not bad given that opposing boards are simultaneously wiped in a Living End. The speed of this gameplan is enough to feel pretty potent in mid-power games. A bit of graveyard hate might be a good idea, since otherwise the deck might run into the issue of opposing graveyards being too juicy to allow for a Living Death later in the game.
- As a proactive combo deck, Braids seems solid, but the combo occupies a pretty large footprintΓÇömore than 20 cards are involved in the combo in some way. This deck's biggest weakness is its interaction suite: two board wipes, three other sorcery-speed removal, and only three instant speed removal spells. My rough guess is this deck combos off around turn 5 on average. I'd fit in more instant speed removal, and cut some non-combo sac fodder for more fast mana artifacts to hit a turn 2 Braids trigger more often.
- Anhelo is a payoff with two different enabling factors that need to line upΓÇöhaving an instant or sorcery worth copying, and having a 2-or-greater power creature worth sacrificing. The deck has 7 different cards that generate creature tokens when instants or sorceries are cast, but these don't work well for getting copying online quicklyΓÇöyou effectively need two spells to duplicate your first spell. I'd swap some counterspells for modal ones like Confounding Riddle, and add Tormenting Voice effects like Big Score and Unexpected Windfall.
- The bigger issue with the Necrobloom deck is how much it relies on NecrobloomΓÇöthe card is starting to get onto peoples' radar as a spooky commander, and it's kind of needed to get stuff into the graveyard efficiently. As long as Astral Slide is in play the deck has a layer of protection, but getting there might be tricky if the first couple Necrobloom casts quickly eat removal. I'd add more protectionΓÇöClever Concealment could protect Astral Slide tooΓÇöand try more graveyard-filling options outside the commander, such as Stitcher's Supplier.
