## Summary

This video responds to viewer critiques of Magic: The Gathering's Eminence mechanic, defending it as fair and well-designed. To the claim Eminence is unfair because it grants a free, uninteractable per-turn benefit, the creator argues players undervalue a traditional command zone, which is effectively a free tutor plus a permanent eighth card. To the suggestion that Eminence should only work from the command zone (losing it if cast), he objects that this discourages casting your commander and that the best designs treat the creature and effect as a cohesive whole (Sidar Jabari of Zhalfir). Edgar Markov shows the real problem isn't access to both, but the effect's unstoppable token stream. He rejects gating Eminence behind casting the commander, since early availability enables a valuable two-tier design (Sidar, Arahbo aggro). On the strongest critiqueΓÇöuninteractivityΓÇöhe compares Eminence to planeswalker emblems, urges players to be flexible, attack multiple axes (life, library, hand), keep backup aggro plans, and target front-most threats rather than the engine. He concedes Eminence should have interactable moving parts and remains open to future designs like Sidar Jabari.

## Key Clips

- To start with I want to start off with probably the weakest argument about Eminence I would say and that is that the mechanic is inherently unfair.
- I think people tend to underestimate the value of a traditional command zone. They take it for granted. You basically get to tutor for a card for free at the start of every game. Think about it as a totally free tutor and a permanent eighth card slot in your hand. That is effectively what your command zone is.
- Another line of critique: Eminence should only work in the command zone. If you want the ability, you leave your commander uncast. But if you want it as a creature on board, it loses Eminence. My problem: people are playing an Eminence Commander for the Eminence effect, so they'll just never cast the creature, like Inalla.
- I think Eminence designs are at their best when they're designed to be a cohesive whole. Sidar Jabari of Zhalfir is a good example: the creature reanimates knights, the Eminence effect gives a little extra filtering with knights, but that combined package is really useful.
- Edgar Markov, the creature, is not very good. In a higher-powered deck you're not casting the creature; you're hitting the table with a billion one-drops. The problem with the card is that the Eminence effect is an unstoppable torrent of one-one vampire tokens ΓÇö very hard to hold back and just super easy value.
- A very cool thing about the Eminence effect is that it allows a two-tier effect. Arahbo is a great example: you get your plus-three plus-three bonus right from the start, but that benefit goes down over time, so later you play Arahbo the creature and get to double the power and toughness of a cat.
- The most popular and most valid critique, in 23 comments: Eminence is bad because you can't interact with it whatsoever. It just is. It's like starting the game with an emblem. I compare this to planeswalker emblems you can get the turn you play them ΓÇö Gideon Ally of Zendikar, Sorin Lord of Innistrad, Kaito Bane of Nightmares.
- If un-interactivity is your issue, I'd say two things. One, it's good if Commander players can be more flexible ΓÇö have a backup aggro/beatdown plan, attack different axes like life total, library, or hand. Two, I agree Eminence effects should have enough moving parts to be interactable-ish ΓÇö requiring an artifact, tokens, or a creature attacking is a thing you can interact with.
