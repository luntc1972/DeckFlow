## Summary

This is a milestone Q&A from the channel creator celebrating growth toward 15k subscribers. He outlines channel direction: weekly Thursday uploads alternating between high-effort deckbuilding deep-dives and lighter content like precon reviews, plus upcoming Patron deck reviews chosen by lottery and a Glissa deck tech on Patreon. On gameplay philosophy, he stresses matching deck power levels and holding rule 0 conversations—checking what opponents find fun before deploying board wipes, combos, stax, or suppressive decks; he'll even ask friends not to run certain decks (e.g., Tergrid). On the EDH ban list, he argues it awkwardly serves two masters: policing cEDH power (e.g., Dockside Extortionist matters little at midrange) versus banning unfun cards (Armageddon), and prefers rule 0 over new bans since EDH is fundamentally social. His pettiest ban target is Farewell, which he calls lazy design that crowds out Merciless Eviction and Austere Command. He praises political mechanics—Council's Dilemma, Will of the Council, goad, and Demonstrate—and wants more political cards using resources like cards in hand. He admires a Tymna/Jeska ball lightning tribal deck.

## Key Clips

- I learned to play magic in 2011, and the way I got into the game is that Mark Rosewater gave a career day presentation at my middle school, and at the end of it he handed all these little 11 and 12 year olds starter decks from M12 (I got the red one) and then I just went from there.
- I've always been a black player, and my first deck that I can remember was a sort of black lifegain-ish deck. It ran a lot of removal because that's just who I am as a player. The specific interaction I can remember was Tormented Soul, an unblockable creature, and Vampire Outcasts, a bloodthirst creature that almost always enters as a 4 mana 4/4 lifelink.
- Ashiok, Nightmare Weaver has always been a card I love. The place I've played with it was in standard Grixis control. I ran 3 of it, and it was fantastic. In the faster matchups it soaked up damage, and in the control matchups all the abilities were great. The +2 you can exile cards, great against Azorious control; the -10, if your opponent lets you get up there, just blows out a control deck.
- For starters it's good to make sure that decks are at similar power levels. Figure out what decks are doing, how fast they're doing them, and make sure things are compatible. For middling to lower power levels, check in with what people's intentions are for the game and what they find fun. There are things like board wipes or combos or heavy duty stax pieces where people might not have as much fun playing against them.
- The edh ban list is tricky because it's trying to be two different things: it's trying to dictate what's too strong for cEDH and also what's not fun for casual. Dockside Extortionist, for example—maybe it should be banned, but that would mainly be a ban for cEDH and top levels of high power casual, because it doesn't matter for random midrange beats decks. Similarly, Armageddon—you'd ban it because it's unfun, not because it's too strong.
- What I would encourage is players having more of a rule 0 conversation, a conversation about what they find fun and what they don't want to see in their game. You shouldn't roll up into a game with strangers and blow up all their lands if they're just trying to play a game with upgraded precons. EDH is a social format fundamentally.
- If I had to ban one card for very petty, personal reasons, it would be Farewell. I think it's very lazy design. It's a 6 mana board wipe that has no downsides other than that it's a 6 mana board wipe; it's probably not broken from a power level standpoint, but I just hate to see it. It also crowds out other cards like Merciless Eviction or Austere Command.
- I really like explicitly political cards. Council's Dilemma and Will of the Council are both cool mechanics with cool design space. I also think goad is a super cool mechanic, and it'd be cool to expand goad outside of creatures. Goad has a natural power limitation, that it's not going to be good above the beaty midrange power level. Demonstrate, the mechanic where you can copy a spell if you let your opponent have a copy, I think that one is kind of cool too.
