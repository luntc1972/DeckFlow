## Summary

The video answers "how many lands should my deck have?" using the creator's 41-land Radha battlecruiser deck (mana-dork commander, four-mana ramp spells, mostly 6-7 drops) as the example. Main factors: mana curve—higher-curved decks need more mana and thus more lands, while lower-curved decks win earlier and can run fewer ("you don't need to play a land on turn 7 if there is no turn 7"). Cheap draw/filtering lets you run fewer lands; expensive draw (four-plus mana) or conditional draw (e.g. Garruk's Uprising) wants more. A deck's ability to spend excess mana matters more than raw land count: big draw, a higher curve, MDFCs, X-costs, mana sinks (Captivating Crew, Panglacial Wurm), and utility lands all reduce flood risk. Radha has 5.33 average nonland cost, nothing under 4, and was bumped 40→41 because each land is effectively two mana when ramped. Mulligans: 42 lands maximizes 3-land opening hands, and running near your ideal land proportion frees mulligans to hunt nonlands (Radha wants ramp every game). Higher counts also enable extra-land-play cards and better timing of Bojuka Bog, Mystic Sanctuary, Mortuary Mire, Talon Gates of Madara. Storm/Jhoira/enchantress decks (high "Cantrip Coefficient") want few lands. He estimates ~38 for the median casual EDH deck

## Key Clips

- **[03:02]** Put simply, you don't need to play a land on turn 7 if there is no turn 7—lower-curved decks that win earlier want fewer lands.
- **[04:30]** When a deck can readily access powerful, mana-expensive draw, running a higher land count is an all-around win: either you haven't hit the mana to cast the draw yet (high count helps you get there), or you've cast it and are churning through cards, making the extra land density negligible.
- **[05:14]** A higher curve alone insulates against flooding, since playing a six-drop is twice as card-efficient as playing two three-drops—drawing lands is more problematic for some decks than others.
- **[06:37]** Each land is effectively worth two mana, so every land drop the deck ramps out instead of playing normally carries an expensive opportunity cost—he bumped Radha from 40 to 41 lands because of games spent paying mana for land drops he could have made normally.
- **[08:56]** 42 is the land count that offers the highest chance for a 3-land opening hand; running close to your ideal land proportion gives more wiggle room to mulligan for nonland cards—Radha wants a ramp piece every game.
- **[09:54]** A higher land count gives control over timing: a 32-land deck often must play a Bojuka Bog on curve to not miss a land drop, reducing its ETB value—same for Mystic Sanctuary, Mortuary Mire, and Talon Gates of Madara.
- **[10:54]** Storm decks, like many Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain builds, care about the top of their library more than any other deck and thus run a very low land count—the "Cantrip Coefficient."
