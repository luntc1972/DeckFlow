- So, I think it'd be really cool to brew symbiote Spider-Man, a blue and black commander that lets you consider equal number of damage you dealt to a player. And the really cool thing is if symbio Spider-Man is dead and in your graveyard, you can activate its ability and graft the symbiote onto another creature you control and pass on its ability, which I think is a really flavorful way to represent how symbiotes work in the Marvel lore.
- When it comes to stealing stuff, I think you should play these cards. Ether snatch, which is like a really flashy way to steal a spell off the stack. And X spells like blue suns twilight, dominate or entrancing melody to take a creature with lesser mana value. Govern the gateless lets you nab a single color creature. Invoke the wind steals a thing or an artifact. Tempted by the orc can steal a ton of things as long as they're cheap. And my personal favorite, the trickster gods heist, which lets us swap two creatures for a pretty cheap cost.
- Because you're stealing people's stuff, you're going to draw removal from the person you stole it from. So, you should play some protection spells like Champions Helm, Change of Plans, Ripples of Potential, and Slip Out the Back.
- Tempo is basically a deck that can shift between aggro and control pretty quickly and wants to play a small lean game where you can play cards like counter spell or lightning bolt to clear the way for your threats to close the game. It's not quite as controlling as hard control and not quite as aggressive as a pure aggro deck. It's something in between. And tempo classically plays a lot of cantrips like opt, pre-ordain, ponder, and consider because since you're playing a small game, being able to dig for the right card for the role you're playing at the moment is really important.
- The one thing that's really important to keep in mind here is the issue of scale, which makes tempo a tougher nut to crack in Commander. When you have one opponent, attacking with one or two threats with one or two card draw per turn might be good enough. When you have three opponents, it throws our whole valuation off. If our opponent plays a big creature and if we bounce it with a cheap spell, we've negated some tempo from our opponent. We haven't gained any tempo. We need a threat.
- When we're talking about scale, it means two things. We need engines that help replenish our cards at a greater rate than one card per turn. Like a trail of evidence that can tack on clues to our cantrips, or wave break hippo camp that can help our cards replace themselves each time we cast them on our opponent's turn. Nimris also achieves the same goal. Fairy Mastermind can generate a lot of cards if our opponents are also playing draw effects. And ledger shredder is a fantastic add because there's three times the potential for the connive trigger to help us loot for the right answer, and it can easily double up as a threat.
- The second point about scale is how we end the game. Since we're playing a lot of cantrips and spells in general, we want to maximize their value by leveraging cast triggers. Playing cards like Metallurgic Summonings, Murmuring Mystic, or Toleran Sky Summoner can help convert our spells into a go wide strategy. And as a pivot, there's also runechanter's pike or Jace's eidetic memory to help grow our main threat and gives us that axis of winning the game via a more voltron-like strategy. And counterbalance also scales really well in multiplayer since as soon as it hits more than once, it's rewarded you with more interaction than your usual one use counter spell.
- We should be playing draw effects that help put symbio into the graveyard like eviscerator's insight, fanatical offering, or nasty end. Deep analysis can be pitched into the graveyard by symbio Spider-Man to be flashbacked later. For removal, drown in the loch can be a counter spell or creature removal. Flare of Malice is another good way to sacrifice her commander, but also force each opponent to sacrifice the greatest mana value planeswalker or creature. There's also toxic deluge to help wipe the board, and since we want symbiote Spider-Man to go to the graveyard, it's totally okay that they get caught in the toxic deluge as well.
