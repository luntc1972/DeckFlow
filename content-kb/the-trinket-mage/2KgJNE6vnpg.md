## Summary

The video examines whether board wipes (wraths) have ruined casual Commander/EDH. The host argues it's more complicated than blaming wraths: the real issue is power creep. Threats have gotten faster and more dangerous (e.g., Adeline as a three-drop versus the old seven-mana Avenger of Zendikar), so wraths were powered up to match, which in turn buffed protection like phasing. Many recent board wipes (Farewell, Game Over, Withering Curse, Ultima, Final Showdown, Avatar's Wrath) are cheaper and exile or hit multiple card types, making them ubiquitous even in budget/precon decks. These full board wipes are best cast from behind as catch-up tools but prolong games, creating 'casual Commander purgatory.' One-sided wraths (Wave Goodbye, Cyclonic Rift) mitigate this only when you can pressure life totals. Wizards likely won't fix it (Farewell is now a game changer), so players must adapt: don't play zero wraths, sandbag cards rather than dumping your hand, add card draw and value engines, prefer selective wipes (Bane of Progress, Wrath of God), use a counterweight commander (Fladge in Boros control), and use goad, pillow fort (Ensnaring Bridge), and fog effects to control boards without clearing them.

## Key Clips

- Many players have ended up here in the bowels of casual EDH with nowhere to go and this is an issue largely caused by board wipes. Right? Well, I actually think it's a little bit more complicated than just blaming wraths.
- One of the main criticisms against board wipes is that it slows the game down. Most decks need some amount of time to build up to a lethal threat. And when you get rid of all that, then the turns it takes to get back to that point of threatening lethal is completely reset, which does make the game go for longer.
- If you got nothing on board and your opponents have tons of stuff, then that's when you get the most value out of a board wipe. And when you're losing on board, the more types of cards the board wipe hits, the better. It is simply the best way to catch up to three players ahead of you.
- Withering Curse and Game Over are some of the lowest cost hard board wipes we've ever seen. Three mana is pretty efficient. You used to have to lose half your life total to do that. Powerful board wipes that can exile all creatures or all of something have been printed a lot more, and ones that hit multiple card types as well.
- Because threats are better, removal gets buffed, which means protection gets buffed. The issue is this rock paper scissors type effect doesn't work all the time. If you don't actually have the protection, then their buffed up removal is just way better and completely wipes your board.
- If everyone is on these wraths that clear everything, the real solution is sandbagging. Simply don't empty your hand. One of the most common ways players lose Commander games is dumping their hand, getting hit by board wipe, and having nothing to do. If you hold an extra piece in hand or don't cast your commander immediately, you can bait out the first board wipe and recover a lot faster.
- You don't actually need a wrath specifically. Goad is one of the best ways to get around a big board without clearing it. Take the bait, taunt from the ramparts, and illusionist gambit can buy you a turn or two without clearing the board. Pillow fort cards work similarly. A ghostly prison, crawlspace, or Kazul can make people think twice about attacking you.
- Play more burst damage so you can turn the corner after a board wipe faster. Sandbag a bit more so these wipes don't take out all your resources. Play some of the other new things Wizards has added like Goad or forcing damage elsewhere with Pillowfort effects. They haven't ruined Commander.
