## Summary

The creator advocates for land-based ramp in blue Commander decks as a sweeper-resistant alternative to mana rocks. Blue's unblockable value creatures—Shoreline Looter, Malcolm, Alluring Scoundrel—are ideal carriers for attack-triggered equipment ramp like Sword of the Animist and Explorer's Scope, since unblockability guarantees triggers. Two synergistic paths branch from this base. First, a graveyard package using flip-lands: Looters fill the graveyard passively, enabling transformations of Search for Survivors, The Everflowing Well, and Mizzix's Mastery-style cards into lands; Dreamscape Artist and Mazalantli also feed this. Second, Dowsing Dagger, which flips into Lost Vault (a land tapping for two mana) when combat damage connects—trivial with unblockable creatures—then gets duplicated via Copy Land and Realmscapes for free mana. Blue's land-and-ability copying (virtue of knowledge, GoGo, Twincast) can clone Solemn Simulacrum, fetchland triggers, or even opponents' ramp spells. Mythic Manipulation grabs duplicate basics for free. The through-line is flexibility: these cards serve dual purposes beyond ramp. The creator argues that against green, out-ramping or denying ramp beats mass land destruction, and encourages creative land ramp in every color.

## Key Clips

- Blue can land ramp, and it's actually surprisingly good at it. Many of its pieces connect very nicely into the other cards in the package, all while offering great alternative utility.
- Blue has access to lots of small evasive value creatures like Shoreline Looter, Malcolm, and Ledger Shredder. They are perfect carriers of equipment ramp like Sword of the Animist (search for a basic on attack) and Explorer's Scope (put a land into play from the top of library on attack). Because Shoreline Looter can't be blocked, it turns from a small value creature into a nasty ramping machine.
- First path: a graveyard package centered around fliplands. Looters passively fill the graveyard, which works with Search for Tomorrow's counter (surveil in upkeep, flips to Ascent the Sunken Ruin at 7+ cards) and The Everflowing Well (mills two, draws two, flips when 8 permanents are in the graveyard). Just lands and some creatures will do fine — no need for a full self-mill package.
- Second path: Dowsing Dagger, a two-mana equipment that flips into Lost Vault, a land tapping for free mana of any color. It's exceptionally strong because you can tap it for mana the moment it flips, refunding most of the investment, and unblockable creatures make flipping trivially easy. Blue's land-copy spells like Copy Land and Realmscape can then copy Lost Vault — and it isn't legendary, so you can have multiple, each tapping for free mana.
- The important through line for blue ramp: many of the cards serve other purposes too. Ascent filters draws, Realmscape can copy a creature, Copy Enchantment and Mirror Mate are good regardless. Flexibility is the strength of blue ramp.
- Cards like Virtue of Knowledge, Adric Mathematical Genius, and Gyome let you copy activated and triggered abilities — copy the activated ability of an Evolving Wilds to search for an additional land. That's just efficient ramping, same mana cost as Rampant Growth. You can also copy your opponent's ramp with Narset's Reversal or Twincast to snatch their Cultivate.
- Mimic Manipulation, a free-mana sorcery, looks at the top seven cards and puts one onto the battlefield if it shares a name with a permanent already there. With an island out, grab another island and put it into play — literally a ramp spell, as long as you play enough basics.
- Against ramp decks, mass land destruction is a stupid idea — they'll just outramp you from state zero. Instead, stop them from ramping in the first place with stax pieces like Aven Mindcensor or Confounding Conundrum, or be more creative with your own ramp. Blue ramp is also much more resilient to sweepers: Planar Cleansing won't hit lands made by Copy Land.
