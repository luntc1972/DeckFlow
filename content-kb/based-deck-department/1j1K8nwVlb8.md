## Summary

A 30-year player gives 10 Commander deckbuilding lessons. Build one big "snowball": every card should support a single win condition; add resilience to the primary plan rather than alternate win-cons, even in combo decks. Prefer reliable "guaranteed good" cards over high-upside situational ones. Run lesser-known cards to surprise opponents: Tangle over Fog, Riot Control or Congregate for life, Daze(?)/thought(?) to bait counters, Massacre Wurm over Toxic Deluge vs tokens. Avoid win-more cards that only work when already winning. He praises high-impact instant-speed cards affecting many permanents: combat tricks (Tangle, Etherize, Domineering Will, others(?)), phase-out (Teferi's Protection, Clever Concealment, Galadriel's Dismissal—doubles as a fog), protection (Dawn Charm(?), Flare of Fortitude, Flawless Maneuver, Heroic Intervention), mass bounce (Evacuation, Engulf the Shore). Fundamentals ("eat your vegetables"): at least 38 lands (non-negotiable; MDFCs count as spells only when dead), 10 mana acceleration, 10 single-target interaction, 10 card-advantage sources, 2+ board wipes (ideally asymmetrical). Keep the curve low; balance proactive vs reactive. Avoid autopilot cards that don't fit (Cultivate/Kodama's Reach with a 3-mana commander; symmetrical wipes like Blasphemous Act/Farewell in Voltron vs Single Combat/Slaughter the Ranks). Run 4+ of any needed category (double for faster games). Build budget decks to sharpen skills.

## Key Clips

- **[01:19]** To get the biggest snowball we need to ensure that every card in our deck supports and synergizes with a very singular focus — whether that's Commander damage, a wide pumped board, or a big flashy combo. A plan can have multiple cards that enable a win, but the strategy or type of win condition should be the same.
- **[02:19]** You should choose a guaranteed good thing over a maybe great thing — choosing cards that are more boring but more reliable over high-upside but more limited cards. That niche card or elaborate combo is great the one time it works, but it clouds your judgment about the nine games where you crashed and burned.
- **[03:22]** Don't just run a fog when you can run a tangle to turn that huge attack into two turns of punishment, or riot control to gain a bunch of life, or a congregate to gain even more life when the table thinks they have you on the ropes. Run cards people least expect.
- **[03:46]** There's nothing quite like declaring a board wipe and then seeing the look on the token player's face after you play a massacre worm — and not a toxic Deluge — and one-shot them.
- **[04:25]** For a card to be win-more it requires your deck to already be doing the thing in order to function. These cards create super high highs but bring many more lows and drag your deck's average performance way down. Instead fill your deck with cards that get you to that win state.
- **[06:59]** Eat your vegetables: run at least 38 lands and that is non-negotiable, at least 10 sources of mana acceleration, at least 10 forms of single-target interaction, at least 10 sources of card advantage, and at least two board wipes — ideally asymmetrical.
- **[10:10]** Autopilot decisions are cards that don't fit what the deck is trying to do — like running cultivate and Kodama's Reach when you have a three-mana commander who is almost always your turn-three play, or running ramp spells when your commander already fulfills that job.
- **[10:26]** If you're running a Voltron commander, don't run symmetrical board wipes that also blow up your commander like Blasphemous Act or Farewell — run ones that work in your favor like Single Combat or Slaughter the Ranks.
