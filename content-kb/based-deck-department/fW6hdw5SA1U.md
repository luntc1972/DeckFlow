## Summary

Deckbuilding advice from a Kratos (God of War Secret Lair) commander review and general Q&A. Kratos: 3-mana mono-red, double strike, all creatures haste, pings each player for creatures that didn't attack. Build as fast aggro/Voltron for bracket 3 (bracket 4 unrealistic—red game changers weak, mono-red can't compete without combos). Core buckets: game accelerants (Furnace of Wrath, Dictate/Gratuitous Violence damage doublers, Descent into Avernus, Mana Barbs/Burning Earth/Spell Shock punisher effects), commander protection, cheap card draw that fires immediately (Grab the Prize, Unexpected Windfall—avoid delayed draw like swords), power-DOUBLING over more double strike, lifelink to survive. Cut colorless/tap lands (hurt turn-3 Kratos), slow equipment, removal (you WANT opponents attacking), ramp that can't accelerate to Kratos. Keep Secret Lair cards (Sulfuric Vortex, Pyrohemia, World at War). Principles: build backward from wincon; cards should fill primary role with secondary bonus; ~11 sources for opening-hand cards, ~6 for late; 5-6 themes per deck; match power to bracket (don't oppress bracket 2); use AI to find cards, not to think.

## Key Clips

- **[11:38]** When I build a deck I give each card a primary category. A card can fulfill a secondary role, but if my deck needs eight to nine cost reducers, I commit that slot to the card that needs to do that job — it has to have a primary function first.
- **[14:05]** People think one color is a downside, but the modern game has homogenized — even red now has removal and protection spells. Some of the most powerful commander decks are one color, so build around whatever commander you enjoy.
- **[23:00]** The problem with a card like Elsha is that if you hit a land off the top you're kind of screwed. So you really want a lot of card draw, so you can draw through the lands when you hit them.
- **[23:47]** Pantlaza is a great beginner commander because its ability is powerful but simple — once per turn a dinosaur enters and you discover. You don't have to build around intricate combos; the game plan is clear and defined.
- **[27:04]** For bracket two, two key things: build a deck that naturally wins around turn nine or ten, not through sandbagging, and don't impact others too heavily — you probably don't want effects like Grave Pact where each opponent sacrifices a creature.
- **[40:33]** To make a Voltron deck consistent, keep your commander protected and keep it big, then draw lots of cards so you keep drawing more protection. If you can keep it protected and connecting, you're onto the right thing.
- **[84:07]** Toolbox commanders like Brea are far better at lower power — bracket two and three — because toolbox decks excel by grinding value over time. The moment you get to bracket four you need a linear game plan to win by turn four or five.
- **[89:29]** When adding ramp, first ask: does my deck actually want ramp, and what is it ramping toward? With a four-mana commander like Kua, a three-mana rock adds no purpose unless you're ramping into big spells you cast later.
