## Summary

This video is an interview where guest Cubrious Snail presents his favorite Commander deck, a budget (~$60) bracket-3 Radha, Heart of Keld build. The deck plays nothing below four mana except the commander: Radha ramps from two to four, enabling a turn-three suite of four-mana ramp spells like Explosive Vegetation, then snowballs via cascade cards that reliably roll into more ramp. Draw is intentionally light because the high curve keeps cards in hand, mana sinks (Kessig Wolf Run, Captivating Crew) spend excess mana, and land-thinning plus cascade raise deck velocity. A small caves package supports Scampering Surveyor, Cosmic Confluence, and sacrifice-lands (Hidden Nursery/Volcano). The deck wins as a slow midrange 'archenemy,' deliberately avoiding fast or haste-granting combos so opponents can fight back; he even house-ruled Bigger on the Inside plus Stone-Seeder Hierophant to disable an accidental storm loop. It leans on repeatable sorcery-speed removal enginesΓÇöGruul Ragebeast, Thornling Mammoth, Screamer-Killer (a budget Terror of the Peaks), Volcanic TorrentΓÇöto suppress opponents' boards. Tyrant's Familiar exploits the expendable commander. Pet/payoff cards like Pang-Glacial Worm, Sunbird's Invocation, Wondrous Crucible, and Soulfire Eruption fit because cutting low-cost filler leaves ample slots.

## Key Clips

- It is my Rada Erdelt deck. Rada Erdelt is a two mana gruul creature that has tap add one green to your mana pool and whenever she attacks you add two red to your mana pool which goes away by the second main phase, but you can use it for instant speed stuff. The idea of the deck is you play her on turn two and then turn three you have a suite of four mana ramp spells like explosive vegetation that find two lands and put them onto the field, and then the whole rest of the deck is higher cost stuff, a lot of which has cascade.
- You play literally nothing that costs less than four mana. Just the commander. Nothing in the deck costs less than four mana. I know there are some versions that could include a really useful three mana card like a tireless provisioner, but I've opted not to do that. Keep it simple. Nothing below four mana.
- The deck is relatively thin on draw for two reasons. For one, the deck is very high curved, which lets it keep cards in hand longer. I'm playing nothing but six, five, seven cost cards. The second reason is the deck is very high velocity. My lands get thinned out quickly by all my ramp spells, and my cascade cards are guaranteed to hit a non-land card, so the deck is effectively smaller than a 99 card deck.
- I consider this a pretty firmly bracket three deck. In principle the game plan is a bracket 2 game plan, but it is so thoroughly optimized that playing it with precons you will just stomp them. One issue is it does not have a whole lot of instant speed interaction, but it is very dense on removal engines. You can knock down key opposing pieces pretty early and it does a decent job of suppressing opponents at sorcery speed.
- Tyrant's Familiar is a seven mana dragon, 5/5 flying haste, with lieutenant. If you control your commander it gets plus two plus two and when it attacks it shoots seven damage to a creature. It's very strong as long as your commander isn't a removal magnet. In this deck I don't really care about my commander past turn five, so opponents can remove my commander but that's not two-for-oneing me.
- On the 'just bolt the Rada on turn two' criticism: this commander hops me from two to four mana. It makes my slow game plan a little faster. If it gets removed, then I play a turn slower, and that's not the end of the world. Bolting the opposing commander early usually results in a huge disadvantage to the person casting that removal because you're essentially skipping your own turn to stop an opponent.
- I had a nondeterministic storm combo with Bigger on the Inside and a stone-seeder hierophant land that untaps on landfall. Tap a land with Bigger on the Inside, get cascade on your next spell, cascade into a four mana ramp spell which untaps the hierophant land twice, add more mana, get stacking cascades, and theoretically go through your whole library. I slipped a note into the card errata-ing that I cannot do it, because that's not what the deck is trying to do ΓÇö I want to cultivate an archenemy vibe and give opponents a chance to fight back.
- Volcanic Torrent is a five mana cascade card that deals X damage to each creature opponents control where X is the number of spells you've cast this turn. Early game it'll typically be two damage but later four, five, six. Even at turn five two damage clears a lot of boards. And at five mana cost it's on the lower end for this list, so my six mana cascade spells can hit it for free extra.
