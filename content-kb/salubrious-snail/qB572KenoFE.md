## Summary

This video argues Stax is not a uniquely objectionable card type in EDH but sits on a spectrum with commonly accepted cards. It frames mass land destruction (Armageddon) as taboo mainly for social reasons, and notes ways to prepare: indestructible lands, creature/artifact ramp, land recursion, Second Sunrise effects, extra land drops, and lower curves. Stax is defined as resource denial that breaks parity. It distinguishes primary resources (cards, mana) from secondary (the win condition), casting Stax as attacking primary resources and control as attacking secondary ones; in EDH the two blur, since board wipes (Cyclonic Rift, Vandalblast) and graveyard/nonbasic hate (Blood Moon) also strip mana. It flags Teferi's Protection and The One Ring as heavily agency-denying yet accepted. For mid-power, it recommends 'incomplete' board wipes like Cataclysmic Gearhulk, land hate that spares some lands (Keldon Firebombers, Urza's Sylex), soft hate (taplands, Endurance/Pit of Offerings/Emperor of Bones for graveyards), tax effects (Thorn of Amethyst), Rule of Law effects, and life-loss 'slug' stax (Liesa, Rug of Smothering) that pressures without removing agency. It praises recent designs (Magebane Lizard, Aven Interruptor, High Noon) and says hard stax stays best in cEDH.

## Key Clips

- **[01:33]** Ways to mitigate mass land destruction like Armageddon: play more indestructible lands, diversify your ramp with creature and artifact-based ramp, emphasize land recursion or run Second Sunrise effects, run more extra land play effects while keeping lands in hand, and run lower-curved decks that need less mana.
- **[07:35]** 'Just play removal' works equally well for a stax piece like Ward of Bones as it does for a turbocharged engine like Chimil the Inner Sun, even though the former draws far more side-eye than the latter.
- **[07:55]** Teferi's Protection shuts down all board interaction and forms of damaging a player for a full turn cycle for only 3 mana, with a counterspell the only counterplay; its effect would be fair at 5. The One Ring has a similar stapled-on invulnerability clause.
- **[14:22]** Reframing proactive vs reactive: Stax attacks primary resources — the basic building blocks (cards and mana) required to play — while control attacks secondary resources, the ones required to win.
- **[18:24]** Green gets a pass: Rampant Growth is the same rate as most mana rocks but isn't destroyed by artifact wipes, so non-green colors must fear wipes eating their mana while green ramp is exempt.
- **[25:00]** A board wipe he likes is Cataclysmic Gearhulk — it's an incomplete wipe letting each player keep one permanent of each type, punishing players who are ahead more, and in his Sharuum deck it's an artifact for reanimation and a creature with an ETB for flicker.
- **[26:07]** Incomplete land hate like Keldon Firebombers and Urza's Sylex is less punishing because players keep some lands; keeping even three lands leaves many spells castable and lets players choose their colors.
- **[28:41]** Life-loss 'slug' stax like Liesa and Rug of Smothering preserve agency by charging life instead of denying actions, enabling a stax-aggro blend that pressures opponents' decisions without flatly telling them no.
